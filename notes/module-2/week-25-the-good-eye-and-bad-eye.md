# The Good eye and bad Eye

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 25 ·  · 1:14:27

**Themes:** rasa-lila · sahajiyaism · good eye and bad eye · hankering · regulative principles · lord chaitanya's mercy

## Summary

Speaking on the Caitanya-caritamrta chapter 'The Lord Tastes Nectar' — which recounts Krishna's confidential pastimes with the gopis that Lord Caitanya relished for eighteen years at Jagannatha Puri in his Antya-lila — Indradyumna Swami explains that neophyte devotees should hear these wholly transcendental pastimes as purifying medicine rather than try to imitate or enter them prematurely. He repeatedly warns against sahajiya cheating (taking devotion cheaply, enjoying the Lord's intimate lilas while still attached to sense gratification), illustrated by the Bengali image of a horse that tries to eat grass while jumping and by Prabhupada rebuking disciples who visit Radha-kunda without being able to follow even the first verse of the Upadeshamrta. The heart of the talk is Prabhupada's instruction to see with 'a good eye and a bad eye': a preacher and Prabhupada's purports reveal two things at once — the face of Maya (the material world seen for the misery it is) and the face of Krishna — so that hearing of Krishna's sweetness awakens a strong hankering for Him instead of envy of materialists. Swami stresses that devotees are not missing anything now, being already surrounded by Krishna's mercy in the holy name, guru, deities, prasada and books, and that the path is 'perfect, more perfect, most perfect,' nectar 'from the word go.' He teaches that the four regulative principles are not austerities but simply giving up suffering, and that the process, though simple, is not cheap — one must throw off the 'sandbags' of attachment, as a hot-air balloon must, to rise. Through many stories — fool's gold in the Sierra Nevadas, a child's bicycle training wheels representing Guru and Gauranga, Prabhupada offering a chain-smoking life member the 'special mercy' of losing everything, and Narada and Shiva relishing a fragment of Krishna's prasada — he shows that devotional service is more powerful than God Himself and is freely bestowed by the magnanimous Lord Caitanya, though it must be received by paying the price of full surrender, with guru-shastra-sadhu as one's balance.

## Key Points

- Neophytes should hear Krishna's confidential rasa-lila pastimes as purifying medicine rather than imitate them; even one agitated by lust is purified because the pastimes are entirely transcendental (Prabhupada's Krishna book). Prabhupada told a life member 'You may be fit to hear rasa-lila, but I do not feel qualified to speak on it' to stress how confidential the subject is.
- Sahajiyaism (cheap imitation of advanced devotion while indulging the senses) is strongly condemned: the Bengali proverb of a jumping horse trying to eat grass, Dinananda Maharaj's 'you can't chew sugarcane and whistle at the same time,' the couple dressed as Radha-Krishna kissing at Keshi-ghat in Vrindavan, and Prabhupada's 'give him a good kick in the ribs' test for the New York man feigning ecstatic symptoms in 1971.
- Central teaching — the 'good eye and bad eye': a preacher and Prabhupada's purports reveal both the face of Maya (the material world seen for the place of repeated birth-and-death misery it is) and the face of Krishna. The purports are 'windows' to both the spiritual and material worlds — like watching rain from inside a dry house, or the Bengali saying of catching a fish without getting wet; and like great painters using clear northern light, the devotee illuminates the subject so there is no doubt.
- Devotees already possess Krishna's full association and mercy now — holy name, guru, Vaishnavas, deities, temple, prasada, Prabhupada's books, and the dhamas (Vrindavan, Mayapur, Jagannatha Puri) — so there is no cause to lament missing the higher pastimes or to envy materialists; the process is 'perfect, more perfect, most perfect' and nectar 'from the word go' (final story of a 1970 Poland/harinama photo mistaken for a recent one).
- The four regulative principles (no meat-eating, intoxication, illicit sex, gambling) are not austerity but simply 'giving up suffering' — like refusing a pharmacy sale on cancer and disease; the process is simple (just chant) but not cheap (one must follow the rules of sadhana bhakti), and one must throw off the 'sandbags' of attachment, as a hot-air balloon must jettison ballast to rise. Bicycle training wheels stand for Guru and Gauranga keeping the beginner's balance.
- Devotional service is rarer and more powerful than God Himself — Srimati Radharani controls Krishna, who therefore does not give bhakti easily but hands wealth, sex, and fame to demons instead; yet Lord Caitanya, the most magnanimous incarnation (Rupa Goswami's 'namo maha-vadanyaya'), freely bestows it on anyone. The Narada–Shiva–Parvati prasada pastime (told by Tamal Krishna Maharaj) shows the Vaishnava's defining compassion — Parvati's wish that ALL living beings receive the mercy — as a reason Lord Caitanya appeared.
- To reciprocate with Krishna as Draupadi (protected by the unlimited sari), the Pandavas (Kunti seeing Krishna in every danger), and Hanuman (embraced by Rama, his heart holding only Sitaram — no dhanam, janam, or sundarim) did, one must have their bhakti and be willing to sacrifice everything; the Sanatana Goswami touchstone story shows the holy name is the most valuable gift. In the Q&A, the pyrite/gold scale is compared to guru-shastra-sadhu and knowing Prabhupada's books as the devotee's balance for testing genuine Krishna consciousness.

## Scriptural References

- Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila — the chapter titled 'The Lord Tastes Nectar,' on Krishna's confidential gopi pastimes relished by Lord Caitanya for eighteen years at Jagannatha Puri (subject of the whole lecture).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 2.3.10 — 'akamah sarva-kamo va moksha-kama udara-dhih' — whether full of material desires, free of desire, or seeking liberation, one should worship the Supreme; cited so that everyone benefits from hearing Krishna's pastimes.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.16 — 'susrusoh sraddadhanasya vasudeva-katha-rucih...' (paraphrased) — by serving devotees free from vice one gains an affinity for hearing the message of Vasudeva.
- Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya 17.133 — 'nama cintamanih krsnas caitanya-rasa-vigrahah' — the Lord's name, fame, and pastimes are identical with Krishna Himself.
- Nectar of Devotion / Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu — 'adau sraddha tatah sadhu-sango...anartha-nivrttih syat' — the progressive stages of devotional service; used to explain that the first symptom of advancement is giving up sense gratification (anartha-nivrtti).
- Nectar of Instruction (Upadeshamrta) by Rupa Goswami — verse 1 ('vaco vegam...' — controlling the urges of speech, mind, and senses) contrasted with verse 10 (glories of Radha-kunda); Prabhupada called disciples 'rascals' for going to Radha-kunda unable to follow even the first verse.
- Bhagavad-gita 15.15 — 'sarvasya caham hrdi sannivisto mattah smrtir jnanam apohanam ca' — from Krishna come knowledge, remembrance, and forgetfulness; even the demon depends on God to forget God.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.88.8 — 'yasyaham anugrhnami harishye tad-dhanam sanaih' — Krishna's first mercy is to take away His devotee's material opulence; quoted (speaker attributed it to the Eleventh Canto) in the story of the smoking life member offered 'special mercy.'
- Bhagavad-gita 8.16 — 'abrahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna' — from the highest planet to the lowest, all are places of misery and repeated birth and death; cited as the transcendental knowledge of the 'bad eye.'
- Bhagavad-gita 4.7 — 'yada yada hi dharmasya glanir bhavati bharata...' — whenever dharma declines the Lord descends personally; Krishna comes once in a day of Brahma.
- Sri Gurvastaka verse 8 — 'yasya prasadad bhagavat-prasado, yasyaprasadan na gatih kuto 'pi' — by the mercy of guru one obtains the mercy of Krishna; cited as the Lord sending guru to the sincere seeker.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.10 — 'tesam satata-yuktanam bhajatam priti-purvakam, dadami buddhi-yogam tam' — to His loving devotees Krishna gives the intelligence to reach Him (the Supersoul in the heart, obscured by the 'static' of false ego).
- Siksastakam verse 2 — 'namnam akari bahudha nija-sarva-saktis...' — the holy name bestows all benedictions and contains all the Lord's transcendental energies, yet 'unfortunate as I am, I have no attraction'; all opulences are present in Hare Krishna.
- Siksastakam verse 4 — 'na dhanam na janam na sundarim kavitam va jagad-isa kamaye...' — no desire for wealth, followers, or a beautiful woman, only causeless devotional service birth after birth; applied to Hanuman's heart (only Sitaram) vs. the neophyte's.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 5.5.8 — 'pumsah striya mithuni-bhavam etam...' — the knot of mutual attraction between man and woman binds one to material life; cited on giving up illicit sex.
- Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya 19.151 — 'brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija' — by the mercy of guru and Krishna the fortunate soul receives the seed of the devotional creeper (bhakti-lata-bija), already planted in every heart present.
- Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya 19.53 — 'namo maha-vadanyaya krsna-prema-pradaya te...' — Rupa Goswami's praise of Lord Caitanya as the most magnanimous incarnation who freely gives krsna-prema to everyone without distinction.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 11.5.32 — 'krsna-varnam tvishakrsnam sangopangastra-parsadam...' — the yuga-avatar description, cited as one of the reasons Lord Caitanya appeared.
- Vaishnava pranama mantra — 'vancha-kalpa-tarubhyas ca krpa-sindhubhya eva ca, patitanam pavanebhyo vaisnavebhyo namo namah' — obeisances to the Vaishnavas who are desire-trees and oceans of mercy, purifiers of the fallen; cited on Vaishnava compassion (Parvati wishing mercy for all).
- Prabhupada's Krishna book — cited for the teaching that Krishna's pastimes benefit everyone (even the low-born and the sex-agitated) and are absolute in every feature.
- Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta (Satsvarupa dasa Goswami) — named as a book to read to know Prabhupada's life and philosophy and avoid being cheated (Q&A on guru-shastra-sadhu).

## Glossary

- **rasa-lila** — Krishna's confidential dance pastime with the gopis of Vrindavan
- **Antya-lila** — the final portion of Lord Chaitanya's pastimes at Jagannatha Puri
- **sahajiya** — one who takes devotion cheaply, imitating advanced stages while still sense-enjoying
- **sadhana-bhakti** — regulated devotional practice following the rules and regulations
- **anartha-nivritti** — the stage of clearing away unwanted things and bad habits
- **Upadeshamrta** — Rupa Goswami's Nectar of Instruction; verse one urges controlling the senses
- **Radha-kunda** — the topmost holy pond in Vrindavan, most dear to Radha and Krishna
- **shastra-chakshush** — seeing things through the eyes of scripture
- **guru-shastra-sadhu** — the threefold authority of guru, scripture, and saints, one's balance in devotional life
