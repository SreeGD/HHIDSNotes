# The Good eye and bad Eye

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 25 ·  · 1:14:27

**Themes:** rasa-lila · sahajiyaism · good eye and bad eye · hankering · regulative principles · lord chaitanya's mercy

## Summary

Speaking on the Caitanya-caritamrta chapter 'The Lord Tastes Nectar' — which recounts Krishna's confidential pastimes with the gopis that Lord Caitanya relished for eighteen years at Jagannatha Puri in his Antya-lila — Indradyumna Swami explains that neophyte devotees should hear these wholly transcendental pastimes as purifying medicine rather than try to imitate or enter them prematurely. He repeatedly warns against sahajiya cheating (taking devotion cheaply, enjoying the Lord's intimate lilas while still attached to sense gratification), illustrated by the Bengali image of a horse that tries to eat grass while jumping and by Prabhupada rebuking disciples who visit Radha-kunda without being able to follow even the first verse of the Upadeshamrta. The heart of the talk is Prabhupada's instruction to see with 'a good eye and a bad eye': a preacher and Prabhupada's purports reveal two things at once — the face of Maya (the material world seen for the misery it is) and the face of Krishna — so that hearing of Krishna's sweetness awakens a strong hankering for Him instead of envy of materialists. Swami stresses that devotees are not missing anything now, being already surrounded by Krishna's mercy in the holy name, guru, deities, prasada and books, and that the path is 'perfect, more perfect, most perfect,' nectar 'from the word go.' He teaches that the four regulative principles are not austerities but simply giving up suffering, and that the process, though simple, is not cheap — one must throw off the 'sandbags' of attachment, as a hot-air balloon must, to rise. Through many stories — fool's gold in the Sierra Nevadas, a child's bicycle training wheels representing Guru and Gauranga, Prabhupada offering a chain-smoking life member the 'special mercy' of losing everything, and Narada and Shiva relishing a fragment of Krishna's prasada — he shows that devotional service is more powerful than God Himself and is freely bestowed by the magnanimous Lord Caitanya, though it must be received by paying the price of full surrender, with guru-shastra-sadhu as one's balance.

## The Chapter and Its Confidential Subject Matter

- This chapter of **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** is entitled **"The Lord Tastes Nectar."** It contains many verses about Krishna's most confidential pastimes with the gopīs of Vṛndāvana.
- Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu relished these pastimes for **18 years at Jagannātha Purī**; this final period of His life is called **Antya-līlā** (the final pastimes).
- We too may relish these pastimes of Krishna with His most intimate gopīs, but we must appreciate them **according to our own level of advancement.** A pure devotee may actually participate in such līlā; our position as neophyte/younger devotees is simply to **hear** these pastimes and become purified.

## Everyone Benefits by Hearing Krishna's Pastimes

- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains in the **Kṛṣṇa book** that Krishna's pastimes are beneficial for everyone — even the low-born and fallen person is benefited; aspirants relish them; liberated souls also relish them.
- **Bhāgavatam principle** (paraphrased): "Whether one is full of material desires, whether one has no material desires, or whether one desires liberation, in all circumstances one should worship the complete whole, the Personality of Godhead." So everyone benefits.
- Prabhupāda states in the Kṛṣṇa book that **one whose mind is agitated by sex desire is recommended to hear the pastimes of the gopīs dancing with Krishna in the rāsa dance.**
  - *Objection*: If one's mind is agitated by sex desire, why hear of the Lord's intimate pastimes with the gopīs?
  - *Answer*: These pastimes are **completely transcendental** and have nothing to do with the material world; anything transcendental purifies our heart. All of Krishna's pastimes are absolute — whether dancing with the gopīs or acting as the **Puruṣa-avatāras** within the material world (**Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu**) — completely free from any tinge of material quality. So even one in the materialistic conception becomes purified by hearing them.
- This is how we associate with Krishna at this stage: we **hear** His pastimes, and gradually, as the heart is purified, we can enter into them.
  - Two devotees hearing rāsa-līlā: one is **relishing**, another is **taking it as medicine** — but both are benefiting.

## The Life Member Who Dressed the Deities in Black

- Prabhupāda once visited a life member in India who had Rādhā-Krishna deities he often dressed in black. When asked why, he said **Krishna is so effulgent that if he didn't dress them in black he couldn't see Him.**
- This life member would never bow down to the deities, because he and his wife imagined themselves as **Krishna's mother and father, Nanda and Yaśodā.** Prabhupāda did not appreciate this.
- On several occasions this member asked Prabhupāda to speak about **rāsa-līlā.** Prabhupāda replied: **"You may be fit to hear rāsa-līlā, but I do not feel that I am qualified to speak on it. So kindly ask someone else."**
  - We know Prabhupāda was fully qualified even to participate in rāsa-līlā, but he was emphasizing that it is confidential subject matter — **we have to qualify ourselves even to hear it**, what to speak of participating (which is a long way off).

## Hearing With Rapt Attention — the Reward Is More Hearing

- When one first comes to Krishna consciousness it is difficult to control the mind and sit patiently, but through the process one gets this reward.
  - Someone asked Prabhupāda, "What is the reward for chanting Hare Krishna?" Prabhupāda: **"More chanting."**
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.16** (*śuśrūṣoḥ śraddadhānasya vāsudeva-kathā-ruciḥ / syān mahat-sevayā...*): by serving devotees who are freed from vice, great service is done, and by such service one gains an affinity (ruci) for hearing the message of Vāsudeva.
- We intimately associate with Krishna by hearing His name and glories, because of the identity of the Lord with His name, fame, form, and pastimes.
  - **Padma Purāṇa / "nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ"**: the name, fame, form, and pastimes of the Lord are all identical with Him. The name of Krishna is identical with Krishna; His fame is identical with Him; His pastimes also. But one must progressively qualify oneself through devotional service to associate more intimately.
- **We are not missing anything at this point.** Our life is full of Krishna. By taking the process seriously we purify our hearts so we can ultimately also dance with Krishna.
  - Prabhupāda said this Krishna conscious movement is meant for **going to Goloka Vṛndāvana** and participating with the Lord in His pastimes.

## Sahajiyā: The Danger of Cheap Imitation

- One who tries to enjoy those pastimes without being properly qualified is called **sahajiyā** — one who takes devotional service very cheaply.
- Our ācāryas have never hesitated to give us the topmost nectar of Krishna consciousness, but **side by side they give clear instructions how to reach that point.** Both are there.
- One who tries to advance without strictly following the rules and regulations (**sādhana-bhakti**) is described in a Bengali proverb: **"ghoḍā ḍiṅgiye ghāsa khāoyā"** — a jumping horse who tries to eat grass at the same time. A horse trying to jump and simultaneously grab a clump of grass will fall on his head and hurt himself.
  - **Śrīdhāra Mahārāja's Spanish version**: "You can't chew sugar cane and whistle at the same time."
  - So one advancing in Krishna consciousness must **side by side give up sense gratification.**
- **The goal is Krishna-līlā; to achieve it we have the process of Krishna consciousness, which entails giving up sense gratification.**
- Technically, one who imitates the confidential pastimes of the Lord while engaging in sense gratification (sahajiyā) is **actually a debauchee.**
  - *Vṛndāvana example*: Last Kārtik, on Parikramā down by **Keśī-ghāṭa**, the speaker saw a man dressed as Krishna and a woman dressed as Rādhā, embracing and kissing — "not divine by any stretch of the imagination." Our ācāryas strongly condemn this.
- **Better to be a neophyte devotee** seriously chanting Hare Krishna and following the four regulative principles **than to imagine oneself an advanced devotee enjoying Krishna-līlā.**

## We Already Have the Lord's Full Association

- Even at this stage we have the Lord's full association — we simply haven't realized it yet. We are surrounded by Krishna:
  - Krishna's **name**; Krishna's devotee, **guru**; the **Vaiṣṇavas**; the **deities**; the **temple**; **prasādam**; **Prabhupāda's books**; **Vṛndāvana, Māyāpur, Jagannātha Purī.** "There's Krishna everywhere."
- We must become receptive and purified to appreciate it. We are not missing anything — **"it just gets better."** The process is **perfect, more perfect, and most perfect.**
- The authorized means of progress: **"ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu-saṅgo 'tha bhajana-kriyā / tato 'nartha-nivṛttiḥ syāt tato niṣṭhā rucis tataḥ..."** (faith, sādhu-saṅga, bhajana-kriyā, anartha-nivṛtti, niṣṭhā, ruci, prema) — **not** "eating grass while jumping."

## The New York Ecstasy Story: How to Test a Pretender

- New York, 1971, during a Sunday ārati: a gentleman came, chanting, fell to the ground as if in ecstasy, went unconscious, shivering, tears coming from his eyes. "Here's a mahā-bhāgavata," the devotees thought — the first time they had seen someone exhibit ecstatic symptoms (apart from seeing Prabhupāda, which was special mercy).
- A devotee called Prabhupāda (in those days you could actually phone Prabhupāda — though he'd say "Better you ask your GBC"). Prabhupāda's instruction: **"You give him a good kick in the ribs. If he's a pretender, he'll know immediately — he'll get up. And if it's for real, nothing will happen."** (The speaker doesn't think they actually did it.)
- The point: **no one is barred from entering the Lord's pastimes, but we must qualify ourselves accordingly, beginning with sādhana-bhakti.**

## Devotional Service Is Scientific

- **Scientific means** you have a hypothesis, an experiment, and a result. Our challenge to people: if they take up this formula for Krishna consciousness, they will eventually get love of God.
- One can understand he is progressing **without** looking for tears in the eyes, faltering voice, perspiration, or hair standing on end.
- **The first symptom of actual advancement is that one gives up sense gratification** and becomes fixed. **Bhajana-kriyā** entails giving up what is not wanted (**anartha-nivṛtti**): *artha* means what is needed, *anartha* means what is not needed. Through the regulative principles we give up what is unnecessary.
- **The longest journey begins with the first step.** We can be happy that at least we are properly situated. It may be slow progress, but at least it is the right direction: **"Better go slowly in the right direction than hell-bent for leather the wrong direction."**
- We can be **fully satisfied now** in Krishna consciousness (we'll become *more* satisfied, but we can be fully satisfied now), knowing we are surrounded by Krishna and may eventually be with Him. **But not sahajiyā-ism.**

## Rādhā-kuṇḍa and the First Verse of Upadeśāmṛta

- Prabhupāda once spoke of the glories of **Rādhā-kuṇḍa** — the topmost place in Vṛndāvana in Krishna's līlā, very dear to Krishna because very dear to **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī**. **Rūpa Gosvāmī** glorifies Rādhā-kuṇḍa in **Upadeśāmṛta.**
- Prabhupāda then said, "But my disciples who are going there are rascals." A devotee objected: "But Prabhupāda, you've told us that advanced devotees go to Rādhā-kuṇḍa to execute devotional service."
- Prabhupāda: "Yes, that is there in the **tenth verse of Upadeśāmṛta.** But they can't even follow the **first verse.**"
  - **First verse — "vāco vegaṁ manasaḥ krodha-vegaṁ..."** — control of the senses (control of the urges of speech, mind, anger, tongue, belly, and genitals). These devotees are rascals because they go there without any qualification.
  - One can go and visit, take a bath, pick a lotus — but **to reside on the banks of Rādhā-kuṇḍa one must be a very advanced soul.** They can't control the mind or the senses.
- Upadeśāmṛta was given by the Gosvāmīs so that one day we may reach Rādhā-kuṇḍa; but at this point, even to follow the first verse is very exalted. **How many in this material world can control mind and senses? Only devotees.**
- So it is exhilarating to come **out of ignorance into passion, out of passion into goodness, and out of goodness into śuddha-sattva (viśuddha-sattva).** To follow the rules and regulations of devotional service is itself **Krishna's special mercy** upon us.

## The Bombay Life Member and "Special Mercy"

- Prabhupāda visited a life member's house in Bombay: they had an altar, offered food, chanted 16 rounds — following three principles, but the man was still **smoking** (the one he couldn't give up).
- The man said: "Swāmījī, we're making nice progress, but I can't give up smoking. Can you arrange some **special mercy** so I can become fully Krishna conscious despite this?"
- Prabhupāda: "Oh yes, I can arrange some special mercy." He told the story from the **11th Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** where **Krishna tells Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira: "The first mercy I show My devotee is that I take away all his material opulence."**
  - So Prabhupāda offered: "I will ask Krishna to take your house, your wife, your children, your Ambassador car, your bank balance — to take everything and put you on the street like a beggar. In such a position you can pray to Krishna, 'I have nothing, completely dependent upon You.' Do you want me to arrange some special mercy?"
- The man said **no** — he was satisfied with *this* mercy: chanting Hare Krishna and following the four regulative principles. **This is also mercy.** We shouldn't think we are less fortunate; by following this process we can one day get Krishna.

## Why We Hear Confidential Pastimes: To Develop Hankering

- We hear the confidential pastimes mentioned in the **Antya-līlā** and the **Tenth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** so that we can **develop a hankering for the spiritual world.** We get a glimpse of Krishna so that we can hanker.
- *Dhruva Mahārāja example*: The Lord appeared to five-year-old **Dhruva Mahārāja**, who was performing severe austerities. Krishna gave him momentary darśana, then as He was disappearing said, **"I cannot be achieved by those who are incomplete in devotional service,"** and disappeared. The result: Dhruva developed a very strong hankering for Krishna.

## Prabhupāda's Purports: Windows to the Spiritual World — and to the Material World

- These purports are **windows to the spiritual world**, giving us a glimpse of what it is to be Krishna conscious, awakening strong hankering.
- But they are also **windows to the material world**: when one is inside a house, he can look outside without getting wet. (Earlier it was raining; we can see it's raining but stay dry inside.)
- **Prabhupāda has built a house in which the whole world can live.** From within its sanctity we can see and hear about the spiritual world and develop hankering for it, and we can look out the window of Prabhupāda's purports and see the material world for what it is — the Bengali saying, **"to catch a fish without getting wet."**
- If you actually see the material world for what it is, then when you hear about Krishna's pastimes you naturally develop strong hankering.

## The Northern Light: How a Devotee Illuminates a Subject

- When Prabhupāda speaks in his books about the material world, he sheds the proper light on it.
- *Painters' analogy (spoken at Bhaktivedanta Manor the day before)*: The great painters — **Rembrandt, Van Gogh, da Vinci, Michelangelo** — paint with the **northern light**, because that light illuminates the subject better than any other. Visit their studios in Italy, France, wherever, and the window faces north; the light is very clear.
- Similarly, when a devotee of the Lord speaks, he illuminates the subject so clearly there is no doubt: "Oh yes, here is the material world." The problem is **we forget** and go back into the material world and see it according to our own vision.
- **We are meant to see things "śāstra-cakṣuṣā"** — through the eyes of scripture, according to the description given in the scriptures. If we always see like that, there is no question of being attracted to material life.
- **When the guru speaks, Māyā is revealed for what she is** — "made naked" in a poetic sense. Māyā is a devotee, but she is exposed by the words of a devotee of the Lord.

## The Good Eye and the Bad Eye

- **We have one bad eye and one good eye.** "Bad eye" does not mean an eye that doesn't work — it means with one eye we see the bad (the material world for what it is) and with the good eye we see the good (Krishna). This is the proper balance; the devotee must see both simultaneously.
- Hearing from the spiritual master reveals **two things**: **the face of Māyā and the face of Krishna.** Go through Prabhupāda's purports — two subject matters: What is Māyā and what is Krishna? (Each has sub-categories, but basically two things.)
- **Prabhupāda said one time: "You have to see things with a good eye and a bad eye."** "Bad" means simply seeing it for what it is.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 8.16** (*ā-brahma-bhuvanāl lokāḥ punar āvartino 'rjuna*): "From the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death take place." **To see the material world for what it is — that is transcendental knowledge.** With that foundation we can understand Krishna's pastimes.
- We *can* hear of Krishna intimately dancing with the gopīs, playing His flute, kissing the gopīs — but what is it meant to do? **Awaken a strong hankering to achieve Krishna's mercy.** After hearing, there is nothing left "that way" — we can only go "this way."
- **Prabhupāda's initiation instruction to Satsvarūpa Mahārāja**: "Never look back and think you've left anything behind, and never envy the position of the materialist." Material life is finished. Then you are safe — a good foundation for progressing. But if you keep looking this way and that way, "you're going to get a sore neck." Keep the mind fixed on Krishna.

## Discerning the Real from the Imitation

- The temptation is to look back: it *looks* like the materialistic people are actually happy — if you don't have the comparison of Krishna consciousness.
- We compare two things to see the superiority of one. *Shopping analogy*: You pick up two items that look the same, but look closer — "this is an imitation, it's plastic; this is the real thing."
  - **Māyā is so expert she can look almost just like Krishna**, making things appear so attractive they seem to equal Krishna consciousness. But by close examination — "this is a fraud, throw it away." You have to be trained.
- *Fool's gold (pyrite) story*: As boys in California, the speaker and his friend Jimmy (age 13–14, doing a school report) went to the **Sierra Nevada** mountains to pan gold. They found a big lump — "Look, we're rich, we can quit school!" — took it into town, and were told: "Sorry, boys, this is worth about a dime. This is **pyrite** — fool's gold." **"All that glitters is not gold."** It looked like gold but wasn't; the expert could tell because he was trained.
- To one untrained in Krishna consciousness, material life appears to have a lot to offer, and devotional service seems to demand many austerities — "why not just enjoy without the austerities?" But one trained in knowledge sees the difference. **We are in a training session now.**

## The Training Wheels: Guru and Gaurāṅga

- *Training-wheels analogy*: Children have trainer wheels on their bicycles so they can ride without falling. The little girl says, "I'm riding my bike just like my father," not realizing that behind her there are two little wheels keeping the balance. She shouldn't forget she is in a training session.
- Similarly, **we should not try to imitate great devotees** (like that couple in Vṛndāvana). We are going nicely on the bike, but there are two wheels — **Guru and Gaurāṅga** — keeping the balance. If we forget Guru and Gaurāṅga, we crash (every kid has scars; the speaker rode confidently "into a big wire").
- We can ride the process of devotional service, but right now we have the trainer wheels — and **thank God we have them; that is Krishna's special mercy.** So we don't have to lament that we can't yet be like the exalted devotees. **Krishna is right with us — our best friend, in our heart, intimately involved even now.**

## "Where Is God?" — The Atheist's Question and the Kindness of God

- *Time magazine article on evil*: A recent ~10-page article whose subject was **evil** — "Why is there evil?" It gave no answer, only described the inauspiciousness of modern society, ending with a big question mark. Materialists challenge: "If there is God, why is there evil? Therefore there must be no God" — adding insult to injury. Some say God has "gone in exile." A classic atheistic article — "what do you expect out of Time magazine?"
- **But God is kind. We know it's all karma, yet God is kind — even to the atheist.**
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 15.15** (*sarvasya cāhaṁ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṁ ca*): "From Me come knowledge, remembrance, and forgetfulness." Even the demon depends upon God to forget God. To forget God, they are completely dependent on God to forget Him.
  - God even empowers **Lord Śiva** to come as **Śaṅkarācārya** to give a philosophy that bewilders such people so they can go where they want. "But you can't go to hell without God." If you want to forget Him, He gives unlimited theories to forget Him; if you want to remember Him, He gives the association of devotees. **Who can say God is not kind?** He is fulfilling everyone's desires.
- *The French and "le bon Dieu"*: The French say **"le bon Dieu"** (the good God), then become agnostics or atheists — "Où est-ce qu'il est, le bon Dieu?" ("Where is the good God?") — if children are born crippled, babies born blind, little old ladies get raped.
- *South Africa story*: On a bus, a man had a big red bleeding leg. The police stopped to help ("Poor man, we'll take you to the hospital"), looked in his pocket, and found **a hand** — he had cut off a woman's hand just to get her ring (couldn't get the ring off, so cut off the hand and put it in his pocket). "Welcome to South Africa." (Aside: this is why Gaura-kṛpā and others were sitting there — the doctor was gone, they all left the same day, and saw that red hand.) **"Where is the good God, the bon Dieu?"**
- Answer, as **Prahlāda Mahārāja** said: **He is everywhere.** Hiraṇyakaśipu said, "Show Him to me, where is He?" — He is everywhere, He is here. (Reference to the **Nṛsiṁha play, Kalyapani, 1985** — "He's in the pillar." Aside: the actor lost his voice for a week after the play, "just like I lost my voice in the kīrtana" — "He comes back though.")
- **With a devotee, one can see God. The materialists see in ignorance — so what can they see?** We see with transcendental knowledge, with light. They see, but in darkness.
- *Kiwi bird / owl analogy*: In New Zealand the **kiwi bird** can only see in darkness. (The speaker took the kids to the zoo in Australia — "otherwise what's the use of going to Australia unless you can see a kangaroo and a kiwi bird?" — a dark room with a fluorescent light so the kiwi thinks it's nighttime; they run around practically blind, and as soon as the man turns on the light, they disappear.) Like an **owl**, which closes its eyes as soon as the sun comes up. So a materialistic person, confronted with the Absolute Truth, closes his eyes; then opens his eyes to so many theories and formulas — very intelligent, but **their intelligence is stolen by illusion.** They see matter but not the soul or the Supersoul — which cannot be seen with these eyes, only with transcendental vision, by the mercy of guru.

## "He Comes Every Day": God's Concern and the Sending of Guru

- Materialists say, "Where is God?" We say, "He's everywhere — He comes every day."
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 4.7** (*yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata / abhyutthānam adharmasya...*): "Whenever there is a decline in dharma, He comes personally."
- **Vedic time is reckoned by demigod standard, not human standard.** (Human time is like "ant standard" — who cares what time it is for an ant? If ants had watches they'd be much different than ours. Real time is demigod time; they are much more important than us.) So **once in a day of Brahmā, Krishna comes** — "Everything all right? Don't be afraid of the demons." Someone comes to your house every day; isn't He concerned? "The same old song: 'Please open up, I've come to save you.'"
- **In His absence, He leaves His dearmost servant to take care of you** — His representative, the guru.
  - **"yasya prasādād bhagavat-prasādo / yasyāprasādān na gatiḥ kuto 'pi"** (Śrī Gurv-aṣṭaka): by the guru's mercy one gets Krishna's mercy. **As soon as you sincerely want Krishna, He sends guru.**
- **Becoming a devotee is always a transcendental phenomenon.** If you asked everyone in the room how they became a devotee, each is a unique, amazing story worthy of *Back to Godhead* — how you met your spiritual master, how you realized "this is my guru," how faith was awakened.
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 10.10** (*teṣāṁ satata-yuktānāṁ bhajatāṁ prīti-pūrvakam / dadāmi buddhi-yogaṁ tam*): Krishna gives the intelligence.
- *Radio-static analogy*: Krishna is in the heart, preaching to us all the time (it's the good consciousness — "I knew I should have done that, but we do it anyway" — the Supersoul), but there is **static**: the **false ego** ("I am the enjoyer") prevents us from hearing what Krishna is saying. (Like trying to tune the BBC overseas but getting Radio Hanoi, Radio Moscow, or Voice of America with too much static.)

## The Pilot Light: Love of God Is Never Extinguished

- Because there is some inkling of desire to become Krishna conscious somewhere in the heart, **"the pilot light never goes out."** (Upstairs the guest facility has a cold shower because the pilot light went out in the heater — but the pilot light of love of God is always there, never goes out, ever.)
- **Love of God is present in the heart of every living entity; it may be covered, but it is never extinguished.** To extinguish the love of God would be to extinguish the living entity — because our **svarūpa**, our nature, is to be a lover of Krishna. We are pervertedly using it as **lust**, which covers the pure desire.

## How Much Mercy We Have Been Given

- Guru has given us so much: **Prabhupāda; the temples; the deities; Vṛndāvana, Māyāpur, Bombay, Jagannātha Purī, Tirupati/Veṅkaṭeśvara; Śṛṅgāra darśana; so many festivals; so many types of sweets** — and also **śāk (bitters)** so we can appreciate the sweets; **Maṅgala-ārati; 16 rounds; Guru-pūjā; Bhāgavatam class; the Sunday Feast** ("all glories to the Sunday Feast — we can hardly take it, there's so much").

Where is the nectar?" **We are swimming in it** — as if fallen into an ocean of nectar, swimming, diving, and surfacing in this process. Sometimes we are so unfortunate we don't realize the mercy we have.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka 2** (*nāmnām akāri bahudhā nija-sarva-śaktis...*): "O my Lord, Your holy name alone can render all benedictions upon the living entities, and thus You have hundreds and millions of names like Krishna and Govinda. You have made approach to You so easy by the chanting of Your holy names, but unfortunate as I am..." — He has invested all His transcendental energies.

## Reciprocation Requires Bhakti: We Must Pay the Price

- We should be **bubbling with enthusiasm 24 hours a day.** Even difficult moments are Krishna's mercy, because they cause us to surrender more; even in difficulty a devotee is peaceful, knowing Krishna's mercy is helping him give up any remnant of attachment to this world.

  - **Draupadī** being disrobed in the assembly of the Kurus and Pāṇḍavas: Duḥśāsana ("the rascal") tried to strip her, but she prayed, "My dear Lord, but for You there is no other shelter," and Krishna became an unlimited sārī. "Just see how Krishna can reciprocate."
  - **Mother Kuntī** appreciated how at every danger Krishna was there to protect the Pāṇḍavas.

- **But to reciprocate with the Lord as Draupadī, the Pāṇḍavas, and Hanumān did, we must have their bhakti** — we must be willing to sacrifice everything for Krishna.
  - **Look at Hanumān's heart**: **"nāhaṁ dhanaṁ na janaṁ..." / "na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīm"** — there is no wealth, no opulence, no desire for fame or reputation, no desire for the opposite sex. **It is just Sītā-Rāma. That's all.**
  - Look in the heart of a **neophyte devotee**: Krishna is there — but also a BMW, a nice house in the country, a big fat bank balance for security. In Hanumān's heart you see only Sītā-Rāma, nothing else; he gave up everything long ago to install the Lord within his heart. **We have to be like that.**
- We can all have this nectar mentioned in the purports — but **we have to pay the price**: give up whatever is not favorable to devotional service.
  - **The saying**: "If you lose your wealth, you lose nothing. If you lose your health, you lose something. But if you lose your Krishna consciousness, you lose everything." ## The Most Valuable Thing Comes in a Small Package

- Yes, you can have it all immediately — "you can have it like that," Prabhupāda said.

  - **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** (*brahmāṇḍa bhramite kona bhāgyavān jīva / guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja*): the seed has been planted in the heart of every individual here — the precious jewel of the mahā-mantra. But to take proper advantage, we have to give some things up.

## Throw Off the Sandbags: The Austerity Is Giving Up Suffering

- *Hot-air-balloon analogy*: To make a balloon go higher you pump in hot air, but **if you don't throw off the sandbags (ballast), it won't go anywhere** — no matter how much air you put in.
  - "You can chant Hare Krishna until you're blue in the face" — Prabhupāda said you can chant for millions of years, but **unless you give up your nonsense you won't make spiritual advancement.** (You are always making *some* advancement, because the holy name is all-merciful — but to reach the goal, throw off the ballast.)
- The four austerities/sandbags to throw off: **no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling.**
  - In universities, when you tell them this, they say: "What's left?" But this is only the beginning.
- **Our only austerity in Krishna consciousness is to give up suffering.** We should never think "we have austerities" in the ordinary sense — our austerity is to give up suffering.
  - *Cancer-sale analogy*: If someone says "don't go to the store," it's like saying there's a sale at the pharmacy (the chemist): "They're selling **cancer** for £3.90 — yesterday it was £5 — and you can get a little dose of diphtheria, typhoid fever, and whooping cough for 30 extra cents." "My God, let's get down there quick and get it!" No — it's foolish to go there. So when the spiritual master says no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, 16 rounds every morning at 4:30, realize we are **giving up pain, giving up suffering.**
  - **Meat-eating** — you get cancer and karma; "every hair on the body of that animal, you take birth and are slaughtered the same way."
  - **Intoxication** — you pollute your body with chemicals, drugs, and alcohol; you grow old very quickly.
  - **Illicit sex** — "you might as well open up your brain and let your brains come out." Persons who engage in illicit sex lose their intelligence for understanding Krishna conscious subject matter; they become intoxicated with the bodily concept of life. (We are already intoxicated with one body — why become intoxicated with another body, and other bodies, and a whole family of bodies?)
  - **Gambling** — throwing our hard-earned money away.
- These "austerities" are just being asked to give up suffering — **throw off the sandbags so we can go up.**

## Why Krishna Gives Material Opulence to the Demons

- **"na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīm..." (Śikṣāṣṭaka 4)**: "O my Lord, I have no desire to accumulate wealth, nor beautiful women, nor followers. All I want is causeless devotional service in my life, birth after birth after birth." This is what we aspire for — **Bhagavad-gītā** *anapekṣaḥ* ideal / "aihituki" — pure devotion.
- Argument: **If these things (opposite sex, wealth, fame, reputation) are so wonderful, why does Krishna easily give them to the demons?** There are so many demons, agnostics, or innocent people with no good qualifications who nonetheless have fame, wealth, men, and women.
  - If you have something very precious, do you give it to someone inimical to you? Remember the material world, when people didn't like you, you had enemies (you don't find that in Krishna consciousness) — would you go to your enemy's door and give him a beautiful ring that God gave you? "Are you crazy? I'm your enemy." You don't give what is dear to you to those who are inimical.
  - So **Krishna would not give something truly wonderful to the demons** — He is **keeping the truly valuable thing for His friends**, His mother and father, His associates. And what is that? **Bhakti, devotional service.**
- **Devotional service is more powerful than God.** God, by definition, means the supreme controller (look in the dictionary: G-O-D, the supreme controller). But what is more powerful than God? Devotional service — **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī controls Krishna**, "wrapped around her little finger." One can achieve Krishna through devotional service; therefore Krishna does not give it easily. **But somehow or other, we've got it.**

## The Munificence of Lord Caitanya
