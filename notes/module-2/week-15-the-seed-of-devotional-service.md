# The Seed of Devotional Service

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 15 ·  · 1:03:09

## Summary

Speaking on Bhagavad-gita 15.7, Indradyumna Swami establishes the true identity of the living entity as an eternal fragmental part and parcel of Krishna, qualitatively one with God (like sun rays sharing the sun's qualities) but not quantitatively equal, and eternally a servant of the Lord. Under the illusion of maya, which both covers reality and drives the soul away from Krishna, the living entity falsely identifies with the gross material body through ahankara (false ego), the strongest material element that survives even death and, as he says, nuclear war. Because of this bodily identification the soul struggles with the six senses and the mind, suffering repeated birth, death, old age, and disease, and searches for happiness in the wrong place. The remedy is transcendental knowledge received from a bona fide spiritual master, who removes the cataract of ignorance like a surgeon's scalpel and plants the seed of devotional service (bhakti-lata-bija) in the disciple's heart through his instructions. This seed is watered by hearing and chanting (sravanam kirtanam), so that dormant love of God, latent within everyone like the kingdom of God within, is awakened by the transcendental sound of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. Swami stresses the simple, scientific process of hearing about Krishna, chanting Hare Krishna, and honoring prasadam, which cleanses the heart of all material desire, and concludes that the goal of all philosophy is to bring one to surrender and loving service under the guidance of a self-realized soul.

## Key Points

- The living entity is Krishna's eternal fragmental part (mamaivamsah), qualitatively one with God but not quantitatively — illustrated by the sun globe and its rays, and by parts of gold still being gold; his eternal nature is to serve, either the Lord in the spiritual world or matter (while thinking himself the master) in the material world.
- False ego (ahankara) makes the transcendental soul identify with the gross body; it is the strongest material element because it survives death and even an atomic bomb, carried by the subtle body (mind, intelligence, ego) as the soul transmigrates — shown by 'beauty is only skin deep,' the car-accident body, and worms happily feasting in the intestines.
- Transcendental knowledge is light that removes fear born of darkness/ignorance; the bona fide guru removes the 'cataract' of material identification with the scalpel of knowledge (Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura's example) so one sees things as they are, like a child realizing a terrifying movie is 'just light on a screen.'
- Material life is a prison of four walls — birth, death, old age, disease — with varying facilities from higher planets to solitary confinement (Prabhupada's prisoner analogy); desire is a subtle conditioning, so 'this body is exactly what you wanted,' and even attraction to a sunset can cause another birth.
- The spiritual master plants the seed of devotional service in the heart through his instructions (not by a magical touch), and it is watered by hearing and chanting until it grows through the coverings of the universe to the lotus feet of Krishna — as the tiny banyan seed grows into a tree extending fifty miles.
- The maha-mantra carries the seed of love of God; transcendental sound (shabda-brahma) awakens the sleeping soul as a noise wakes a sleeper to a thief, and its real glory (per Haridasa Thakura) is not liberation or freedom from karma but awakening dormant love for God.
- The practical process is simple and scientific: hear about Krishna, chant Hare Krishna, and honor prasadam (controlling the tongue); one must give up independent speculation, take shelter of a self-realized soul, and surrender — the purpose of all philosophy and of Prabhupada's purports, echoed by the arguing philosophers vs. the devotee who simply offers the fallen apple to Krishna.

## Scriptural References

- Bhagavad-gita 15.7 — 'mamaivamso jiva-loke jiva-bhutah sanatanah, manah-sasthanindriyani prakrti-sthani karsati'; the main verse and Prabhupada's purport, on the living entity as Krishna's eternal fragmental part struggling with the six senses including the mind.
- Bhagavad-gita 2.13 — 'dehino 'smin yatha dehe...'; the embodied soul passing from childhood to youth to old age and, at death, into another body.
- Bhagavad-gita 2.20 — 'na jayate mriyate va kadacit...'; the soul is never born and never dies, is unborn and undying, not slain when the body is slain (the sober 'dhira' is not bewildered).
- Bhagavad-gita 18.66 — 'sarva-dharman parityajya mam ekam saranam vraja...'; Krishna's final instruction to abandon all dharmas and simply surrender, cited as the goal of all philosophy.
- Guru-pranama / invocation — 'om ajnana-timirandhasya jnananjana-salakaya caksur unmilitam yena tasmai sri-gurave namah'; the guru opening the eyes with the torchlight of knowledge, with 'salaka' explained as a surgeon's scalpel removing the cataract of ignorance.
- Srila Rupa Gosvami's pranama — 'sri-caitanya-mano-'bhistam sthapitam yena bhu-tale...'; recited in the opening invocation.
- Siksastakam, verse 2 — 'O my Lord, Your holy name alone can render all benediction... You have hundreds and millions of names like Krishna and Govinda... and there are no hard and fast rules for chanting'; on the potency and accessibility of the holy name.
- Caitanya-caritamrta (Madhya 19.151) — 'brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija'; the seed of the devotional creeper (bhakti-lata-bija) received by the mercy of guru and Krishna — the central theme and title of the lecture.
- Sravanam kirtanam visnu-smaranam (nine processes of devotional service, Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23) — hearing and chanting as the 'watering' of the seed, from which remembrance of Krishna arises.
- Srila Rupa Gosvami's prayer glorifying Lord Caitanya — 'namo maha-vadanyaya krsna-prema-pradaya te...' (Caitanya-caritamrta Madhya 19.53); of all incarnations Lord Caitanya is most merciful because He freely bestows love of Krishna.
- Srila Rupa Gosvami's prayer — 'tunde tandavini ratim vitanute...' (Caitanya-caritamrta Madhya 1.99); the nectar waters of Lord Caitanya's activities flowing over the 'desert-like tongue.'
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.30 (Prahlada Maharaja) — 'matir na krsne...adanta-gobhir visatam tamisram punah punah carvita-carvananam'; sense-addicted materialists 'chewing the already chewed,' applied to the daily newspaper.
- Caitanya-caritamrta — the two potencies of maya: she covers reality (that we are eternally servants of God) and throws the living entity away from Krishna.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura's song ('jiv jago, jiv jago,' Arunodaya-kirtana), voicing Lord Caitanya — 'Wake up, wake up, sleeping souls! How long will you sleep in the lap of the maya witch?'
- Sukadeva Gosvami / Srimad Bhagavatam — cited for the point that the many subject matters ('grama-katha,' village talk) heard in the material world never satisfy the soul, which yearns to hear about, see, touch, and love Krishna.
- Nectar of Devotion / bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu teaching — the allegory of the devotional creeper growing through the coverings of the material universe to rest at Krishna's lotus feet and bear the fruit of love of God.
