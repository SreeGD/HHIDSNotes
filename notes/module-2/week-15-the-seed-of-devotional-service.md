# The Seed of Devotional Service

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 15 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:03:09

**Themes:** soul's identity · false ego · transmigration · bhakti-lata-bija · holy name · surrender

## Summary

Speaking on Bhagavad-gita 15.7, Indradyumna Swami establishes the true identity of the living entity as an eternal fragmental part and parcel of Krishna, qualitatively one with God (like sun rays sharing the sun's qualities) but not quantitatively equal, and eternally a servant of the Lord. Under the illusion of maya, which both covers reality and drives the soul away from Krishna, the living entity falsely identifies with the gross material body through ahankara (false ego), the strongest material element that survives even death and, as he says, nuclear war. Because of this bodily identification the soul struggles with the six senses and the mind, suffering repeated birth, death, old age, and disease, and searches for happiness in the wrong place. The remedy is transcendental knowledge received from a bona fide spiritual master, who removes the cataract of ignorance like a surgeon's scalpel and plants the seed of devotional service (bhakti-lata-bija) in the disciple's heart through his instructions. This seed is watered by hearing and chanting (sravanam kirtanam), so that dormant love of God, latent within everyone like the kingdom of God within, is awakened by the transcendental sound of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. Swami stresses the simple, scientific process of hearing about Krishna, chanting Hare Krishna, and honoring prasadam, which cleanses the heart of all material desire, and concludes that the goal of all philosophy is to bring one to surrender and loving service under the guidance of a self-realized soul.

## The Verse and Prabhupada's Purport (Bhagavad-gita 15.7)

- The class opens by reading the verse and Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 15.7** — *mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ / manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi prakṛti-sthāni karṣati*: "The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts. Due to conditioned life, they are struggling very hard with the six senses, which include the mind."
- Key points drawn from the purport:
  - The identity of the living being is clearly given: he is the **fragmental part and parcel of the Supreme Lord, eternally.** It is not that he assumes individuality only in conditioned life and merges into the Lord upon liberation — he is eternally fragmented. The word **sanātana** (eternal) is used.
  - The Lord expands Himself in innumerable expansions: the primary/personal expansions are **viṣṇu-tattva**, and the secondary/separated expansions are the living entities. By His personal expansion He appears as Lord Rāma, Nṛsiṁhadeva, Viṣṇu-mūrtis, and the predominating deities of the Vaikuṇṭha planets. The separated expansions are eternally servitors.
  - As fragmental parts, living entities share fragmental portions of the Lord's qualities, of which **independence** is one. By misuse of that minute independence, one becomes conditioned; by proper use, one is always liberated. In either case he is qualitatively eternal like the Lord.
  - Not only humans and cats and dogs, but even Brahmā, Śiva, and Viṣṇu are all parts and parcels — all eternal, not temporary manifestations.
  - The word **karṣati** ("struggling," "grappling hard") is significant. The conditioned soul is bound as if by iron chains — bound by false ego, with the **mind as the chief agent** driving him. In goodness his activities are good; in passion, troublesome; in ignorance, he travels the lower species.
  - When liberated, the material covering perishes and the spiritual body manifests in its individual capacity. In the spiritual body one sees, hears, and speaks to the Supreme Personality of Godhead face to face.
  - **Smṛti** is cited — *vasanti yatra puruṣāḥ sarve vaikuṇṭha-mūrtayaḥ*: in the spiritual planets everyone lives in bodies featured like the Lord's; there is no difference in bodily construction between the part-and-parcel living entities and the Viṣṇu expansions.
  - **mamaivāṁśa** — "fragmental parts and parcels" — does not mean a materially broken part. As established in the Second Chapter, spirit cannot be cut into pieces; this fragment is not "materially conceived." **dehino 'smin yathā dehe** — in each individual body the fragmental portion of the Lord is present.
  - The living entity is qualitatively one with the Lord, "just as the parts and parcels of gold are also gold."

## The Identity of the Living Entity

- The verse and purport establish the actual identity of the living entity: he is **eternally a part and parcel of the Supreme Personality of Godhead** — whether in the conditioned state of material existence or in the eternal liberated state as a devotee (in the Vaikuṇṭha planets or in the supreme abode, Goloka Vṛndāvana).
- **Qualitatively one with God, but not quantitatively.**
  - **Analogy of the sun:** There is the sun globe and there are the sun's rays. Qualitatively they are one — the rays possess all the qualities of the sun planet — but obviously not in quantity. Likewise, we living entities, being part and parcel of Kṛṣṇa, have the same qualities as God but not in the same quantity.
- When we forget our relationship with the Lord as His servant, we fall into material existence and serve matter instead.
- **The eternal propensity for service** (a point Prabhupāda always stressed): the living entity has a propensity for service eternally. In the liberated state he serves the Lord; in the material world he serves the material energy — but the illusion of māyā makes him think he is the master.

### Māyā's Two Potencies (Caitanya-caritāmṛta)

- Māyā has two potencies, described in **Caitanya-caritāmṛta**:
  1. She **covers** reality.
  2. She **throws** the living entity away from Kṛṣṇa.
- What is the reality that is covered? That we are eternally servants of God. Because it is covered, we see ourselves as the master and controller — and the result of that mentality is that we have to suffer. This is material existence.
- As Kṛṣṇa states, due to conditioned life the living entity struggles hard with the six senses including the mind. Under illusion we see the material body as a source of enjoyment, but with a little intelligence we can see the body is the source of all our problems.
- The contrast: in the spiritual world the living entity is **eternal, full of knowledge, and full of bliss** (*sat-cit-ānanda*) in his original position; but because he identifies with the material body he experiences just the opposite — **temporary, full of ignorance, and full of suffering.** This is "a very amazing thing."

### Relationships With the Lord

- In the spiritual world there are many relationships one can have with the Lord, described in the Vedic literatures: one can become the **friend** of God, the **servant** of God, the **parent** of God, or the **lover** of God. This is a very exalted situation.

## Ahaṅkāra: False Ego and Identification With the Body

- Due to **ahaṅkāra** (false ego / the illusion of māyā), that force is so powerful that the transcendental soul identifies with the gross material body. Ahaṅkāra is the potency of māyā that causes the soul to identify with gross matter.
- **The mirror:** We are all spiritual by nature, but looking in the mirror we think "here I am." Is this actually our self — the blood, bones, mucus, hairs?
- **"Beauty is only skin deep":** This is a fact. If you peeled off just one millimeter of skin — enough to fill half a thimble — it greatly changes the situation. So what is the real beauty? The real beauty is the **soul**, because as soon as the soul is no longer present, the body becomes detestable. When we say we love someone, we are usually meaning we love the body.
- **The car-accident analogy:** Sometimes the body is "nicely displayed" by the material energy after a terrible accident — an arm here, a leg there, a head, some hair, a pool of blood. It is the same body, but we think that because it is arranged in a certain attractive way, this one is attractive and that one is not. But the substance is all matter. This is the illusion of material existence — that we identify with this gross matter.

### False Ego as a Material Element

- Ahaṅkāra (false ego) is itself described as one of the material elements. The elements constituting material nature: **earth, water, fire, air, mind, intelligence, and false ego.**
- Of all these, **false ego is the strongest** — because it survives the death of the gross body.
- **Transmigration at death:** When the body dies, the soul goes into another body. **Bhagavad-gītā 2.13** — *dehino 'smin yathā dehe kaumāraṁ yauvanaṁ jarā / tathā dehāntara-prāptir dhīras tatra na muhyati*: just as the embodied soul passes from the body of a baby to a boy to a youth to an old man, similarly the soul passes into another body at death.
- What accompanies the soul is not the gross body but the **subtle body — mind, intelligence, and ego.**
  - This is not difficult to understand: devotees can often perceive what a person must have done previously by his present state of existence. Those very sinful in past lives are born into sinful conditions; those who were good are born into so-called good conditions.
- **The atomic bomb:** The gross body is destroyed but not the subtle body. Even an atomic bomb cannot destroy the subtle body. A bomb obliterates everything within a radius — not even a speck of dust is left — yet the ahaṅkāra survives even nuclear war. See how strong it is.

### The Depth of the Fall

- This attachment to the body/matter is our obstacle to overcome. Therefore so many verses in Bhagavad-gītā and the Vedic literature, and the teachings of so many great ācāryas, instruct us that we are not this body. We have heard "you are not the body" many times, but it is not easy to realize, because the ahaṅkāra is so strong.
- The living entity may identify not only with the human form but even with very degraded species. **Prabhupāda's example:** the worm in stool is enjoying his situation — this is how far the living entity can fall.
- **The doctor's office story:** The speaker recently visited the doctor and saw pictures of different worms/parasites that come into the body — round worms, ring worms, flat worms, tape worms, hook worms (a picture "coming from India"). We react "how horrible," but those living entities are enjoying like anything, thinking, "This dark, dirty intestine — what a feast we have!" Everyone knows what is in the body, but the worm thinks, "What a feast we are going to have today." This is illusion, because we are not the body — we are the soul within.

## Seeing Things As They Are: Transcendental Knowledge

### The Movie-House Analogy

- Going to the movies, we become very involved — movies make us laugh, cry, become angry; so many emotions come forward. Yet it is just light projected off a screen, certain images created by light, not really happening.
- **The terrified child:** Something terrifying happens on the screen and a little kid is screaming his eyes out; the mother says, "It's just a movie, calm down, it's just a movie." As soon as he is told the reality, he is freed from the fear.

### The Dhīra — the Sober Soul

- The embodied soul passes through all these changes, including death, but the sober person is not bewildered — like the man in the movie house who stops and thinks, or is told "it's just some light, it's just a story."
- **Bhagavad-gītā 2.13** — the *dhīra*, the sober soul, by dint of transcendental knowledge sees things as they are (*dhīras tatra na muhyati*). He knows there is no such thing as death for the soul.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 2.20** — *na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin... na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre*: the soul is never born nor dies; nor, having once been, does he ever cease to be. He is unborn, eternal (primeval), and undying — he is not slain when the body is slain.

### Knowledge Is Light, Ignorance Is Darkness

- Transcendental knowledge sheds light on the subject and frees us from the fearful condition of material existence. Knowledge is compared to light, ignorance to darkness.
- **Walking alone at night:** If you walked out of this house right now and down the streets alone, you would be afraid — in the dark so many inauspicious elements are present: thieves, robbers, dacoits, even ghosts, hobgoblins, spirits. But as soon as the sun rises, all fear is removed because we can see things as they are.
- Likewise, the light of transcendental knowledge removes the living entity's fear. The fear is caused by identification with the body: we are in ignorance, in anxiety, not knowing what will happen next; there is **danger at every step** and no guarantee of protection against the stringent laws of material nature.
- **The sun dissolving fog:** Just as the powerful sun dissolves the fog, the knowledge of Kṛṣṇa consciousness dissolves the ignorance of the living entity. Things are not always as we see them.

### Approaching the Spiritual Master

- Therefore, to see things as they are and understand the truth, we must approach a bona fide spiritual master — a representative of the Lord who sees things as they are — and by hearing from him get proper vision. Because we are in ignorance, we offer respectful obeisances to the spiritual master.
- **The obeisance prayer** (invoked in the maṅgalācaraṇa): the guru opens our eyes, blinded by the darkness of ignorance, "with the torchlight of knowledge" (*ajñāna-timirāndhasya jñānāñjana-śalākayā*).
- **śalākā = scalpel / knife.** The example is given by **Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura:** the eye may be present but covered by a cataract (an infection on the eye), so it does not function. When the cataract is removed with the scalpel, one can again see. Likewise, when the living entity's ignorance — his identification with the material world — is removed by transcendental knowledge, he can see things as they are. In the diseased condition we are not even aware of the nature of this material world; we think we can enjoy here.

### Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura — the Holocaust

- The great ācārya **Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura** said, in effect: here we are in the middle of the holocaust, trying to enjoy ourselves. We must be aware of the nature of this material world, which is pervaded by **four main miseries: birth, death, old age, and disease.**
- **The prison of four walls:** Just as a prison has four walls — north, south, east, and west — the prisoners are confined within them and their freedom is limited.
- **The solitary-confinement analogy** (Prabhupāda's classic example): compare the prisoner in solitary confinement with the prisoner who, due to good behavior, has certain liberties — a television, a nice room, so much facility. Nowadays prisons in some countries are made so nice that people commit another crime just to come back. But whatever our situation in the material world — from the highest planet with some facilities down to the lowest, solitary confinement — repeated birth and death take place.
- **The first step in self-realization** is to understand my awkward position in this material world.

## Desire as Conditioning; Reviving the Spiritual Body

- We speak about God and also about the spiritual energy (part of God's creation), to convince the living entity that he cannot be happy here and must take to spiritual life.
- Through our desires we develop this material body; similarly, through cultivating spiritual desire we regain our original spiritual body.
- **Prabhupāda: "Desire is a subtle form of conditioning"** in the material sense. As we desire certain things and take certain actions, that desire conditions us in a certain way.
- **"This body is exactly what you wanted":** Thought of in reverse — if you are 20 years old, then 20 years ago when you "died," you got this body, which is exactly what you wanted. Whatever mentality one has at the moment of death transfers him to another womb. So whatever desire we have determines our future state of existence.
- If we cultivate spiritual desire as devotees, acting on the platform of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, we gradually revive our dormant Kṛṣṇa consciousness — the real self.

### The Body Is a Boil on the Soul

- Although we take the body to be something wonderful, it is actually an embarrassment — "a **boil on the soul**." Just as a boil on the face is embarrassing (the speaker saw someone with a big boil right on the tip of the nose — horrible-looking), this body is nothing to be proud about.
- How does one get boils? Due to infections, due to dirty things in the body. Likewise, due to the **dirty things in the heart** — the desire to be separate from God, to act independent of God — we get this material body.

### The Medicine and the Diet

- The cure: take the **medicine of chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa**. And when a doctor gives medicine, he also gives a proper diet — at least in **Āyurvedic medicine**, a bona fide doctor changes your diet, because the food we assimilate plays an important role in the condition of the body ("don't eat this, eat this, stay away from that").
- So the two combinations — **the medicine of the mahā-mantra** (Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma...) and **the foodstuff of prasādam** — will cleanse the heart of all unwanted desires that give us this material body.
- **Prabhupāda's warning:** Even if we have one small material desire left, we have to come back to fulfill it. He actually said that if we are merely attracted to a sunset, that desire will cause us to take birth again in this material world. This shows how scientific the process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is.

## Transferring Attachment From Māyā to Kṛṣṇa

- We must transfer our desires for material life to the devotional service of the Lord. Instead of being attracted to the material forms of this world — which are nothing more than material elements — we must regain our original attraction to the beautiful form of Lord Kṛṣṇa. We must transfer the attachment **from māyā to Kṛṣṇa.**
- **How?** Instead of engaging the senses in the service of māyā, we engage them in the service of the Lord. Eventually, with those **purified senses**, we can perceive Kṛṣṇa.
- **Prabhupāda: "You can meet God face to face"** — but you must have the qualifications. With these material senses you cannot see, but within you is the ability to see God face to face.
- **The small child analogy:** A small child is basically ignorant, with no intelligence (no offense to the mothers), but we know intelligence is **dormant**; as the child matures, the intelligence matures with her and she can do intelligent things. In the same way, within each of us is the dormant ability to see and relate to God.
- **"The kingdom of God is within you":** As the Christians say — it is a fact. The question is how to evoke that consciousness. That is the process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness — basically **chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra.**

### Haridāsa Ṭhākura on the Real Glory of Chanting

- As **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** said: the real glory of chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa is not that one gets liberation or even that one gets freed from his karma (sinful reactions) — the real glory is that one **awakens one's dormant love for God.**

### The Potency of Transcendental Sound

- Just as a sleeping person comes to life, a materialistic person can come to the life of Kṛṣṇa consciousness through **transcendental sound.**
- **The thief in the night:** If you are sleeping and a thief enters your room, how will you become aware of the danger? Not by sight, touch, or taste — your senses cannot help. But through **sound** — if he steps on something or bumps the dresser — even in deep sleep you can be awoken to the danger.
- In the same way, we are all in the sleeping condition of the material world, having forgotten who we are, slumbering in māyā.
- **Lord Caitanya's call** (from *Aruṇodaya-kīrtana* / "Jīva Jāgo"): "Wake up, wake up, sleeping souls! How long will you sleep in the lap of the witch Māyā?" Through the transcendental sound of the mahā-mantra, the soul is awoken — it is that simple.
- **The potency of sound:** Even a blind person who cannot see can be made to laugh, cry, or become angry by hearing music. See how potent sound is — it can evoke so many emotions and thoughts. So **śabda-brahma**, transcendental sound, can evoke our dormant Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- **The kīrtana just held:** A few minutes ago, during kīrtana, no one was on the bodily platform — everyone was absorbed in the chanting. See how potent the chanting is to bring us to the transcendental platform of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** describes Lord Caitanya as the most merciful of all incarnations because He freely bestows love of Kṛṣṇa in the form of His mercy, the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra.
- So we should take to this chanting seriously. If we do, Kṛṣṇa promises we can overcome the material energy — even the very difficult-to-overcome ahaṅkāra, the attachment to this useless gross body — simply by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa.
- Some may doubt: how can something so simple overcome such great delusion? But "nice things come in small packages" — do not underestimate the potency of chanting the mahā-mantra.

## The Seed of Devotional Service (Bhakti-latā-bīja)

- Within the mahā-mantra are the seeds of love of God.
- **The banyan seed:** Even within the material world a small seed has great potency. The seed of a banyan tree is tiny — practically insignificant — but if planted, cultivated, and nurtured properly, over many years it becomes a gigantic, massive tree.
  - **Prabhupāda's example:** There is a banyan tree in Bengal (near Calcutta) that extends for 50 miles. The banyan grows up, and its roots come down to become branches, which go back down to become roots — growing up and down and extending over 50 miles. Yet it was all contained within a little seed. If this is true in the material sense, how much more it applies in the spiritual sense — the seed of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is there in the mahā-mantra.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya 19.151)** — *brahmāṇḍa bhramite kona bhāgyavān jīva / guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja*: wandering through the universe, a fortunate soul, by the mercy of guru and Kṛṣṇa, receives the seed of the creeper of devotional service.

### How the Spiritual Master Enlightens the Disciple

- When one comes in contact with the bona fide spiritual master, how does he enlighten the disciple? **Not** like the old classic story where the guru simply touches the head, an electrical charge passes, and the disciple becomes electrified and sees everything — "it's not like that; you risk getting burned out."
- What the spiritual master actually does is **plant the seed of devotional service within the heart of the disciple in the form of his instructions.**
- **Story — "What is the best way to please you?":** Once the devotees asked Prabhupāda the best way they could please him. Prabhupāda said, **"Chant Hare Kṛṣṇa."** It is as simple as that, because everything is based on and evolves from the chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa.

### The Watering Process

- The seed is planted in the heart by the mercy of the spiritual master, and the **watering process is hearing and chanting** — **śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ.** From hearing and chanting about the Lord comes remembrance of Lord Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa (*smaraṇam*).
- As we water that seed daily through chanting and hearing about the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the seed of love of God begins to grow and become manifest.
- **The allegorical growth of the creeper:** The seed grows and grows, passing all the way through the coverings of the material universe, breaking through the covering, entering the spiritual sky, and coming to rest at the lotus feet of Lord Kṛṣṇa, where it produces the fruits of love of God. This is an allegorical description, but that is what it is like: if we cultivate this chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa, we can one day have the association of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

### Lord Caitanya's Śikṣāṣṭaka on the Holy Name

- As Lord Caitanya prayed (**Śikṣāṣṭaka 2**) — *nāmnām akāri bahudhā nija-sarva-śaktis...*: "O my Lord, Your holy name alone can render all benediction upon the living entities, and thus You have hundreds and millions of names like Kṛṣṇa and Govinda and Nṛsiṁhadeva. In all these names You have invested Your transcendental energies, and there are no hard and fast rules for chanting these names. But, O my Lord, unfortunate as I am, I have no attraction for them."
- Although we have been given the gift of spiritual life, we cannot take advantage, so we have to **constantly hear.** A saintly person "sees through his ears, not through his eyes." We must hear from self-realized souls to get the real picture of things, and when we get the real picture we begin to act in the proper way.

## The Purpose of Philosophy: Coming to a Conclusion

- Philosophy is not meant for armchair speculation; it is meant to come to a **siddhānta** — a conclusion. We hear from a self-realized soul and then we know how to act, rather than discussing back and forth for days, months, and years ("fool philosophy... philosophies for fools" — for people enamored by the material energy, in the bodily concept of life; they need philosophy to come to the point of knowing how to act properly).

### The Apple and the Crow

- **Story:** Two men sit on a bench when an apple falls to the ground. One says, "Did you see that crow? He took off and caused the apple to fall." His friend objects: "You nonsense — the apple fell and it scared the crow away." The other insists the crow flew away and the apple fell as a result. They grab each other by the lapels (in French: *"Monsieur, c'est pas comme ça!"*) and begin a big argument, nearly coming to blows — just like our modern philosophers, "no one can be a philosopher in this age unless he has a different opinion."
- Then a **Hare Kṛṣṇa devotee** comes walking down the road chanting, sees the apple, picks it up, and says, "My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa is so wonderful that He has provided foodstuff in the form of this tasty green apple, so juicy and sweet" — and he offers it to the Lord. The devotee understands the real purpose behind everything: the apple is meant for offering to the Lord and taking the remnants. This is what philosophy is.

### The Point of All the Purports: Surrender

- All the purports of Śrīla Prabhupāda and the whole Bhagavad-gītā are simply to bring us to the point of surrender.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 18.66** — *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja / ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ*: "Abandon all varieties of dharma and just surrender unto Me; I will protect you from all sinful reactions." Kṛṣṇa is begging the living entity, trying to convince us through our intelligence and attract us through our senses.
- **Prabhupāda "tricked us into this movement":** He told some of his first disciples, "Yes, yes, you're out there wandering around; I tricked you into this movement. Everyone's looking for sense gratification in the material world, but in the spiritual world there's real sense gratification — sense gratification in relationship to Kṛṣṇa."
- **How the senses are actually pleased:** by seeing the form of Kṛṣṇa/the Deity, hearing transcendental subject matter — very pleasing to the heart.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka 1 quoted** — *ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam...*: "My most merciful Lord Caitanya, may the nectarean waters of Your transcendental activities flow on the surface of my desert-like tongue; beautifying these waters, the lotus flowers are singing and dancing in the loud chanting of Kṛṣṇa's holy name." The senses are pleased when serving Kṛṣṇa: we see the Lord and His devotee, hear the mahā-mantra, touch the form of the Lord and the feet of the devotee.

### Controlling the Tongue

- One of the most perceptible ways of appreciating Kṛṣṇa is **prasādam** — the tongue. It is described that the best way to make spiritual advancement in this age is to **control the tongue.** Spiritual life begins by controlling the tongue.
- How to control the tongue? **Speak about Kṛṣṇa, glorify the Lord, His transcendental pastimes** (using the tongue for speaking about Kṛṣṇa and for tasting prasādam).

### Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī and the Newspaper About Kṛṣṇa

- One might wonder how long one can talk about Kṛṣṇa. This question was put to **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī** (Guru Mahārāja) when he wanted to start a newspaper about Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Mundane materialists challenged him: they could understand writing a newspaper every day about material events, but were they serious about putting out a newspaper every day about God?
- **The reply — the material world is a tiny cloud:** This challenge is merely a calculation for our finite brains. The spiritual world is unlimited, and the material world is only "a little cloud in some corner" of that unlimited spiritual creation — a little cloud somewhere in the corner, which is where we are stuck now in the "muck" of material existence. In the unlimited spiritual world there are the Lord, unlimited devotees, unlimited opportunities for devotional service, and so many pastimes in complete bliss and knowledge — there is so much to write about. Yet the materialists want to write about only this little **mahat-tattva** in the corner.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.5.30** — *punaḥ punaś carvita-carvaṇānām... adānta-gobhir viśatāṁ tamisram*: persons unable to control their senses, addicted to material pleasure, gradually glide toward hellish conditions and "repeatedly chew that which has already been chewed."
- **The chewed-again newspaper:** The news we read every day we have already chewed — we have already heard about the rapes, the deaths, the politicians. If it is such wonderful subject matter (mere *grāmya-kathā*, "village talk"), why, after spending money to read it, do we use it to line the doggie cage or the garage floor (so oil doesn't spill), or throw it up, or wrap something in it? If it were so important we would not just throw it away.
- **The airplane observation:** Getting on the airplane, if you stand up and look down the aisle, everybody's face is in the newspaper, up and down, up and down. The first thing everyone does is get out the newspaper — but the subject matter is just material existence, the same boring subject matter, and one is never satisfied.
- **Śukadeva Gosvāmī:** Of the so many subject matters we hear in the material world, none actually satisfy. The soul is yearning to hear about Kṛṣṇa, to see Kṛṣṇa, to touch Kṛṣṇa, to love Kṛṣṇa — but out of ignorance we think we can find that pleasure here.

### Looking Under the Lamppost

- **Story (told on Sunday):** A man drives into his garage, takes out the key, locks the car, and drops the keys. It is dark and he cannot see, so he walks 50 meters down the road to look under a lamppost. His friend asks what he is doing; he says he is looking for his keys. "Where did you drop them?" "Up there in my garage." "Then why are you looking down here?" "Well, the light's better." This is the idea — **we are looking in the wrong place** for happiness.

## The Practical Process: Hearing, Chanting, Prasādam

- We can turn our attention and be attracted back to our original blissful Kṛṣṇa consciousness by engaging our senses in the service of the Lord. Everyone can have this experience: engage some of your senses in service and you can experience a revival of your Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- **The easy combination:**
  - The easiest way is to **hear about Kṛṣṇa.**
  - After getting a little restless from sitting on the floor for 45 minutes, we can **chant Hare Kṛṣṇa.**
  - When we get tired chanting, we can **take prasādam.**
  - That combination guarantees us to become Kṛṣṇa conscious. We should adopt this process in our life — it is not a difficult thing.
- **Only the pretender cannot benefit.** As Prabhupāda said: **if a man is sleeping you can easily wake him up, but if he is pretending to be asleep you will never wake him up.** If someone is sincere about advancing in spiritual life, this process will work; but if someone is pretending — coming to Kṛṣṇa consciousness for some other reason than actually wanting to become Kṛṣṇa/God conscious — there will be some benefit, but he won't make much progress.

### Taking Shelter of Self-Realized Souls

- Therefore we must take shelter of self-realized souls; we cannot listen to our own mind. **The mind is "the best friend or the worst enemy"** (cf. BG 6.6) — this very mind got us into this position.
- We must surrender to the representative of the Lord and take shelter of the devotees. If we remain independent, making our own decisions and trying to figure things out, we will always be at a loss. But if we take shelter of the bona fide spiritual master, he takes charge of us: **his intelligence becomes our intelligence, his knowledge becomes our knowledge, his devotion becomes our devotion.**
- So we have to find someone to whom we can surrender and offer service, and be guided in spiritual life. The whole process is based on **devotional service** — we have to find someone to whom we can offer that service. This is the first step for a serious candidate in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The Lord Himself states: just find a self-realized soul who can give you Kṛṣṇa consciousness (cf. BG 4.34).

## Glossary

- **mamaivamsa** — 'My own fragmental part'; the living entity as an eternal part and parcel of Krishna

- **shabda-brahma** — transcendental sound, which awakens the sleeping soul like a noise waking a sleeper to danger
- **bhakti-lata-bija** — the seed of the devotional creeper, planted in the heart by the guru's instructions
- **sravanam kirtanam** — hearing and chanting about Vishnu/Krishna; the 'watering' of the seed of devotion
- **dhira** — the sober person who, by transcendental knowledge, is not bewildered by the body's changes
- **siddhanta** — the settled philosophical conclusion at which real philosophy aims, not endless speculation
