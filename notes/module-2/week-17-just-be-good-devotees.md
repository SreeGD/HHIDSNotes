# Just be Good devotees

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 17 · Foundations in Bhakti · 50:48

**Themes:** kali-yuga · taking shelter · surrender and protection · sadhu-sanga · remembrance of the Lord · fearlessness

## Summary

Speaking on Srimad Bhagavatam 1.15.36, Indradyumna Swami explains that the age of Kali became fully manifest the very day Lord Krishna left the earth, and that its influence falls only on those of a poor fund of knowledge who are not sheltered by the Lord. He reviews the grim Twelfth Canto predictions of Kali-yuga—the daily decline of religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, memory, strength and lifespan, and future famine, heavy taxes and drought that will force people to survive on leaves and roots—stressing that, unlike Nostradamus, these are the infallible apauruseya predictions of shastra. The worst prophecy, he says, is that eventually there will be no devotees, for it is the association of devotees that lets one tolerate any austerity and keep remembering the Lord. Yet the true sadhu or guru always follows his analysis of the age's miseries with the positive solution—take shelter of Krishna—in contrast to a mere analyst like Jean-Paul Sartre, who dissected the material world but, offering no solution, ended in despair. Through the stories of the crabs on the Bombay beach, Lord Shiva watching Rama slay Ravana ('rakhe Krishna mare ke'), Prahlada's fearlessness before Nrsimhadeva, and the saving of Draupadi, he shows that protection, mercy and love come in exact proportion to one's surrender. The real prayer of a devotee is not for bodily safety but to be protected from maya so as never to forget the Lord's lotus feet, though one may also pray to protect a body meant for Krishna's service. He concludes that a devotee's heart is pure or being purified, so Kali's dangers—reactions meant for the sinful—do not apply; just as an honest citizen need not fear the government while a thief lives in anxiety, a sincere devotee should not be fearful but simply 'be a good devotee' and trust the Lord's protection, which comes because devotees carry the important responsibility of sharing their good fortune with others.

## The Verse and Purport (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.15.36)

- This morning's reading is from **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Canto 1, Chapter 15**, entitled *The Pāṇḍavas Retire Timely*, **Text 36.**
- **Translation:** "When the Personality of Godhead, Lord Kṛṣṇa, left this earthly planet in His selfsame form, from that very day Kali, who had already partially appeared, became fully manifest to create inauspicious conditions for those who are endowed with a poor fund of knowledge."
- Points from Prabhupāda's purport:
  - The age of Kali ensued just after the Battle of Kurukṣetra, but it could not exert its influence because of the presence of the Lord.
  - The Lord left this earthly planet in His own transcendental body, and as soon as He left, the symptoms of Kali-yuga — as envisioned by Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira prior to Arjuna's arrival from Dvārakā — began to manifest. Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira rightly conjectured on the departure of the Lord from the earth.
  - As already explained, the Lord left our sight just as the sun, when it sets, is out of our sight.
  - Kali imposes its influence only on those who are not fully developed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness; one who keeps himself under the full supreme care of the Personality of Godhead can counteract all the effects of Kali.

## The Terrible Age of Kali

- The verse speaks of the terrible age of Kali — the **fourth of the four ages** (yugas) the material cosmos passes through.
- More detail on the anomalies of this age is given in the **Twelfth Canto** of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Unfortunately, the predictions are not very good.
- **Twelfth Canto prediction:** In this age, **religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, tolerance, mercy, duration of life, bodily strength, and memory** will all decrease day by day, due to the powerful influence of Kali. We should therefore consider this prediction very seriously.

### "Don't Come Back" — Prabhupada's Warning

- **Story:** A disciple once told Prabhupāda, "Prabhupāda, I am so attached to preaching that I may come back life after life to do this service." That is a nice sentiment — *ahaitukī apratihatā*, unmotivated and uninterrupted devotional service.
- But in that particular instance Śrīla Prabhupāda frowned and said, **"No, don't come back. This Kali-yuga is only going to get worse."** Better to finish up in this one lifetime and go back home, back to Godhead — and by the causeless mercy of the spiritual master and Lord Caitanya, that is possible.

### Lord Caitanya More Merciful Than Krishna

- Lord Caitanya, who is Kṛṣṇa Himself, is even more merciful than Kṛṣṇa. (At the same time, there is no difference between Kṛṣṇa and Lord Caitanya — but He is more magnanimous.) Especially for the devotees of Lord Caitanya, if one is very, very serious about Kṛṣṇa consciousness, it is possible to finish up this business quickly.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 7.19** — Kṛṣṇa says that after many, many births, one who is actually in knowledge (*jñānavān*) surrenders unto Him (*bahūnāṁ janmanām ante jñānavān māṁ prapadyate*).
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura**, in a lecture in the early 1930s, said he saw no reason why all of his disciples could not go back to Godhead in one lifetime.

### What "Serious" Means

- One has to be very serious. **Serious means:**
  - Being very strict in observing the rules and regulations, and particularly in the chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa.
  - To the best of our ability, helping our spiritual master and Lord Caitanya spread this movement.
- Because if we do have to come back and take birth again, we should know it is going to be worse than it is now — and already it is bad enough.

### The Predictions Are Śāstra

- There are so many predictions, and they will come true, because it is **śāstra** — not like Nostradamus, whose predictions were subtle, some coming true and some not. This is śāstra, **apauruṣeya** (not created by any mundane being) — perfect, so whatever is there is going to happen.
- **Twelfth Canto:** There will be **famine** and very heavy taxes, so people will be forced to eat leaves, roots, flesh, flowers, and fruits (and eventually there will be no water; things will become completely ruined). Right now we complain when the prasādam is not perfect — a little uncooked or overcooked, a little too spicy — but as Kali-yuga advances, people will be eating leaves. So even though we still complain the prasādam is cold or the ingredients are fewer, if we consider this explanation we realize we do not yet have to go to the trees. (Half-joking: if there won't be excellent prasādam, "then we should get out of here.")

### The Worst Prophecy: No Devotees

- Perhaps the worst prophecy of Kali-yuga is that **in the future there will be no devotees.**
- As members of the **International Society for Krishna Consciousness**, we are often preaching, so we voluntarily put ourselves in austere conditions — but we can tolerate it because we have the **association of other devotees.**
- **Story — Viṣṇujana Swami's letter:** A disciple (Viṣṇujana Swami) wrote to Prabhupāda describing/complaining of the difficult conditions of saṅkīrtana in England. Prabhupāda wrote back: "I can understand — sometimes there is not enough food, travel is very inconvenient, sometimes you cannot get proper rest. But because this is saṅkīrtana, we should take it as **a child's play.** Because we are devotees of Kṛṣṇa preaching together, we can tolerate all these conditions, since we are all together." But without the association of devotees, it is very difficult to do devotional service.
- So Kali-yuga will get worse and worse, but the worst condition of all is that there will be no devotees.
- **Everyone born a śūdra; demons in good families:** Even now, śāstra says everyone is born with the quality of a śūdra. In this Kali-yuga, demons will get the facility of taking birth in very good families — so those born even in brāhmaṇa families may actually be demons.
- **We can tolerate anything for Kṛṣṇa's service** except the loss of the association of His devotees, His representatives.
- **Even devotees preaching in hell** — how is it possible? They too must have the association of devotees, so for them it is like a child's play, absorbed **in the pastimes of the Supreme Lord.** Wherever you are immersed in those things, you are actually in Vaikuṇṭha, actually in Goloka.
- So there will be fewer and fewer Vaiṣṇavas. At the end there will be only the demoniac, and **Kalki** will come with His sword.

### Making the Best of a Bad Bargain

- It is not a good age to be born in, but as Prabhupāda said, **"We have to make the best use of a bad bargain."** In Kṛṣṇa consciousness there is always a solution to the problem, and there is only one conclusion.

## The Sadhu Points Out the Problem and Gives the Solution

- Prabhupāda points out the anomalies (the bad things) of the age, and in the next sentence gives the **positive alternative.** He often describes a bad situation and then gives a turnabout / conclusion.
- This is the work of the **sādhu** — the word means "one who cuts": he cuts our material attachments by revealing the futility of material life, and then gives us a solution to the problem. A saint destroys the material and gives the conclusion to solve the problem.

### The Philosopher Who Committed Suicide

- You do not necessarily need the Bhāgavatam to understand the evil of this age — you can pick up one Monday newspaper, full of descriptions of Kali-yuga. But can the newspaper give any conclusion? No.
- There was a certain **philosopher** who was very expert at analyzing the material world. His books read like a summary of the Gītā — e.g., **Bhagavad-gītā 8.15**, *duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam*: this is a temporary place full of suffering. He really took apart the material energy, piece by piece, and analyzed it — concluding that all problems arise from having this material body and mind. **But he gave no conclusion** — and the result was that **he committed suicide.**
- Prabhupāda also studied this material world (he had "so many" analyses of it), but **he gave the conclusion:** we need to catch hold of Kṛṣṇa's fruit.
- **Jesus:** You cannot worship mammon (matter) and God simultaneously.
- **Prabhupāda's actual quote:** "Sense gratification and devotional service go ill together" — they do not go well together. Trying to enjoy sense gratification and Kṛṣṇa consciousness at the same time — that formula does not work.

### The Spiritual Master's Duty and Buddha's Lesson on Death

- So the duty of the spiritual master is to **convince the disciple to give up the materialistic life** and take shelter of the Lord's lotus feet. There are so many angles of vision to convince the student to give up.
- **Story — Buddha and the grieving mother:** One of Buddha's followers, a woman, came to him crying: "My lord, my infant child has just died — why has this happened to me?" Buddha told her to go from house to house begging rice, but she could eat only the rice from a house where no one had ever died; she had to first ask, "Has anyone in your family died?" — and if they said no, she could take that rice.
  - She returned at night empty-handed. Buddha asked why. In the first house the father had died; in the second, the father died; in the third, a daughter; in the next, a brother; in the next, a cousin — and so on through the whole village.
  - Buddha said: "So you are not special. You came to me asking why your child has died — **death is all-pervading in this world.** Take it as a lesson to become renounced."
- This is what we must learn. First we must become clean at heart, free from any material contamination — then our devotion can begin.
- **Prabhupāda** said in a lecture that **real bhakti begins when one is completely freed from lust.** But unfortunately we still cling tightly to these things. So a saint's duty is to convince the disciple, in various ways, to give up these contaminated things.

## The Positive Alternative: Take Shelter of the Lord

- We have the alternative — we do not have to kill ourselves in frustration. We have an alternative to all these problems.
- After describing some difficulties of Kali-yuga, Prabhupāda very quickly says: **one can neutralize the effects of Kali by taking shelter of the Lord.** No matter how bad the situation, we can find a conclusion.

### Taking Shelter: Tornadoes and Bomb Shelters

- **The tornado:** In America there are tornadoes — very powerful whirlwinds that come through the middle of the country and destroy everything in their path: houses, cars, telephone poles. The speaker wondered how anyone survives a tornado, where you take shelter. An article showed a picture of a town in the Midwest where nothing was left — yet everyone survived. How? Because **they knew where to take shelter** — they took shelter in the underground cellars of their houses.
- **Bomb shelters:** When the speaker was a child in the 1950s, everyone was afraid the Russians were coming to bomb America with nuclear bombs, so it was popular to build bomb shelters. If the Russians attacked, people would hide in the shelters (stocked with food) and survive.
- So however bad the situation, if we have knowledge we can find a way to safety.

### The Crabs Take Shelter in the Ocean

- **Story (Prabhupāda walking with scientist devotees on the beach):** There were crabs on the beach, and as the devotees approached, all the crabs disappeared into the ocean. (Crabs never go straight forward or back — they always go sideways.) Prabhupāda asked why they did this.
  - He explained: the crabs saw that danger was coming, and although they are very small, they knew the ocean is very great, so they would be protected in the ocean.
  - In the same way, we are very small, and there are so many dangers in this world — but if we **take shelter in Kṛṣṇa, who is the supreme great,** we are safe.

### Krishna Can Protect or Kill — Uttara and Prahlada

- **Uttarā's famous prayer** (from the teachings of Queen Kuntī): "O Lord of lords, Lord of the universe, You are the greatest of all mystics. Please protect me, for there is no one else who can save me from the clutches of death." In this material world full of duality, no one else can protect one from the threat of death.
- Kṛṣṇa can protect a person and can also kill a person; **if Kṛṣṇa wants to kill him, who can protect him? If Kṛṣṇa wants to protect him, who can kill him?**
- **Śiva and Pārvatī watching the Battle of Laṅkā:** From the higher planetary systems, Pārvatī (seated on the lap of her husband Śiva) was watching the battle at Laṅkā as Rāmacandra fought Rāvaṇa. Toward the end, Rāmacandra placed His arrow in His bow and was just about to shoot Rāvaṇa for the final time.
  - Rāvaṇa was a great devotee of Śiva (sometimes Śiva is "like blind," giving demons benedictions; Rāvaṇa worshiped Śiva). So Pārvatī turned to Śiva and said, "Prabhu, just see — your pure devotee is about to be killed by Rāmacandra; you should save him."
  - This is very instructive to those who think Śiva is supreme. (Some think Śiva, being an exceptional personality in his own tattva, is supreme; there is a Śiva Purāṇa, and his followers hold this.) But here Śiva himself says: **if the Supreme Lord wants to protect someone, who can kill him; and if He wants to kill him, who can protect him?**
- This is the mercy of the Lord — those who surrender to Him receive some protection even in this world of birth and death.

## When Difficulties Come, Don't Lose Faith

- With that protection, we can peacefully continue our devotional service. Of course, we are all still subject to birth, old age, disease, and death. When these things befall us, we should not lose faith or think, "In class we heard the Lord protects us, but I'm having trouble — why am I not protected?"
- **The real prayer of the devotee** is not necessarily to protect the physical being, but to protect us from the influence of the illusory energy, so that we never forget the lotus feet of the Lord. **That is the real protection.**

### Prahlada's Only Fear (Nrsimhadeva)

- This was the mood of the great mahājana **Prahlāda Mahārāja.** When Lord **Nṛsiṁhadeva** offered him a benediction ("Do you want some benediction? What do you want?"), a discussion took place between them. (The Seventh Canto is especially dear because it contains so many instructive exchanges and many explanations.)
- Prahlāda said, in effect: **"I do not fear You"** — "You are very fearsome to look at: Your angry eyes, Your pointed ears, Your lolling tongue, Your mane soaked with the blood of my father. But I am not afraid of these things. **What I am afraid of is coming under the influence of this powerful material energy.**"
- This is the only fear a devotee knows: the material energy is very powerful. Even great demigods like Brahmā, Śiva, and Indra sometimes fall under its influence — what to speak of "puny little earthlings like us."

### Praying for Smaranam — Remembrance in Love

- Our whole life, our whole attempt in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, is to get some **smaraṇam** — to be able to remember the Lord under any and all circumstances. We hanker for the smaraṇam to remember the Lord **in love.**
- We can only remember a person if we love them or hate them. **Kaṁsa** remembered the Lord constantly because he hated Kṛṣṇa — but the devotee wants to remember the Lord only in the mood of loving devotion.
- That comes in small increments, according to **Brahma-saṁhitā** — there is a nice verse that the devotee advances in small, tiny steps; it is not easy. That which is gained after many lifetimes, we do not want to lose — we do not want to lose that precious memory of the Lord, that remembrance in devotion. So we pray for that protection: "Let me never forget Your lotus feet."

### Praying for the Body Only for Service — Gajendra

- We should pray for the protection of this body only when we want to use it for devotional service — because that is selfless, for the Lord's service.
- This is described in the **Eighth Canto**, in the deliverance of **Gajendra.** In Prabhupāda's purport he asks the question: is it right that a devotee like Gajendra asks for physical protection — is this something selfish?
  - **The answer:** No. If the devotee is asking for protection of his body so that his service to the Lord remains undisturbed — which means Kṛṣṇa is not disturbed — then that type of prayer is **pure bhakti**, a prayer full of loving devotion. And if that is our mood, the Lord will protect.

### The Lord's Promises of Protection

- Bhagavad-gītā is full of verses where the Lord promises to protect His devotees:
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 9.31** — *kaunteya pratijānīhi na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati*: "O son of Kuntī, declare it boldly — My devotee is never vanquished."
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 9.13** — *mahātmānas tu māṁ pārtha daivīṁ prakṛtim āśritāḥ*: the great souls, those who are not deluded (not in māyā), work under the protection of the divine energy.
- There are many practical examples in the Bhāgavatam of devotees who took shelter of the Lord and were protected, so they could go on with their service, giving great pleasure to Kṛṣṇa: **Parīkṣit** protected in the womb, **Kuntī**, the **Pāṇḍavas**, **Mahārāja Ambarīṣa**, **Prahlāda Mahārāja.**

## Protection Comes in Proportion to Surrender

- Protection also comes in proportion to surrender — like anything else: protection, mercy, or love. We are asking Kṛṣṇa for all these things — we are very small and tiny, begging the Lord for protection, for service, for mercy; ultimately we are begging Him for **love.**
- We would like to have all that the great devotees have — but we get those things only in proportion to how surrendered we are to Kṛṣṇa. We would like the Lord to embrace us as He embraced **Hanumān**; we would like Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva to appear and protect us as He protected Prahlāda Mahārāja; we would like the affectionate glance of the Lord as He gives the **Brajabāsīs** in Vṛndāvana. But to get those things we must have the same **bhakti** as those devotees.
- So our desire must be of a particular quality. **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.6** — *sa vai puṁsāṁ paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokṣaje / ahaituky apratihatā yayātmā suprasīdati*: that devotion must be **unmotivated and uninterrupted** (*ahaitukī apratihatā*). Then Kṛṣṇa can reciprocate fully with the devotee. Our desire should be with no motive, completely selfless — that is how Kṛṣṇa will return to all of us.

### Draupadi's Rescue

- **Story:** Draupadī once came to Kṛṣṇa after her painful experience — having been lost in the gambling match and then humiliated by Duḥśāsana, who tried to strip her naked before so many men. She asked, "Lord, why did it take You so long to come to my rescue?"
- Kṛṣṇa smiled and explained why He had held back:
  - "First, you took shelter of **Arjuna and Bhīma**, saying to them, 'Can't you see what is happening to your chaste wife right before your eyes?' At that time I thought I was not really needed — you didn't call for Me." (Though in a certain sense Arjuna and Bhīma did nothing.)
  - "Then you took shelter of **Droṇa**, the teacher of the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas — 'You are a brāhmaṇa; how can you tolerate this?' So you didn't call Me; I thought I was not needed."
  - "Then you took shelter of **Bhīṣma**, the powerful warrior and respected grandfather of the dynasty. He didn't help, so you went to **Dhṛtarāṣṭra** and so many kings."
  - "Then finally you tried to protect yourself by holding on to your sari with both hands. I was thinking, 'She thinks she is very strong; she can protect herself,' so perhaps I should just walk away — not needed."
  - "**Only when you let go and raised both of your hands, and called out 'Hey Govinda! Hey Kṛṣṇa!' — did I come. Because I knew you wanted only Me.**"
- The point: full protection comes when we fully surrender to Kṛṣṇa. To the degree that you surrender, you will be protected.
- So the purport of the whole verse is that in this Kali-yuga we can be saved and protected from its influence only by taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa.

## Why Krishna Protects: We Have Important Responsibility

- Once we get that protection — then what? Kṛṣṇa protects His servants because they have a very important responsibility.
- **The government / soldiers:** A government spends millions and millions of dollars supplying its soldiers who go to fight on behalf of the country — the best weapons, the best clothes — because they are doing very responsible service.
- Similarly, why do we pray for protection, mercy, or love? Because we have an important duty: **to always share our good fortune with others.**
- The Lord Himself comes to this material world — not whimsically, but with a particular mission to deliver the conditioned souls — and those who help Him naturally receive some special facilities for that purpose. So we must always remember this is a **preaching movement**; the Lord and His representatives descend for that purpose.

---

## Conclusion: Just Be a Good Devotee

- Again: if we are very strict in our own practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness — strictly following the rules and regulations and chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa — and, according to our capacity, very active in helping our spiritual master and Lord Caitanya preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness, we can expect these things — **protection, mercy, and love** — to appear within our lives as well.
- Because a devotee's heart is so pure, the very unfortunate things of Kali-yuga will not befall the devotee.
- **So do not be in anxiety, do not be fearful. Just be a good devotee — and the Lord will protect you in your important services.**

## Glossary

- **Kali-yuga** — the present age of quarrel and hypocrisy, fully manifest since Krishna's departure from earth
- **apauruseya** — not made by any mundane being; why the predictions of shastra are infallible, unlike Nostradamus
- **shastra** — revealed scripture, the perfect authority whose predictions are certain to come true
- **smaranam** — remembrance of the Lord; the devotee's constant hankering is to remember Krishna in love under all conditions
- **bhakti** — devotional service; pure bhakti includes even praying to protect a body used only in the Lord's service
- **ahaituki apratihata** — unmotivated and uninterrupted; the quality of devotion with which Krishna fully reciprocates
- **sudra** — the laborer class; in Kali-yuga it is said everyone is born with sudra qualities
- **sankirtana** — the congregational chanting and preaching mission that devotees carry as their important responsibility
- **Mukunda** — a name of Krishna as the giver of liberation and protector of His devotees
- **Kalki** — the future incarnation who will appear with a sword when only the demoniac remain at the end of Kali-yuga
