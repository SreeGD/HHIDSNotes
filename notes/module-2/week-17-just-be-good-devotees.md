# Just be Good devotees

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 17 · Foundations in Bhakti · 50:48

## Summary

Speaking on Srimad Bhagavatam 1.15.36, Indradyumna Swami explains that the age of Kali became fully manifest the very day Lord Krishna left the earth, and that its influence falls only on those of a poor fund of knowledge who are not sheltered by the Lord. He reviews the grim Twelfth Canto predictions of Kali-yuga—the daily decline of religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, memory, strength and lifespan, and future famine, heavy taxes and drought that will force people to survive on leaves and roots—stressing that, unlike Nostradamus, these are the infallible apauruseya predictions of shastra. The worst prophecy, he says, is that eventually there will be no devotees, for it is the association of devotees that lets one tolerate any austerity and keep remembering the Lord. Yet the true sadhu or guru always follows his analysis of the age's miseries with the positive solution—take shelter of Krishna—in contrast to a mere analyst like Jean-Paul Sartre, who dissected the material world but, offering no solution, ended in despair. Through the stories of the crabs on the Bombay beach, Lord Shiva watching Rama slay Ravana ('rakhe Krishna mare ke'), Prahlada's fearlessness before Nrsimhadeva, and the saving of Draupadi, he shows that protection, mercy and love come in exact proportion to one's surrender. The real prayer of a devotee is not for bodily safety but to be protected from maya so as never to forget the Lord's lotus feet, though one may also pray to protect a body meant for Krishna's service. He concludes that a devotee's heart is pure or being purified, so Kali's dangers—reactions meant for the sinful—do not apply; just as an honest citizen need not fear the government while a thief lives in anxiety, a sincere devotee should not be fearful but simply 'be a good devotee' and trust the Lord's protection, which comes because devotees carry the important responsibility of sharing their good fortune with others.

## Key Points

- Kali-yuga became fully manifest the moment Krishna departed the earth; its influence affects only those not developed in God consciousness, and it can be neutralized by taking shelter of the Personality of Godhead (SB 1.15.36 and purport).
- Twelfth Canto predictions: religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, tolerance, mercy, lifespan, strength and memory diminish day by day, and famine, excessive taxes and drought will drive people to eat leaves, roots, flesh, fruits and flowers—infallible because shastra is apauruseya, unlike the subtle guesses of Nostradamus.
- Prabhupada told a book distributor who wished to return birth after birth, 'No, don't come back—this Kali-yuga is only going to get worse'; Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati said all his disciples could return to Godhead in one lifetime if very serious (strict in rules and chanting, and active in preaching).
- The sadhu/guru cuts material attachment by exposing the futility of material life and then supplies the solution—contrasted with Jean-Paul Sartre, whose books read 'like a summary of the Gita' (duhkhalayam asasvatam) but gave no solution, leaving him in despair; a newspaper likewise shows Kali's evils but offers no remedy.
- Buddha sent a grieving mother door to door to beg rice only from a house where no one had died; finding death in every home taught her that loss is universal, not a personal exception—a lesson in renunciation and cleansing the heart before bhakti can begin.
- Protection comes in proportion to surrender: shown by the retreating crabs and Shiva's saying 'rakhe Krishna mare ke, mare Krishna rakhe ke,' by Prahlada fearing only maya and not Nrsimhadeva's ferocious form, and by Draupadi being saved only when she let go of her sari and raised both hands crying 'Govinda!'
- A devotee's real prayer is to be protected from illusion so as never to forget Krishna's lotus feet (protecting the body is pure only when the body is used in His service); devotees receive special protection, mercy and love because of their responsibility to share Krishna consciousness, so one should 'just be a good devotee' and be free of anxiety, like an honest citizen who need not fear the government.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.15.36 — 'yada mukundo bhagavan imam mahim / jahau sva-tanva sravaniya-sat-kathah...': Kali became fully manifest when Krishna left the earth, to trouble those of poor knowledge; the day's lecture verse.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.2.1 — 'tatas canu-dinam dharmah...': the decline of religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, lifespan, bodily strength and memory in Kali-yuga.
- Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 12 — the prediction that famine, excessive taxes and drought will force people to live on leaves, roots, flesh, fruits and flowers ('shaka-mula-...').
- Bhagavad-gita 7.19 — 'bahunam janmanam ante jnanavan mam prapadyate / vasudevah sarvam iti sa mahatma sudurlabhah': after many births the wise soul surrenders to Krishna.
- Bhagavad-gita 8.15 — 'duhkhalayam asasvatam': the material world is a temporary place full of suffering (applied to Sartre's analysis of matter).
- Bhagavad-gita 9.31 — 'kaunteya pratijanihi na me bhaktah pranasyati': O son of Kunti, declare boldly that My devotee never perishes; cited as one of many verses promising the Lord's protection.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6 — 'sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhoksaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati': the supreme dharma is unmotivated, uninterrupted devotion that satisfies the self.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23 — 'sravanam kirtanam vishnoh smaranam...': the nine processes of devotional service; cited for the devotee's hankering after smaranam (remembrance of the Lord).
- Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 7 — Prahlada Maharaj before Lord Nrsimhadeva (Prabhupada's favorite section for lecturing); Prahlada declares he fears not the Lord's ferocious features but only falling under the influence of material energy.
- Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 8 — the Deliverance of Gajendra; praying that one's service to the Lord remain undisturbed is described as pure bhakti.
- Brahma-samhita — an unspecified verse cited to show that remembrance/devotion of the Lord is gained in small increments over many lifetimes and should not be lost.
- Teachings of Queen Kunti (A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada) — the account of Parvati and Lord Shiva watching Rama's battle with Ravana at Lanka.
- Bengali proverb quoted by Lord Shiva: 'Rakhe Krishna mare ke, mare Krishna rakhe ke' — if Krishna protects a person none can kill him, and if Krishna would kill him none can protect him.
