# Appreciating Devotees

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 27 ·  · 1:21:17

## Summary

Speaking at a temple 'Festival of Appreciation,' Indradyumna Swami takes up Prahlada Maharaja's prayer from the Seventh Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam, in which Prahlada rejects material opulence, liberation, and mystic perfection (bhukti, mukti, siddhi) and begs only to be placed in the service of a pure devotee, using it to explain why appreciating and associating with devotees is central to Krishna consciousness. He teaches that Krishna decorates his surrendered devotees with all good qualities and places them in prominent positions so ordinary people will recognize and follow them, comparing this to war heroes honored with medals after World War II. Drawing on the verse titiksavah karunikah (SB 3.25.21), he describes the genuine devotee as tolerant even in calamity, compassionate (compassion being the crest-jewel of Vaishnava qualities), and a friend to every living being with no enemies, because he sees everyone's original nature as an eternal servant of Krishna. Because we are the Lord's tiny marginal energy (tatastha-shakti), we take on the qualities of whatever we associate with, like a chameleon, so bad association, including the subtle 'one-eyed guru' of television, music, and idle talk, can quickly drag down even great souls (illustrated by Ajamila, the tortoise and the hare, and swine flu), while a single moment's association with a devotee can award all success and transmit bhakti like a contagious 'good disease.' The secret for devotees living among materialists, he says, is to give association rather than take it. He then summarizes the five most potent limbs of devotion, associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing the Bhagavatam, residing in Mathura, and worshiping the deity, arguing that association of devotees is the 'salt' that brings out the flavor and result of all the others, since chanting must be learned through initiation from a bona fide guru and the Bhagavatam heard from a living 'person-bhagavata,' not merely read. He urges the devotees to recognize and serve the advanced Vaishnavas present in this fleeting generation, honoring every devotee's service great or small (like the tiny screw that holds a harmonium together and the 'silent soldiers' behind the feast). He closes by having everyone fold their hands and recite the Vaishnava-pranama prayer to honor all the Vaishnavas present and throughout the world.

## Key Points

- Krishna decorates his surrendered devotees with all good qualities (sadhu-bhusana) and places them in leadership so the public can recognize and follow them, just as post-World War II heroes wore medals and were honored; installing Yudhisthira as emperor of the world was one purpose of the Kurukshetra war.
- The marks of a real devotee (SB 3.25.21): tolerant and unmoved in calamity (titiksavah), merciful and eager to share his good fortune (karunikah, the crest-jewel of Vaishnava qualities), and a friend to all beings with no enemies (suhrdah sarva-dehinam), because he sees everyone as an eternal servant of Krishna covered only by lust, anger, and greed (as Christ forgave his executioners).
- A devotee is honored for his service since joining, never judged by his past; even a one-week-old devotee has made a 'quantum leap,' and even fallen or departed devotees are honored for service Krishna never forgets.
- As the Lord's infinitesimal tatastha-shakti we adopt the qualities of our association like a chameleon changing color; bad association (asat-sanga), especially the subtle 'one-eyed guru' of TV, music, and mundane talk, can ruin even great souls in a moment (Ajamila fell from one glance; swine flu / H1N1 fells the strongest; the overconfident hare loses to the determined tortoise), so one must stay 'afraid of Maya.'
- The secret to living among materialists without being affected is not to take their association but to give association (Prabhupada's instruction: never look back or envy the sense enjoyers); conversely, even a moment's association with a devotee awards all success (sadhu-sanga) and transmits bhakti like a good, contagious disease, as when the young Indradyumna Swami stayed awake through Prabhupada's Hindi lecture: 'It is transcendental subject matter, it will act.'
- The five most potent limbs of devotion (CC Madhya 22.128) are associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing the Bhagavatam, residing in Mathura, and worshiping the deity; association of devotees is the 'salt' that brings out the flavor and result of the other four, since chanting must be learned through initiation from a bona fide guru and the Bhagavatam heard from a living 'person-bhagavata,' not merely read (Bhaktisiddhanta: 'you have only seen stone').
- Every devotee's service, big or small, is essential like the tiny screw that holds the harmonium together, and each generation of Vaishnavas is a unique passing 'wave'; devotees should recognize, serve, and take advantage of the advanced souls present now, and honor everyone who has turned their back on Maya ('just say no') and turned toward Krishna.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.28 (Prahlada Maharaja's prayers, Canto 7 Ch. 9) — the base verse and Prabhupada's purport: Prahlada rejects bhukti, mukti, and siddhi (anima, laghima, mahima, etc.) and prays only to be placed in touch with and to serve a pure devotee.
- Bhagavad-gita 2.20 — 'na hanyate hanyamane sarire': the soul is never vanquished even when the body is destroyed; the intelligent man cares for the eternal spirit soul.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.9 — one must understand Krishna in truth, and this is possible only by serving a pure devotee.
- Bhagavad-gita 3.21 — 'whatever a great man does, common men follow; whatever standard he sets, the world pursues,' cited for why the Lord puts devotees in the limelight.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.34 — 'tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya': approach the spiritual master, inquire submissively, and render service; the guru-disciple relationship is one of hearing.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.10 — 'dadami buddhi-yogam tam yena mam upayanti te': to those who serve with love the Lord gives understanding (illustrated with the joke that Krishna gave the intelligence for the iPod to Steve Jobs).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 3.25.21 — 'titiksavah karunikah suhrdah sarva-dehinam / ajata-satravah santah sadhavah sadhu-bhusanah': Kapiladeva's description of the qualities of a saintly devotee, the thematic backbone of the talk.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6 — the supreme occupation (para dharma) for all mankind is loving devotional service to the Lord that is unmotivated and uninterrupted, by which the self is fully satisfied.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.18 — 'nastaprayesv abhadresu nityam bhagavata-sevaya': by regularly hearing the Bhagavatam and serving the pure devotee, all that is troublesome in the heart is destroyed and loving service becomes fixed.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.18.13 — Suta Goswami: a moment's association with a devotee of the Lord cannot be compared to attaining the heavenly planets, liberation, or material prosperity meant for those who are to die.
- Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 6 (Ajamila) — the story of Ajamila, a pakka brahmana boy who fell to become a thief after one moment of inattention seeing an amorous couple, cited on the danger of even brief bad association.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 22.54 — 'sadhu-sanga sadhu-sanga sarva-sastre kaya, lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya': even a moment's association with a sadhu yields all perfection.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 22.128 — the five most potent limbs of devotion: sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-sravana, mathura-vasa, and sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana (deity worship).
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 22.87 — 'asat-sanga-tyaga, ei vaisnava-acara': a Vaishnava should give up intimate association with non-devotees (quoted as the teaching of Mahaprabhu / Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati).
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 20.108 — 'jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya-dasa': every living being is by constitution an eternal servant of Krishna, covered only by past sin and ignorance.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi-lila 9 — Lord Chaitanya as the transcendental gardener with the fruit of love of God who alone cannot distribute it and calls for helpers.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Antya 5.131 — 'bhagavata pada giya bhagavata-sthane': one should study/hear the Bhagavatam from a self-realized person-bhagavata, the transparent via-medium.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 8.248 (Ramananda-samvada) — of all distresses the most painful is separation from a devotee of Krishna.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 5 (Saksi-gopal) — the deity walking to bear witness; the young brahmin's faith: 'Lord, if you can talk, you can walk.'
- Siksastakam, verse 3 — 'trnad api sunicena taror api sahisnuna...': one should chant in a humble state of mind, lower than the straw, more tolerant than a tree, offering all respect to others.
- Sri Gurv-astaka, verse 3 — 'sri-vigraharadhana-nitya-nana-srngara-tan-mandira-marjanadau': the spiritual master is always engaged in the temple worship of Sri Sri Radha-Krishna and engages his disciples in it; deity worship is really assisting the guru.
- Sad-gosvamy-astaka — the six Goswamis of Vrindavan were dear/popular to both the gentle and the ruffians, cited on how divine qualities attract even non-devotees.
- Madhurashtakam (Vallabhacharya, Vallabha-sampradaya) — the song on the sweetness of Krishna's feet, body, and flute ('sweeter than the sweet'), cited on hankering for Krishna once the senses are purified.
- Narottama dasa Thakura, Prarthana ('sei sanga...' / 'ei sab chadi ara') — 'unable to attain the association of Lord Gauranga and his devotees, Narottama simply weeps.'
- Mahabharata — 'mahajano yena gatah sa panthah': one should follow the path shown by great personalities such as Prahlada Maharaja.
- Nectar of Devotion (Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu) — the 64 items of devotional service, of which the five most essential limbs are singled out.
- Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's verse ('...hari hari...') — his prophecy that from the moment the holy names of Hari appeared, Vaishnavas adorned with tilak and neckbeads and equipped with the Mahamantra would manifest everywhere and purify the atmosphere in the age of Kali; also cited for association of devotees plus chanting being the two essentials of this age.
- Bible, Gospel of Luke 23:34 — Jesus on the cross: 'Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do,' cited as an example of a devotee befriending even the inimical.
- Vaishnava-pranama — 'vancha-kalpa-tarubhyas ca krpa-sindhubhya eva ca / patitanam pavanebhyo vaisnavebhyo namo namah,' recited collectively at the end to honor all the Vaishnavas.
