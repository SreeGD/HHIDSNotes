# The Heart of a Vaisnava

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 20 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:09:50

## Summary

Speaking on the appearance day of Lord Nrisimhadev, Indradyumna Swami reads Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.8.34, which describes how, after slaying Hiranyakashipu, Lord Nrisimhadev fearlessly took his seat upon the demon's throne while everyone present trembled in awe. He explains that just as a victorious warrior enjoys the spoils (vibhuti) of battle—illustrated by his own boyhood memory of the battlefield bayonet his father gave him at age thirteen—the Lord reclaimed the throne that had originally belonged to Indra, since Krishna as sarva-loka-maheshvara is the true owner and enjoyer of everything. Drawing on Bhagavad-gita 4.8, he argues that of the three reasons the Lord descends, only the deliverance of His pure devotees actually requires His personal appearance; demons can be destroyed by natural calamities and dharma can be restored by sincere preachers, and Prabhupada joked he could reestablish dharma in forty-five minutes. He stresses that Nrisimhadev appeared not because Hiranyakashipu terrorized the universe but because His devotee Prahlada was persecuted, and that a pure devotee's suffering is never a karmic reaction, since karma is burned at initiation. This protective mood continues today: Nrisimhadev scratched Chand Kazi's chest to stop him persecuting Lord Chaitanya's sankirtana, and He answered Srila Prabhupada's prayer to remove Mr. Nair, the cheater who blocked the Juhu Beach temple land—Prabhupada explaining that one killed by the Lord attains at least liberation. Through the Shikshashtaka and the verse "the pure devotee is always in the core of My heart," the Swami describes the mutual love between the Lord and His devotee, contrasting Prahlada's unwavering faith (he never once prayed to be rescued) with the anxious demigods, who are Vaishnavas but not pure devotees. He concludes that the Lord accepts only the positive—sitting on the impure throne without hesitation because Hiranyakashipu was really the fallen Vaikuntha gatekeeper Jaya—and that we should even feel grateful to Hiranyakashipu, whose "negative propaganda" (as Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati wrote in 1927) revealed this glorious pastime.

## Key Points

- Reading SB 7.8.34: after killing Hiranyakashipu, Nrisimhadev sits fearlessly on the demon's trophy throne (which originally belonged to Indra); as sarva-loka-maheshvara Krishna is the rightful owner of everything. Illustrated by the Swami's boyhood war-trophy bayonet given by his father.
- Of the three reasons for the Lord's descent (BG 4.8, paritranaya sadhunam...), only delivering pure devotees requires His personal appearance; destroying demons and restoring dharma can be done by calamities or by preachers (Prabhupada's 'forty-five minutes' joke).
- The Lord intervenes for pure devotees, not for mass suffering that is itself karmic purification; a devotee's suffering is not karma because karma is burned at initiation. Nrisimhadev came specifically to save Prahlada, not merely to end universal terror.
- The pastime is not mythology but recurs in history: Nrisimhadev clawed Chand Kazi's chest to protect Chaitanya's sankirtana, and answered Prabhupada's prayer to kill Mr. Nair in the fight over the Juhu Beach land—death by the Lord granting the demon liberation.
- A pure devotee's faith: Prahlada never once prayed for protection (no such verse exists in the Bhagavatam)—'if the Lord wants to kill me none can save me; if He wants to protect me none can kill me, so I simply continue my service.' The anxious demigods are Vaishnavas but not on this platform; Prahlada is counted among the twelve mahajanas.
- The heart of a Vaishnava (Shikshashtaka: na dhanam na janam na sundarim) and the heart of the Lord contain only mutual love—'the pure devotee is in the core of My heart and I in his'; a unity of loving devotional service, like Jesus's 'I and the Father are one.'
- The Lord accepts only the positive: He sat on the impure throne without hesitation because Hiranyakashipu was really the cursed Vaikuntha doorkeeper Jaya. We should even thank Hiranyakashipu, whose 'negative propaganda' (Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, The Harmonist, 1927) glorified Nrisimhadev and revealed this lila.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.8.34 — the verse read for the class: Lord Nrisimhadev, of fearsome effulgent countenance, takes His seat on Hiranyakashipu's throne while none dares approach to offer respects.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.8 (paritranaya sadhunam vinashaya cha dushkritam / dharma-samsthapanartaya sambhavami yuge yuge) — the three reasons the Lord descends; the Swami argues only deliverance of devotees needs His personal presence.
- Bhagavad-gita 5.29 — 'sarva-loka-maheshvaram,' Krishna as the supreme owner and enjoyer of all worlds and demigods.
- Bhagavad-gita 7.19 — 'bahunam janmanam ante,' one surrenders to Krishna only after many births; even Krishna admits surrender is not easy.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.31 — 'kaunteya pratijanihi na me bhaktah pranashyati,' declare it boldly, O son of Kunti, My devotee never perishes.
- Shikshashtaka, verse 4 (Lord Chaitanya) — 'na dhanam na janam na sundarim,' the pure heart of a Vaishnava free of material desire.
- 'The pure devotee is always in the core of My heart, and I am always in the heart of My pure devotee; he knows no one but Me and I know no one but him' — Srimad-Bhagavatam 9.4.68 (sadhavo hridayam mahyam...), from the Ambarisha–Durvasa pastime.
- Nrisimha prayer 'mrityu-mrityum namamy aham' (ugram viram maha-vishnum jvalantam sarvato mukham...) — obeisances to Nrisimhadev, terrifying yet auspicious, who is death personified to death itself.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi-lila, Chand Kazi episode) — Nrisimhadev appears at night to Chand Kazi, scratching his chest, to stop his persecution of the sankirtana movement.
- 'Vaishnava Ke?' by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura — book analyzing 'what kind of Vaishnava are you,' and how a devotee's level (madhyama/uttama) is measured by faith.
- Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, article in The Harmonist (1927) — credits Hiranyakashipu, even more than Prahlada, with glorifying Lord Nrisimhadev through 'negative propaganda.'
- The twelve mahajanas — Prahlada Maharaja cited as a foremost pure-devotee authority (list given in SB 6.3.20).
