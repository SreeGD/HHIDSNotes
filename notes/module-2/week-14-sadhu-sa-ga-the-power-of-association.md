# Sadhu-saṅga: The Power of Association

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 14 · Foundations in Bhakti · 32:18

**Themes:** sadhu-sanga · five limbs of bhakti · hearing from Vaishnavas · kirtan · deity worship · holy dhama

## Summary

Drawing on Srila Rupa Goswami's Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (Nectar of Devotion), Indradyumna Swami explains that of the 64 items of devotional service, five are singled out as the most essential limbs (angas): chanting Hare Krishna, hearing Srimad Bhagavatam, worshipping the deity, living in or visiting a holy place, and associating with devotees. He argues that sadhu-sanga (association with devotees) is the most essential of the five because it draws the essence out of the other four, comparing it to salt that brings out the flavor of every dish. Although chanting the holy name is the yuga-dharma that anyone can perform to remove sinful reactions and material desires, attaining prema requires receiving the mantra from a bona fide guru and chanting congregationally in the association of devotees. Each of the other limbs likewise depends on hearing from pure devotees: the Bhagavatam must be heard from a Vaishnava, since hearing it from impure professional reciters is like drinking milk poisoned by a serpent's lips; deity worship and knowledge of Krishna's personal features come only through the guru-parampara; and the transcendental glories of the holy dhama are revealed not by sightseeing but by hearing from saintly persons, for 'we see through our ears, not our eyes.' He illustrates these points with personal stories of Srila Prabhupada and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, and cites Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's verses declaring that one is cured of the snakebite of material existence by chanting the names of Bhagavan in the company of Vaishnavas. He concludes that because Krishna consciousness cannot be perfected in isolation, Prabhupada founded ISKCON so devotees could associate, and that one should always honor devotees, beg forgiveness for offenses, and happily chant together.

## The Five Most Essential Limbs of Devotional Service

- In the **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu** by Srila Rupa Goswami, **64 items of devotional service** are mentioned as favorable for a devotee's progress.
- Out of those 64, five are recommended by Rupa Goswami as **especially favorable** — Prabhupada describes them as *angas* (limbs) of devotional service. As our goal is to advance in Krishna consciousness, we often discuss these five:
  1. **Chanting Hare Krishna** (the holy name)
  2. **Hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam**
  3. **Worshipping the Deity**
  4. **Living in or regularly visiting a holy place (dhama)**
  5. **Associating with devotees (sadhu-sanga)**
- Of these five, the most essential is actually **associating with devotees** — because even though chanting the holy name is the **yuga-dharma**, specifically prescribed for advancing in this age, without the association of devotees it would not be possible to perfect our chanting.

## Chanting Requires the Association of Devotees

- When Mahaprabhu appeared, He taught us the art of chanting **in the association of devotees.** The incarnation of the Lord for this age is recognized by the fact that He is singing and dancing to the holy names, always surrounded by His devotees who are singing (the theme of **Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.5.32** — *krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam... yajnaih sankirtana-prayair yajanti hi su-medhasah*).
- To perfect the chanting of Hare Krishna, we require the mercy of the Lord.
- **The benefits of chanting** (discussed the previous day): freeing our hearts from all previous sins, uprooting our material desires, and attaining **prema**.
  - Anyone can chant and get free from sinful reactions; anyone can chant and uproot material desires.
  - But if you want **prema** — the highest goal of devotional service, the **param gati** (ultimate destination) — you must receive that mantra from a bona fide spiritual master coming in the disciplic succession. Without the association, instruction, and mercy of a devotee, we cannot perfect that chanting; we have to receive it from a devotee.

## Congregational Chanting — Glimpses of the Goal

- The type of chanting we are meant to do is **congregational chanting** (like the kirtan done earlier), because there the holy name is chanted very loudly, penetrates the ignorance, and goes straight to the heart.
- In his **Bhakti-sandarbha**, **Jiva Goswami** describes that this congregational chanting (*kirtanam*) is so important because **sometimes even a neophyte devotee may experience ecstatic symptoms** through it — a little glimpse of the goal.
  - **Cloudy-day analogy:** The goal is very far away. It is like a cloudy, dark, miserable day when suddenly the clouds part and a ray of light comes in — beautiful. So sometimes we get these little glimpses, a little experience, as an impetus to keep going.
- Although chanting Hare Krishna is the process, without the association of devotees it would be very hard to perfect.

## Hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam — From the Mouth of a Vaishnava

- Through the association of devotees we enter into the mysteries of **Srimad-Bhagavatam** (another of the five processes). The Bhagavatam should be heard specifically **from the mouth of a Vaishnava.**
- Others also comment on the Bhagavatam — professional reciters who recite it for a living — but because their own lives may not be clean (impure, engaged in sinful activities, with ulterior motives), hearing the Bhagavatam from such persons **does not have the real effect of cleansing the heart.**

## The University "Blitzkrieg" Story

- As a young devotee in America (1970–71), the speaker's party did what they called a **"blitzkrieg"** — not just going to dorms, but walking into university **classrooms** in their robes, announcing that they were devotees of Lord Krishna distributing books by their spiritual master that could solve all the problems of life. They were bold — sometimes students came forth and bought books, sometimes teachers kicked them out.
- **University of Kentucky** (conservative part of the country): The speaker knocked, walked into a class of about 60 students and a teacher: "Hare Krishna! I'm from the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, distributing books on the science of self-realization," and recited the pranati **"om vishnu-padaya... A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, coming in the line of Srila Rupa Goswami"** ("a little fanatic").
  - The teacher said, "Very nice, young man. Sit down. I'm giving a course on **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu** to graduate students" — an advanced class on theology and religion, studying the works of the **six Goswamis**, speaking from the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*. From what little the speaker knew, the teacher was rendering it "pretty much as it was."
  - The speaker listened for 45 minutes (his book collection was very low that day). **But as soon as the teacher stopped speaking that elevated philosophy, the students stood up, lit cigarettes, chewed gum, boys and girls started hugging or holding hands, and began talking nonsense from the news.**
  - The lesson: though the teacher may have intellectually grasped the philosophy, because he lacked purity it had no effect on his audience — they remained attached to mere intellectual activity. "A good lesson for me."

## Milk Touched by the Lips of a Serpent

- A strong statement: hearing from an unqualified person is like **milk touched by the lips of a serpent.**
  - In India, ladies boil milk to remove impurities and set it on the window ledge to cool before offering it to the family or making milk sweets. Sometimes a **snake** comes and drinks a little of the milk — because drinking the milk makes its venom more powerful. But in doing so, some of its venom goes into the milk, and unknowingly the housewife serves it to the family — "and you can imagine."
- So if we hear shastra from unqualified persons, it has an adverse effect. But if we hear from **sadhus, saintly persons, Vaishnavas**, it has a very good effect.
- Supporting verse (paraphrased from **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.18**): **by regularly attending the Bhagavatam class and rendering service to the pure devotee, all that is inauspicious within the heart is destroyed** (*nashta-prayeshv abhadreshu nityam bhagavata-sevaya*), and irrevocable loving service to the Lord becomes manifest in the heart. If we sit and hear from qualified devotees, the effect is that we very quickly give up our material attachments and become eager for devotional service. So we must hear the Bhagavatam — one of the five angas — from a devotee to get the real effect.

## Worshipping the Deity — Learned From the Pure Devotee

- We have beautiful historic Deities in **Seattle**, going back to the very beginning of the movement. But who would know anything about the art of **dressing the Deity, cooking for the Deity, and praying to the Deity** unless Prabhupada taught us these details?
- The third verse of the **Gurv-ashtakam** reveals that the spiritual master takes great pleasure in training his devotees to dress the Deities in beautiful ornaments, because he personally knows what the Lord is wanting.
- **The kichari pastime:** As a young boy, Prabhupada was nicknamed **"kichari"** because he loved kichari so much that when he went to school he would sometimes put some in his pocket as a snack.
  - In the early days Prabhupada once cooked kichari, offered it, and distributed it to his disciples. A godbrother said, "Prabhupada, I have never tasted a kichari like that." Prabhupada said, **"That's because it's Mother Yashoda's recipe."** The disciple's jaw dropped — "Mother Yashoda's recipe." You won't find that recipe listed in any cookbook. This was an intimate revelation of Sri Rupa Goswami's / Prabhupada's identity as an eternal resident of Vrindavana who knew exactly what pleases Krishna.
- To worship the Deity we require the **guru-parampara**, the pure devotee, to reveal to us exactly what Krishna looks like — we give Him the **flute**, the **Vaijayanti garland** (five types of flowers reaching down to His ankles) — these specific details about the Supreme Personality.
- **Why the name "Krishna Consciousness":** When Prabhupada was about to incorporate the movement in **1966**, a name was suggested — the "International Society for God Consciousness." Prabhupada said no: **"We will call it the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, because Krishna is God."**
  - This is the beauty of the movement — it reveals the personality of Godhead. Without knowing the details of Godhead, how would we fall in love with Krishna? How can you love someone you don't know? The impetus of love is the way a person looks, walks, talks, dresses, and eats. So Prabhupada revealed all these details about Krishna, and thus we dress Him accordingly, so that when people come they can be attracted and fall in love with Krishna.
- Without the association of a devotee we would have our own concocted ideas — like the **Israelites**, who wanted to worship God and so made a statue of a **golden calf**. Forevermore the Christians say we cannot worship idols — we agree, but that does not mean you cannot worship the **Deity, the real form of God.** Prabhupada was so kind to introduce Deity worship into the Western and Southern countries — something very unique, that we can approach the Lord and say, **"My dear Lord, You are not a statue; You are directly the son of Maharaja Nanda."** And how to worship the son of Maharaja Nanda, Krishna — that we learn from our spiritual master.

## Living in a Holy Place — Hearing From the Sadhus Who Live There

- When we go to a holy place, to really understand the transcendental glories of the **dhama** we require the association of the **sadhus** who live there.
- **Story from a great Parikrama in the 1930s:** So many devotees came that they couldn't fit into the temple room (in the Gaudiya Matha the temple room is basically just for **darshan**, with the lecture hall elsewhere). While **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** was lecturing, the conch blew for the **arati** nearby, and several of his disciples stood up, left the lecture, and went to see the arati. When they came back, Bhaktisiddhanta stopped and asked:
  - "Where were you?" — "We went to see Krishna." He said, **"Sit down. If you want to see Krishna, you have to hear from me. You just saw stone. Better you had sat here and heard my lecture to understand Krishna than having gone [to see the arati] in the middle of my [instruction]."**
- **We see through our ears, not through our eyes.** To go to the dhama in a "touristic mode" — just to look and try to understand — is fine, no harm; but if you want to enter into the mysteries of the dhama, the spiritual master is described as **the revealer of the dhama**, and he reveals through his speaking.
- **The umbilical cord analogy:** Our main connection with the guru is hearing from him — just as the child in the womb's main connection with the mother is the umbilical cord; if the cord is severed, the child will die. So in our relationship with the spiritual master, our lifeline is hearing from him.

## "Why Are You Sleeping While I Am Talking?" — Transcendental Sound Acts

- **Story in Hyderabad (the farm):** On a very hot day Prabhupada was giving darshan to local people, speaking in Hindi. Tired from travel and festivities, and not having eaten, the speaker fell asleep.
  - In the depths of his slumber he heard Prabhupada's voice: **"Why are you sleeping while I am talking?"** Prabhupada's face was very angry; 30 or 40 people were present — terrifying to be chastised.
  - Instead of just saying "I'm sorry," the speaker offered a lame excuse: "But Prabhupada, I don't understand Hindi."
  - Prabhupada replied: **"It does not matter. This is shabda-brahma — this is transcendental sound. It will act. Stay awake."** (In those days devotees, working hard and not getting enough rest, sometimes did fall asleep; but it never happened to the speaker again.)
- **Medicine analogy:** "It will act" — like taking medicine on the authority of the doctor: you don't question, you just take it because you have faith; you don't know how it is working in your body. So take this medicine, take this mercy, take this grace **through the ear** — we hear.

## Manifest and Unmanifest Pastimes — "There's a Speck in Your Eye"

- When we go to the dhama, if we want to understand it **as it is**, it's not just a question of seeing — there is much more going on that you can't see, because there are **manifest and unmanifest** activities.
  - When the Lord was here 5,000 years ago, His pastimes were manifest; when He left, He wound them up and they became **unmanifest**. Krishna went back to the spiritual world, but **Gokula is the same as Goloka** — an eternal abode; there is no difference, and the pastimes are always going on. We just don't have the eyes to see — but we will see if we hear from our spiritual master. One day we'll see.
- **Prabhupada on Parikrama:** Prabhupada was describing the glories of Vrindavana to the first few fortunate disciples who took him on Parikrama — an idyllic description (a perfect description of the beauty of that place): where the *rasa* dance took place, the relationships Krishna has with His devotees, the cows, the beautiful forests (*vanas*).
  - One disciple raised his hand: **"You're speaking so nicely about the dhama, but I don't see it like that. I just see rickshaw-wallahs trying to get my money, pigs, open sewers, and diseased people in dilapidated buildings."**
  - Prabhupada said: **"That's because there's a speck in your eye. When that speck is removed, you will see the dhama as it is."** The "speck" is your desire for sense gratification, your material desires — only when those desires are removed will you see the dhama as it is.
  - **How to remove that desire?** Krishna says, **"Listen to Me. Listen to this."** (Like the speaker's mother: "Listen to me, young man — take the garbage out." We become distracted.) By listening, everything will be revealed — it all begins with hearing.
- So to go to the dhama and not hear from the saintly persons there means we'll miss the point — we won't really understand.
- That is why **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** once said (although he did encourage going out to preach): **"You can simply stay here at the Krishna temple. You don't have to go anywhere. You have kirtan; you discuss these things among yourselves."** He was a little wary the devotees might go out and get improper, bad association. (Nowadays the movement is stronger and devotees more advanced, so we can go — but the point stands: you can just sit in one place, hear from the sadhus, and the dhama will be revealed.)

## Association as the Salt — Drawing Out the Essence

- Out of the five — chanting Hare Krishna, hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam, worshipping the Deity, living in a holy place, and associating with devotees — all are essential, and we must focus on these five (and on the 64 for that matter, but life is short, so at least don't miss these five).
- To draw the essence out of those essential practices, without the association [of devotees] they would be lost. **Gour Govinda Maharaja** often uses the analogy of **salt**:
  - What does salt do? It brings out the flavor of the food. How many times have you been served a meal that wasn't properly salted? You say, "Please pass the salt." The speaker likes salt so much he even travels with his own — a natural herbal salt made in Austria — because food can be bland by nature's way. It is one of the little intricacies of this world that you put salt on food and the flavor comes out.
- **Prabhupada and the salted apple (Vrindavana):** After class, "like little ducklings," the devotees followed Prabhupada back to his little room in Vrindavana. Prabhupada asked for breakfast; a mataji brought fruit, and there was **salt** on the plate.
  - The speaker (an American boy used to cereal with fruit and sugar) thought it was a mistake — "it should be sugar" — and was about to point it out. But Prabhupada took a little pinch of the salt, put it on the apple, ate it, and enjoyed it. "I'm learning something new" — now the speaker also puts salt on his apple. People may think it crazy, but try it: salt by nature brings out the flavor.
- **In the same way that salt brings out the flavor of a variety of foods, the association of devotees — sadhu-sanga — brings the essence out of the other four processes** (chanting Hare Krishna, hearing the Srimad-Bhagavatam, worshipping the Deity, and going to the holy place). The essence is drawn out in the association of devotees.

## Why Prabhupada Created ISKCON — For Association

- Therefore, congregation. **Prabhupada created the International Society for Krishna Consciousness specifically so that people could come together and associate**, developing their devotional Krishna consciousness — so important.
- Therefore Prabhupada said: **if someone thinks he can become Krishna conscious outside of this movement, outside of the association of devotees, he is "living in a hallucination."** We should live in the association of devotees — specifically to get the full benefit of chanting Hare Krishna, which is the *yuga-dharma*.

## Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's Hundred Verses — Chanting Plus Association

- **Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya** found Lord Chaitanya unconscious on the floor of the Puri (Jagannatha) temple. Attracted, he wondered whether these were ecstatic symptoms or sickness (epilepsy). He took the young sannyasi back to his house — **which still exists in Puri today, the same walls** (a wonderful place to visit on pilgrimage).
- He said, "You're a young, handsome, 24-year-old man; maybe you took sannyasa a little early. Either way I will instruct you in the science of **Vedanta**." At the end he asked, "Why aren't you listening?" — "I'm listening, but you're not giving the proper understanding." — "Oh, you think you can do better? Who are you? What is your opinion?"
- So the Lord started speaking, revealed the proper understanding, and revealed Himself as the Lord. As soon as Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya realized he did not just have a sannyasi in his court but the Supreme Personality of Godhead before him — being a great pundit — **from his mouth flowed 100 beautiful shlokas glorifying Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, just like the Ganges flowing down.**
- **One of those verses stresses the importance of chanting Hare Krishna in the association of the devotees:** one gets relief from **the snakebite of samsara** by the medicinal herb (the natural process) — **"asadhu... bhagavan-nama"**, chanting the names of Bhagavan, of Krishna (Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare). Then he says **"...sri-vaishnava..."** — in the association of the devotees, by living in association with the devotees and serving them.
  - These two things are very potent: **chanting Hare Krishna**, and **living and serving in the association of devotees.** Independently they are potent, but put them together — **"nitroglycerin"**: nitrogen and glycerin are not so [explosive] separately, but put them together and... So you have the process of chanting Hare Krishna, done in association with devotees, in association with this movement (ISKCON, which Prabhupada said is non-different). If you chant Hare Krishna with the devotees in this movement, at great functions like this, you can be **cured from the snakebite of samsara**, perfect your Krishna consciousness, and go back home, back to Godhead.

## Closing — Begin the Day by Honoring the Devotees

- Therefore we begin our day by **offering obeisances to all the devotees**, honoring them, and asking forgiveness for any offense we may have made, consciously or unconsciously.
- Because **without the mercy of devotees, without their association, we cannot take even one step forward** in Krishna consciousness. We should always honor them and never dishonor them, always glorify them, live in their association, and happily chant together — Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.
- The lecture concludes with kirtan.

## Glossary

- **sadhu-sanga** — association with saintly devotees; the most essential limb of devotional service
- **anga** — a 'limb' or item of devotional service (five of the sixty-four are singled out as essential)
- **yuga-dharma** — the recommended religious process for the age; in Kali-yuga, chanting the holy name
- **prema** — pure love of God, the ultimate goal (prayojana) of devotional service
- **kirtana** — congregational chanting of the holy names, chanted loudly so it penetrates the heart
- **dhama** — the Lord's transcendental holy abode or place, revealed through hearing from saints
- **shabda-brahma** — transcendental spiritual sound, which acts on the soul even without intellectual understanding
- **Vaijayanti** — the garland of five kinds of flowers, reaching to Krishna's ankles, that adorns the deity
- **Vaishnava** — a devotee of Vishnu/Krishna, from whom Bhagavatam must be heard for its real effect
