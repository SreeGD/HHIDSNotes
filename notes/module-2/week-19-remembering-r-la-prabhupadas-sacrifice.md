# Remembering Śrīla Prabhupada's Sacrifice

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 19 · Foundations in Bhakti · 54:19

**Themes:** srila prabhupada · sacrifice · vrindavan · sankirtana mission · book distribution · missionary spirit

## Summary

Speaking during the Kartik parikrama at the Radha-Damodara temple in Vrindavan, Indradyumna Swami reminds the devotees that the patch of land between Rupa Goswami's samadhi mandir and his bhajan kutir was called by Srila Prabhupada, during his Nectar of Devotion lectures, the most sacred place in the entire universe. He explains that it is even more sacred for Prabhupada's followers, because it was here that Prabhupada resided for years, translated the First Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam, did his solitary bhajan, and received from Rupa Goswami the inspiration to carry Lord Chaitanya's sankirtana movement to the West. Prabhupada lived as a very simple, ascetic sadhu, begging his food and even the bus fare to Delhi and the money and paper to print his books; the Swami calls it a one-man show, a theater of a single actor. The central theme is that Prabhupada, unlike the many revered sannyasis who come to Vrindavan on kshetra sannyasa and never leave, was a missionary who chose to sacrifice a peaceful life and certain liberation in the dhama to go to the West with no guarantee that even one person would listen. Setting this in the lineage of great reverses and revivals in Vaishnava history, the Swami recalls how Bhaktivinoda Thakura single-handedly revived Lord Chaitanya's movement and his son Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, a ray of Vishnu, spread it across India, giving Prabhupada the formula: print books and distribute them everywhere. He urges the devotees, 'lest we forget,' to remember Prabhupada's sacrifice, and marvels that by Prabhupada's potency he changed not only the West but Vrindavan itself, raising the standard of Deity worship and softening the hearts of the Vrajavasis, who now joyfully greet Western devotees. He closes with living proof of the ongoing miracle, a young woman from St. Petersburg who by chance met devotees at the Kiev airport and joined the parikrama, and invites all to sit in Prabhupada's rooms and kitchen to pray for their own mission to succeed, before handing over to a scholar to glorify Rupa Goswami.

## Welcome and Setting: Sri Sri Radha-Damodara Temple

- Welcome to the sacred temple of **Sri Sri Radha-Damodara**, the beloved deities of Srila Jiva Goswami.
- Announcements before the discourse:
  - His Holiness Bhakti Bhringa Govinda Maharaja is not present this morning — he has gone to Delhi on important work relating to Kazakhstan, and also to purchase a very nice sound system so that everyone at the back can hear the discourses.
  - Every evening, as a very special part of the Parikrama program, there will be bhajans in the courtyard of Maharaja's house between 6 and 8 pm.
  - His Grace Dina Bandhu Prabhu was expected, but at the last moment His Holiness Gopal Krishna Maharaja requested him to accompany him and several devotees on parikrama to Govardhan. Hope is expressed that Dina Bandhu Prabhu can join this parikrama later.

## "The Most Sacred Land in the Universe"

- The gathering is seated in the space between two sacred buildings: the **Samadhi Mandir** where the divine form of **Srila Rupa Goswami** rests, and his **bhajana-kutir** (currently being rebuilt), the place where he did his bhajana.
- Srila Prabhupada described this space between the two buildings as **the most sacred land in the universe** — stated during his famous **Nectar of Devotion** lecture series.
  - In the early days it was all dust; there were trees and the samadhi was breaking down a little. Prabhupada would come here with his few disciples and hold discussions on the Nectar of Devotion. In that lecture he declared: "This territory here, this is the most sacred place in the whole universe."

## The Deities and the Principle of Reflection

- The Radha-Damodara deities on the altar are **Pratibhu** — non-different reflections of the original deities.
  - When the Muslims attacked Vrindavan on several occasions, most of the principal deities of Vrindavan were taken to Jaipur for protection.
- Layers of reflection/expansion:
  - The Vrindavan we are living in for the month of Kartik is itself a **replica of the original Vrindavan in the spiritual world, Goloka Vrindavan**.
  - **Jaipur** is likewise a replica or expansion of this Vrindavan, because most of the original deities of the Goswamis have gone there and are very nicely worshipped by the residents of Jaipur.
  - Srila Prabhupada expanded this concept even further by taking Vrindavan to all the major cities of the world — establishing deities there and propagating Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement in those cities.

## Why This Place Is Most Sacred to Us

- Srila Jiva Goswami's Samadhi Mandir, holding his divine form, is just on the other side of this building.
- Jiva Goswami speaks of the principle of **perfect, more perfect, and most perfect**. This place is most sacred to us in particular because it is where our Divine Master, **Srila Prabhupada**, resided for so many years, planning his Sankirtan and taking Lord Chaitanya's movement to Western countries.
- Just as it is the eternal residence of several illustrious Goswamis, we also consider it the eternal residence of Srila Prabhupada.
  - When Srila Prabhupada finally renounced family life to fully take up the instruction of his spiritual master, **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura** — to preach Krishna consciousness in the English-speaking language — he came to Vrindavan, stayed in a few different places, but eventually lived most of the time here.
  - The Goswamis living here now are taking very nice care of Srila Prabhupada's quarters. The devotees are asked to seek their blessings.

## Srila Prabhupada's Simple Plan and Monumental Task

- Here Prabhupada worked on his plans for spreading Krishna consciousness worldwide. **The plan was quite simple**, following the clear indication of his Guru Maharaja, Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura:
  - "If you ever get money, print books and spread these books all over the world."
  - Just as Lord Chaitanya sent the six Goswamis here to write books, establish deities, and rediscover the lost places of Krishna's pastimes — that formula worked very well in India, and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati considered it an **absolute formula** that would work all over the world as well.
- What was Prabhupada doing in these rooms? He faced a huge, monumental task:
  - He had to translate the books from **Sanskrit and Bengali** into English.
  - He had to write **purports that Western people could understand**.
  - It could not be a casual translation, because Prabhupada understood — as he once said — that these books would become **"the gospel of Krishna consciousness for the next 10,000 years."**
- He concentrated on the **Srimad-Bhagavatam**, which he chose as the **Grantharaj, the king of all scriptures**. The **First Canto** of the Bhagavatam was translated and commented on here in these sacred rooms at the Radha-Damodara temple.

## Prabhupada's Practical Daily Life Here

- Like the other residents and pilgrims of Vrindavan, Prabhupada followed a daily routine:
  - He would visit the **seven principal temples** of Vrindavan.
  - He would chant on his **japa mala**.
  - He would prepare some prasad for himself and offer it to the deities in this little kitchen here.
- Most likely, except for a few fortunate souls, no one recognized what was going on — how this pure devotee was preparing a movement that would revolutionize the entire world.
- He lived very simply and ascetically:
  - **Begging his food.**
  - **Begging even the money** he needed to take the bus to Delhi.
  - **Begging money from businessmen** around Delhi to get the paper to print and bind those books.
  - "It was a one-man show in the ultimate sense of the word" — "a theater of one actor at those times."

## The Place of Inspiration to Go West

- The significance of this place is not only that Prabhupada translated the First Canto and did his bhajana here — it is here that **he received his inspiration to go to the West** to preach Krishna consciousness.
- Prabhupada once commented on this directly:
  - He would be cooking here (there are two windows). While cooking, he would look out and see the **Samadhi Mandir of Rupa Goswami**. By gazing at that samadhi, he would receive so much inspiration from Rupa Goswami for going to the West to preach.
- This is a place of great inspiration not only for Srila Prabhupada, but for his followers who want to continue the same mission:
  - In your free time, come here, offer your **dandavats**, and pray to Rupa Goswami for the inspiration to continue the Sankirtan movement that Srila Prabhupada brought to the West. **That is your duty.**

## A Missionary Movement — The Difference from Other Sadhus

- In these lectures it is often repeated: **this is a missionary movement.** With all due respect, this was the difference between Srila Prabhupada and so many other respectable sadhus living in Vrindavan.
  - Many sannyasis come and take **kshetra-sannyasa** — they come and never leave, giving a vow never to depart Vrindavan. This is glorious and worthy of respect.
  - Walking in from Krishna-Balaram Mandir, one sees the sadhus in saffron who have no plan of going anywhere else — because it is said: **if you are born in Vrindavan, live in Vrindavan, and die in Vrindavan, that is your qualification for going back home, back to Godhead.**

### Respect for All Residents of Vraja

- We are taught to be respectful to all the residents of Vraja — all living entities, even the "little rascal monkeys who will steal your glasses just like that." They are naughty, but this is their last birth; they are going back home, back to Godhead.
  - Personal detail: over the years, Maharaja has lost **exactly seven pairs of glasses** to these monkeys — the last time just ten days ago ("This is my last pair, please"). Warning to be careful with glasses, especially in Vrindavan village, where the monkeys are all trained to steal glasses.
- Prabhupada explained (in a lecture later printed in **Back to Godhead** magazine): when a sadhu in the dhama commits some sinful activity, the reaction is not that he loses the opportunity to go back to Godhead — rather, he takes birth as a donkey, cow, or insect, and by living that way he is freed from the reaction, and then goes back home.
- The highest goal of devotional service is not merely to be delivered from material existence, but to be delivered **to the lotus feet of Krishna for eternal seva** (devotional service). Knowing this, sadhus come here and do not leave.

### Vrindavan's Extreme Climate

- "You are here now in the best part of the year: not too hot, not too cold." By Radharani's mercy, **Kartik** comes at this pleasant time.
- During the hot season, Vrindavan is one of the hottest places in India; during the cold season, one of the coldest — very extreme, "strong weather."
- Prabhupada said Krishna arranges the climate like this **so that the mayavadis don't come here and pollute this place.**
- Sadhus and Vaishnavas tolerate this because they know the spiritual value and the great reward of retiring here, and then going back home, back to Godhead.

## But Prabhupada Was Not That Type of Sadhu

- Prabhupada came to Vrindavan **to prepare his mission for going to the West** — and to get the inspiration required to preach to Western people and convince them to come and live in Vrindavan themselves. He was not a sadhu who simply wanted to live in Vrindavan and liberate himself alone.

### The Difficulty of Convincing Western People

- It is not easy to convince Western people of Krishna consciousness. They come into this world specifically to enjoy their senses, interested in nothing else, and trying to convince them of the opposite can be very frustrating work.
- Prabhupada understood this. His poems/prayers written aboard the **Jaladuta**, before even arriving in America, reveal that he understood the mentality of Westerners and materialistically inclined people.
- In a darshan, Prabhupada told his disciples his own thoughts before arriving:
  - He imagined that when he arrived in America and explained the basic rules — no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling — plus the constant chanting of "Hare Krishna," the American people would say: "Swamiji, thank you very much, but we cannot do this. Go back to India."
  - He did not know how it would turn out. "Now it's all history, and it's so easy to look back and see how it happened. But there was no guarantee."
  - **There was no guarantee** that Americans would put on saffron robes, shave their heads, put on tilak, give up sense gratification, and chant the name of "a Hindu god" 22 hours a day. "It's actually miraculous."

## Reverses in Vaishnava History and the Great Revivers

- Just as often in the history of Vaishnava culture there have been great reverses and setbacks — sometimes for hundreds of years before Krishna consciousness can prosper again.
- After the departure of Lord Chaitanya, there was a quiet time when Krishna consciousness was not spreading — or was spreading in the wrong way, with so-called Vaishnavas of immoral habits, so that people looked down on Vaishnavism.
  - There was some **revival by Shyamananda, Srinivasa Acharya, and Narottama (das Thakura)**.
  - But by the time of **Bhaktivinoda Thakura**, Vaishnavism was held in low regard — "practically dead, finished."
- **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura single-handedly revived** the sacred teachings and movement of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Without him, it appears there would have been no International Society for Krishna Consciousness. But at that point it was only in Bengal.
- His son, **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**, took it all over India.
  - Bhaktivinoda Thakura had prayed to Lord Chaitanya: "I might die soon; please send someone from your personal camp, **a ray of Vishnu**." Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati was that ray of Vishnu.
  - It took such a personality to spread Krishna consciousness even in the homeland — to convince the Indian people of the value of Gaudiya Vaishnavism.

### Preaching Even to Indians and Westerners

- When preaching to Indian people, one hears: "Oh yes, I know Krishna — He's an incarnation of Vishnu, the lover of the girls of Braj, I know all about Him." Or "I'm a devotee of Ram." Or "I'm a Shaivite." Or worse: "I'm a Mayavadi." Even here it is difficult to convince people of Krishna consciousness.
- What to speak of the West? The Western concept of God is often just an **order supplier** — you go to church, you pray, and God gives you what you want; that is religion.
  - "God becomes the great baker in the sky. 'God, please give us our daily bread.' He's always baking the bread and throwing it down to the earth."
  - This is not entirely fair, of course, because there are many sincere Christian people and Muslim brothers. But even the best of that can hardly compare to the culture we are providing and the caliber of saints and sages we introduce.

## The Support System We Have — and What Prabhupada Had

- You may say, "I preach on the street every day." That is not so bad — **but you have a temple to go back to**, full of devotee association.
  - If you don't live in the temple, you have a Krishna conscious wife, husband, or children to go back to.
  - You have books to read.
  - You have the Internet: type in "Krishna" and there is so much information. In 1965, the Internet didn't exist; you might have gotten one dictionary reference.
  - "You have such a support system."
- But when Prabhupada was meditating on coming to the West, what infrastructure or support system did he have?
  - He was coming **alone** to introduce a foreign culture into a Western society very much convinced that they were doing everything right and that people in India were doing things wrong.
  - It took great inspiration, motivation, and compassion to come with **no guarantee that even one person would listen** to what he had to say.
  - He told Hayagriva one time: **"If I told you what it was like the first year, struggling alone in the wilderness, you would not believe me."**

## "Lest We Forget" — The Sacrifice

- We must remember the sacrifice Prabhupada made in coming to the Western countries. "Lest we forget."
- He could easily have stayed here his whole life:
  - He probably would have lived longer, since the rigors of travel deteriorate one's health — perhaps to the ripe old age of 100, doing his bhajan in these rooms, chanting Hare Krishna.
  - Without a doubt, as soon as he left his body, he would have gone back to the spiritual world.
- **But where would all of us be right now?** Close your eyes and imagine where you would be if Prabhupada had decided, like most other sadhus, simply to stay in the glorious dhama of Vrindavan — where you would be without Srila Prabhupada, without Krishna consciousness.

## The Potency of a Pure Devotee: Prabhupada Changed Vrindavan Too

- By going to the West and coming back with all of you, Prabhupada also **changed Vrindavan itself**. This is the potency of a pure devotee — such is his power that he not only taught Westerners to worship Krishna, but even instructs the Vrajabasis, through the example of Western devotees, how to improve their service to Krishna.
- This is the meaning of Prabhupada: **"a master at whose feet all masters sit."**

### Example: The Standard of Deity Worship

- When Maharaja first came to Vrindavan and visited the temples, sometimes he couldn't even see the deities — it was so dark, there was no light, and the clothes were not of a high standard.
  - (This is not a comment on devotion — one can have no material facility and still serve Krishna with pure devotion. But to the naked eye, the deity worship was not "extraordinaire," as we say in French.)
- Now, because Prabhupada established the **Krishna-Balaram Mandir** and an exalted standard of deity worship, all the temples have beautiful clothes and nice offerings — due to Prabhupada's transcendental influence, gracefully accepted by the residents of Vrindavan.

### Revealing the Dham to the Vrajabasis

- Many people are inspired just by seeing Westerners come here and take shelter of the mission. The Vrajabasis have very soft hearts (a devotee has a soft heart), so they are not envious — they are very happy, because they know everyone is an eternal servant of Krishna.
  - When they see, by Prabhupada's grace, even Westerners bowing down before their worshipable deities, it gives joy to the residents of Vrindavan.
- Walking in from Krishna-Balaram Mandir, the residents greet you: **"Shri Radhe! Jai Radhe! Haribol! Jai Prabhupada!"**
- So Prabhupada, as a great **Maha-bhagavata purusha**, is revealing the Dham not only to the Western world, but by his potency also to the Vrajabasis.
- Even though there are different approaches and schools of worship in Vrindavan, the Vrajavasis are generally so happy to accept us — all by Prabhupada's mercy.

## Maharaja's Personal Reasons for Coming to Vrindavan

- Whatever one's personal realizations, Maharaja comes to Vrindavan for **two reasons**:
  1. For **inspiration from his spiritual master to go back out and preach**.
  2. To go deeper into his understanding of Krishna consciousness and, by Prabhupada's grace, his relationship with the divine couple, Radha and Krishna (since his spiritual master lives here eternally).
- Whenever he comes, he comes to this most sacred land between the bhajana-kutir and the samadhi of Rupa Goswami, bows down, and prays for inspiration to continue the mission — "because I'm only the servant of my spiritual master" — to continue in some humble way the missionary spirit of Krishna consciousness that Prabhupada inaugurated from this very place.
- Sometimes he sneaks into that little kitchen room and stands by the same window, looking at the same corner of the samadhi, and prays to Rupa Goswami — following in the footsteps (as **Rupanuga** followers): "You please inspire me in divine Krishna consciousness, and help me help you and my spiritual master continue this missionary movement all over the world."

## The Same Mission Continues — Little Miracles

- The same mission is still going on. "There's still little miracles. Lots of miracles. You are little miracles. All together, you're one big miracle."
- **Story of Zhenya, a young lady from St. Petersburg** (asked to stand up):
  - She was on her way to India, knowing nothing about the movement. In the airport in Kyiv, she happened to meet several devotees who preached to her about Krishna consciousness.
  - They invited her to come to Vrindavan and join the Parikrama, instead of going to Goa, Rishikesh, Varanasi, or Pune.
  - Without any prior knowledge, she joined — she now wears a sari and tilak and chants Hare Krishna.
- The magical, mysterious world of Sri Vrindavan Dham is forever going on, and we are tasting some of that nectar — by Prabhupada's mercy. And by that same mercy, these miracles are happening not only in Vrindavan but in faraway places like St. Petersburg and Kyiv.

## Prabhupada's Eternal Quarters and Final Instructions

- This is the most sacred place in the universe, especially because these are Prabhupada's eternal quarters. Prabhupada said: **"After my departure, you maintain my rooms here."** He said he would live here forever.
- Even though the parikramas are busy with little time to rest, the devotees are encouraged to find time — especially in coming years — to come to Prabhupada's room and his kitchen, and **pray for their mission to be successful.**

## Handing Over: Who Is Srila Rupa Goswami?

- The question is posed: Who is this Srila Rupa Goswami, in front of whose Samadhi Mandir we are sitting?
- Rupa Goswami is beautifully described in a Gaudiya Vaishnava prayer as one who **understood the mind, the inner mood, of the most mysterious incarnation, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** — under whom we have all taken shelter as Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
- The scholar **Panditji Sri Prahlada Prabhu** is invited to elaborate on that verse and help enter into the mysteries of Rupa Goswami's greatness.

## Glossary

- **samadhi mandir** — tomb-shrine where a great saint's sacred body is entombed
- **bhajan kutir** — small hut where a saint performs his solitary worship and chanting
- **parikrama** — sacred circumambulation or pilgrimage walk around a holy place
- **Kartik** — the holy autumn month especially auspicious for worshiping Radha-Krishna
- **sankirtana** — congregational chanting of the holy names; Lord Chaitanya's movement
- **pratibhu** — a representative or reflection Deity, non-different from the original
- **Goloka Vrindavan** — the topmost spiritual planet, Krishna's eternal abode
- **kshetra sannyasa** — a vow to remain in a holy place such as Vrindavan and never leave
- **Grantharaj** — the king of scriptures, a title of Srimad Bhagavatam
- **Vrajavasi** — a resident of Vraja (Vrindavan)
