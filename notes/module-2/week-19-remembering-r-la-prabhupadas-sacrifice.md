# Remembering Śrīla Prabhupada's Sacrifice

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 19 · Foundations in Bhakti · 54:19

**Themes:** srila prabhupada · sacrifice · vrindavan · sankirtana mission · book distribution · missionary spirit

## Summary

Speaking during the Kartik parikrama at the Radha-Damodara temple in Vrindavan, Indradyumna Swami reminds the devotees that the patch of land between Rupa Goswami's samadhi mandir and his bhajan kutir was called by Srila Prabhupada, during his Nectar of Devotion lectures, the most sacred place in the entire universe. He explains that it is even more sacred for Prabhupada's followers, because it was here that Prabhupada resided for years, translated the First Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam, did his solitary bhajan, and received from Rupa Goswami the inspiration to carry Lord Chaitanya's sankirtana movement to the West. Prabhupada lived as a very simple, ascetic sadhu, begging his food and even the bus fare to Delhi and the money and paper to print his books; the Swami calls it a one-man show, a theater of a single actor. The central theme is that Prabhupada, unlike the many revered sannyasis who come to Vrindavan on kshetra sannyasa and never leave, was a missionary who chose to sacrifice a peaceful life and certain liberation in the dhama to go to the West with no guarantee that even one person would listen. Setting this in the lineage of great reverses and revivals in Vaishnava history, the Swami recalls how Bhaktivinoda Thakura single-handedly revived Lord Chaitanya's movement and his son Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, a ray of Vishnu, spread it across India, giving Prabhupada the formula: print books and distribute them everywhere. He urges the devotees, 'lest we forget,' to remember Prabhupada's sacrifice, and marvels that by Prabhupada's potency he changed not only the West but Vrindavan itself, raising the standard of Deity worship and softening the hearts of the Vrajavasis, who now joyfully greet Western devotees. He closes with living proof of the ongoing miracle, a young woman from St. Petersburg who by chance met devotees at the Kiev airport and joined the parikrama, and invites all to sit in Prabhupada's rooms and kitchen to pray for their own mission to succeed, before handing over to a scholar to glorify Rupa Goswami.

## Key Points

- The land between Rupa Goswami's samadhi mandir and his bhajan kutir at Radha-Damodara was declared by Srila Prabhupada, during his Nectar of Devotion discourses, the most sacred place in the whole universe; it is most sacred for Prabhupada's followers because he lived, did bhajan, and planned his sankirtana mission there.
- The Radha-Damodara Deities are pratibhu (representative) Deities, since many original Deities were taken to Jaipur during Muslim attacks; just as this Vrindavan is a replica of Goloka Vrindavan and Jaipur an expansion of Vrindavan, Prabhupada expanded Vrindavan further by establishing Deities and Lord Chaitanya's movement in cities all over the world.
- Prabhupada undertook the monumental task of translating scripture from Sanskrit and Bengali into English with purports Westerners could grasp, focusing on Srimad Bhagavatam (the Grantharaj, king of scriptures), whose First Canto was translated in these rooms; he said these books would be the 'gospel of Krishna consciousness for the next 10,000 years.'
- Prabhupada lived as a simple, begging sadhu, a 'one-man show' / 'theater of one actor,' and while cooking would gaze out his kitchen window at Rupa Goswami's samadhi to draw inspiration to preach in the West; devotees are urged to come there, offer dandavats, and pray to Rupa Goswami as Rupanugas to continue the mission.
- Unlike sannyasis who take kshetra sannyasa and never leave Vrindavan (born, living, and dying in the dhama qualifies one to go back to Godhead), Prabhupada was a missionary who came only to prepare and inspire his journey West; illustrations include the naughty monkeys who steal glasses (the Swami has lost seven pairs) and Prabhupada's point that Krishna makes Vrindavan's weather extreme so mayavadis will not come and pollute it.
- Convincing Westerners was daunting because their idea of God is an 'order supplier' or 'great baker in the sky' giving daily bread; Prabhupada's Jaladuta prayers show he understood the materialistic mentality, and he feared Americans would refuse and tell him to go back to India, making the movement's success miraculous and unguaranteed.
- Vaishnava history has great reverses and revivals: after Lord Chaitanya the movement declined, was revived by Shyamananda, Srinivasa Acharya and Narottama, nearly died again until Bhaktivinoda Thakura single-handedly revived it, and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura (a 'ray of Vishnu' prayed for by Bhaktivinoda) spread it across India; the Swami warns 'lest we forget' Prabhupada's sacrifice, since he could have stayed, lived to a hundred doing bhajan, and gone back to Godhead.
- By his purity Prabhupada changed not only the West but Vrindavan itself: by establishing Krishna-Balaram Mandir and a high standard of Deity worship he raised the standard of the local temples, and the soft-hearted Vrajavasis now joyfully greet Western devotees ('Jai Radhe, Jai Prabhupada'), so Prabhupada, a master at whose feet all masters sit, reveals the Dham even to the Vrajavasis; the recent convert Zhenya from St. Petersburg, met by chance at Kiev airport, is offered as living proof the miracle continues.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam — called the Grantharaj, 'the king of all scriptures'; its First Canto was translated and given purports by Prabhupada in his rooms at the Radha-Damodara temple.

- Prabhupada's instruction 'after my departure, you maintain my rooms here' (paraphrased) — cited for why the Radha-Damodara quarters are especially sacred as his eternal residence.

## Glossary

- **samadhi mandir** — tomb-shrine where a great saint's sacred body is entombed
- **bhajan kutir** — small hut where a saint performs his solitary worship and chanting
- **parikrama** — sacred circumambulation or pilgrimage walk around a holy place
- **Kartik** — the holy autumn month especially auspicious for worshiping Radha-Krishna
- **sankirtana** — congregational chanting of the holy names; Lord Chaitanya's movement
- **pratibhu** — a representative or reflection Deity, non-different from the original
- **Goloka Vrindavan** — the topmost spiritual planet, Krishna's eternal abode
- **kshetra sannyasa** — a vow to remain in a holy place such as Vrindavan and never leave
- **Grantharaj** — the king of scriptures, a title of Srimad Bhagavatam
- **Vrajavasi** — a resident of Vraja (Vrindavan)
