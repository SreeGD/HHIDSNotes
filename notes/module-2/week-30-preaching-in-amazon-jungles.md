# Preaching in Amazon Jungles

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 30 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:33:06

**Themes:** preaching in the jungle · sankirtana · divine provision · holy name · Lord Chaitanya's mercy · prasadam distribution

## Summary

In 1987 Indradyumna Swami was invited to preach Krishna consciousness in Brazil, and after touring all 37 temples of the successful Brazilian yatra he became determined to carry the sankirtana movement into the remote Amazon jungle. Flying to the tiny preaching center in Manaus, where nine devotees had not seen another devotee in two and a half years, he found they had no money and no boat, the only means of jungle travel, so he prayed to Lord Chaitanya for a way to go. At a Sunday feast he met a heavyset, aristocratic woman who turned out to be the wife of the state governor; calling herself only 'a tiny servant of Krishna,' she miraculously arranged the governor's own palatial white riverboat with a seven-man crew, 2,000 US dollars for fuel, a ton of grains, and months of provisions, even drawing her pistol to make the crew agree to become vegetarian. Traveling up the Amazon past alligators, piranha, and giant piraiba fish, the devotees overcame their fear and held kirtan for isolated tribal villagers, who danced, chanted 'Haribol,' took prasadam, and thought the white-skinned devotees were gods from heaven; Maharaja explained who Krishna is by narrating the pastime of Aghasura, the giant serpent demon Krishna killed in Vrindavan. Because the simple people could not accept the four regulative principles, he preached only chanting the holy name, and after each program the villagers wept and begged them to stay, the chief kneeling and saying they wanted to change their lives and live like the devotees. Over roughly two months they visited some 60 to 70 villages and distributed about 65,000 plates of khichari and many books, surviving poisonous moths, snakes, a near-fatal bout of dysentery cured overnight by a jungle medicine man, a quicksand trap in a swamp full of alligators, and a hurricane-like storm. On the return journey even the boat's captain, who had taken prasadam for two months including Mahaprasadam of Lord Nrisimhadeva, spontaneously proclaimed 'All glories to the sankirtana movement of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu' and remained vegetarian two years later, while the governor's wife met them joyfully at the Manaus dock, insisting there had been 'no problems' because Krishna had brought them safely back. Maharaja concludes that the story reveals the unstoppable power of Lord Chaitanya's mercy, which can transform even the lowest of people, and that with determination and enthusiasm devotees can preach and distribute books to anyone.

## The Invitation to Brazil and the Pull of the Amazon (1987)

- In 1987 Maharaja was invited to Brazil to preach Krishna consciousness. At first he wondered, "What is there special? Why should I go there?"
- Looking at the map, he noticed the Amazon jungle in the middle of Brazil, and it caught his imagination.
- He telephoned the devotees and asked whether anyone had ever gone into the jungle to preach Krishna consciousness to the native Indians. They said no — but there was a tiny preaching center on the edge of the Amazon.
- Hearing this, he decided at once: "I'm going."

## Touring the Brazilian Yatra

- He flew from America to Rio de Janeiro and began visiting all the temples in Brazil — **37 temples**, roughly 500 devotees, who had together distributed over **5 million books**. A very successful yatra.
- He spent about a month traveling: every day a different temple, every night a different class.
- Throughout the whole tour, he kept thinking about the Amazon, convinced it would be a real adventure to go there and preach.

## Flying into Manaus

- Reaching the north of Brazil (the town of Fortaleza), he told the devotees, "This is enough, I want to go to the Amazon."
- He and one devotee took a small old private plane and flew into the jungle, landing at **Manaus**, where the little preaching center was.
- There were 9 devotees at the center who had not seen any other devotee in **two and a half years** — they were overjoyed to see visitors at the airport.
- Stepping off the plane, they were hit by a blast of jungle heat, "like being in a sauna," and were immediately covered with mosquitoes. The temple was very, very poor.

## Deciding to Go Into the Jungle

- Maharaja asked whether they ever preached in the jungle. The devotees said no — the natives "shoot with arrows."
- He insisted: "Krishna will protect us, let's go," and eventually convinced them.
- Practical obstacles: they had no money, and the only way to travel in the Amazon is by boat — there are no roads. Without money there could be no boat.
- Still, he reasoned, "If we really desire it, Krishna is going to provide," and prayed: **"O Lord Chaitanya, let us go preach in this place where they have never heard about Krishna."**

## The Sunday Feast and the Aristocratic Lady

- That Sunday they held a Sunday feast program with about 50 guests, a big kirtan, everyone dancing.
- Maharaja noticed one older, heavy-set woman, dressed like an aristocrat, who was chanting and dancing very nicely and who especially relished the prasadam.
- He asked a devotee who she was. The reply: "That is the wife of the governor of the state."
- Maharaja was astonished no one had told him. He sent the devotee to bring her: "Tell her I want to speak to her."

## Meeting the Governor's Wife

- Greeting her — "You are a very important lady, the wife of the governor" — she humbly replied, **"No, I am only a little, tiny servant of Krishna."**
- He asked, "Would you like to do some service for Krishna?" She said yes — and complained that the local devotees had never once asked her to do any service ("Nonsense devotees!").
- He proposed the plan: "We want to go up the Amazon to preach to the Indians." She approved warmly — "Mucho bueno" — but warned it was very dangerous:
  - There are **alligators**.
  - There are **piranha** — if you fall in the river they can strip your whole body to a skeleton in three minutes.
- The devotees, unenthusiastic, suggested just doing kirtan in Manaus. Maharaja insisted: "No, we are going, Mataji is going to help us."
- She said the only way to go is by boat, and promised to call back in a few days.

## The Governor's Boat

- Four days later she phoned: "Come down to the river, I have a surprise for you."
- Running to the river, the devotees saw many Amazon riverboats, and among them one beautiful big white boat.
- Maharaja joked to a devotee, "Wouldn't it be nice if it was that boat?" As they searched for Mataji and passed the white boat, she suddenly stuck her head out of a window: "Oh, Swamiji, how are you going? That's the boat."
- It turned out to be the **personal boat of the governor of Brazil**, and it was like a palace:
  - Crystal chandeliers, bathrooms with marble.
  - A kitchen full of modern appliances — ovens, burners, juicers, even a soap-dispensing device.
  - A big room for dancing.
  - Everything set up "as for the king."
- She said, "Swamiji, this boat is yours for two months — whatever you want to do."

## The Crew, the Money, and the Provisions

- Out came **seven crew members**: one captain, one engine mechanic, three cooks, and two cleaners — all his servants for two months.
- He asked the captain to start the engine just to hear it run.
- Concerned, he asked, "Who is going to pay for the gas?" It is very expensive to drive such a big boat up the Amazon. She replied, "Krishna will provide," and reached into her pocket and handed him **$2,000 U.S.** in cash.
- Not wanting to sound like a neophyte, he asked about food to eat and distribute. She lifted the bay under the boat to reveal **one ton of grains** — wheat, corn, and many cereals — for distribution to the Indians.
- For the devotees themselves, she opened big refrigerators full of butter, honey, sugar, and milk — "enough for us to eat for six months." Then: "Now, Swamiji, go preach." She drove off in her Jeep.

## "You Must Be Vegetarian" — The Pistol

- Maharaja told the seven crewmen there was one condition: they must be vegetarian. They refused — "What, no fish? No possibility."
- He phoned the governor's wife: "Mataji, one problem — the crew won't be vegetarian." She said, "Those rascals! I'll be there in two minutes."
- A Jeep came racing up; she ran up the gangplank and pulled out her pistol. Going to the captain: "There's some problem — you don't want to be vegetarian?" Suddenly: "No, no, no problem." Down the line, every man: "No problem."
- Maharaja reflects that Brazil was "still like the wild west, like the cowboys."
- He told her, "I think maybe you are the incarnation of Lakshmi, the goddess of fortune." She insisted, **"No, I am simply a tiny servant of Krishna."** To this day he still wonders if she was some demigoddess — "but she was too fat to be a demigod."

## Setting Up and Departing

- It took about three days to load everything under the boat. They brought the deities of **Lord Nrisimhadeva** and **Gaura-Nitai** and made a nice temple room for Them.
- After three days they left the dock and started up the Amazon, rounding the bend into the jungle. Maharaja thought, "This is the real thing."
- **Not Disneyland:** he contrasts it with the Disneyland jungle boat ride, where for $2 you pass plastic-and-metal alligators. Here, looking over the side, there were real alligators looking up at him.

## The Captain's Warning (Ista-goshthi)

- The captain called a meeting to warn them:
  - "If you fall off the boat, there is nothing I can do to save you."
  - The **piranha** will finish you "in 3 minutes and 12 seconds."
  - Worse, there is the **piraiba**, a huge fish "as long as this room" with an enormous mouth. These big fish knock over the natives' canoes and swallow the people one by one. Two of them together can even capsize the boat. ("That's no exaggeration, that's true.")
- The captain also gave them injections against **yellow fever** and **dengue fever** (spread by mosquitoes) — some five or six shots in all.

## The First Village

- After about four days going up the river, they saw smoke in the distance and reached the first village.
- These villages are extremely isolated and rarely see outsiders. Christian missionaries had preached there for a few centuries, so the natives had seen a few white people and spoke **Portuguese**, but visitors were very rare.
- As the boat approached, the whole village — about a hundred Indians — came onto the shore to stare at them, and the devotees stared back.

## The Reluctant Devotees and the First Kirtan

- Maharaja called out, "Okay Prabhus, let's go — Sankirtana!" But the devotees suddenly grew nervous, making excuses: "I don't feel very good," "I didn't finish my rounds today," "I didn't clean my room yet."
- Nine of them piled into a small motorized boat carrying a big pot of **khichari**, books, mridangas, and kartals, and set off toward the shore.
- Fearful the Indians might attack, the devotees objected. Maharaja answered:
  - "That's not our philosophy, Prabhu. Krishna will protect us."
  - "But Krishna's in the temple." — **"Prabhu, Krishna is in His holy name."**
  - "And stop eating that khichari."
- They began a very sincere kirtan: "I never heard the devotees chant so nicely."

## The Indians Begin to Chant

- As they drew closer, a wonderful thing happened: the Indians started hearing the kirtan and began dancing and swaying — the old people, the middle-aged, and the young — everyone moving to the kirtan.
- Seeing this, the devotees couldn't believe the effect of the kirtan and became completely fearless, chanting louder and louder.
- The Indians raised their hands in the air and began calling out, **"Haribol! Haribol!"**
- The boat began to rock; Maharaja cried, "Prabhus, Prabhus — piraiba, piranha, hold on!"
- Finally they landed, and all the Indians came forward and pulled the boat up onto the land.

## "They Thought We Were Gods"

- On shore Maharaja led a very simple Hare Krishna kirtan. The crowd swelled to **300–400 Indians** chanting ten times louder than the devotees.
- The Indians thought the white-skinned devotees were **gods come down from the heavens**. Having black skin themselves, some suspected the devotees had painted themselves white and came up to rub the skin to find the "black underneath."
- Remembering the governor's wife's words, Maharaja told them: **"No, no, we are not gods. We are little tiny servants of Krishna."**

## Explaining "Who Is Krishna?" — The Story of Aghasura

- The Indians asked in Portuguese, "Who is Krishna?" How to explain Krishna to simple jungle people?
- Maharaja said: **"Krishna is the biggest of the biggest, the most powerful of all persons, the father of everyone."** They already had some conception of God.
- To make it clearer, he told the story of the demon **Aghasura** (the demon in the form of a great snake) — apt, since the natives know huge snakes:
  - A demon took the form of an enormous snake that no one could kill, and came to Vrindavan.
  - When he opened his mouth, his upper lip touched the sky (the clouds/sun) and his lower lip rested on the ground.
  - The Indians were amazed: "Really? A snake that big? We have never seen such a snake."
  - Maharaja concluded, "And Krishna was the one who killed that snake." — **"Ah, Krishna God, Krishna God!"** Now they understood who Krishna is.
- One Indian said, "But the missionaries never told us about God like that." Maharaja replied, "Yes — that is why we have come, to tell you about God: what He looks like, what He does, who His friends are, where He lives."

## Two Days in the Village — Preaching the Simplest Message

- They stayed two days, taught the people how to make **prasadam**, and Maharaja gave very simple daily classes, because the people were very simple.
- It was even difficult to preach basic philosophy. Reflecting on why he could not press the four regulative principles:
  - **No meat** — impossible; all they ate was fish, and they did not know how to grow anything from the ground.
  - **No illicit sex** — impossible; their children "die like flies" (of ten children, seven or eight die before age one), so their whole race would be finished.
  - **No intoxication** — meaningless; they had no liquor or drugs and did not even know what intoxication was.
  - **No gambling** — meaningless; no dice, no poker; they did not even know these things.
- So he simply preached: **"Chant Hare Krishna. Chant Hare Krishna. And only chant Hare Krishna."**

## The Chief's Plea

- After three or four days, as they prepared to leave, the village chief came with the senior elders. Somewhat embarrassingly, the chief knelt down (as Christians do) and, weeping, begged through a translator:
  - "Swami, before you came, we were not happy. Now you have come, and now we are happy. Please, you cannot go."
  - "We want to be like you. We want to live like you are living. We want to change our life."
- Maharaja reflected: **"Just see the mercy of Lord Chaitanya."** Only kirtan, prasadam, and the association of devotees had transformed these wild Indians — this is the power of Krishna consciousness.
- Since many villages lay ahead, they had to leave. As they boarded and pushed off, the whole village stood like statues, unable to move, weeping and throwing flowers (a sign of great respect in their tradition). Even two kilometers up the river, the villagers were still standing on the shore watching.

## Reflection: The Power of Lord Chaitanya

- Watching them, Maharaja marveled at the power of Lord Chaitanya — that these simple people felt so much attraction for Krishna in just a few days.
- **"Who can stop this movement?"** If we go out and give our association to anyone, even the lowest of people, Lord Chaitanya gives them mercy and they change.
- This should give great faith to preach. Like the **six Goswamis**, who never made distinctions about who was qualified or unqualified for Krishna's mercy — they preached to everyone.

## Village After Village — The Drums

- Day after day they visited different villages; every one was the same story. The only difference was that now the villages knew they were coming, because the natives sent messages by **drums** (barabans).
- The captain explained that the drums pass messages to the next village. Maharaja asked what message. The reply: **"White men coming in a big white boat, giving very good food."**
- At each new village, the little canoes would come out to meet the big boat, the natives already crying, "Prasadam! Prasadam! We need more!" They already knew the word prasadam, and the devotees prepared prasadam 24 hours a day.
- Like this, one month passed.

## The Dangers of the Jungle

**Poisonous moths at night**
- One kind of Amazon moth is deadly — its bite is more poisonous than a cobra's, and a person bitten dies within about two minutes.
- At night, when the lights were turned on, these moths would come everywhere. One had to move very carefully, holding a flashlight in front while going to the bathroom, shaving, or showering, watching that no moth landed and bit.

**Snakes and the Indians' trick**
- Deep-jungle villages required walking through the jungle, where snakes hang from the trees.
- The Indians taught a trick: when a line of people walks a path, the first person wakes the snake but is not bitten; the second passes while the snake is still waking; but the **third person gets bitten**. So they would deliberately leave the "third position" empty (a gap in the line) so that no person occupied that spot — and the one walking behind the gap could spot and avoid the snake.

## Dysentery and the Jungle Medicine Man

- One very hot day, Maharaja foolishly drank water directly from the river and contracted a severe dysentery. For **14 days** he could not eat; all the fluids drained from his body, only blood came, and he looked like a skeleton.
- Local Indians said he had perhaps three days before he would die. The devotees wanted to rush him by small boat back to Manaus and a doctor — but that trip would take ten days, and he had only three.
- One local said, "Take him to the witch doctor [medicine man]." Maharaja at first refused ("No way"), then became delirious and could no longer decide. Out of desperation the devotees took him to the village.
- He woke inside a little grass hut where the medicine man was mixing terrible-smelling substances. "Open your mouth. Close your mouth." — After one dose, Maharaja was completely cured.
- Later he told a doctor about the dysentery; the doctor said such a dysentery takes at least **three months** to cure and refused to believe he had been cured in a day, dismissing it as "an old folk tale." But it was true.
- Lesson: there are many things in the jungle that modern medical science does not yet know — like Ayurvedic medicine.
- Within three days Maharaja was on his feet, eating and walking, completely cured, "stronger than ever" though thin.
- The medicine man **would not take any money**: "If I take money, I lose my power." So they gave him prasadam and held a kirtan in his house.

## Deeper Into the Jungle — The Swamp and the Quicksand

- Into the second month, the Indians grew wilder and the villages harder to reach. They heard of a village deep inside a **swamp** — an area of trees, stumps, and alligators, not a clear river.
- By day they crossed the swamp in the small boat, reached the village, and did a program. But leaving in the dark, they could not find the right path and **got lost** in the middle of the swamp — very hot, pitch black, millions of mosquitoes, everyone exhausted after a day of preaching.
- Danger: alligators can smell fear on people and are drawn to it. Everyone grew seriously afraid.
- The boat suddenly got stuck. The captain said it must be sand and that someone had to get out and push. The captain refused ("I have been vegetarian for two months — that's enough"). Everyone then looked at Maharaja: "You are the sannyasi, the fearless, pure devotee of the Lord."
- Taking the one small flashlight, he saw only black water. He stepped out and put his leg in — **quicksand.** Like in the movies, the more he moved the deeper he sank; it sucked in his leg, his body, then his hand.
- At first the devotees thought he was just having kirtan. Realizing the danger, he led "the fastest kirtan I ever led," his heart pounding.
- Six devotees grabbed him and pulled; the force that pulled him out also broke the boat free from the sand at the same moment.
- Looking back with the flashlight, he saw many little yellow eyes. He asked the captain what they were: **"Cucurillo"** — in English, **alligators.** The alligators wait by the quicksand because animals (birds, etc.) come to drink water, get stuck, and cannot escape — so the alligators eat them.
- The captain made a joke Maharaja did not find funny: **"Oh, Swamiji, big prasadam for alligator."** Maharaja: "Thank you, captain — just get us out of the swamp." An hour later they reached the main boat.

## The Storm

- One night a big storm arose. Amazon storms are almost like hurricanes; wrecked boats can be seen along the riverbanks, and no one comes to save you — you are a thousand miles from civilization, so if your boat capsizes you are finished.
- The storm began about 7:00 at night, so strong that going out on the deck would blow you into the river. They had to stay in their rooms while the boat pitched and everything inside — cups, pans, chairs, tables — crashed to the floor.
- All night the devotees, scattered in different compartments, could be heard screaming **"Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna."** They came through it safely. It took a whole day to put everything back in place.

## Turning Back

- After a month and a half to two months, they had to turn around. In all they visited about **60–70 villages**, distributed **65,000 plates of khichari**, and gave out many books (people paid with money, or with shells or other things).
- By now most of the devotees were sick — you cannot live in the jungle that long without falling ill. They were bearded, brown, and thin — "we looked like Indians." Their medicine was finished.
- The captain had to be back by a certain day or lose his job. Coming up slowly with many stops had taken time; the return would take about 14–18 days.
- **Horse analogy:** like a horse that plods away from the stable but runs eagerly on the way back home because it knows its food is there, the boatmen were happy to head home.

## The Last Village — The Captain's Transformation

- Two days back down the river they reached the last village they had visited. They planned to pass all the villages and go straight home, but suddenly heard the drums — "They know we're coming."
- The captain said, "We're not going to stop — I don't want to lose my job; I have to be back in 14 days." Maharaja agreed.
- Rounding the bend, the drums grew louder, and there stood the whole village on the shore **having kirtan** — they knew the entire mantra, beat their sticks and drums, "Hare Krishna! Hare Krishna!", jumping and twirling, knowing all the moves.
- Maharaja urged the captain, "Look, look at them!" The captain refused — then looked, and burst out in Portuguese: **"All glories to the Sankirtan movement of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu!"** He was only repeating what he had heard so many times — but he actually meant it.
- The captain had taken prasadam for two months, including the **Maha-prasadam** of Lord Nrisimhadeva from every offering, and he completely changed. Two years later he was still a vegetarian.
- On the way back they stopped at almost every village again, ending up 15 days late.

## Return to Manaus

- Approaching Manaus, Maharaja saw one person standing on the dock — it was the **Mataji**, the governor's wife, jumping up and down.
- She greeted him: "No problems, right?" He said, "Mataji, there were alligators, I was very sick, there were piranha." She replied, **"No, no, Swamiji, no problems. Krishna has brought you back. You did lots of preaching."** She invited them to her wonderful house for a fantastic feast.
- The captain worried he had lost his job for being late. She reassured him: not only would he keep his job, he would get a promotion and a bigger job.
- The next day Maharaja left Manaus for Rio de Janeiro. She came to the temple, then drove off in her Jeep. Watching her go, Maharaja thought again: **"It must be a demigoddess — it couldn't be an ordinary human being."**

## Conclusion: Determination in Preaching

- After hearing a Sankirtan story like this, we should see our preaching increase.
- **"If we can convert Amazon Indians, we can easily convert Gorbachev."**
- All that is required is to be very determined. With a big desire and enthusiasm, that is enough to distribute lots and lots of books.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10, Chapter 12 (the pastime of Aghasura, the giant serpent demon killed by Krishna in Vrindavan) - narrated to explain Krishna's supremacy to the villagers; recounted as a story, not quoted verbatim.

## Glossary

- **sankirtana** — Congregational chanting and preaching, including distribution of books and prasadam.
- **kirtan** — Musical chanting of the holy names of Krishna.
- **prasadam** — Sanctified vegetarian food first offered to Krishna.
- **maha-prasadam** — Especially sacred food remnants from the Lord's own offering, as from the deity Nrisimhadeva.
- **khichari** — A cooked rice-and-dal dish distributed to the villagers (transcribed 'kittri').
- **Nrisimhadeva** — Krishna's half-lion incarnation; the deity worshiped aboard the boat.
- **Aghasura** — The serpent demon killed by Krishna in Vrindavan, used to explain Krishna's greatness.
- **yatra** — A temple community or organized preaching mission, as the Brazilian yatra.
- **Haribol** — 'Chant the name of God!' — a call the jungle villagers eagerly took up.
