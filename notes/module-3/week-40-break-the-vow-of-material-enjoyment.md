# Break the vow of material enjoyment

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 40 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:00:55

## Summary

Speaking on Bhagavad-gita 9.34 (Chapter Nine, 'The Most Confidential Knowledge') in San Jose, California, Indradyumna Swami explains that Krishna's instruction to always think of Him, become His devotee, offer obeisances, and worship Him is the essence of all spiritual practice, echoing the Padma Purana's directive to always remember Krishna and never forget Him. He argues that such constant remembrance cannot be achieved mechanically but only through love, and that we fail because we remain 'unchaste' toward God, keeping our affection for Maya (the material world) as a paramour on the side, illustrated by the disgraced New York governor who publicly fought vice while secretly visiting prostitutes. To awaken love one must purify the heart by learning what pleases and displeases the Lord, which means approaching a bona fide, tattva-darshi spiritual master (Bhagavad-gita 4.34) and following the 'do's and don'ts,' since Krishna is pleased by devotion and the preserved tradition (His flute, yellow dhoti, Radharani, prasadam such as halava and puri) and displeased by irreligion, cow slaughter, and intoxication. He contrasts the fearful, hateful remembrance of the demon Kamsa with the pure, favorable (anukulya) devotion defined by Rupa Goswami, and recounts Srila Prabhupada lovingly wiping a blemish from Radharani's marble cheek while installing the Deities in Paris to teach that 'attention to detail is a sign of love.' The goal is pure love of God in the mood of separation (Siksastakam, weeping while chanting) and the ultimate reward is going home to Goloka Vrindavan rather than Vaikuntha, with the real test, quoting Bhaktivinoda Thakura, coming at the moment of death when one can spontaneously call Krishna's name. He urges everyone to become philosophers armed with knowledge, practice sadhana-bhakti honestly without falsely imitating advancement (sahajiya), and mature into unmotivated, uninterrupted devotees who please the spiritual master, Lord Chaitanya, and Radha-Krishna. He closes with an extended Sankirtana story about ISKCON's 'Krishna's Village of Peace' at Poland's giant Woodstock festival and the gradual spiritual awakening of its organizer, Jurek Owsiak, who now carries a Shalagram Shila everywhere he goes, showing how patient, favorable devotional service can attract even the most materially powerful people.

## Key Points

- Always remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him (Padma Purana) is the essence of all instruction; it can only be sustained by love, not mechanical effort. Measure your progress by how much of the day you remember versus forget Krishna (the 'stopwatch' test), and recall the two-sided Australia t-shirt: 'always remember Krishna' / 'and never forget Krishna.'
- We are 'unchaste' to God because we keep affection for the material world (Maya) as a paramour; the fallen New York governor who preached morality while visiting prostitutes is really us, pointing one finger while three fingers point back at ourselves.
- To love God, learn what pleases and displeases Him and follow the do's and don'ts (rules = don'ts, regulations = do's); Prabhupada's 'do-not shop' joke about devotees smoking and dunking donuts on the Bowery after class illustrates the purificatory discipline.
- Approach a bona fide, tattva-darshi (one who has seen the truth) spiritual master (BG 4.34); Prabhupada is the shiksha-guru for the whole of Kali-yuga, and whether one is initiated by him or by a follower, everyone has equal access, like an electric line that gives full potency whether you plug in at the beginning, middle, or end.
- Remembrance must be favorable (anukulyena, per Rupa Goswami): the demon Kamsa remembered Krishna constantly but in fear and hatred, so it was not bhakti; a real devotee serves with loving attention to detail, as when Prabhupada stepped off the Vyasasana to wipe a blemish from Radharani's marble cheek in Paris ('detail is a sign of love').
- The goal is pure love of God and going home to Goloka Vrindavan (not Vaikuntha); success is weeping while chanting in separation (Siksastakam), and the final test of one's bhajan is calling out Krishna's name spontaneously at the moment of death (Bhaktivinoda Thakura). The greatest gift to the spiritual master is to advance and join him in the Lord's eternal nitya-lila.
- Extended Sankirtana story: ISKCON's football-field-sized 'Krishna's Village of Peace' at Poland's Woodstock festival (110,000 plates of prasadam in 72 hours, Ratha-yatra three times a day, book distribution) and the deepening faith of organizer Jurek Owsiak, who was taken to Muktinath (108 spouts on the Kali Gandaki), Radha Kunda, and Vrindavan, now carries a Shalagram Shila everywhere ('he, not it, is always protecting me'), and dreams of retiring to Vrindavan in a dhoti, demonstrate how patient, favorable service attracts even powerful materialists.

## Scriptural References

- Bhagavad-gita 9.34 - 'man-mana bhava mad-bhakto mad-yaji mam namaskuru, mam evaisyasi yuktvaivam atmanam mat-parayanah' (engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee, offer obeisances, and worship Me); the lecture's main verse from the chapter 'The Most Confidential Knowledge.'
- Bhagavad-gita 18.65 - noted as an almost-verbatim repetition of the first lines of 9.34 ('engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee...').
- Padma Purana - 'smartavyah satatam vishnur vismartavyo na jatucit' (always remember Vishnu/Krishna and never forget Him); cited as the parallel instruction (mis-transcribed in the audio as 'Pratma Purana').
- Kurma Purana, as quoted by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati in his Anubhashya on Chaitanya-charitamrita Adi-lila 5.41-48 - Krishna is non-different from His body and mind (drawn from Prabhupada's purport to BG 9.34).
- Bhagavad-gita 4.34 - 'tad viddhi pranipatena pariprashnena sevaya, upadekshyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darshinah' (approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively and render service; the self-realized who have seen the truth impart knowledge).
- Nectar of Instruction (Upadeshamrita) verse 5, Rupa Goswami - accept an uttama-adhikari who has 'seen' Krishna as guru, or, if such a soul is not present, the Lord's bona fide representative who has full faith in guru, shastra, and sadhu and is fixed and exemplary.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Madhya 19.151 - 'brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva' (wandering through the universe since time immemorial, a fortunate soul receives the seed of bhakti); paraphrased as 'since time immemorial we've been here.'
- Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 1.1.11, Rupa Goswami (also quoted at CC Madhya 19.167) - 'anyabhilashita-shunyam jnana-karmady-anavritam, anukulyena krishnanushilanam bhaktir uttama' (first-class devotion is free of material desire, the tendency to merge via jnana, and fruitive work, and is performed favorably, anukulyena).
- Siksastakam verse 7 (Chaitanya Mahaprabhu) - 'yugayitam nimeshena chakshusha pravrishayitam, shunyayitam jagat sarvam govinda-virahena me' (in separation from Govinda a moment feels like twelve years and tears flow like torrents of rain).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6 - 'sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokshaje, ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati'; the source of his call to become 'unmotivated, uninterrupted' devotees.
- Srimad Bhagavatam / Mahabharata, Canto 10 (Rajasuya sacrifice; Shishupala pastime, approx. SB 10.74) - Krishna tolerates one hundred offenses and then beheads the blaspheming Shishupala with His chakra; used to show Krishna's dislike of demons.
- Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 10 - the pastime of Kamsa and the aerial oracle foretelling that Devaki's eighth child would kill him; cited as remembrance of Krishna in fear and hatred rather than favorably.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Madhya-lila (Saksi Gopala pastime, chapter 5) - 'you are not a statue... you are directly the son of Nanda Maharaja'; the Deity who could walk and talk, invoked for the principle of Deity worship.
- Ten Offenses to the Holy Name - maintaining material attachment even after hearing so much about the matter (the speaker numbers it the 8th offense; in the standard list this corresponds to the tenth namaparadha).
- Bible - Jesus: one cannot love/serve God and mammon at once (Matthew 6:24); 'the meek shall inherit the earth' (Matthew 5:5); 'my cup runneth over' (Psalm 23:5).
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura - the real test of one's bhajan comes at the moment of death, whether one can spontaneously call out Krishna's name (paraphrased; specific work not identified in the audio).
- Srila Prabhupada (paraphrased sayings, source works not specified) - 'religion means following the laws of God'; 'lust means for me and love means for Krishna'; 'faith means to put your trust in something sublime'; 'material enjoyment and Krishna consciousness don't go well together'; and his teaching that World War II was collective (global) karmic reaction for mass cow slaughter.
