# Appreciating Devotees (The Importance of Sadhu Sanga)

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 39 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:21:17

## Summary

Speaking at a temple 'Festival of Appreciation' meant to honor every devotee present, Indradyumna Swami takes up a verse from the Seventh Canto, Ninth Chapter of Srimad Bhagavatam (Prahlada Maharaja's prayers to Lord Nrisimhadeva), in which Prahlada rejects all material opulence, whether bhukti (sense enjoyment), mukti (liberation), or siddhi (mystic perfection), and prays only to be placed in touch with a pure devotee whom he can serve. From this he develops the theme of appreciating devotees: Krishna decorates His surrendered devotees with all divine qualities, tolerance, compassion, friendliness to all, and freedom from enmity, and places them in prominent positions so that ordinary people know whom to follow. The heart of the lecture is the supreme importance of sadhu-sanga, association with devotees: because the living entity is the infinitesimal tatastha-sakti of the Lord, it takes on the qualities of whatever it associates with, like a chameleon changing color, so one must carefully avoid materialistic association (asat-sanga), including the subtle 'one-eyed guru' of television, mundane talk, and popular music, and instead seek out devotees, giving association rather than taking it while out in the world. He stresses the reciprocal principle that even a moment's association with a pure devotee surpasses heavenly elevation or liberation, that one 'catches' bhakti the way one catches a cold, and that transcendental hearing acts even when one cannot understand it, recalling how he once dozed during Prabhupada's Hindi lecture and was told the sound would purify him regardless. He then presents the five most potent limbs of devotional service, associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing Srimad Bhagavatam, residing in Mathura, and worshiping the deity with faith, arguing that sadhu-sanga is the 'salt' that brings out the flavor and result of all the others. He closes by urging the congregation to take shelter of Srila Prabhupada through his books and recorded lectures, and of senior Vaishnavas while they are still present, to honor every devotee's service however large or small, and to keep turning their backs on maya to face Krishna.

## Key Points

- The base verse from Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9 shows Prahlada Maharaja rejecting bhukti, mukti and siddhi and praying only to serve a pure devotee; every intelligent person should follow him and aspire to be the servant of the servant of the Lord rather than an enjoyer.
- Krishna adorns His devotees with all good qualities and puts them in the limelight and in responsible positions so the masses know whom to follow (compared to decorated World War II veterans wearing their medals); the crest jewel of Vaishnava qualities is compassion, the desire to elevate even a fifth-class man to first-class.
- The soul is the tiny marginal energy (tatastha-sakti) of the Lord, so it absorbs the qualities of its association, illustrated by the chameleon that turns the color of whatever it sits on; in the material world a devotee should give association, not take it, and beware subtle asat-sanga through television (the 'one-eyed guru'), mundane conversation and music.
- It takes very little to fall: Ajamila, a pure brahmana boy, fell from one moment's inattention on seeing an illicit couple, and even a strong athlete is felled by a tiny virus (swine flu / H1N1); the tortoise-and-hare race warns against the complacency of thinking oneself too advanced to fear maya.
- The same principle works in reverse for good association: even a moment's sadhu-sanga exceeds heaven and liberation, one catches bhakti like catching a cold from a devotee speaking Bhagavatam, and transcendental hearing acts even without understanding (the Swami's story of dozing during Prabhupada's Hindi lecture and being told the sound would still act).
- The five most essential 'express' limbs of bhakti are sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-sravana, mathura-vasa and worship of the deity with faith; sadhu-sanga is the 'salt' that draws out the flavor and result of the other four, since chanting must be received in parampara and done humbly, the Bhagavatam must be heard from a living person-bhagavata (not as a paid seven-day business), holy places benefit chiefly through the sadhus living there, and deity worship is really assisting one's guru.
- Take shelter of Srila Prabhupada, who brought the deities and wrote his purports through the night so his books would 'become gospel,' and of senior Vaishnavas while they are still present; honor every devotee's service, big or small (the small screw is as vital to the harmonium as the keys), keep turning your back on maya ('just say no') to face Krishna, and if you fall down nine times get up ten.
