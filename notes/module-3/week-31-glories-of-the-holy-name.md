# Glories of the Holy Name

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 31 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:20:07

**Themes:** holy name · yuga-dharma · chanting offenses · harinam initiation · faith and taste · universal religion

## Summary

Delivered at ISKCON Atlanta (New Panihati Dham) during a Harinam-diksha (initiation into chanting the holy names), this lecture establishes chanting the holy name of God as the prescribed yuga-dharma for the age of Kali. Drawing on a commentary by Srila Jiva Goswami, Indradyumna Swami explains that although each age has its own primary process, meditation in Satya-yuga, fire sacrifice (agni-hotra/yajna) in Treta, and Deity worship (archa-vigraha) in Dvapara, the Hare Krishna maha-mantra was also secretly practiced by a few fortunate souls in every age. He argues that the purpose of human life, self-realization and the reawakening of dormant love of God, remains constant across all ages because the Vedic teaching is absolute truth valid in past, present and future, and that the holy name is non-different from God Himself, so that chanting purifies the impure chanter through direct association with the supremely pure Lord. He shows that emphasis on God's name is universal to every bona fide tradition, quoting the Psalms, the Lord's Prayer, the Buddhist teaching to call upon the Lord's name, and the Islamic remembrance of Allah. The heart of the talk is a systematic treatment of the ten offenses against the holy name (nama-aparadha) that the initiates must avoid, stressing that while offending God can be absolved by chanting His name, offending the holy name itself forfeits one's deliverance and is one's 'last chance.' He teaches that our only real deficiency is a lack of faith and taste, illustrated by the jaundiced patient to whom sweet jaggery tastes bitter and by the analogy that conception requires both a potent male and a fertile female, and that the goal of all chanting is ecstatic love of God culminating in tears of love. The ceremony concludes with the four regulative vows, the promise to chant sixteen rounds daily, the giving of initiated names, and encouragement to distribute Srila Prabhupada's books.

## Opening Appreciations and the Two Paths

- Maharaja opens by honoring the host devotee, who has given many years of dedicated service to Srila Prabhupada and the Atlanta temple — "a very wonderful Vaishnava," always blissful and one of the best dressers in the movement. ("We should dress like ladies and gentlemen.")
- He thanks the devotees managing the center, and offers respects to his godsister with "the million-dollar smile." He laments that, though a devotee for some thirty-odd years, he only recently gained her association.
- This host devotee is always **upbeat and positive**, equipoised in happiness or stress, ups or downs, and always positive about the outcome — because our future in Krishna consciousness is very bright.
- Sometimes, due to past conditioning, a devotee may fall prey to disappointment, bewilderment, or depression, but we should always remember: **through thick and thin, we are on the right path — the path back home, back to Godhead.**
- **The two paths in this world:**
  - **Pravritti-marga** — the path of material sense gratification.
  - **Nivritti-marga** — the path of gradual renunciation of this world, culminating in love of God.
- A materialist dedicated only to enjoying this world, who does not acknowledge God or his eternal loving relationship with God, may sometimes *seem* to be going up, but is actually going down.

## The Deities and the Atlanta Temple

- Maharaja glorifies the beautiful altar: the deities who came by the request of Srila Prabhupada, the beautiful Jagannatha, and **Radha-Anandini** — "Anandini" is a name for Radha, meaning that she is never separated from Krishna.
- These are very happy deities — "the type of deities that when you see them, you can't stop looking at them." All deities are absolute, but Prabhupada had his favorites, and these are exclusively beautiful.
- He notes the temple's sprawling property and its famous **Panihati festival**, recalling the pastime in Chaitanya-charitamrita, and jokes about Jayapataka Swami, who always associates Panihati with Atlanta.

## This Is Harinama Diksha

- The occasion is **Harinama Diksha** — initiation into the chanting of the holy names. *Diksha* means initiation into Harinam.
- A careful study of the shastra shows that **the chanting of the holy names is the prescribed method (yuga-dharma) for awakening our dormant love of God in this age.**

## The Holy Name Is Present in Every Age (Jiva Goswami)

- Maharaja cites a commentary by **Srila Jiva Goswami**: the chanting of the holy names of God — particularly the **Hare Krishna maha-mantra** — was present in *every* age, not only Kali Yuga. (*Dharma* = the recommended duty/process; *yuga* = the age.)
  - **Satya Yuga** (the golden age): the yuga-dharma was **meditation** — but side by side, for a few fortunate souls, there was the simple process of chanting Hare Krishna.
  - **Treta Yuga** (the silver age): the process was **ritualistic fire sacrifice** — the *agni-hotra* (Hindi *havan*), where the Lord kindly appears as the sacred fire and accepts our oblations, meaning our gifts, service, and love. All sacrifice, penance, and austerity is ultimately meant for the pleasure of God, since we are all eternally His servants. But again, for a few fortunate souls in the forest, the confidential secret was there — just chanting Hare Krishna.
  - **Dvapara Yuga** (the age when Sri Krishna Himself, *Svayam Bhagavan*, visited this planet): the process was **temple worship** — going to the temple and beholding the merciful *archa-vigraha*, the Deity form of the Lord.
    - The Lord, controller of matter and spirit, can turn spirit into matter and matter into spirit.
    - **Bible example** — the *Old Testament*: the Lord appeared to Moses as a **burning bush** and spoke to him through it. A seemingly mundane material object was utilized by the Lord to instruct Moses. So the Lord can appear in matter.
    - Vaishnavas do not concoct a form of the Lord; we refer to shastra and to the *sadhu* (the spiritual master and previous saints) to learn exactly how the Lord appears in the spiritual world, carve the form accordingly, and request the Lord to appear. Out of kindness He appears so we can develop a very personal relationship with Him.
    - **The word "religion"** comes from the Latin verb *religio*, meaning "to connect [again]." The goal of life and religion is to reconnect with God in an intimate, sweet, loving relationship. Even today in India one can see hundreds of thousands of temples where devotees worship the Deity. But side by side, for a few fortunate souls in the forest, there was the open secret — Hare Krishna.
  - **Kali Yuga** (the present age): the yuga-dharma *is* the chanting of Hare Krishna, now given openly for all.

## The Four Ages Decline From Best to Worst

- Just as the year passes through four seasons (Maharaja notes the beautiful dogwood trees of Atlanta and the arrival of spring), the creation passes through four ages.
- Contrary to scientists who *speculate* (i.e., guess) that things go from worse to better, according to Vedic science the ages go **from best to worst** — because scientists do not hear from proper authorities (from *sadhu*).
- We are in the worst age: **"the burnt remnants of the past ages," the leftovers** — we did not do the meditation, the Vedic rituals, or the temple worship. Yet the purpose of life remains the same.

## The Purpose of Life Is Unchanged — Athato Brahma-jijnasa

- Although shastra says people born in Kali Yuga effectively have no qualification for spiritual life — too addicted to sin, too absorbed in the bodily concept — the purpose of life is still the same.
- **Vedanta Sutra:** *"athato brahma-jijnasa"* — "Now [that you have attained the human form], is the time to inquire into the Absolute." This is the ABC of spiritual life, applicable in past, present, and future.
  - The *Veda* is a vast body of knowledge (the Vedas, Puranas, Itihasas, Upanishads, Mahabharata, Ramayana). But because life is short, the essential message is given in **code form** — the Vedanta Sutra. (Analogy: **Morse code**, which Maharaja had to learn in the Marines.)
  - **"Elegance is truth spoken concisely"** — get to the point. Now that you have come to the human form, self-realization is the time-of-the-essence business, because time has covered our dormant love of God through many centuries and millions of births.

## University Story: "What Is the Value of a 5,000-Year-Old Book?"

- Maharaja recalls lecturing at a university in **Manipal, near Udupi, Karnataka**, to very intelligent IT students (only the most intelligent were allowed to attend).
- Speaking from the **Bhagavad-gita**, a student raised his hand: *"Sir, what is the value of this book, which was spoken 5,000 years ago?"* — pointing to the university, science, technology, and advanced medicine.
- Maharaja's answer: **it depends on what you consider the goal of life.**
  - If the goal of life is material enjoyment, then Bhagavad-gita has no value for you.
  - But if you are actually intelligent and understand that the goal of life is something more — **liberation from the repetition of birth and death (*samsara*)**, reconnecting with God, entering the most sublime, blissful relationship of loving service to God — then that book is as relevant now as it was then, and will be as relevant 5,000, 50,000, or 15 million years from now.
- **Absolute vs. relative truth:** relative truth is bound by time, place, and circumstance; absolute truth is valid in all phases of time — past, present, and future.

## Finding a Balance — and the Danger of Bad Association

- Even born in Kali Yuga, the purpose of life is still liberation culminating in love of God. This does not mean stopping all other activity — we must find a **balance**, with the stress placed on developing love of God.
- We may be highly qualified materially but highly *unqualified* spiritually. The nature of this age is so full of bad association that it is almost worse when one tries to become good.
- **The internet** ("I would rather call it *internaz*") is very dangerous. Parents can use "firewalls" or "cyber sitter" to limit what children can access.
  - Children raised at home learn the morals and values of our spiritual tradition, but when they go out to schools, the *peer* association can be very bad. Although teachers may be good, what would once have been shocking is now normal.
  - When Maharaja was young (he is "almost 60"), things like abortion, intoxication, and gambling were not the norm. He grew up in "Pleasantville, USA" (Kentfield) — "do good, be good, see no evil, hear no evil, do no evil." Now these things have become the norm.
  - **Bhagavatam predicts** that in Kali Yuga children would be sent for "higher education," but it calls those places **slaughterhouses** — spiritually dangerous. So even if you want to be good, so much bad association tends to drag you into these activities. Practically there is no hope — yet the purpose of life is the same.

## The Lord's Concession — Only God Can Enunciate Religion

- It appears contradictory: we seem condemned in this age, yet the purpose of life is the same as for those in past ages. Therefore a **concession** is made — by the Lord.
- **Only God can enunciate a principle or tradition of religion.** When ordinary charismatic people start their own sect and gather followers, they eventually deviate from the original teaching and it becomes chaotic.
- **Shastra:** *"dharmam tu sakshad bhagavat-pranitam"* — real religious principles are enacted directly by God. This is why history shows so many bona fide spiritual traditions — Christianity, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Islam — all given for the people.
- In this age the Lord has given a **process for the fallen people**: you do not have to perform Vedic rituals, hundreds of years of meditation, or study the vast body of Vedic knowledge. The concession is: **Hare Nama — just chant the name of God.**

## "Just Three Words?" — Do Not Underestimate the Name

- At the same university, another person objected: *"You have to be kidding me — three words, Hare, Krishna, and Rama?"*
- Maharaja corrected him and had him repeat the full mantra: **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.** (The student slipped and said "Hare Yama"; Maharaja gently corrected.)
- **"We do not underestimate the potency of His names."**
- **Verse — *"nama chintamani krishnas chaitanya-rasa-vigraha..."* (Padma Purana):** the holy name is a transcendental touchstone, the very form of Krishna full of all rasa. There is **no difference between the name, the fame, the form, the pastimes, and the paraphernalia of God** — they are all absolute.
  - In other words, **God is present in the sound of His name.** God is the supreme pure. If we, in our impure state, associate with the Lord through His name, we become purified — what could be more pure than God Himself?
  - Even the artifacts used in the service of God are sacred (the Deity, the cross); how much more pure is the Lord Himself.

## The Same Principle in Every Bona Fide Tradition

- This special opportunity is not only in our tradition:
  - **The Psalms of David:** "From the rising of the sun to the setting of the same, the Lord's name is to be praised — with drums and cymbals." "Hallowed be Thy name; Thy kingdom come; Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven." (Why is God's name *hallowed*/sacred? Because the Lord is present in the sound of His name.)
  - **The Lotus Sutra of Buddha:** "Anyone who calls upon my name shall be delivered."
  - **Islam:** great stress is placed on the name of God — *Allah*; *"la ilaha illa-llah"* ("there is no God but God, and Muhammad is His prophet"); the chanting of *"Allahu Akbar"* ("God is great"). Maharaja notes he spends most of his time in Muslim countries — Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Pakistan, Afghanistan (and a few he cannot name).
- **In every bona fide tradition that comes from God, the same central principle is found**, because chanting the name is *the* dharma, the duty, the opportunity afforded to the living entities of this age.

## Why We Choose "Krishna" — Primary and Secondary Names

- You can chant any name of God; the process is not sectarian.
- We prefer to chant **Hare Krishna** because we have information that on the topmost planet in the spiritual world, the Lord is present in His original form — **a cowherd boy with a walking stick and a little butter in His hand** — the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- **Primary vs. secondary names of God:**
  - *Secondary* names describe His functions or opulence: God, the Father, **Bhagavan** ("owner of all opulence").
  - *Primary/intimate* names: **Krishna** means "the most beautiful person." God is the source of all beauty, so He is the most beautiful person. Therefore we prefer to chant Hare Krishna.

## The One Good Quality of Kali Yuga (Shukadeva to Pariksit)

- These names are the source of our deliverance in Kali Yuga.
- **Verse — *"kaler dosha-nidhe rajann asti hy eko mahan gunah / kirtanad eva krishnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet"* (SB 12.3.51):** Shukadeva Goswami tells Maharaja Pariksit — "O King, this age of Kali is an ocean of faults/inauspiciousness, but there is one good quality: simply by *kirtan*, chanting the name of Krishna, one becomes liberated and attains perfection."

## Transcendental Sound, Not Ordinary Singing

- Some say, "I like to sing — I have rock and roll, pop, hip hop, heavy metal, black metal." Maharaja (mostly among Muslims who don't know these) lists the genres and jokes about "pumpkin," skinheads, etc.
- **No** — that is *not* transcendental sound. The kirtan is different: **"golokera prema-dhana, hari-nama-sankirtana"** (Narottama Das Thakur) — this transcendental sound comes from the spiritual sky, from the kingdom of God (*Goloka*), to deliver us back home to the spiritual world. *Kirtanad eva krishnasya* — you must sing the name of God.

## The Glory and the "One Problem" (Sikshashtaka)

- **Verse — Sikshashtaka 2 (*"namnam akari bahudha nija-sarva-shaktis..."*):** "O my Lord, Your holy name alone can bestow all benedictions upon the living entities. Thus You have hundreds and millions of names, like Krishna and Govinda. In these names You have invested all Your transcendental energies, and there are no strict rules for chanting them. You have made approach to You so easy through the medium of Your holy name."
- **The one problem** (same verse): *"durdaivam idrisham ihajani nanuragah"* — **"but I have very little attraction."** Because of our conditioned state, we are not attracted to things spiritual. We have a fleeting curiosity, or call out to God in distress, but no burning desire.
  - We have more faith in matter than in spirit, more faith in ourselves than in God, more faith in material enjoyment. We cannot even conceive that there can be happiness in spiritual life — we imagine it is "all rituals and prayers."
  - But **Krishna says in Bhagavad-gita that religion should be joyfully performed** (*"susukham kartum"*) — singing, dancing, feasting.

## The Diseased Person Analogy (Jaundice and Sugar)

- We have very little attraction, like a diseased person with hepatitis/jaundice.
- The cure for jaundice is **sugar** (natural sugar like *gur*/sugarcane) to cleanse the liver — but to the diseased person, the sweet *gur* tastes **bitter.**
- Maharaja gives himself as an example: as a young man traveling in India he got very bad hepatitis (an occupational hazard). His friend giving him the *gur* was tasting it and saying "it's so sweet," but when Maharaja tried it, he spit it out — it was bitter, because he was diseased.
- Likewise, though we have this golden opportunity to chant Hare Krishna, we do the bare minimum, or worse, struggle to complete our rounds. **This is our great misfortune.**

## Both Ingredients Needed — Faith in the Holy Name

- **Analogy:** to conceive a child, two things are required — a potent male and a fertile female. If either the male is impotent or the female infertile, conception will not take place.
- We have the bona fide process, but it is up to us to **develop faith in the holy name**, so that when we chant we get the ultimate benefit.

## The Holy Name Is More Merciful Than the Lord

- **Padma Purana:** if a sinful person surrenders to God, he will be protected and freed from all sinful reaction.
- **The tolerance of the Lord — Bhrigu Muni story:** the sages wanted to test who among Brahma, Shiva, and Vishnu is the most tolerant (tolerance being a symptom of a saintly person). Bhrigu Muni had to "offend" each. He offended Brahma and Shiva, who grew a little angry; but when he **kicked Vishnu in the chest**, Vishnu — who so loves His devotee — said, **"My dear devotee, have you hurt your foot?"** This demonstrates how tolerant the Lord is.
- If you offend the Lord (considered quite severe), you can be protected by taking shelter of the holy name. **The holy name is all-merciful — you could say more merciful than the Lord Himself** (though they are identical); it is His mercy incarnation. By chanting Hare Krishna one can be relieved of the sin of having offended God.
- **But Padma Purana points out:** if you offend the *holy name*, there is no chance of being delivered — that is your last chance.
  - **"Father Knows Best" analogy:** like Maharaja's own father on the TV program, who would say, "Son, this is your last chance." The holy name is the most easy, sublime means of deliverance in Kali Yuga; if you offend it, there is no further recourse.

## Why We Must Study the Offenses

- Therefore, in a discussion of the glories of the holy name, we must also point out the **offenses to the holy name**, which the initiates and all of us must carefully avoid.
- **Prabhupada:** "Deathlessness begins at the moment of initiation." You can achieve immortality by going back to Godhead — you do not take birth again (Krishna says so in Gita).
- But if we offend the holy name, our progress is either slowed down (we "get stuck in the mud"), or in extreme cases stops entirely, or we go back to "square one." So this is serious subject matter.

## The Goal: Ecstatic Symptoms of the Holy Name

- **The end result of chanting Hare Krishna — Sikshashtaka 7 (*"yugayitam nimeshena..."*):** "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I am feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence." — the ecstasy of crying tears of love, the hair standing on end.
- Maharaja notes he was supposed to give two lectures but will give only one, because he has chanted only two of his sixteen rounds and must go home to chant. (The most important instruction of the spiritual master is to chant 16 rounds a day.)
- **The test (paraphrase of the symptom):** upon chanting the name of God, if your voice does not choke up, if tears do not well in your eyes, if your hair does not stand on end, and you do not fall to the ground semi-consciously — it means you are committing one or a combination of these offenses. We chant, but it is like "burning the candle at both ends": doing the wrong thing alongside, so we don't get the full benefit.

## The Ten Offenses to the Holy Name

### Offense 1 — Blaspheming Devotees Who Propagate the Holy Name

- The first offense is to blaspheme devotees who have dedicated their lives to propagating the holy name of the Lord.
- **Story:** once a devotee, lecturing, said this offense means we should not offend the *spiritual master* — because in the disciple's eyes the spiritual master is the very best devotee. Prabhupada, who was sitting there, corrected: **no, it means *any* devotee who is chanting the holy name — actually anyone who is chanting the names of God, in any religion.** We should be respectful and never consider ourselves better than anyone; at most we may consider ourselves *more fortunate*, but never better.
  - **Job-application analogy:** when you apply for a job, you can say anything, but they look at your *history*. What is our history? Nothing wonderful. We are simply fortunate to have contacted the pure devotee and come under his guidance.
- **Look into your own heart first:** if you point a finger at someone, three fingers point back at you. We must be free of a fault ourselves before we can instruct someone else in it.
  - **Dentist story:** a mother, seeing her child's teeth decaying from too many sweets, took him to the dentist and asked him to tell the boy to stop. The dentist said, "Come back in one week." Puzzled, she returned the next Monday; the dentist then told the boy to stop eating so many sweets. Asked why he could not have said it a week earlier, he admitted: "I could not say it with all truthfulness, because I myself was addicted to eating too many sweets. So I stopped, and now I can tell him."
  - **Jesus:** "Let him who is without sin cast the first stone" — when they wanted to stone the prostitute.
  - **Gaura Kishora Das Babaji story:** when this divine personality left this world, his body remained. In his humility he had once said his body was worthless — "drag it through the town like a donkey when I die." One neophyte follower proposed to do just that. But his disciple **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** said no: the spiritual master's body is considered *divine*, because it was fully engaged in the Lord's service — body, mind, and words — so it is not an ordinary material form.
    - Bhaktisiddhanta challenged the so-called renunciates: "Any of you who have not had illicit sex in the last *year*, come and touch this body and take it away." No one could. "Any of you who have not had illicit association with a woman in six months." No one. "Anyone who has not had illicit association with a prostitute last night." They all put their heads down in shame and left the road.
- **This is the *hati-mata* (mad-elephant) offense — the worst offense.** Like an elephant entering a garden and trampling all the nice plants, it destroys the creeper of devotion.
- Better to err on the side of caution: always say something nice about the Vaishnavas. If something is genuinely wrong, go **humbly to the proper authority** — "I think Prabhu needs some direction like this" — but never engage in *prajalpa* (village gossip: "did you hear? did you know she did this?"). There is a time and place; rather than *shravanam kirtanam vishnu-smaranam*, we indulge those other thoughts.

### Offense 3 — Disobeying the Instructions of the Spiritual Master

- What is our connection to God? We cannot approach God directly, just as you cannot approach the president directly — you must go through the proper channel (your senator, governor, etc.). **The Lord's representative is the spiritual master.**
- Every religion has a representative — Lord Jesus Christ, Muhammad, Buddha. In our tradition it is the spiritual master, the **ambassador**: one individual who represents the Lord.
- This is a very weighty position — *"guru"* means "heavy." It is not meant for becoming wealthy, comfortable, or famous; the guru's position is as a **transparent via-medium** to the Lord, and he must keep himself very pure.
- Once you accept his instructions as the instructions of the Lord, to disobey them is to **cut the umbilical cord** — as a pregnant mother nourishes the child through the umbilical cord, cutting it cuts the very life of the baby.
- Related points:
  - Never disrespect the articles used by the spiritual master; always say his name with awe and reverence.
  - **Offer obeisances** — not just once a day, but several times during the day (though not necessarily every time he enters the room), to train ourselves in the servant position so that we can hear properly. If you properly respect someone, you hear attentively; if you do not respect him, you may not take his message seriously — and our tendency is to forget.
  - **Take his remnants:** honoring the guru's *prasadam* remnants is easy and valuable. Gurus grow "nice and shodha" (they become plump — actually an austerity, since it is not appropriate for a saintly person to be plump), so they leave remnants for the disciples. **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** used to mix his sweets and salties all together with lots of salt for the remnants, to teach the *absolute nature of prasada* — that there is no good or bad, tasty or untasty prasada. Take the mercy that comes of its own accord.

### Offense 2 — Considering the Names of Demigods Equal to the Name of the Lord

- To consider the names of demigods like Shiva or Brahma to be equal to the name of the Lord.
- The posts of the demigods (except Shiva) are taken up by the most pious living entities at the beginning of creation — *shiva-atmas*, very pious persons on their way back to God, who receive the sole responsibility of administering the creation.
- We do not accept the scientific view that everything runs by mechanical law. Not a blade of grass moves without the desire of God — yet God does not personally labor day and night; **"Let there be light, and there was light."** He engages some of His devotees to take care of the administration. They are very powerful, but their power comes from God.
- Therefore the name of God is different from the name of any great personality within the creation. **His name is absolute.** Your parents could name you anything — it is just a label — but God's name is absolute.

### Offense 4 — Blaspheming the Vedic Literature

- To blaspheme the Vedic literature or literature in pursuance of the Vedic version.
- What would we do without these scriptures? How much can we know and remember when our spiritual master comes and goes?
  - **Memory test:** Maharaja asks whether anyone could stand at the end of the lecture and give ten points he made. He asks a disciple, Rasika, to try — it is not easy. He praises another disciple, **Rangavali**, for listening very attentively (a good quality; a candidate for initiation was accepted because "he listens very carefully" — and "one who listens carefully will one day speak nicely"). Still, remembering ten points is hard.
- Therefore the Lord gave us **shastra** at the beginning of creation, in various forms — like a **map (a spiritual "MapQuest") out of the material world.** We can refer to it at any time for proper direction on how to get out of the material world into the spiritual world.
- Shastra is so valuable it is **worshipable, non-different from the Lord Himself** — we place it on the altar. We should never disrespect it: never put it on the ground, fold the pages, or underline it. And we should not disrespect the scriptures of any other bona fide religion either, since all are given according to time, place, and circumstance.

### Offense 5 — Considering the Glories of Chanting to Be Imagination/Exaggeration

- To consider the glories of chanting Hare Krishna as imagination.
- **The hierarchy of names:** it is said that **1,000 names of Vishnu equal one name of Rama, and three names of Rama equal one name of Krishna.**
  - **"Shri Vishnu Shri Vishnu" story:** in the old days the devotees were very zealous; when going out on Harinam they would turn on the water fountain, chant "Shri Vishnu, Shri Vishnu, Shri Vishnu," and then drink the water.
  - **Carrefour store story:** in France, young men hungry from hard days of distributing books would go into the store, take out their japa beads, and offer the whole store at once (except the butcher's section). So when they later took cream cheese, butter, eggs, ice cream, etc., it was already offered, and they would tell devotees, "I'm going to the Carrefour — the whole store is offered."
- **What does one name of Krishna do?** According to **Jiva Goswami**, one name of God chanted sincerely destroys more sinful reactions than you have accumulated in millions of births.
- To think "they are just saying that to get me to chant" is this offense. Our tiny brain cannot comprehend the opulence, beauty, and mercy of God; we have to accept these things on the basis of shastra. Do not be a **"doubting Thomas"** — Thomas never got the full mercy. Rather, have faith (yes, Lord Rama has four heads; yes, the Purusha of the *Purusha-sukta* has millions of arms). The scientists are mere "speculators."

### Offense 6 — Giving an Interpretation of the Holy Name

- To give some interpretation of the holy name.
- Example: "Krishna" also means "black" in the Sanskrit dictionary; "black" can mean "unknown," the dark unknown universe — so someone interprets Krishna as "the unknown, mysterious force behind everything."
- **No, sir** — Krishna means the most beautiful personality, the Personality of Godhead.

### Offense 7 — Committing Sinful Activities on the Strength of the Holy Name

- **What is sin?** Sin means disobedience of the law of God. And where are the laws of God? In the scripture — you have to read.
  - **Analogy:** if a cop pulls you over for doing 85, "I didn't know" is no excuse — you should have read the driving manual. As human beings, we must read the divine scripture to know how to act properly.
- **Sin can be accidental or premeditated:**
  - *Accidental* — a devotee slips up, out of past bad habit or fatigue. This does not mean one should commit suicide; we are not fanatical. Prabhupada said you can break once, break twice, but three times means "not sincere." "The spirit is willing but the flesh is weak."
  - *Premeditated* — deliberately planned. Even in law there is a distinction between manslaughter (unintentional) and a fully planned act (like the years of planning behind 9/11), which is very serious.
- The offense: thinking, "This holy name is so powerful, it destroys the sinful reactions of many lifetimes — so I'll rob that bank to solve my problems, and then go home and chant Hare Krishna." This is *premeditated*, and it is the worst.

### Offense 8 — Considering Chanting to Be One of the Auspicious Ritualistic (Karma-kanda) Activities

- To consider the chanting of Hare Krishna to be one of the auspicious ritualistic activities mentioned in the Vedas (*karma-kanda*).
- Those ritualistic activities are bona fide and necessary, but the *karma-kanda* section is meant to *regulate* the sense gratification of a very uncontrolled person — to make him civilized, become **Aryan**, and acknowledge that the results come from a higher authority (ultimately God, through the demigods).
- **Say thank you:** if you take without saying thank you, you cannot expect much in return. We learn this as children — Maharaja's mother taught him to say "thanks" *from the heart.* We have received so much mercy from parents, family, teachers, society — but our *ultimate* thanks goes to God, the supreme provider. An atheist who thanks everyone but not God is a **hypocrite.**
- One way of saying thank you is Hare Krishna. But note the difference: *karma-kanda* takes you to the heavenly planets; **chanting Hare Krishna takes you back to God in the spiritual world.**

### Offense 9 — Instructing a Faithless Person About the Glories of the Holy Name

- Maharaja cites a commentary (again, he believes by **Jiva Goswami**): **faith is an essential ingredient to chanting.** It is an offense to initiate someone who does not have sufficient faith in the holy name — and this offense falls on the *guru.*
- If the guru initiates someone who lacks faith and the strength to follow the vows, it is the **guru who suffers** — he takes the disciple's reaction. This is one reason a spiritual master gets sick.
  - When the spiritual master gets sick, the disciple should not think he is merely suffering past karma; rather, an exalted personality may be **suffering the disciple's sin.** Prabhupada said that sometimes the guru's sickness is a way of instructing the disciples: "Be careful — do you want to see your guru in a sad condition?"
  - When Prabhupada was on his deathbed, he said (from one angle of vision), **"This is because I accepted too many unqualified disciples."** So the spiritual master must also be sober and not take more disciples than he can handle.
- **How does the guru absorb these reactions?** By chanting Hare Krishna, and some of it goes to Krishna, who is happy to help His spiritual children on the path like a father (with all the accompanying "headaches" — Maharaja jokes about a godbrother whose hair turned gray, not blond, from raising a teenage daughter).
- **The burden of love:** being a guru is a "risky business" — willingly drinking poison. What is karma? The sinful reactions of oneself or others. Who wants to drink poison willingly? But **Shiva drank it** and, retaining it, became **Nilakantha** (blue-throated). The guru does it only on the order of his own guru — "along with the order comes the ability to do it."
- Therefore disciples should remain pure in their vows and chanting so as not to burden the spiritual master, who is "out on the front lines, leading the charge."
  - **Soldiers analogy:** just as citizens want to honor the commitment of soldiers in Iraq (not supporting the war, but supporting the men) and ensure hospitals like Walter Reed can care for the wounded, so we support our spiritual master's mission by remaining true to our vows. **Initiation means giving our life to our spiritual master to help him take us back home.**
  - Householders can also help the mission of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu: pray for their guru, give donations, and raise beautiful children to "lead the charge" in the future.
- So one definition of this offense is to initiate those without sufficient faith. Maharaja assures the candidates that he would not initiate them unless he felt they had the strength to follow the vows and some taste for chanting Hare Krishna.

### Offense 10 — Not Having Complete Faith, and Maintaining Material Attachments

- To not have complete faith in the chanting of the holy name, and to maintain material attachments even after understanding so many instructions.
- We should be **making progress.** When we enter the church, mosque, temple, or synagogue and engage in our spiritual activities, we should come out a little better — not go merely for social life (dominoes, bingo, card clubs) or "because we need some ish." That is all right initially, but we should practice so that ("scrub-a-dub-dub") the heart gradually becomes free of **lust** — which is the converted reflection of love for God — as well as greed, anger, and envy, all replaced by love for God, for the spiritual master, and for all living beings.
- The spiritual master wants to see the disciples advance — just as a mother (Maharaja references the devotee Edie) wants to see her little daughter grow into a mature, well-educated woman chanting Hare Krishna.
- **What the spiritual master really wants:** not material comfort or remuneration, but to see the disciples become ecstatic lovers of God — to one day **cry a single sincere tear of love of God** while chanting Hare Krishna. "If you want to give your guru something, give him one tear of love of God. That will be enough." Keep your billions, your ranch, your company, your fame.
- The road back to God is long and strewn with obstacles, but with the helpful hand of the spiritual master we can pass through it safely — never alone, always in the association of the Vaishnavas and the spiritual master. So learn to take shelter of the spiritual master by always following his instructions and giving your life to serving his mission — which is Prabhupada's mission and Lord Chaitanya's mission.

## Glossary

- **Harinam** — The holy name of Hari (God); the chanting of God's names, whether privately or in the streets.
- **Diksha** — Spiritual initiation; here, formal initiation into chanting the holy names.
- **Yuga-dharma** — The recommended spiritual process for a given cosmic age; in Kali-yuga it is chanting the holy name.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded Age of Quarrel and hypocrisy, last of the four ages.
- **Nama-aparadha** — Offense against the holy name that blocks one from its full mercy.
- **Maha-mantra** — The 'great chant': Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.
- **Archa-vigraha** — The worshipable Deity form in which the Lord mercifully appears within matter.
- **Hati-mata** — The 'mad elephant' offense of blaspheming devotees, which tramples the garden of one's devotion.
- **Prasada** — Sanctified food, or the remnants, of the Lord or the spiritual master.
- **Vaishnava** — A devotee of Vishnu / Krishna.
