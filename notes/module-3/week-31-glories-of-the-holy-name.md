# Glories of the Holy Name

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 31 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:20:07

## Summary

Delivered at ISKCON Atlanta (New Panihati Dham) during a Harinam-diksha (initiation into chanting the holy names), this lecture establishes chanting the holy name of God as the prescribed yuga-dharma for the age of Kali. Drawing on a commentary by Srila Jiva Goswami, Indradyumna Swami explains that although each age has its own primary process, meditation in Satya-yuga, fire sacrifice (agni-hotra/yajna) in Treta, and Deity worship (archa-vigraha) in Dvapara, the Hare Krishna maha-mantra was also secretly practiced by a few fortunate souls in every age. He argues that the purpose of human life, self-realization and the reawakening of dormant love of God, remains constant across all ages because the Vedic teaching is absolute truth valid in past, present and future, and that the holy name is non-different from God Himself, so that chanting purifies the impure chanter through direct association with the supremely pure Lord. He shows that emphasis on God's name is universal to every bona fide tradition, quoting the Psalms, the Lord's Prayer, the Buddhist teaching to call upon the Lord's name, and the Islamic remembrance of Allah. The heart of the talk is a systematic treatment of the ten offenses against the holy name (nama-aparadha) that the initiates must avoid, stressing that while offending God can be absolved by chanting His name, offending the holy name itself forfeits one's deliverance and is one's 'last chance.' He teaches that our only real deficiency is a lack of faith and taste, illustrated by the jaundiced patient to whom sweet jaggery tastes bitter and by the analogy that conception requires both a potent male and a fertile female, and that the goal of all chanting is ecstatic love of God culminating in tears of love. The ceremony concludes with the four regulative vows, the promise to chant sixteen rounds daily, the giving of initiated names, and encouragement to distribute Srila Prabhupada's books.

## Key Points

- Chanting the holy name (harinam-sankirtan) is the yuga-dharma for Kali-yuga; per Jiva Goswami the maha-mantra was present in every age alongside each age's own primary process: meditation (Satya), fire sacrifice (Treta), and Deity worship (Dvapara). The four ages descend from best to worst, and we in Kali are the 'burnt remnants' of past ages, yet the goal of life is unchanged.
- The holy name is identical to God (nama-nami-abheda): God is fully present in the sound of His name, so associating with the supremely pure Lord through chanting purifies the conditioned soul; even articles used in His service (Deity, cross, scripture) are thereby sacred.
- Emphasis on God's name appears in every bona fide tradition because it is the dharma for this age, evidenced by the Psalms, the Lord's Prayer ('hallowed be thy name'), the Buddhist promise that whoever calls on the Lord's name is delivered, and the Islamic remembrance of Allah; the name 'Krishna' (the all-attractive, most beautiful person) is preferred as an intimate primary name over secondary names like God or Bhagavan.
- The core teaching is the ten offenses to the holy name (nama-aparadha) which initiates must avoid: (1) blaspheming devotees who spread the name, the 'mad elephant' offense (hati-mata) that tramples the garden of devotion; (2) considering demigods like Shiva or Brahma equal to God; (3) disobeying/disrespecting the spiritual master; (4) blaspheming Vedic and other bona fide scripture; (5) considering the name's glories to be exaggeration or imagination; (6) giving one's own interpretation of the name; (7) committing sin on the strength of the holy name; (8) equating chanting with ordinary Vedic ritual (karma-kanda); (9) instructing/initiating the faithless; (10) retaining material attachment and lacking faith even after hearing instruction.
- Offending God can be absolved by chanting His name, illustrated by Bhrigu Muni kicking Vishnu's chest to test tolerance, upon which the Lord solicitously asks if Bhrigu has hurt his foot; but offending the holy name itself is unforgivable, as it is the only and most sublime means of deliverance, making the name even 'more merciful than the Lord' since it is His mercy incarnation.
- Our sole real problem is a lack of taste and faith, illustrated by the jaundice/hepatitis patient to whom sweet jaggery (gur) tastes bitter (Swami's own story of getting hepatitis in India), and by the analogy that conception needs both a potent male and a fertile female, the process is potent but we must supply faith. The spiritual master accepts the disciple's karmic reactions (the 'burden of love,' drinking poison like Shiva becoming Nilakantha), which is why gurus may fall sick; Prabhupada on his deathbed said it was from accepting too many unqualified disciples.
- The aim of pure chanting is ecstatic love of God, tears, choked voice, and hair standing on end; the guru's only wish is that a disciple shed one sincere tear of love before death. The initiation concludes with the four regulative principles (no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling), the vow of 16 rounds, the proper order of obeisances (Prabhupada/param-guru, Deities, siksa-gurus, godbrothers), and 'spiritual mathematics': sharing Krishna consciousness and distributing Prabhupada's books multiplies one's fortune (1 minus 1 equals a million), like a spark landing on dry versus wet grass.

## Scriptural References

- Vedanta-sutra 1.1.1 — 'athato brahma-jijnasa' ('now, therefore, is the time for inquiry into Brahman / self-realization'); cited to argue the goal of human life is the same in past, present and future.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 6.3.19 — 'dharmam tu saksad bhagavat-pranitam' (real religion is enunciated directly by God); used to argue only God can establish a genuine religious tradition, hence the several bona fide traditions.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.3.51 — 'kaler dosa-nidhe rajann asti hy eko mahan gunah / kirtanad eva krsnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet'; Sukadeva Goswami tells Maharaja Pariksit that Kali is an ocean of faults but by chanting Krishna's name one becomes liberated.
- Padma Purana — the verse on the holy name's identity with God, 'nama cintamanih krsnas caitanya-rasa-vigrahah...' (quoted in Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 17.133), establishing no difference between the Lord's name, form, fame, pastimes and paraphernalia; the Padma Purana is also the source enumerating the ten offenses to the holy name and the point that a sinner who chants is freed while an offender of the name is not.
- Siksastakam verse 2 — 'namnam akari bahudha nija-sarva-saktis...' (the Lord has invested all His energies in His holy names yet 'I have no attraction for them'); basis for the teaching on lack of taste/faith.
- Siksastakam verse 7 — 'yugayitam nimesena caksusa pravrsayitam...' ('O Govinda, in Your separation a moment feels like twelve years, tears flow from my eyes like torrents, the world seems vacant'); cited as the goal and result of pure chanting.
- Siksastakam verse 6 — the ecstatic symptoms of chanting (choked voice, flowing tears, hair standing on end); used diagnostically ('if these do not appear when you chant, you are committing offenses').
- Chaitanya-charitamrta Adi 17.22 — 'kali-kale nama-rupe krsna-avatara' (in Kali-yuga Krishna descends in the form of the holy name); underlies 'the Lord appears as His name.'
- Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 20.108 — 'jivera svarupa haya nitya-krsna-dasa' (the constitutional position of the living entity is to be Krishna's eternal servant); given at the close as the essential message.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.2 — 'su-sukham kartum avyayam' (this knowledge/dharma is 'joyfully performed'); paraphrased as 'religion should be joyfully performed,' with singing, dancing and feasting.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.9 (also 8.16) — one who attains Krishna does not take birth again ('tyaktva deham punar janma naiti mam eti'); cited for 'deathlessness begins at initiation.'
- Srimad Bhagavatam Canto 10 (ch. 89) — the sages' test of Brahma, Shiva and Vishnu; Bhrigu Muni kicks Vishnu's chest and the Lord responds by asking after Bhrigu's foot, illustrating the Lord's supreme tolerance toward His devotee.
- Narottama Dasa Thakura, bhajan 'Sri Krishna Chaitanya Prabhu Nityananda' — 'golokera prema-dhana, hari-nama-sankirtana' (the treasure of divine love from Goloka is the congregational chanting of the holy name); cited that transcendental kirtan descends from the spiritual world to deliver us.
- Bible — Psalms (cf. 113:3) 'from the rising of the sun to the setting of the same, the Lord's name is to be praised,' with 'drums and cymbals' (cf. Ps. 150); the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-10) 'hallowed be thy name... on earth as it is in heaven'; 'let him who is without sin cast the first stone' (John 8:7, the woman accused of adultery); and Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3), cited to show the Lord can appear through matter, as in Deity worship.
- Buddhist teaching (attributed to Buddha's 'Lotus Sutra') — 'anyone who calls upon my name shall be delivered'; and Islamic remembrance — the kalima ('there is no God but God, and Muhammad is His prophet') and 'Allahu Akbar' ('God is great'); cited as cross-traditional parallels showing the holy name is central wherever the tradition comes from God.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — 'along with the order comes the ability to carry it out'; paired with the account of Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura protecting the divine body of Gaura Kishora Dasa Babaji by challenging pretender 'renunciates,' told to illustrate the first offense (blaspheming/dishonoring devotees) and the divine nature of the pure devotee's body.
- Srila Prabhupada — 'if you want to please me, distribute my books'; and his teaching on three classes of students (the third disobeys, the second obeys, the first anticipates and carries out the instruction unasked), cited during the initiation to encourage book distribution and attentive service.
