# The best thing is purity

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 43 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:03:45

**Themes:** purity · engaging the senses · fault-finding · humility and gratitude · surrender to guru · compassion in preaching

## Summary

Speaking on the theme of controlling versus engaging the senses (rooted in Prahlada Maharaja's warning in Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.30 that persons of uncontrolled senses glide toward hell, endlessly 'chewing the chewed'), Indradyumna Swami argues that spiritual life is not merely negating the senses but re-engaging them in Krishna's devotional service. He contrasts this with the impersonalist (Mayavadi) error of trying to kill desire: desire cannot be killed, only purified and redirected to the Lord. Through the story of Narada Muni's cobra disciple (told not to bite but still allowed to hiss and scare, like a snake with its fangs removed) and a story of Jesus curing a disciple's tapeworm by ordering twenty days of fasting and then forbidding him to drink warm saffron-honey milk, he shows that the guru's restrictions and seemingly impossible tests exist to purify the disciple, even when the instruction is not understood. He warns that sense gratification breeds an 'I, me and mine' mentality that hardens the heart and turns to fault-finding and criticizing devotees, which is the first offense to the holy name; a real Vaishnava first sees a devotee's service and only then his faults, just as a hospital is full of sick people who are nonetheless there to become well. The remedy is humility (thinking oneself lower than a blade of grass), gratitude (readiness to say 'thank you' and 'I'm sorry'), and the reciprocal exchange of gifts, which he illustrates with his own back-and-forth of gifts with a congregation member who repaired his car. He stresses full faith in and surrender to the spiritual master, never wanting to embarrass the guru who recommends the disciple to Krishna, and recalls his own first contact with Vishnujana Swami and other sannyasis and his early longing for the devotees. Finally he teaches that freedom from envy and material desire produces the compassion needed for effective preaching, as the Six Goswamis attracted even rogues because they came only to give and not to take; since the only thing that can stop the movement is the internal enemy of material desire, the conclusion is that 'books are the basis, preaching is the essence, utility is the principle, and purity is the force' - so when asked the best preaching technique, the best thing is purity.

## The Theme Verse: Sense Gratification Drags the Soul Toward Hell

- The class is built on **SB 7.5.30** — *matir na kṛṣṇe parataḥ svato vā... adānta-gobhir viśatāṁ tamisraṁ punaḥ punaś carvita-carvaṇānām* — "the mind cannot turn to Krishna": because of their **uncontrolled senses (adānta-gobhiḥ)**, persons too addicted to material life gradually glide toward the darkest, hellish conditions, "**repeatedly chewing that which has already been chewed**."
- Neither their own efforts, nor the combined efforts of others, can make such people Krishna conscious, because sense gratification itself is dragging them toward a hellish condition.
- The whole lecture develops the antidote: **restrict the senses, but then engage them in the Lord's devotional service** — and the highest expression of that is *purity*.

## Prabhupāda Tests His Disciples with "Impossible" Instructions

- The spiritual master sometimes gives an instruction that seems impossible. It is a **test** — and by struggling to fulfill it the disciple actually advances and intensifies his Krishna consciousness.
- **Buddhimanta and the gas station:** Buddhimanta was driving, nearly out of gas, far from the temple. He stopped to fill up but had no money — "We're going to pay for it." He decided, "I'll offer this man a Krishna book." The station owner filled the tank and asked for $10. Buddhimanta showed him the book — *"Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead,* written by my spiritual master; it describes the goal of life and how to attain it; you are not this body..." The man asked the price; Buddhimanta said $10; the man said, "Great, it's a deal." Buddhimanta thought, "I did it! Prabhupāda asked us to distribute books and I did it!" — then ran back with the idea: just go out, present Krishna as He is, preach, and depend on Prabhupāda and Krishna. From this, big book distribution began.
- Devotees went out to the streets and discovered they could distribute the books. Prabhupāda had thereby engaged them in more devotional service, intensifying their Krishna consciousness and their desire to please the spiritual master. He was testing them.

## The Caitanya-caritāmṛta Printing Marathon

- A parallel instance of an "impossible" instruction. Prabhupāda came to Los Angeles and said: "I am translating so many books and you are not printing. I have done the entire *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*. If you do not print it within a certain time (about two or three months), I will stop translating."
- The devotees were shocked — "Prabhupāda, I won't translate anymore?!" — and performed a miracle "as far as material life is concerned" (the story is in the *Līlāmṛta*). They stayed up all day and all night, taxing their brains to fulfill the instruction.
- One devotee was sent to India with only about **ten days** to travel from Delhi to Calcutta and down south — everywhere Lord Caitanya had gone — photographing all the holy places, and to return in time.
- Artists painted so many paintings that they were literally falling asleep on their canvases. When printing time came, publishers would take the canvas away from the artist even before it was finished. (Analogy: to an artist that "really hurts" — like a devotee cooking a feast downstairs and, before it is ready, someone takes everything out and serves it.)
- On the very day Prabhupāda had wanted, they finished the final edition and sent it to him. Prabhupāda sent back a simple telegram: **"Thank you very much. I am very satisfied with all your service."** That was all the devotees needed to go into complete ecstasy.
- By giving that seemingly impossible instruction, Prabhupāda made the devotees advance — they increased their intensity of service and made so much spiritual advancement that Prabhupāda said they would go back to Godhead as a result of their effort.

## Restrict the Senses, but Engage Them — Jesus and the Tapeworm

- A man had a **tapeworm** and was eating and eating but never getting satisfaction — a picture of material life: "can't get no satisfaction." A tapeworm lives in the intestines and takes the nutrition from everything you eat; the more you eat, the less satisfied you are. So too with sense gratification: the more you indulge, the more frustrated you become, because **desire is always there** and the taste never comes.
- IDS's aside on "worm in stool": he and Trivanga saw a doctor's-office chart of worm species (tapeworm, hookworm, whipworm, roundworm). He realized what "a worm in stool" literally means — living entities who live in that obnoxious substance, thinking it their happy home with family — showing how low a soul can fall (one of the 8,400,000 species).
- **Scientific proof of the principle:** countries with the highest material facility have the highest frustration. Sweden — highest per-capita family income of any nation — has among the highest suicide rates. Those with the most sense gratification are the most frustrated; they have everything and more is coming, but the taste is not there, so they shoot themselves.
- **The Māyāvādī mistake:** out of that same frustration, the impersonalists conclude, "Let us kill desire." But you cannot kill desire — desire is intrinsic to the living entity. The point is to **purify** the desire, not kill it.
- **The Jesus story:** Jesus told the man to fast. "Fast? I can't fast." "Fast for twenty days, then come to me." After twenty days the man came. Jesus said, "Open your mouth, but don't drink the milk" — a glass of warm milk with honey (and a little saffron), the soft thing one uses to break a long fast. The disciple must have wondered, "What is this guru doing? Torturing me? He must be wrong." No — it was a test of sincerity.
  - Lesson: sometimes the guru gives an instruction the disciple doesn't understand. Prabhupāda said one should **not try to understand the mind of his guru**; the mood is always **awe and reverence**, because the guru is delivering one from material existence. One should simply follow. ("Yours is not to reason why; yours is but to do and die" — into the valley of death rode the six hundred.) Of course the guru will never instruct anything that harms us, but the principle of full surrender must be there.
- **The result:** the disciple abstained from food and drink; the tapeworm, having nothing to eat, smelled the warm saffron milk and came out of the disciple's mouth; Jesus took two stones and killed it. The restriction had been a test — and the end result was that the disciple was **actually purified**.
- Application: **we may restrict our senses, but then we must also engage them.** Not that we simply leave the material world to come and eat and sleep in the temple. Prabhupāda said **"the temple is not a hotel for eating and sleeping — it is a base for going out and preaching Krishna consciousness."**

## Nārada Muni's Cobra Disciple: Stop Biting, but You May Still Hiss

- Nārada Muni has many disciples throughout the universe. One was a **cobra**. Cobras are envious creatures that like to bite — just like the senses, which lash out to enjoy anything, with a poisonous effect.
- The remedy is to "de-tooth" the cobra — remove its fangs — so its bite has no effect. So too, if the senses abstain from sense gratification but are engaged in the Lord's devotional service, there is no bad effect: we take out the enjoying spirit and try to please Krishna, and then engaging the senses has a *good* effect.
- Nārada accepted the cobra as a disciple on one regulative principle: **"Don't bite people anymore."** (For us: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, no illicit sex; for a snake, one principle — "Don't bite" — very tough for a snake.)
- The cobra went about its business, and the children, knowing it would not bite, threw sticks and stones at it — "pow, pow, pow." The battered cobra came back: "Nāradajī, Guru Mahārāja, you told me not to bite, but all day people throw sticks and stones at me." Nārada replied: **"I told you not to bite, but I didn't say you couldn't scare them a little."**
- IDS's aside on Indian snake-charmers (common around Navadvīpa/Māyāpur): they carry defanged poisonous snakes in baskets, drape them around their necks, let them "bite" harmlessly, and sell little "snake charms" for two or three rupees, claiming the charm neutralizes snakebite. IDS once bought one, opened it, and found only sand inside.
- The principle: we stop sense gratification but we do not stop activity — the snake shouldn't bite, but it may still hiss and scare. We stop our sense gratification and then engage in the Lord's devotional service.

## Sense Gratification Makes the Heart Hard: "I, Me, and Mine"

- Sense gratification (per SB 7.5.30, *matir na kṛṣṇe...*) takes one toward hellish conditions **and makes one hard-hearted** — i.e., "I, me, and mine." One thinks only of himself.
- In the **Bhagavad-gītā**, Prabhupāda says the problem of the material world is that everyone is thinking of himself and no one is thinking of Viṣṇu/Krishna — "whatever *I* can get; it belongs to *me*; it is *mine*." The demoniac person's speech is all "I, I, I" ("**so much I have today, so much more I will gain, I have killed this enemy**" — the demoniac declarations of BG Ch. 16).
- **Devotees' speech is the opposite** — "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna"; and when they do speak of themselves, it is in relation to glorifying Krishna. Prabhupāda said his own spiritual master judged the importance of a disciple's paper by **how many times it said "Krishna."**
- The conditioned soul always likes to put himself forward; the devotee is humble and always puts the Lord first. So this movement is the **International Society for Krishna Consciousness** — whereas so many other movements (the Yogananda movement, the Sai Baba movement, the Radha Swami movement) put the leader forward as if he were the Supreme, to be worshiped. **Prabhupāda never did that** — he always put himself back, always Krishna first, always the spiritual master first.
- A hard-hearted person becomes insensitive to others' feelings, because sense gratification is always at the expense of others.

## The Tongue: The Hardest Sense to Control

- Of all the senses, the **tongue** is the most difficult to conquer. Cited from **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's prasāda prayer** (*Śarīra Abidyā-jāl*): *"śarīra abidyā-jāl, joḍendriya tāhe kāl, jīve phele viṣaya-sāgare / tā'ra madhye jihwā ati, lobhamoy sudurmati, tā'ke jetā koṭhina saṁsāre"* — the body is a network of ignorance, the senses are its deadly agents casting the soul into the ocean of material enjoyment, and among them the tongue is the most greedy and hardest to conquer.
- Therefore we take **prasādam** to full satisfaction — engaging the tongue in the Lord's service rather than in sense enjoyment.

## The Fault-Finding Tongue and Offenses to the Holy Name

- Because a person addicted to sense gratification always wants to put himself forward, a favorite way to do so is to **put others down — criticizing.** We must be very careful, coming to Krishna consciousness, not to bring in this impurity of the uncontrolled tongue.
- The **first offense to the holy name is to blaspheme devotees** who have dedicated their lives to preaching the Lord's glories. This fault-finding tendency is warned against throughout Prabhupāda's books — and Prabhupāda said "these books are for my disciples." Though we are being trained as disciples, we still have to overcome our *anarthas*. **Prajalpa** — unnecessary talk, criticism — is a dangerous offense.
- **What fault-finding means** (discussed with Trivanga while driving): fault-finding means you **first see the fault, then the service.** A real Vaiṣṇava **first sees the service** — he appreciates the devotee for what he is, a devotee — and only then, if at all, considers the fault.

## See the Service First: The Hospital, the Demigods, and the Thief

- **Hospital analogy:** In a hospital everyone is sick — but you don't conclude the people are worthless; they are there **to be purified**, i.e., to become healthy. (IDS and Trivanga visited a congregation member in the hospital; Trivanga noted that temple life is so nice we may forget the real nature of the material world, so it is good to go out and see it — crying, ambulances, sirens, blood.)
- Similarly, everyone living in the temple is there **to make advancement in devotional service.** If someone does this or that wrong, "that may be, but that's the disease, not the heart." His real self is a soul who wants to be Krishna conscious.
- We offer respects to **all** the Vaiṣṇava devotees of the Lord — even those who are struggling — because they are devotees. (Compare the demigods: we criticize those who *worship* demigods for material things, but we never criticize the demigods themselves, because they are devotees.)
- If we always first accept a devotee's **service** — "What is this devotee doing to serve Krishna?" — we will never become envious of another devotee; there will be no animosity, only love and trust. As long as a person is in the society of devotees trying to be a devotee, that principle of love and trust holds when we respect him for his service.
- **The thief story:** A thief, very addicted to stealing, saw the Vaiṣṇavas and thought, "These are nice, saintly persons — I'd like to be like them." A little faith arose by association. Isn't that what we all want? Even with our bad habits, even if we fall down, we want to be like the devotees, so we stay. That first impression — "I want to be like them" — is what keeps us.

## How I Met the Devotees (IDS's Own Conversion Story)

- Because he was so much in māyā, Krishna arranged a big dose of devotee association. Interested in spiritual life, IDS went to a **museum exhibit on Krishna** — first time he'd heard the name; it seemed new and a little bizarre.
- Walking out down the university steps he heard "Hare Krishna" — and saw **four sannyāsīs** on the lawn (a rare sight): **Viṣṇujana Mahārāja, Śubha Mahārāja, Brahmānanda Mahārāja, and Gargamuni** (they were Mahārājas then). He sat down, transfixed by these effulgent personalities. Viṣṇujana Mahārāja said, "Sit down, I will tell you about my spiritual master." IDS was hooked.
- He invited them to stay with him for three days in the broken-down building where he lived. **First taste of prasādam:** walking to the store, Viṣṇujana said they wanted to offer some prasādam to Krishna; IDS didn't know what prasādam was and thought it was one item. Brahmānanda (a big man) kept piling rice, vegetables, fruits, and spices into the cart — "take this, take that, take more" — until IDS's whole two months' pay was gone. They took it back and cooked an unbelievable feast, chanting Hare Krishna and speaking of Krishna the whole time. Brahmānanda made two big plates for himself and finished before IDS had eaten a quarter of his — "these guys are special."
- IDS offered to wake them at 6 a.m. for his meditation (he did Maharishi-style meditation). Viṣṇujana smiled: "We get up at **2:30** — we'll wake *you* up." "What do you do?" "We chant prayers to our spiritual master." "Who is he?" **"A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda"** — and he showed IDS a picture (they still have it). They woke him at 2:30, chanted **Hare Krishna and the Śrī Gurv-aṣṭaka prayers**, and Mahārāja gave a lecture.
- Then one day they disappeared. IDS came home from work eager to see them, but they were gone, leaving only a note (from Śandana): **"In the same way that the river brings together different sticks and flowers and brush and in time separates them, the Lord brings us together and He separates us."** A twenty-year-old grown man, IDS cried like a baby.
- They left a **picture of Krishna and a picture of Prabhupāda** — and did not even tell him a temple existed anywhere (there wasn't one nearby). Every day IDS chanted Hare Krishna before the pictures, trying to remember what they had cooked. The one dish he could reproduce was **mashed fried bananas ("squash bananas")**; friends teased that all he ate was squash bananas, but that banana "tasted like nectar" because it made him remember the Mahārājas, Prabhupāda, and Krishna (he tried to offer it, though he didn't know the prayers). Finally, by Krishna's grace, he found the temple and the devotees again.

## The Devotee Is Grateful: Accepting Service on Krishna's Behalf

- The point of that story: **sense gratification makes us hard-hearted to others' feelings.** A devotee, because he serves Krishna and is not interested in sense gratification, is immediately grateful when served. The "I, me, and mine" person takes the service as his due — "yes, this is for me" — and won't even say thank you. So **gratitude is a sign of a devotee.**
- We should accept service **on behalf of the spiritual master and Krishna**, not for ourselves.
- **The car and Alan story:** IDS got an old, cheap Colt Galant. A congregation member, Alan Lutchman, very kindly overhauled the whole car, unasked. In return IDS brought him a present — a **framed picture of Lord Śrī Nṛsiṁhadeva**, with a ribbon and a thank-you card. (He should not think, "He should fix my car because I'm the temple president"; rather, we are simply **engaging others in service**, and if someone offers us something, the materialistic "I, me, and mine" person takes it just for himself, whereas our duty is to engage others in service.)
- The next day Alan called: "Mahārāja, thank you for the card and the picture — now I want to give you a **tape deck** for your car." IDS thought, "When is it going to stop? A car, a picture, a tape deck..." — this is the process of devotional service: **always acknowledging the service of others**, back and forth, encouraging each other. (IDS's reciprocation: "Can I come preach at your Nāma-haṭṭā program?")
- This is why in the **Upadeśāmṛta** Rūpa Gosvāmī lists **exchanging gifts** as a principle of devotional service (*dadāti pratigṛhṇāti* — the six loving exchanges) — a sign of advancement: you recognize someone's service and want to encourage him.
- We chant the Lord's name in a **humble frame of mind** (echoing Śikṣāṣṭaka 3): thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street, more tolerant than a tree, devoid of all false prestige ("I, me, and mine") — not wanting respect for oneself but always wanting to **recognize the service of others.** Not "everyone should give me gifts and recognition," but when gifts come, immediately thinking of returning them to encourage that person's devotional service.

## Being Able to Say "Thank You" and "I'm Sorry"

- Cultivate the ability to say **"thank you"** and **"I'm sorry."** Karmīs never want to admit they are wrong — "Hey, I'm right!" — and invent the "**white lie**," a lie one won't confess to, which somehow "makes it all right."
- A devotee should be able to say "I'm sorry." If he offends the Vaiṣṇavas, he should respect them and apologize. But the **false ego** makes it hard — you know you should say sorry or offer obeisances, but you just can't, because of "I, me, and mine": the same sense gratification of the material world carried back into devotional service.
- A real devotee is very careful **not to make offenses.** We do make offenses — otherwise we'd be Krishna conscious in an instant; **the reason we are not yet Krishna conscious is that we are making offenses** to the holy name and the devotees. Devotees are our shelter; by their mercy we are being delivered — not by "my" devotional service. "It is the mercy of the Vaiṣṇavas and my spiritual master and Krishna that I am even able to do anything." (Bhajan sentiment: **"Your mercy is all that I am made of."**) So when we misuse that mercy, we feel embarrassed and want to apologize.

## Even the Spiritual Master Accepts Nothing for Himself

- Prabhupāda said the spiritual master **should not accept disciples as a facility for his own sense gratification** — he must not use his disciples' money or facility for his own enjoyment, but must use it all in the Lord's service. Prabhupāda said money coming during the day should be **spent by the next morning** — so much comes, and we take none of it.
- The spiritual master accepts that facility (Lakṣmī) and offers it to *his* Guru Mahārāja, who offers it back to Krishna, the original enjoyer — everyone offering it in the Lord's service. That is the principle for the spiritual master, who is meant to be the best devotee, the ācārya, the perfect example.
- **He doesn't even accept the worship.** When the disciples offer **guru-pūjā**, the spiritual master is not thinking, "Yes, this fan and these flowers and this praise are for me"; he offers it to *his* spiritual master, thinking, **"Without my Guru I am a dog only."** If he thinks otherwise he is not guru — because **guru means transparent medium**, the via medium to the spiritual world.
- The same principle applies to everyone else: we accept nothing for our own but offer everything in the Lord's devotional service. With the mentality that we are receiving service **on behalf of the movement, the spiritual master, and Krishna**, we will naturally want to reciprocate.

## Gratitude Keeps One from Falling Down

- One problem in our society: we become **familiar** with all the mercy and think we deserve it, losing the attitude of gratefulness.
- **"If one is grateful to the spiritual master, he will never fall down."** If one actually appreciates the mercy the guru has given, it would be too embarrassing to fall — "The spiritual master is delivering me from material existence, and I want to go back to eat the vomit?"
- **Vāntāśī** — like a sannyāsī who returns to material life is said to eat his own vomit. The same applies to any devotee: at initiation we promised the spiritual master to follow the four regulative principles, chant sixteen rounds, and take prasādam; to break that promise is like *vāntāśī* — embarrassing.

## Don't Embarrass the Spiritual Master

- When any devotee falls down, it is a great embarrassment for the spiritual master, because it is **he who recommends us to Krishna** — we cannot approach Krishna on our own ("Who are we?").
- **Analogy:** Trivanga is a vice-president. IDS brings a boy he preached to on the street — "He's really nice" — giving his word of honor the boy will cause no problems. On IDS's word, Trivanga lets him stay. That night the boy breaks the **huṇḍī** (donation box), starts stealing, and even tries to steal IDS's computer. IDS is embarrassed because he recommended him. (This actually happened — "remember that boy?")
- So we should never want to embarrass the spiritual master, never embarrass Prabhupāda. We may be "rotten to the core," but if we do something wrong, who gets the blame? Prabhupāda. At least if we are attached to Prabhupāda, that attachment restrains us.

## Full Faith in the Guru Is the One Essential Thing

- **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.23** — *yasya deve parā bhaktir yathā deve tathā gurau* — to one who has full faith in the spiritual master and Krishna, the full import of Vedic knowledge is revealed. **This is the most important thing to develop.**
- How does one develop faith? **By submissive service and by hearing.** With such faith, when tempted, one thinks, "It would be so embarrassing to Prabhupāda if I did that" — and can thereby control himself. **The instructions of the spiritual master are the pride of the disciple.** (Bhajan sentiment: **"Attachment to your lotus feet is the perfection that fulfills all desires."**)
- We hesitate to surrender because in the material world surrendering always meant getting cheated. But as long as you cannot surrender to the spiritual master, it means you are still seeing him as an ordinary man; therefore start seeing through the **eyes of revealed scripture.**
- Material life means "you put your head on my lap, and I cut your throat." We have all been cheated. But the **Vaiṣṇavas are not like that** — **Vaiṣṇava-praṇāma:** *vāñchā-kalpa-tarubhyaś ca kṛpā-sindhubhya eva ca* — they are desire-trees and oceans of mercy, purifiers of the fallen. So we should not distrust the Vaiṣṇavas; we should surrender to the Guru and to Krishna without hesitating ("he who hesitates is lost"). That surrender and faith are needed for progress.

## Along with the Order Comes the Strength

- A sign of one who is surrendered is that he is **fully engaged in devotional service without a doubt**, because he knows Guru and Krishna are the ones actually acting, not himself — so "I can do whatever I am told."
- Prabhupāda's order: **double book distribution every year; a set of books in every house.** We think, "We can't do it" — but that is the problem: thinking *we* can't. Prabhupāda, Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī, Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī, Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura — *they* want it done; **we are just the instruments** of their execution of devotional service. **Along with the order comes the strength to execute it.** If we simply have faith and try our best, by their mercy it will be done.
- To say "a set of books in every house is not possible" means we are thinking the spiritual master is an ordinary man. The truth is the opposite — *we* are the ordinary men and women; the problem is that **we are not up to the standard, not fully surrendered.**
- **The Japanese philosopher** (told to Prabhupāda when he was president): "The only thing that can stop this movement is **internal difficulty**. Nothing external can stop it — not even an atomic bomb; the devotees will find some way around it." (Prayer sentiment: **"My Lord, You are here, You are there, You are inside and outside — please purify my heart, protect me."**) So the only thing stopping us is ourselves — our **material desires.**

## Humility: More Humble Than a Blade of Grass

- Material desires are embarrassing; therefore one never presents himself as worthwhile before the guru but always as very fallen. When one goes before the spiritual master he **places a blade of grass in his mouth and falls down.**
- **Sanātana Gosvāmī:** though highly qualified — knowing Persian, Arabic, Sanskrit, many languages, mathematics, astrology, and holding a high post in the Nawab's government — when he came before Lord Caitanya he put a blade of grass in his mouth, fell down, and said, **"People are saying I am something, but I am nothing."** **Brahmā said the same** ("People think I am something wonderful, but I am nothing").
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka 3** — *tṛṇād api sunīcena* — one should be more humble than a blade of grass (a blade of grass, everyone steps on it). Humility is essential because **only when one is humble can he receive instruction.** Pride means "I think I know something nobody else knows"; one must be devoid of pride to approach the Lord's representative and the Lord.
- This is why before the **deities** one does not stick his feet out, cross his arms, etc. — these are symptoms of pride. We train ourselves to always stand in **awe and reverence** before the Lord, exhibiting no pride.

## Correcting Others with Respect and Compassion

- With the devotees too we must always be respectful — **even when correcting someone.** We respect that person's real interest in his Krishna consciousness; we are not trying to smash him ("You rascal, nonsense, get out of here!" — which the person also cannot accept).
- **Gurukula in France:** when a boy has misbehaved and you call him in to chastise him, **first give a little compliment** to reassure him you have faith in him — "You're doing really good, I appreciate what you're doing... but what you did this morning was completely nonsense." If you just call someone a rascal, he thinks you have no real affection for him.
- We are meant to **train** people in Krishna consciousness, not abuse them. We don't hit or kick; if someone does wrong we correct him, we don't come down to his platform. Like teachers in the material world who abuse students — nobody wants such a teacher. A real teacher is **compassionate** and tolerates the students' abuse, understanding "kids are like this, but I have to get the message through," so he leads with encouragement ("You're great, I liked your report yesterday") because the student knows the teacher likes him.

## Freedom from Envy Means Compassion — the Qualification to Preach

- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's prayer** (*Kabe Ha'be Bolo Se-dina Āmār*): "When will that compassion for all living entities manifest itself? And with a lowly heart I will go out to preach the divine command." If we think we are so much better than the karmīs, how will we preach to them? We are better only by **Prabhupāda's mercy**, and we must share that mercy — otherwise we could again become "a rat."
- Devotees living outside sometimes note that in Krishna consciousness we can wrongly think, "We are the greatest; everyone else is a demon." But the others are also our brothers and sisters, spirit souls — and it was only weeks, months, or years ago that we ourselves were "out there."
- An **envious** person cannot be compassionate. So one sign that a person is free from envy is that he is **compassionate — he can preach.** Someone who likes to go out and deliver conditioned souls (harināma, book distribution, festivals, university programs), or who maintains the temple to a high standard so visitors are impressed with Krishna consciousness, is becoming free from envy — **freedom from envy means compassion.**
- If we still have material desires, how can we be truly compassionate? We'll be thinking, "Hey, they've got something nice out there."
- **Prabhupāda to a sannyāsī:** "I am giving you sannyāsa, but remember two things: (1) never look back and think you left something behind by leaving your family — never think 'maybe I made a mistake, that was something I could have enjoyed'; and (2) **don't be envious of the householders**" (his beautiful wife, his security, his children, his house, his money in the bank).
- So we cannot envy the karmīs or their position. With too much material desire in the heart, when we go out on saṅkīrtana we will be disturbed. Our duty: read the books, chant sixteen rounds, follow the four regulative principles strictly — then we get the strength to see the difference between their life and the merciful life Prabhupāda has given, and, being a little above it, we can preach effectively.

## The Six Gosvāmīs Came to Give, Not to Take

- The six Gosvāmīs were popular both with the gentle **and** with the ruffians — dacoits, thieves, robbers. How? Because **when the Gosvāmīs came, they came only to give, not to take.**
- In the material world, people fear one another and are envious, because "you have something I want, or you want something I have." But the Gosvāmīs came in loincloths — Prabhupāda said they were skinny and always sick, having no time for eating and sleeping, wanting only to glorify Krishna. So people did not feel challenged by them; they felt the Gosvāmīs were coming to give mercy.
- **We become more effective in preaching when we become more pure.**

## "The Best Thing Is Purity" (The Key Point)

- Prabhupāda's dictum: **"Books are the basis, preaching is the essence, utility is the principle, and purity is the force."**
- Devotees ask, "What is the best saṅkīrtana technique? Should I say this or that?" **The best thing is purity. Purity.** Then people sense, "This person is not coming to take."
- Materialists are always being challenged — even within their own families: competition between husband and wife, sons fighting and quarreling. **Krishna consciousness means no such competition — only giving devotional service.** So when we make a little advancement — free from material desire, thus free from envy, thus compassionate — we can go out and preach with full force and effectiveness, and the movement will be successful.
- Again the Japanese philosopher: the only thing that can stop this movement is **internal difficulty**, and internal difficulty manifests as **material desire.**

## Accepting Instruction Uproots Material Desire (Close of the Main Talk)

- Therefore, before the deity or the spiritual master, knowing he is a conditioned soul with material desires, one always maintains a mood of **awe and reverence: "Please instruct me."**
- Even if one still has material desires, **if he accepts the instruction of the spiritual master with full submission and surrender, that is the formula that uproots those material desires.** That is success. So the most important principle is **having full faith in the spiritual master and Krishna.**

## Struggle Is Necessary; Deity Worship Is a Purifying Burden

- The disciple has to struggle a little. A neophyte may think that struggling means something is wrong with the process — "Why should I have to struggle?" — but **a little effort is actually necessary.** Instructions are given so the disciple has to exert himself.
- Prabhupāda said **deity worship and saṅkīrtana are a burden** — deliberately so. Deity worship: you fetch water, clean the altar, gather all the paraphernalia, cook for the deity, do the shopping, offer, clean, undress and re-dress the deities every day. Prabhupāda said **once you start deity worship you can never stop.** He gave a **śālagrāma-śilā** to Pradyumna (a secretary), saying, "I am giving you this śālagrāma, but you can never, ever stop worshiping."
- That labor is **purifying to the heart.** As soon as the "labor of love" is gone, one becomes complacent.

## Bhaktisiddhānta Kept the Gauḍīya Maṭha in Debt

- Prabhupāda said his spiritual master, **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī**, used to build so many temples and run so many programs **to keep the Gauḍīya Maṭha in debt.** One might think being in debt is bad management, but in this way the godbrothers were always working very hard.
- The nature of that work is not ordinary work — it is **devotional service**, which purifies the heart. Our tendency, however, is not for devotional service but for sense gratification; therefore **a little pushing and chastisement is necessary.**
- Prabhupāda said the spiritual master's duty is to **chastise** the disciple so he does not become puffed up or complacent — sometimes chastising even when nothing is wrong, just to keep the proper relationship of awe and veneration, so the disciple does not become too familiar. Otherwise we are dragged to hell by our senses (again SB 7.5.30, *adānta-gobhiḥ*).

## Negation Is Not Enough: Purify Desire, Don't Kill It

- Merely negating the senses is insufficient. In this verse's purport Prabhupāda notes the **impersonalists** also understand that sense gratification is the cause of entanglement in the material world. In the **Bhagavad-gītā**, Prabhupāda says **"desire is a subtle form of conditioning."**
  - We are all conditioned now — a particular set of senses, body, mind, and intelligence — and we don't remember planning this exact body, because desire operates as a subtle principle. Based on our previous desires we obtained this gross body with its particular senses and intelligence.
- Both schools — Vaiṣṇavas and impersonalists — want to abstain from sense gratification. But the impersonalists err by **not engaging in spiritual "sense gratification."** We loosely say "no sense gratification, Prabhu," but this must be clarified: the living entity *does* have senses, and **the real gratification of the senses is to engage them in the Lord's service.**

## The Airport and Dvārakā: Purifying the Principle of Attraction

- Seeing a Vaiṣṇava is deeply satisfying — after being away, wasn't it nice to see the devotees again? (E.g., the joy when Śrī Hari returned from India — "Haribol! Haribol!")
- **Airport story:** IDS and Trivanga were at the airport as families waited for a South African Airlines flight; little children were so happy, waiting for a mother they hadn't seen in two months; when the plane came, everyone got excited to see parents, brothers, sisters. IDS said to Trivanga: **this is a perverted reflection of what it must be like in Dvārakā.**
  - When the Lord returns to Dvārakā, the inhabitants bow down, ladies throw flowers on His path, some do cāmara, some recite prayers, some sweep — everyone in a mood of great anticipation to see the Lord (as in Rāma Dāsa's painting of the Lord entering Dvārakā, reciprocating according to each one's rasa).
  - At the airport, "all these material bodies flying in, and all these other material bodies can't wait to see them" — yet the *mood* was appreciable. If it is that exciting to wait for material relatives, what must it be to become Krishna conscious and be anxious to see Krishna? (Rūpa Gosvāmī: as young boys long to see young girls and vice versa, "so that my attention be attracted to You.")
- The whole idea is to **purify that principle of attraction.** When we become attracted to mundane family and society it ends in misery, because those relationships are temporary and cannot satisfy — **unless the Lord is in the center.** On saṅkīrtana you see homes nicely arranged for sex life — the house, home, paraphernalia all arranged, but "one thing missing: Krishna," so the whole thing falls apart. It is matter trying to enjoy matter — no rasa, no relationship. As soon as Krishna is in the center, it becomes fulfilling.
- So when will *we* become eager to see Krishna? Not while we are "glancing at the side" (e.g., looking across the temple room at the Mātājīs; the Mātājīs looking at the border). **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** (*Kabe Ha'be Bolo*): "When will that day be mine when, my offenses ceasing and the taste for the name increasing, I will desire Krishna?"

## Cling to the Lotus Feet of Guru and Gaurāṅga

- Therefore we have our beautiful deities, temples, Vaiṣṇavas, and philosophy — the most attractive things. We must concentrate: **restrict ourselves from sense gratification with our intelligence and engage in the Lord's service.** How? Because the spiritual master tells us to, and his instructions are the pride of the disciple.
- To fall down into sense gratification is a shameless activity; to stay in Krishna consciousness is the real glory. **How do you stay in Krishna consciousness? By clinging tight to the lotus feet of Guru and Gaurāṅga** — which means taking the instructions to heart, executing them with a concentrated mind, and not deviating.
- That faith (again *yasya deve parā bhaktiḥ*), even if "a little blind" in the beginning when we don't fully understand or appreciate the spiritual master, is higher and more important than any material attachment.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.34** — *tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā, upadekṣyanti te jñānaṁ...* — one must surrender to the representative of the Lord (approach him, inquire submissively, render service); and that instruction acts to **control the mind and senses**, because one has faith and gratitude.
- So we restrict ourselves from sense gratification and engage in the Lord's devotional service under the guidance of the spiritual master — and thereby the material designations go away, and the strong material desires are uprooted.
