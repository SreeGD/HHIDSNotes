# Importance of Festivals

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 41 · Festivals and Kirtans · 25:22

**Themes:** festivals · vaidhi-bhakti · cleansing the heart · kirtan · spiritual world · Sunday Love Feast

## Summary

Speaking at a multi-day Memorial Day weekend festival, Indradyumna Swami explains why festivals are essential to Krishna consciousness. The Vaishnava calendar is filled with festivals, and the Nectar of Devotion states it is an offense (aparadha) to ignore them. Festivals mirror the spiritual world — in Goloka Vrindavan "there is a festival every day" — and Krishna (5,000 years ago), then Lord Chaitanya (who uniquely invited even conditioned souls to participate through chanting and dancing), and finally Srila Prabhupada (who introduced the Sunday Love Feast) each brought that festive mood into this world. Festivals matter because ordinary busy life leaves little time for full absorption; a festival delivers a concentrated "big dose" of Krishna consciousness that cleanses the mirror of the heart (ceto-darpana-marjanam), like spring cleaning, turning slow incremental progress into a quantum leap. He urges everyone not to hold back but to give themselves fully to chanting, dancing, hearing, and honoring prasadam.

## Key Points

- The Vaishnava calendar is full of festivals; the Nectar of Devotion says ignoring them is an offense (aparadha).
- Festivals reflect the eternal festive mood of Goloka Vrindavan, where every day is a festival.
- Krishna, Lord Chaitanya (who let conditioned souls join through the holy name), and Prabhupada (the Sunday Love Feast) each introduced this festive mood to the world.
- Ordinary life leaves little time for absorption; a festival gives a concentrated 'big dose' of Krishna consciousness.
- Chanting cleanses the mirror of the heart (ceto-darpana-marjanam) — a festival is like spring cleaning.
- Don't be shy: give yourself fully to the process — chanting, dancing, hearing, and prasadam.
