# The Miracle of Krishna Consiousness

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 37 · Festivals and Kirtans · 48:25

**Themes:** krishna consciousness · devotee association · illusion (maya) · detachment · goal setting · material suffering

## Summary

Speaking in Detroit, the city where he joined ISKCON 36 years earlier, Indradyumna Swami presents Krishna consciousness as a supremely powerful process capable of uprooting even deeply embedded material desires (anarthas) whenever one takes it up, provided one embraces the philosophy, association, and service and not merely the chanting, dancing, and feasting. He stresses that spiritual advancement is nearly impossible in isolation, recalling Srila Prabhupada's teaching that ISKCON exists to give the association of devotees and that anyone hoping to become Krishna conscious outside that association is living in 'hallucination.' Using the paramahamsa (swan) analogy, he urges devotees to take the essence, Krishna consciousness, and leave aside the 'water' of material life, living simply while maximizing spiritual practice ('simple living, high thinking'; Gandhi's 'need, not greed'). The heart of the class is a reading of Bhagavad-gita 8.16, where Krishna declares that every planet from the highest to the lowest is a place of repeated birth and death, yet, as the best of poets, He ends 'on a high note' by promising that one who attains His abode never returns. He depicts the illusory, suffering nature of the world through the law of the jungle ('jivo jivasya jivanam,' one living being is food for another), the story of Duryodhana bewildered in the illusion-filled Maya-sabha, and Maya's two great tricks: making us think we are the body and, harder still, that we are God (Mayavada). He calls the guru a 'sadhu,' one who cuts away our attachments like a doctor diagnosing disease, and encourages goal-setting by envisioning where one wants to be at the moment of death, aspiring for freedom from lust, anger, and greed and a deep taste for the holy name. Weaving in memories such as a seven-year-old Vaishnavi in Vrindavan who dreamt of serving Radharani, and Prabhupada's teaching that one later remembers material life only 'like a bad dream,' he concludes that although the work is hard, its reward, pure devotional service and a fully satisfied heart (Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6), is unlike any material paycheck.

## Opening: An August Assembly and a Homecoming

- The speaker thanks everyone for inviting him this evening into this **"august assembly of Vaishnavas and Vaishnavis."**
  - "August" is a complimentary term, because everyone here is clearly very attached to chanting Hare Krishna — which is the **yuga dharma**. Anyone who has understood that, is practicing it, and is spreading the glories of the holy name is a great soul.
- Generally sannyasis travel to give association, but in assemblies like this the sannyasi often feels he is the one *getting* the association — the devotees are chanting very loudly, blissfully, and happily.
- **Coming back home:** While driving in, he reflected that in one sense he is coming back home, because this (Detroit) is where he joined the Hare Krishna movement — 36 years ago.
  - "How many of you are under 36?" — some present took their birth after he joined (jokingly, on Brahma Loka, Indra Loka).
  - He was born in California but migrated out to the Midwest / Detroit. So in essence he has come full circle — "what goes around comes around" — going to the four corners of the earth and finding himself back here.
  - By the "curse" of the sannyasa order, he cannot stay long: a sannyasi is not supposed to stay in one place more than 72 hours ("a rolling stone gathers no moss"), so he is here only briefly.
- He was here before he was a devotee, and that was **a nightmare** — but this, Krishna consciousness, is the reality of life.
- He also has many deep, beautiful memories of his first days, weeks, and months in Krishna consciousness, by the grace of his spiritual master, who **swept him up in his own ecstasy of devotional service** and taught him to swim — to dive and surface — in the nectarian ocean of devotional service to the Lord.

## Do Not Dwell on the Past: "Chewing the Chewed"

- It's nice to return and have those spiritual memories, but the *other* memories — of life before Krishna consciousness — we should not dwell on too much. That is **"chewing the chewed."**
- **Bubble-gum analogy:** Like a little child who chews bubble gum, sticks it under the school desk, and comes back to chew it again the next day — and keeps this up for two weeks.
  - "Kids will be kids, boys will be boys" — not the girls ("sugar and spice and everything nice"); the boys are "frogs and snails and puppy dog tails."
- We laugh, but we're really laughing at ourselves — because we have been very obstinate, determined to enjoy life after life after life on the chewed remnants of material existence. That is nothing very wonderful, and we don't want to keep that impression.

---

## The Power of Krishna Consciousness at Any Age

- But Krishna consciousness is so powerful, at whatever stage or age we take it up — teens, 20s, 30s, 40s, 50s. Some are "late bloomers," but as they say, **"It's not how you start, it's how you finish."**
- Though we have a deep impression of material life in our hearts — which gives birth to so many unwanted **anarthas** (material desires) and is quite overwhelming — we are very fortunate that the process of Krishna consciousness is even more powerful.
- If we're serious: the chanting, dancing, and feasting is not so hard. It's the **philosophy, the association, and the service** that are so powerful they will overcome the material impressions.

---

## Uprooting Every Material Desire Before Death

- **Krishna says whatever you remember at the moment of death, that impression will carry you to your next birth** (the teaching of Bhagavad Gita 8.6). If you have any last remnant of taste for this world, you have to come back.
- **Bacteria analogy:** It's like a disease — pneumonia, typhoid, diphtheria — where if even one germ is left, chances are it will come back.
- **Cancer analogy (even better):** The doctor operates and cuts out the cancer, but he also cuts a wide circle of flesh around it to make sure not a single cancerous cell is left, because if there is, further down the road there is more misery.
- So before we die, we have to uproot **all the lust, all the anger, all the greed.** Then, and only then, can the **bhakti-lata** — the creeper/seed of devotional service — planted in that fertile ground actually fructify.

## No Excuse — the Necessity of Good Association

- We have the process, so **there's no excuse.**
  - His mother used to say: "There's no excuse for you not getting a good education, boy. The problem is you spend all day at the beach."
  - So we have no excuse for anything other than perfection in Krishna consciousness. The problem is we procrastinate — we put off for tomorrow what we can do today. But time is short; the sand in the hourglass is falling.
- But in the good association of devotees it is possible to always remain inspired. On your own it is very difficult. So **these programs are essential** — it is *Vaishnava-sanga.*
- Prabhupada said he created the International Society for Krishna Consciousness for the purpose of **giving good association**, and that anyone who thinks he can become Krishna conscious *outside* the association of devotees is living in a dream — he actually said **"hallucination."**
- So as difficult as it is to come here after a hard day's work, as "dangerous" as it is to go down to the temple in a bad part of town, as hard as it is to sacrifice some summer vacation for the Ratha Yatra — it's all to our benefit.

## The Swan and the Crow: Becoming a Paramahansa

- **"Birds of a feather flock together."** So even if you're a black crow, in the association of so many white swans — the miracle of Krishna consciousness — you can also become a **Paramahansa.**
- What is a Paramahansa? A "swan-like man." (Analogies are meant to give an understanding of philosophy, which is sometimes a little esoteric.)
- The **hamsa (swan)** has the unique ability that if you pour milk into water, the swan, by God's grace, can drink the milk without taking the water.
- **Story — testing the swans:** He once tried it (not that he's the swan — "I'm the crow"). He went to a lake with swans and poured milk into the water. The swans came and drank the milk, and as they drank, the water came out of their beak (the nostrils, for breathing). They were actually separating the milk from the water — unbelievable. It increased his faith in Krishna and in Prabhupada's analogy.
- We have to be like that — Paramahansas. As Jesus said, we are "to be in this world but not of this world." We have to be transcendentalists: take the **essence of life, which is Krishna consciousness**, and leave the rest aside.

---

## Simple Living, High Thinking

- Of course you need a little "water" — a little sustenance: some food, a house, a car — but not more than that. **Simple living with high thinking.**
- Nowadays it has become complicated living with no time to think, always trying to "keep up with the Joneses." Take what is necessary.
- **Gandhi said** the problem is not that people are taking according to their *need* — people are taking according to their **greed**, and this creates the problem.
- So keep it simple materially, but spiritually **maximize everything.**
- "Too much of a good thing is bad" — that may be true in material life (too much ice cream, too much sun). But you can never say that for Krishna consciousness: you can never get enough medicine, purification, or nectar.

## Reading Bhagavad Gita 8.16 — "Attaining the Supreme"

- This evening's reading is from **Bhagavad Gita As It Is** by his spiritual master, **Om Vishnupada Paramahamsa Parivrajakacharya Sri Srimad A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Srila Prabhupada** — translation, commentary, and a few realizations.
- He expected only 10 or 15 people since tomorrow is a work day, but walked in and said "wow," and appreciates that everyone came.
- Reading from the **8th chapter, "Attaining the Supreme," text 16.**
- **Translation (BG 8.16):** "From the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery wherein repeated birth and death take place. But one who attains to My abode, O son of Kunti, never takes birth again."
- **Points from the purport:**
  - All kinds of yogis — *karma, jnana, hatha,* etc. — eventually have to attain devotional perfection in *bhakti yoga* (Krishna consciousness) before they can go to Krishna's transcendental abode and never return.
  - Those who attain the highest material planets, the planets of the demigods, are again subjected to repeated birth and death. Just as persons on earth are elevated to higher planets, people on higher planets — **Brahmaloka, Chandraloka, Indraloka** — fall down to earth.
  - The practice of sacrifice called **panchagni-vidya**, recommended in the **Chandogya Upanishad**, enables one to achieve Brahmaloka — but he has to return to earth.
  - Those who progress in Krishna consciousness on the higher planets are gradually elevated to higher and higher planets, and at the time of universal devastation are transferred to the eternal spiritual kingdom.
  - **Baladeva Vidyabhushana**, in his commentary on the Gita, quotes a verse: when there is devastation of this material universe, Brahma and his devotees, who are constantly engaged in Krishna consciousness, are all transferred to the spiritual universe and to specific spiritual planets according to their desires.

## Krishna Ends on a High Note

- This is a very famous, important, sober verse — but it has a good ending. Krishna explains the real nature of this world: from the highest planet down to the lowest, it is all misery. But it ends on a high note.
- When someone gives a musical recital or recites poetry, the art is to always end on a high note. Of all poets, **Krishna is the best poet** — He ends on a high note.
- **"Punar janma na vidyate"** (from BG 8.16): despite this miserable condition, one who attains to My abode never takes birth again.
- Hearing the first part, you might become depressed — and you should: you should give up all faith in the false pretense of happiness in this world. But then Krishna is very kind and gives the positive alternative: **you can go back home, back to Godhead, and never come back.**

## Do We Remember the Material World? (Story)

- One time a disciple asked Srila Prabhupada: **"When we get back to the spiritual world, do we remember that we were in the material world?"** (The speaker thought this a really good question, having always wondered the same.)
- Prabhupada answered: **"Yes. You remember it like a bad dream."**
- **His reflection:** Dreams are an accumulation of the things we see and do during life. He travels to some very different parts of the world — you can see heaven and hell even on this planet — so sometimes he has a bad dream, and when he wakes he doesn't want to go back to sleep, afraid the dream will continue. If it's a good dream, he turns off the alarm clock and goes deeper into it.
- He does not have Krishna conscious dreams. His godbrother **Sivarama Swami** is always dreaming about spiritual life "in technicolor" — such an advanced devotee.

## The Little Vaishnavi Who Dreamt of Radharani (Story)

- He met one little Vaishnavi in Vrindavan, seven years old, part of ISKCON. He asked, "Do you dream of Krishna?" She said, "No. But I dream of Radharani."
- "Really? What do you dream?" "Last night I dreamt of **Srimati Radhika**. I was asked by one of the **sakhis** to go into the bowers, into the **kunja**, into the forest, and wake up Radha and Krishna."
  - He was amazed — he hadn't even read that part of the **Govinda-lilamrita**, and this child was telling all the details.
- She said she was put in charge of waking **Radha** (not Krishna).
  - **Background she referenced:** Normally it is a monkey that wakes Them. **Vrinda-devi** engages the different animals in service in Vrindavan; a monkey makes a big noise, and that is how Radha and Krishna wake from Their night pastimes and hurry home before Their superiors find Them.
  - In her dream the monkey did *not* wake Them — so she very gently woke Radha, who got up, took Her time, and was yawning.
- Then Radha **patted the girl on the head**, and she woke up.
- He was so jealous and said, "I wish that Radharani would pat *me* on the head." The girl made a face: "She wouldn't pat you on the head." "Why not?" "Because you're a male."
- Those kinds of dreams we want. But **this dream of this life is nothing but a nightmare.** Even the best dream is a nightmare, because in this world it all ends in death — there is no good conclusion.
- So Prabhupada said: Yes, you remember it — but because it's like a nightmare, you have no attraction to it.

---

## The Lord's Opinion vs. Our Secret Desires

- This is the Lord's opinion, and we should take it to heart, because we have a different opinion — we still have some **secret desires** in the heart, some "security measure" that if Krishna consciousness doesn't work out, we can always go back. We maintain material attachments.
- **One of the offenses to the holy name is to maintain material attachments** even after hearing so much on the subject matter.
- We are not pure devotees — we don't cry when we chant, our hairs don't stand on end — so we still have some attachment to this world.

---

## The Sadhu Who Cuts Our Attachments

- We overcome this by hearing from the **sadhu**. *Sadhu* means "one who cuts" — when he speaks, he cuts; not a physical cut, but he cuts our attachments to this world. That is a sadhu.
- It's a little painful sometimes to go to the lecture, or to get direction from the spiritual master. He's like the doctor — not supposed to tell you how *well* you are, but to point out where you're sick and give you the medicine.
- When the sadhu speaks, he speaks the **naked truth** — just like the **Naga Babas**, those *tyagis* who walk naked, not wearing even *kaupins*, "their clothing being the ten directions," to demonstrate they have no attachments to this world.
- The naked truth is **"duhkhalayam ashashvatam"** (from BG 8.15): this world is nothing more, nothing less than a temporary place full of suffering.
- But it takes a while to get the message, because we're thick-headed. We think ourselves so intelligent that, rather than give up our attachments immediately upon hearing from guru, we take 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 years — 50 lifetimes.
- **Prabhupada said:** don't make the business of the spiritual master difficult by making him come back to deliver you.

---

## Why God's Word Is Authoritative

- This is God's opinion — actually not an opinion, but simply fact. He knows.
- **Architect analogy:** Doesn't the architect know a building he designed better than anyone? When a roof collapses, they call the original architect: "What was wrong here, sir?" "I'm not at fault." "No — you built it, you know this building better than anyone."
- **Patent analogy:** Doesn't the owner of a patent know the product better than anyone else?
  - **Family story:** His grandfather patented an improvement to petroleum storage. He saw that petroleum tanks had a space between the liquid and the top, so the petroleum was evaporating and companies were losing money. He designed a **floating-roof concept** with no space between the petroleum and the top, so there was no evaporation. He patented it and became a millionaire (the speaker "never got a cent of it").
  - So the person who patents a product or concept knows it better than anyone.
- **"Aham sarvasya prabhavo mattah sarvam pravartate"** (BG 10.8): Krishna says, "I am the source of all that is material and all that is spiritual." He knows this world inside and out — better than you, because you're in one little place, while He is in **every atom of creation.**
- What is your opinion worth? You might say this world is nice as you head to Disneyland — but what about Iraq, Darfur, and the wars on the heavenly planets between the demigods and the Asuras?
- God is **abhijna svarat** (from Srimad Bhagavatam 1.1.1): completely independent and all-pervading. So if He says something, we accept it.
- **Steve Jobs / Bill Gates analogy:** If you want to know about the Apple Macintosh — he has a Mac Pro (15-inch screen) and wouldn't even take a PC if you gave it to him — you listen to **Steve Jobs**; when Jobs speaks about the Mac, iPhone, or iPod, his ears go up: what's new, how to upgrade? He's the authority (in that field, not in Krishna consciousness). Or **Bill Gates** is the authority on Microsoft — even retiring, he'll still be there for consultation. So when **God** speaks about this world, we should listen.

---

## Repetition Is Necessary in This Age

- **"A-brahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna"** (BG 8.16): all planets up to Brahmaloka are subject to return.
- A devotee might say, "Maharaj, I've heard that before. I've been a devotee four or five years — you don't have to tell me this world is a miserable place."
- But **Prabhupada said repetition of something is necessary to fully understand it** — what to speak of *realizing* it — especially in this age, because our intelligence is not very good and we are losing it quickly.
- He notes he has now quoted this verse five times. Prabhupada said **if you quote a verse seven times, it's yours.** So tomorrow night he'll quote it again and it's his — meaning it's loaned to us by Krishna in order to glorify Him and realize Him.

---

## The One Good Quality of Kali Yuga

- **Srimad Bhagavatam 12.2.1 (paraphrased):** "O King, religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, austerity, duration of life, physical strength, and memory will all diminish day by day because of the powerful influence of Kali."
  - You may think you're very intelligent, but just living in the polluted environment of Kali Yuga means your intelligence, memory, strength, and duration of life are all diminishing.
  - **Detroit analogy:** He heard that just to breathe the air of Detroit — the "Motor City," with all its fumes — is like smoking half a pack (or a pack and a half) of cigarettes a day. Just to live here means your body is affected, what to speak of your heart, because Kali Yuga is such a polluted thing.
- **"Kaler dosha-nidhe rajann asti hy eko mahan gunah / kirtanad eva krishnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet"** (SB 12.3.51): Shukadeva Goswami tells Maharaj Parikshit that this age of Kali is like an ocean of inauspiciousness, but there is **one good thing left** — by **kirtan** of Krishna one becomes free of material association and attains the supreme. That kirtan purifies the atmosphere.

### Story: Prabhupada Purifies the Calcutta Courthouse

- One time Prabhupada walked into a court in Calcutta, obliged to sign a paper with his disciples. He stopped and said, **"This place is very impure, very impure. Chant."**
- Right there — with the judge, the lawyers, and the people present — the devotees started chanting Hare Krishna. Then Prabhupada said, "Okay, now it's purified."
- **Achyutananda** asked, "What was the impurity?" Prabhupada said, **"All the lawyers' lies."** ("Who's a lawyer here? We get in trouble.")

## The Beauty and the Brutal Struggle of This World

- So this is the world we live in, and by hearing like this we become detached.
- But we're not fanatical — we do see beautiful things. He saw a couple of nice parks in Detroit and doesn't want to slam the city too much (joke: they'll stop him at the airport next time); Detroit is one of the more important cities in the country.
- Even Krishna points out the beauty of this world (from the **Vibhuti Yoga, BG chapter 10**): "Of fishes I am the shark; of mountains I am Meru; of sages I am Narada." There are so many beautiful things because the world is created by God, who is all-beautiful — you see some of His artistry here in beautiful nature.
- But the **sum total is not very attractive.**
- **Forest analogy:** When you first look at a beautiful forest, you think, "What a beautiful place" — green trees, deer roaming. You go to Yosemite National Park, so beautiful and preserved. But actually a **bitter struggle** is going on there: if you camp, you watch all the animals competing for food, and many are not vegetarians. That's the real picture, not what you take home in the camera.
- You go home and tell your parents you went to Yosemite. "What did you see?" **"Jivo jivasya jivanam"** (from Srimad Bhagavatam 1.13.47): those devoid of hands are prey for those with hands; those devoid of legs are prey for the four-legged; the weak are the subsistence of the strong; and the general rule holds that **one living entity is food for another.**
- "All that glitters is not gold." Look in the sea: one little fish is eaten by a bigger fish, eaten by a bigger fish... and finally man. Nobody eats man? Yes — little tiny guys eat man; they're called **cancer cells.** Everyone is subject to *jivo jivasya jivanam.*

## Maya the Great Trickster

- Therefore it's an illusion, and Krishna is trying to point it out: **wake up.** (Referencing Sri Prahlad — "village of peace" — wake up, wake up.) When you're sleeping you're dreaming, and it's all very nice, but you have to wake up and go to school, go to work. So wake up and listen to the sadhu who's cutting through your illusion.
- What does **Shukadeva Goswami** say? This whole material existence is **gandharva-pura** — a house/city in the sky. Elsewhere he says the very conception of material happiness is a **"horse egg."**
  - "Excuse me, Swamiji, there's no such thing as a horse egg, and houses don't exist in the sky." Yes, sir — and in the same way, there is no real happiness in this world.
- But **Mayadevi**, under the direction of the Lord, is very powerful — she can make it look like something it's not. She's so expert. The **art of war is called deception** — the real trick of winning a war is to deceive your enemy in various ways. So Maya can make it look very real.
- As it says in the **Bhagavatam (paraphrase of SB 3.7.9):** "By Him even the great sages and demigods are placed into illusion, as one is bewildered by the illusory representations of water seen in fire, or land seen in water."
  - Just as on the ocean it sometimes looks like there's land far away, but it's only a reflection of the sun on the water; or in the desert you see water, but it's just a reflection of the sun on the sand. So Maya can create a **phantasmagoria** we believe we can enjoy. She's a good trickster.

## Duryodhana in the Palace of Illusion (Story)

- The **Mahabharata** describes how **Duryodhana** once visited the imperial palace of the Pandavas, which had been constructed by **Maya Danava.**
- Entering the mystical palace on his way to a meeting with the Pandavas, he saw what appeared to be water. Seeing no way through, he lifted his skirt (kshatriyas wore skirt-like garments with long stockings) and revealed his underwear — but there was no water; to everyone else he was just walking along lifting his skirt. The ladies of the palace, up on the higher floor, looked down and laughed and laughed.
- Then he came to a *real* pool of water and thought, "I'm not going to be tricked again," walked straight in, and fell flat on his face, getting all wet.
- Then he came to a crystal door which he thought was open, walked into it, and bumped his head — another illusion — and the ladies laughed and laughed.
- It's funny because Duryodhana is one of the bad guys, so we like to see him embarrassed. But actually **we're laughing at ourselves,** because we are falling for all the tricks of illusion in this world.

---

## The Two Snares: "I Am the Body" and "I Am God"

- What's the greatest trick? Maya has two:
  1. She causes us to think **we are the body.**
  2. She causes us to think **we are God.**
- These are the two snares of material existence. It's really hard to get over the first, but even harder to get over the second.
- It's hard to come to the point of accepting, believing, and acting on the spiritual platform — giving up the bodily concept of life. But once you get over that, there is another trick: **"I am God."**
- There are transcendentalists who have absolutely no attachment to this body, yet meditate thinking "I am God" — they are called **Mayavadis.**
  - Mayavadis are very advanced persons; we should be careful when we criticize them. They have no attachment to this world — do you? So be careful.
  - They are transcendentalists, but they have the last snare of material illusion: they think they are God.
- One way to defeat that: **Prabhupada said, "God is always God. He doesn't become God."** You're meditating to *become* God, but God is always God — He doesn't become God.
- So these are the two things we have to overcome.

---

## The Great Task: Setting Spiritual Goals

- We think, "I've got a lot of work — the office, my taxes (April's coming), my accountant, cleaning the yard, a business trip to increase business overseas." Hold on — are you working on **overcoming the bodily concept?** Are you avoiding the misconception that you can become God?
- **Story — Vishvamitra Muni and King Dasharatha:** When **Vishvamitra Muni** approached the palace of **King Dasharatha**, the king stood up to receive the sadhu and asked him, **"How is your endeavor to conquer over the repetition of birth and death going, sir?"**
  - He did *not* ask, "How's the business? How's the wife? How's the kids?" — the way we say, "How you doing, mate?" or (in South Africa) "How you keeping?" Everyone answers "fine," but you should say **"terrible — I'm still in the material world."**
- So are you putting in as much effort into overcoming the bodily concept of life as you are into satisfying the needs of this body? **The yin and the yang — it's all about finding the balance.**
- You can't be all material, but you can't be all spiritual either — you *can* eventually, but for most of us to give up material life altogether would be artificial. It's a constant struggle to give more emphasis to the spiritual side. It's a rare soul who can truly do that, but that should be our vision of ourselves further down the road.
- **Everyone should have a vision of themselves** 5, 10, 20, 30 years down the road, at the moment of death.
  - In good management there is **"goal setting"** — you set a goal for where you want your business to be. You should also have a vision of yourself: close your eyes and envision yourself 10 years from now — where would you like to be, and what do you have to do to get there?
  - If you don't have goals, you won't be responsible; you'll procrastinate, waste time, fool around, and put in the least effort. But if you know where you want to be in 10 years, you know exactly what to do.
- **The Goswamis have set the goal for us:** **"adau shraddha"** (Rupa Goswami's progression of devotional service, from *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*) — come into the association of devotees, get some faith, begin to practice regulative principles, get initiated, be strict, chant and serve.
  - And by the time you're 50, you want to be free of all material desires — that's **sannyasa** ("no more material life, only Krishna, Krishna, Krishna"). Technically a man would retire from worldly life at 50 and devote the whole rest of his life to God.
  - How many years do you have left to 50? If you're 35, only 15 — not much time. If you're 55, you're way behind. This is how we have to think.
- **What vision do we want?** "I'd like to be free from lust, anger, and greed — it's ruining my life. I'd like to be free from mental speculation and from doubts." And on the other side: "I'd like to have a deep appreciation for the holy name."
  - **"Namnam akari bahudha..."** (Shikshashtakam, verse 2): by a certain age, "I would like to realize that Krishna is none different than His name."
  - Don't set your goals *too* high, because that can be artificial — **sahajiya.** But when death comes, where do you want to be?
  - **"O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more; tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I am feeling all vacant in Your absence."** (Shikshashtakam, verse 7) — we should all aspire for that.
- Just as **Jesus said, "Be like thou Father who art in Heaven"**: don't be God, but **be godly** — you want to be godly by the time you leave this body.
- These are our most cherished goals, and they should be more dear to us than building up the business, turning it over to the kids, and retiring to a condo in Florida.

## Revealed Bit by Bit — and Worth the Work

- We have a great task ahead, because one time **Prabhupada said, "If I revealed all your material attachments to you, you would faint."**
- Sometimes we don't hear the whole story — like the doctor who doesn't tell you everything at first. He helps you, reveals a little bit, and when you're ready he tells you: "Well, you have cancer." If he told you right away, you'd fall apart.
  - So he breaks it gently: "This is a serious problem, but we're going to tackle it. It's going to be tough, but we have the means in modern medicine to deal with this. You have my support and your family's support." "What is it, doc?" "It's going to be all right — we just got a little cancer, that's all, but we can help."
- So if the Lord revealed everything, we might just give up; but He reveals it to us **bit by bit,** we take care of that, and we surrender.
- It's worth working for, because the goal is so nice.
  - **Paycheck analogy:** In the factory you work very hard and get a measly little paycheck — $7 an hour — not very inspiring. Everybody would prefer a job like his son's accountant, who gets $250 an hour: you work hard, but the results are so nice.
- So we have to work as hard at Krishna consciousness as we do at making a living — but making a living will never bring the real reward; you're left with nothing at the end.
- In Krishna consciousness you work very hard too — the chanting isn't easy, it's hard to follow all the regulative principles, hard to always come for the programs — but what is the result? So wonderful: **pure devotional service.**
- **Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6:** "The supreme occupation (*dharma*) for all men and women is that by which they can attain to the loving devotional service of the Lord" — service that must be unmotivated and uninterrupted.
- And what happens when you reach that final goal of Krishna consciousness? **The heart of hearts is completely happy in Krishna's service.**

## Glossary

- **Paramahamsa** — 'Supreme swan'; a topmost self-realized soul who takes only the spiritual essence and leaves the rest.
- **Anartha** — An unwanted material desire or impediment lodged in the heart.
- **Maya** — The Lord's illusory material energy that bewilders the conditioned soul.
- **Mayavadi** — An impersonalist who, though detached from the body, wrongly thinks he is God.
- **Sadhu** — A saint; described as 'one who cuts,' severing our attachments to this world.
- **Vaishnava-sanga** — The association of devotees, essential for advancement in Krishna consciousness.
- **Brahmaloka** — The highest planet within the material universe, still subject to rebirth.
- **Sannyasa** — The renounced order of life; the Vedic ideal is to take it by age fifty.
- **Duhkhalayam ashashvatam** — A description of the material world as a temporary place full of suffering.
