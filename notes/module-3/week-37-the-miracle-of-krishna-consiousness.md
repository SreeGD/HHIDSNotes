# The Miracle of Krishna Consiousness

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 37 · Festivals and Kirtans · 48:25

## Summary

Speaking in Detroit, the city where he joined ISKCON 36 years earlier, Indradyumna Swami presents Krishna consciousness as a supremely powerful process capable of uprooting even deeply embedded material desires (anarthas) whenever one takes it up, provided one embraces the philosophy, association, and service and not merely the chanting, dancing, and feasting. He stresses that spiritual advancement is nearly impossible in isolation, recalling Srila Prabhupada's teaching that ISKCON exists to give the association of devotees and that anyone hoping to become Krishna conscious outside that association is living in 'hallucination.' Using the paramahamsa (swan) analogy, he urges devotees to take the essence, Krishna consciousness, and leave aside the 'water' of material life, living simply while maximizing spiritual practice ('simple living, high thinking'; Gandhi's 'need, not greed'). The heart of the class is a reading of Bhagavad-gita 8.16, where Krishna declares that every planet from the highest to the lowest is a place of repeated birth and death, yet, as the best of poets, He ends 'on a high note' by promising that one who attains His abode never returns. He depicts the illusory, suffering nature of the world through the law of the jungle ('jivo jivasya jivanam,' one living being is food for another), the story of Duryodhana bewildered in the illusion-filled Maya-sabha, and Maya's two great tricks: making us think we are the body and, harder still, that we are God (Mayavada). He calls the guru a 'sadhu,' one who cuts away our attachments like a doctor diagnosing disease, and encourages goal-setting by envisioning where one wants to be at the moment of death, aspiring for freedom from lust, anger, and greed and a deep taste for the holy name. Weaving in memories such as a seven-year-old Vaishnavi in Vrindavan who dreamt of serving Radharani, and Prabhupada's teaching that one later remembers material life only 'like a bad dream,' he concludes that although the work is hard, its reward, pure devotional service and a fully satisfied heart (Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6), is unlike any material paycheck.

## Key Points

- Krishna consciousness is powerful enough to uproot deeply rooted anarthas at any age, but it requires the full process, philosophy, association, and service, not just chanting, dancing, and feasting.
- Devotee association (Vaishnava-sanga) is essential: Prabhupada founded ISKCON to give good association and warned that trying to become Krishna conscious without it is 'hallucination.'
- The paramahamsa/swan analogy: as a swan separates milk from water, a transcendentalist takes the essence (Krishna consciousness) and leaves material life aside, being 'in the world but not of it' (simple living, high thinking; Gandhi's need vs. greed).
- Bhagavad-gita 8.16: the whole material creation, from Brahmaloka down, is temporary and full of misery, yet one who reaches Krishna's abode never takes rebirth; Krishna, the best poet, ends on a high note.
- The guru/sadhu 'cuts' our material attachments (like a doctor who points out sickness and gives medicine); maintaining attachment after hearing is an offense to the holy name, and since God is the source of all (BG 10.8) His verdict on this world should be trusted.
- Maya's two snares are thinking 'I am the body' and (harder to overcome) 'I am God' (Mayavada); illustrated by Duryodhana's bewilderment in the illusory Maya-sabha and by the water-seen-in-fire analogy of Srimad Bhagavatam 1.1.1.
- Set spiritual goals with the moment of death in view (like corporate goal-setting; the Vedic ideal of renunciation by age 50), aspiring for freedom from lust/anger/greed and love in separation like Siksastakam verse 7; the reward is pure devotional service (SB 1.2.6).

## Scriptural References

- Bhagavad-gita 8.16 — the main verse read ('abrahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna'): all planets from highest to lowest are places of repeated birth and death, but one who attains Krishna's abode never takes birth again ('punar janma na vidyate').
- Bhagavad-gita 8.15 — 'duhkhalayam ashashvatam': this material world is a temporary place full of misery.
- Bhagavad-gita 8.6 — paraphrased: whatever state of being one remembers at the moment of death, that state one attains in the next life.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.8 — 'aham sarvasya prabhavo mattah sarvam pravartate': Krishna is the source of all that is material and spiritual, so He knows this world best.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.23, 10.26 & 10.31 — Krishna's opulences cited to show the world's God-given beauty: 'of mountains I am Meru' (10.23), 'of sages I am Narada' (10.26), and 'of fishes I am the shark/makara' (10.31).
- Chandogya Upanishad — panchagni-vidya (the five-fire sacrifice), mentioned in the BG 8.16 purport as a means to reach Brahmaloka, from which one must still return.
- Baladeva Vidyabhushana's commentary on Bhagavad-gita — quoted in the BG 8.16 purport that at universal devastation Brahma and his devotees are transferred to the spiritual world.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.2.1 — religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, austerity, duration of life, physical strength, and memory all diminish day by day under the influence of Kali.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.3.51 — 'kaler dosha-nidhe rajan... kirtanad eva krishnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet': despite Kali-yuga's ocean of faults, by chanting Krishna's name one becomes free and attains the supreme.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.13.47 — 'jivo jivasya jivanam': one living entity is the food/livelihood of another (the struggle for existence in the forest and sea).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.1.1 — the Lord bewilders even great sages by illusion, like water seen in fire or land seen in water; basis for Maya's 'phantasmagoria.'
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6 — 'sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokshaje': the supreme occupation for all men and women is that by which they attain unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service, fully satisfying the self.
- Siksastakam verse 7 — 'yugayitam nimeshena...': feeling Govinda's separation, a moment seems like twelve years, tears flow from the eyes like torrents of rain, and the whole world feels vacant.
- Padma Purana / Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 17.133) — 'nama cintamanih krishnah...' (alluded to as 'namacintamani'): the holy name is nondifferent from Krishna Himself.
- Mahabharata — Duryodhana bewildered in the Maya-sabha (the palace built by Mayadanava), mistaking the floor for water and a crystal door for an opening, an illustration of illusion.
- Ramayana — Vishvamitra Muni received by King Dasharatha, who greeted him by asking how his 'endeavor to conquer the repetition of birth and death' was progressing.
- Govinda-lilamrita — referenced via a child's dream of being sent by a sakhi into the kunja to wake Radha and Krishna.
