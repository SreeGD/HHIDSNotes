# Glories of Snakirtan

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 38 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:11:02

**Themes:** sankirtan · book distribution · holy name · japa rounds · gopi-bhava · preaching adventure

## Summary

Speaking on the glories of Sankirtan, Indradyumna Swami builds his talk on Jiva Goswami's Bhakti-sandarbha, which grades Krishna's moods across three abodes: Dwaraka (Krishna as king, the mood of awe and reverence), Mathura (his birthplace, more intimate), and Vrindavan (the gopis' pure, selfless, most perfect love), likening the difference to 9-karat versus 24-karat gold. He applies the same gradation to chanting the holy name: solitary japa is perfect, congregational temple kirtan is more perfect, but carrying the name to the people through street Sankirtan and book distribution (Brihat Mridanga) is most perfect, because the holy name is a person (nama cintamanih krsnah) who wants to go out and rescue the fallen souls who left the spiritual world. He stresses that chanting sixteen quality rounds daily is the foundation that gives a devotee the tejas (spiritual potency) to preach, since "people don't buy the book, they buy the book distributor," illustrating with stories of Prabhupada finishing his rounds in Los Angeles ("now I can do any damn thing I want"), a shy Latin American devotee who won book-distribution marathons in France simply by saying "Hare Bol" while shining like the sun, and a godbrother whose only qualification was that he "came running" to the maha-mantra. To show the auspiciousness that loud congregational chanting spreads, he recounts Krishna's headache being cured only by the dust of the gopis' feet and Chaitanya Bhagavata's account of dust from Mahaprabhu's kirtan party awakening ecstatic symptoms in distant farmers, adding that even the trees hearing kirtan are fortunate. Sankirtan devotees, he says, witness "little miracles" daily as strangers who came to forget God buy a book to remember him, and Krishna's promise "I am adventure" is realized on the street corner, which he calls the real temple where one meets God. Most of the lecture is a long autobiographical story from his book-distribution days in Paris: how he infiltrated locked fashion houses on the Champs-Elysees by picking locks and riding delivery trucks, tracked prospects with colored incense marks, and was eventually beaten by security guards, arrested, tied to a pole, and driven into a forest fearing he would be executed. The police chief then suddenly turned gentle, apologized, tended his wounds and fed him, revealing that a week earlier the swami had preached at his home, patiently listening to his life story for over an hour and reading the whole Bhagavad-gita with him chapter by chapter, comparing him to Arjuna, so that the former criminal came to believe in God and now read the Gita nightly with his family. Concluding that God had arranged the arrest so the two could meet again, Indradyumna Swami urges devotees to leave their comfort zone, depend on Krishna, and go out on Sankirtan, promising that this opportunity will remain available for ten thousand years.

## Opening: The Sweetness of the Holy Name — and Sweeter Still, Distributing It

- He is "in a little trouble," because he was given one hour: supposed to sing for half an hour and speak for half an hour.
- What to do? The holy name is **"madhur, madhur, madhur"** — so sweet, sweet, sweet — it can't be beat.
- **But even sweeter than chanting the holy name is distributing the holy name.** This sets up the whole theme of the talk.

## Three Towns, Three Rasas (Jiva Goswami's Bhakti-sandarbha)

- In his **Bhakti-sandarbha**, Jiva Goswami describes different moods (*rasas*) in three principal towns associated with Krishna:
  - **Dwaraka** — where Krishna is the king. This is **aishvarya-bhava**, the official/opulent mood of the spiritual world: Krishna is the King and we are His servants. "Long live the king."
  - **Mathura** — Krishna's *Janmabhumi*, where He took birth. This is described as a little more *rasik*, with more intensity of love — more perfect. There is some **madhurya**, some conjugal feeling, with the ladies of Mathura.
    - **Illustration:** When Krishna and Balarama came from Vrindavan into Mathura to fight the wrestlers, on the invitation of **King Kamsa**, all the young ladies of Mathura looked out through the little windows of their homes and palaces, and tears of love for Krishna flowed from their eyes.
  - **Vraja / Vrindavan** — most perfect. The milkmaids of Vrindavan, the **gopis**, have the purest, sweetest, nectarian love for Sri Krishna — **selfless love, everything for Krishna, nothing for me.**
- **Gopi-bhava** is expressed in Lord Chaitanya's **Shikshashtakam (verse 8):** "I know no one but Krishna as my Lord, and He will always remain so — even if He handles me roughly by His embrace, or makes me brokenhearted by going away. He is always my worshipable Lord, unconditionally." (Rendered: *O my Lord, O Sri Krishna, even if You break my heart by not appearing before me, still You will remain my master life after life, and I will always serve You.*)

## Narada Muni and the Dust of the Devotees' Feet

- When Krishna once had a headache (a migraine), the demigods decided that the only thing that would cure it was **the dust from the feet of His devotees.**
- There are so many wonderful devotees throughout creation, so **Narada Muni** went everywhere pleading: "Please, Krishna has a migraine. They say that if you give the dust of your feet for His head, He'll get better."
  - But everyone refused: "No, no — the dust of *my* feet on Krishna's head? That's a great offense; we'll go to hell." (And it's true — that is the correct sentiment.)
- There was just one place left: **Vrindavan.** Narada thought, "That's a good place to go, because there's lots of dust."
  - **What is Vrindavan?** Vrindavan is made of **chintamani**, transcendental touchstone (as described in **Brahma-samhita**). Because there is dust everywhere, you can see the very footprints of Krishna as He walks through the forest with Balarama. When the gopi girls are cleaning the forest area and come across Krishna's footprint, they leave it — they don't dust it away.

## Degrees of Love: The 24-Karat Bhava of the Gopis

- Jiva Goswami describes the depth of love the way we speak of grades of gold — **9-karat, 18-karat, 22-karat, and 24-karat gold.**
- **Analogy:** Husbands, ask your wives whether they want 9-karat gold earrings or 24-karat — of course they'll choose 24-karat. ("Yes? Better get out on Sankirtan," he jokes.)
- The gopis have the **24-karat bhava** for Krishna.

## Three Ways to Chant: Japa, Kirtan, and Sankirtan

- In the same way, there are three ways we chant the holy names:
  1. **Japa** — personal chanting.
  2. **Kirtan** in the temple — congregational chanting with our godbrothers and godsisters.
  3. Going out on the streets to chant, or **Brihat Mridanga** — distributing books.
- Paralleling Dwaraka / Mathura / Vraja:
  - **Japa is perfect.**
  - **Chanting in the temple room with our mates is more perfect.**
  - **Taking it to the people is most perfect.**

## The Foundation: Sixteen Rounds a Day

- In a purport in **Srimad Bhagavatam**, Prabhupada said the **most important instruction of the spiritual master is to chant 16 rounds a day.** That is the foundation of our spiritual life — if you don't build a good foundation, the house will crash down. First thing, no question.
- Chanting the **Maha Mantra** (Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare) — 16 rounds — then you are protected from the influence of the material energy, and you have the inspiration to share mercy with others.
- You can feel that protection and strength when you chant your rounds.
- **Krishna said, "I am the strength of the strong"** (Bhagavad Gita 7.11). The name **Rama** has different connotations, but one of its meanings is **spiritual strength.** By chanting, you gain that strength.

## Sadhana Is the Source of Preaching Power

- **Armed only with the holy name and faith in Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, Prabhupada conquered the world** — he conquered the hearts of everyone. In due course of time you will see it: Prabhupada conquers the hearts of the people of this world through his devotion to the holy name and the instructions of his Guru Maharaj.
- **Story ("Now I can do any damn thing I want"):** One time in Los Angeles, Prabhupada was chanting; when he finished his rounds, he put his beads on the table and said, "Now I can do any damn thing I want to do." (The speaker was personally present.)
  - The point: our preaching directly depends on our **sadhana.**
- If you have good sadhana, it doesn't matter if you're a really shy, introverted person who doesn't feel you can distribute books. You'll have that **tejas** — that spiritual power.
- **People don't buy the book; they buy the book distributor.** They don't know what's in the book, but they like *you*, and on your recommendation, that's how you sell books.

## Story: The Latin American Who Won Every Marathon

- Many years ago in France they held Christmas marathons. In France you have to speak French.
- But every year the same devotee — a Latin American who didn't even speak English, let alone French — would win the marathon, distributing an enormous number of big books in 30 days.
- The speaker asked him, "How do you do it?" The devotee pulled his beads out of his bead bag (and just chanted Hare Krishna).
- The speaker had often watched him in the mornings: every day he chanted his holy names very carefully and completely — whether he was just starting or finishing his rounds, his pronunciation of the holy name was always full.
- He also watched him on Sankirtan: he'd approach, barely say a few words, and people would immediately take out money and buy his books.
- The speaker suspected he had a memorized sales mantra in French and asked what it was. The devotee said, "No. I tell the people, **'Hare Bol.'**"
- Not believing him, the speaker secretly put a microphone in the devotee's kurta one day and later listened to the tape: **"Hare Bol. Hare Bol. Hare, Hare Bol."**
- People loved him because **he was shining like the sun.** Prabhupada said the soul is more brilliant than ten thousand suns rising simultaneously.
- **Lesson:** chant your japa in an ideal, complete way.

## Congregational Chanting Is More Perfect

- Just as mercy increases going from Dwaraka to Mathura, the process of chanting *japa* and then **all of us chanting congregationally together** has an even greater effect. Why?
  - Because you're not just hearing yourself chant — you're hearing five thousand devotees chant.
- What is the effect? **"Ceto-darpana-marjanam"** (Shikshashtakam, verse 1) — the mirror of the heart is cleansed. "Goodbye lust, goodbye anger, goodbye greed — and don't come back."
- That's why **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** said that whenever he heard the tap of the mridanga and the chiming of the kartals — meaning a kirtan was about to start — the joy in his heart knew no limits, no boundaries, and he would run to the kirtan.

## "You Came Running" — Prabhupada's Assurance

- **Story:** One time one of the speaker's godbrothers spoke to Srila Prabhupada, crying: "Prabhupada, I don't know how I'm here. I was so sinful before — you don't know how degraded I was. I caused such pain, which you can't even imagine. But somehow, I'm here."
- Prabhupada said: **"That's okay, because when you heard the chanting of the maha mantra, you came running."**
- It's one of the speaker's favorite Prabhupada stories: "You were what you were, but when you heard the chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra, you came running — and that's your qualification."

---

## The Auspiciousness of Loud Kirtan (the Dust of the Kirtan Party)

- When there is loud chanting of the holy name with many devotees, a great atmosphere of auspiciousness is created.
- We don't comprehend it yet, but these trees are so fortunate to get this kirtan every year — they may make it back to Godhead before us. So auspicious.
- **Story from Chaitanya Bhagavata:** When Mahaprabhu would do kirtan with **Nityananda, Gadadhara, Advaita, and Srivasa,** the devotees would kick up such dancing that they raised a cloud of dust into the air.
  - That dust cloud would be blown out into the countryside and settle on the heads of farmers working the land.
  - **Chaitanya Bhagavata** says the farmers would immediately experience the **eight ecstatic symptoms of love of God**, just from getting the dust of the kirtan party on their heads.

---

## Most Perfect: Taking the Holy Name to the People

- What is more auspicious? **Take it to the people.** That is Mahaprabhu's desire, and when you fulfill the desire of the master, the servant becomes happy.
- Mahaprabhu wanted this chanting — in the form of the kirtan party, or the very potent **Brihat Mridanga** (book distribution) — to go to every town and village of the world. That is the most pure, the most perfect. (Perfect, more perfect, most perfect.)
- **Why most perfect? Because the holy name is a person.**
  - **"Nama chintamani krishnash chaitanya-rasa-vigrahah"** (Padma Purana, quoted in the Chaitanya Charitamrita): there is no difference between the name, fame, form, pastimes, and paraphernalia of Krishna — it is all absolute. **The holy name is Krishna, 100%.**
- And where does the holy name want to go? The holy name wants to **go out and rescue.** Krishna wants to rescue all His lost sheep — they have all left the herd, left the spiritual world — and the holy name wants to reclaim them.
- So if you facilitate that desire of the holy name, you get more mercy than you can probably handle.

---

## The Miracle of the Holy Name / Krishna as Adventure

- Otherwise, why are Sankirtan devotees going out against all odds, with so much penance and austerity? Because they see the **miracle of the holy name**, transforming people practically immediately into their first genuine spiritual experience — feeling the joy of Krishna consciousness. That is the reward for Sankirtan.
- You see people's eyes light up; they smile and say, "Wow — can I keep in contact with you guys?"
- **A miracle is something that contradicts the standard laws of nature.** We hear of it in Christianity (walking on water) and among the yogis (bringing something from a distant place). But Sankirtan devotees see little miracles **every day**, because people buy the book: they came out to *forget* God, and now they're buying a book to *remember* God — just because they met you, the devotee who chanted your good 16 rounds in the morning.
- Krishna says in the Gita, **"I am adventure."** Everyone dreams of adventure, but this is the real adventure: take up your book bag, walk out on the street, and you'll have amazing experiences every day, guaranteed.

## The Twin Book Distributors of Georgia

- He points to a pair of twins in the audience ("Look at the twins — stand up!"), disciples of **Chaitanya Chandra Charan Prabhu.** These girls are "totally mad" for sharing the mercy of Prabhupada in the form of his books.
- They went to **Georgia** (about which he has heard horrific stories), distributing books, and they told him, "Oh, so much nectar! You've got to come there, Maharaj, and distribute books in Georgia."
- He thinks they are two sadhus who took birth in this lifetime just to serve the mission of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
  - Because they are so dedicated, year after year, we should request their guru **Chaitanya Chandra Charan Prabhu** to bless these two girls with unlimited, fathomless mercy — because they will take full advantage of it, going back to Georgia, to Azerbaijan, wherever, distributing books with big smiles on their faces, since there is no higher taste than sharing Krishna consciousness.
- **Analogy of Bhaktisiddhanta and his best disciples:** Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura had a huge number of disciples. When someone brought Srila Prabhupada to be introduced, Bhaktisiddhanta said, "Yes, I know this young man; he listens very attentively to all my lectures, and I will happily initiate him." In the same way, though a guru has many disciples, some — like these two girls — sacrifice everything: they gave up their youth and go out to preach, no matter what, in the most difficult places. They are the best.
- **On association:** Our association is built with exalted devotees, with devotees on the same level, and with junior devotees. We hanker especially for the association of exalted devotees, because they have taste, and by their association we too catch that taste for preaching.

## Association, the Coming Marathon, and Passing the Torch

- The **Christmas marathon** is coming in just a few months. You should already be knocking on your temple president's door, begging him: "I want to go on Sankirtan! I want to go on Sankirtan!"
  - And the temple president will say, "Okay, okay, everyone wait — because I already have 50,000 applications for Sankirtan, all those who attended the Sankirtan festival during the Russian festival." Everyone has equal opportunity. The spiritual world emanates a huge amount of mercy, so **go out, distribute, and obtain mercy.**
- He is getting older now — 70 — and can hardly dance anymore ("or can I?").
- So there will come a time when, if his disciples go out, it's like the father who likes to see the son take over the business: if his disciples go out, then he will relish the Sankirtan mood on the street *through* his disciples. "Please, please, go out."

## A Sankirtan Story from Paris: Into the Fashion Houses

- He now tells a personal Sankirtan story. Much of his own Sankirtan was done in Paris, France.
- Although most of the men went out in pants and shirt, he — a brahmachari at the time — would go out in his **dhoti and kirtan chadar.** He'd make sure to get the garland of **Radha-Paris-Ishvara** every morning (a big garland), shave his head every day, put on a big tilak, and carry his books, incense, and candies.
- **His childhood shyness (backstory):** As a boy he was very shy; his mother would tell him, "Speak up! I can't hear you — what are you mumbling?" But as soon as he joined the temple, they pushed him out onto the street to distribute books, and gradually, day by day — even on Janmashtami — he distributed books 11 years in a row. Nothing makes him as happy as Sankirtan; if it weren't for his present responsibilities, he would go straight back to France, get his old backpack (kept in a disciple's apartment), fill it with books, and go out on the street distributing to the end of his days.
- He always went to the **most difficult places.** Why not just go somewhere easy? Because when you go to the most difficult place and distribute a book, **"Param vijayate Sri Krishna Sankirtanam"** (Shikshashtakam, verse 1) — it is a real victory.
- He'd go to the **Champs-Élysées** and the **Arc de Triomphe** — the big arch and the long street lined with all the fashion houses of Paris. They were all locked, so you couldn't get in to distribute anything. Only the big garage doors would open for delivery trucks, and then the doors would close.
- **The infiltration:** He met some thieves in Paris and asked them how to pick locks ("you can dovetail anything for Krishna"). When a delivery truck slowed down, he'd jump on the back, pick the lock, and climb inside among all the fashion clothes. The truck would drive into the building; before the drivers got out, he'd sneak out and hide in the basement until the truck left.
  - Then he'd take the elevator from the bottom basement floor up to the 15th floor. The elevator would open into a big room where 20 or 30 people were making clothes or doing secretarial work.
  - He'd walk out: "Bonjour, madame, monsieur. Hello — I'm American, I've just come back from India, I'm a monk, and I have the most amazing books." And they'd stare at him.

---

## The Colored-Incense System and the Personal Touch

- In France they like perfume. He carried a big bag of books and a big bag of incense — in those days called **Spiritual Sky incense**, each flavor a different color (yellow, orange, blue, green).
- To get people interested, he'd come around and let them smell the incense, which would leave a colored mark on them, so he'd know he'd already visited that person. He developed a system:
  - **Yellow mark** = the person showed a little interest.
  - **Green mark** = maybe somewhat interested / still in doubt.
  - **Blue mark** = not interested at all; don't go back to them.
- After visiting all 30 people, he knew exactly who wanted what and whom he'd already approached.
- He was a young man, 19-20 years old. He'd chat, converse, and win people over. Within 15-20 minutes almost everyone wanted to invite him to lunch.
  - **"In like a needle, out like a plow."**
  - **"Float like a butterfly, sting like a bee."**
- Then he'd pull out the books. By then they trusted him and would even phone the neighboring floors: "Oh, we've got a cool guy here offering wonderful incense and books." And so he distributed many books.
- **It's the personal touch.** Everything in life comes down to the personal touch — especially in Krishna consciousness, because each of us needs the mercy of Krishna.

---

## Caught by Security

- One day he did the 15th, 14th, 13th, and 12th floors. On the **11th floor**, just about to sell a book, the elevator opened and the **main security chief** for the building walked in.
- Using very bad language, the chief demanded: "What are you doing here? You cannot sell things in this building! You must have made a million dollars on the last three floors above. I'm going to throw you out!" He had huge muscles.
- The speaker said, "No problem, I'll surrender to you" — then ran at him, rolled under his legs, reached the elevator with a big smile on his face, and "ding!"
- Did he go home? **No.** He went to the 2nd floor: "Bonjour, madame, bonjour, monsieur. Je suis un jeune moine — I'm American, I'm a monk, I've just come back from India with this nice incense and book." Then the 3rd floor, the 4th floor.
  - It was about 4 p.m.; he'd been there since 8:30 that morning, with a big bag of money.
  - When he'd come back from Sankirtan, he'd enter the temple room, open the bag, and show it to Radha-Krishna: **"C'est pour vous"** — "This is for You."
- On the **5th floor** — "bing!" — out of the elevator came the security guy again: "I've been looking for you on 15 floors." He had two big guys with him.
- The speaker still had five books left and needed to distribute them past these big guards.
- **Disabling the guard:** He took out his incense. (One way to disable someone who wants to fight is to say something so stupid that he goes, "What?") He asked the big guard, "Are you married?" "What? I'm arresting you." "No, but I want to know if you're married." He gently scratched the guard's hands and said, "I have a present for your wife," and took out the blue incense.
  - Very quickly — boom, boom, boom — he put three packs of incense into their hands, one, two, three. They stood there looking. He ran to the elevator, "bing," and pushed the button.
- He now had five books left and needed to make a little extra money, since he'd given away three free packs of incense.

---

## Arrested and Driven into the Forest

- He debated: 6th, 7th, or 8th floor? He chose the **6th** — it was a mistake.
- On the 6th floor, distributing his last book at a table while a lady looked on — suddenly "boom, boom" on his back. The guards came out of the elevator and got him.
  - It was surely the same chief (still with the blue incense streak under his neck), and the others had the audacity to keep the incense in their back pockets. They were as angry as a disturbed hornet's nest and very rough with him.
- Fortunately he was young and strong. They literally threw him into the elevator (he could easily have broken his neck). As it went down to reception, the big guard stood on his back so he couldn't breathe, and another twisted his arm up very high (to this day, if he raises his arm high, he feels the pain).
- Before taking him out, they slapped and punched him a few times. When the door opened, the three or four receptionists said, "Hey, leave that boy alone!"
- The head security guy ordered: "Call the police immediately. This is a trespasser. I'm going to make sure he spends one year in jail." Then — boom — he punched him and knocked him down. Every time he tried to get up, the guard punched him again, so he just lay on the ground.
- And he started **singing the Nrisimha prayer:** **"Namas te narasimhaya prahladahlada-dayine / hiranyakashipor vakshah-shila-tanka-nakhalaye"** — he sang like that, and he was happy.
- The police wagon arrived — a big van with six policemen — to arrest one young American boy. They brought a long pole and tied him to it like an animal, his hands and feet bound, so he swung from it.
  - As he was carried out, he kept singing "Hare Krishna, Hare..." The guard said, "Get that smile off your face." He didn't stop.
- The security chief kicked him and told the police chief, "You take him to jail — you know what to do with him." The police chief said, "We know what to do with him."
  - **Grim negotiation:** the security guy said, "12 months in jail — we got a deal?" The police chief said, "13 months in jail." (He wasn't smiling then.)
- They threw him in the back of the truck; the police chief was very bullish ("Yeah, we're going to get this guy"), and they drove him **deep into the forest.**
- He got a little scared. The police chief said, "Stop the van. Let's attend to this young man." The doors opened; six policemen. He sat there in the lotus position **like Prahlad Maharaj**, chanting Hare Krishna, and asked, "Can I have a last meal? Can I write a last letter to my mother?" — he seriously thought it was all over.

---

## The Police Chief's Transformation

- The police chief suddenly put out his arms and pushed all the policemen back: **"Step back. Be respectful."**
- Then he came and said, "Please, young man, take my hand. I'm so sorry." Very carefully he picked him up and took him out, then began brushing him off. Seeing a cut on his face where they'd hit him, he called, "Get the first aid kit," had him sit down, and told some of the men to massage him — "Don't do it so hard; he's not strong like us French people, he's an American boy."
- He had food brought from the van: **"Not meat. He's vegetarian."** (How did the police chief know he was a vegetarian?) He cleaned the cut with alcohol and cream. "You still have your teeth." "Yeah, I've got my teeth."
- The incense and bags were strewn in the back of the van; the chief told the men to gather them and put them properly back in his bag. The speaker was "in heaven."
- Another car arrived — the chief's personal car. "I'm going to drive you back to the street." One of the men wanted to come along, but the chief said, "No — it's just me and him." He gave the speaker a bottle of water, and they drove off.

---

## "You Knocked on My Door": The Book That Changed His Life

- As they drove, the chief said, "You don't remember me, do you?" "No — I never met the police chief of Paris." "No, no, take a good look." He looked: "Oh, I remember you! I met you in one of those big apartment buildings last week — I was distributing books."
  - On Sundays they'd go to the apartments and knock on doors. "That's you!" "That's me. You knocked on my door. I wasn't dressed like a police officer — I was in my civilian clothes — and I invited you in."
- The chief said: "I'm not a religious man, but there was just something about you that was interesting. I never met somebody in my life who was so happy. And your clothes — you looked like an angel. And that big flower garland — it wasn't plastic, it was real flowers. And that incense with the yellow mark — you can ask my wife, I didn't wash my face for three days; I just wanted to keep the experience with me."
- "Don't you remember? You spent three hours in my home. I didn't believe in God. I had a rough life — I was a criminal, but I became a policeman. And I told you my whole life story. You sat and listened — no one ever listened to me before. You really cared about me; you listened to me for an hour and a half, my whole life story. Then I asked you for advice, and you gave me the best advice."
- "You opened that book, the **Bhagavad Gita**, and compared me to one person, whose name was **Arjuna.** You took me chapter by chapter for two hours. And — miracle — by the end of your visit, I believed in God. And remember the big donation I gave you? Three hundred dollars."
- The chief said, "Here — here's another two hundred dollars," and gave it to him in the car. "Actually, I can't repay you for what you've done for me and my family. Every night before bed, we read the Bhagavad Gita."
  - To test him, the speaker asked, "What's your favorite chapter?" "The ninth chapter." "But the biggest mistake I made was that I didn't ask for your address so I could meet you again."
- The chief said, "I think God arranged for you today to be captured by that big security man — otherwise I wouldn't have met you. So God arranged for you to get caught." The speaker said, "I don't really know about that" — but the man had become such a spiritual person.
- In those days there were no cell phones or email, so he gave the chief his address, and over the years they exchanged a couple of letters.

## Conclusion: The Street Corner Is Your Temple
