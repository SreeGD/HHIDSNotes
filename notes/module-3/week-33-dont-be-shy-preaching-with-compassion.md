# Don't Be Shy (Preaching with Compassion)

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 33 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:26:28

**Themes:** preaching · compassion · prahlada maharaja · book distribution · sadhana bhakti · sharing good fortune

## Summary

Speaking on Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.44, Indradyumna Swami expounds Prahlada Maharaja's prayer that he does not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside the 'poor fools and rascals,' but wants to bring everyone back to the shelter of the Lord's lotus feet. He praises Prahlada as everyone's hero because he perfected Krishna consciousness as a small boy, and warns that success in bhakti is difficult and time is short: using the image of a swimmer checking a lap-card mid-race, he asks how far along the path of sadhana-bhakti each of us really is after 20, 30, or 50 years. Rather than becoming less active with age, devotees should 'turn up the fire' like a cook rushing a slow preparation, chanting and studying more, not less. Maharaja teaches that natural compassion for the suffering, ignorant living entities is a key symptom of advancement, illustrating it with Prabhupada's analogy of a once-poor man who, on becoming wealthy, freely helps others in poverty because he remembers his own destitution. Because every soul was destitute in material existence, every Vaishnava should feel that same bleeding-hearted compassion and share the wealth of the holy name. He insists no one should be shy about preaching, since qualification comes through the act of preaching itself, and that even hearing this class, cooking cookies for the deity, or simply going out so that others can render one small service (aghatana-ghatana / sukriti) is itself preaching. He cites the movement's growing acceptance by world leaders and closes with a long story from his youth of book distribution on the Champs-Elysees in Paris, where he was chased, arrested, and then quietly freed by a police chief who turned out to have earlier bought a Bhagavad-gita from him door to door and was reading it with appreciation.

## Prahlada Maharaja's Prayer — The Wish to Deliver Others

- The class opens with the prayer of Prahlada Maharaja (**SB 7.9.44**): *"My dear Lord Nrisimhadeva, I see that there are many saintly persons indeed, but they are interested only in their own deliverance. Not caring for the big cities and towns, they go to the Himalayas or the forest to meditate with vows of silence. They are not interested in delivering others. As for me, however, I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals. I know that without Krishna consciousness, without taking shelter of Your lotus feet, one cannot be happy. Therefore, I wish to bring them back to the shelter of Your lotus feet."*

## Prahlada Maharaja — Our Hero

- Prahlada Maharaja is everyone's hero in Krishna consciousness because he was **successful** in Krishna consciousness — and not merely successful, but *glorious* in his devotional service to his spiritual master and Krishna.
- A devotee does not merely want to be successful, "graduate" from this material world, and go back to Godhead. **The real essence of a devotee's heart is to always glorify Guru and Gauranga** — to praise the glories of his spiritual master and the predecessor acharyas, and to always advertise widely, publicly, on a worldwide scale, the glories of Sri Sri Radha and Krishna.

## It Is Not Easy to Be Successful

- We should not take lightly that it is genuinely difficult to be successful in Krishna consciousness. Krishna analyzes our "opposition," Maya:
  - **BG 7.14** (*"daivi hy esha gunamayi mama maya duratyaya"*): this divine energy, consisting of the three modes of material nature, is very difficult to overcome.
  - **BG 7.19** (*"bahunam janmanam ante..."*): after many, many births, one who is in full knowledge surrenders fully to Krishna.
- **Traditional Gaudiya wisdom** (said in Gaudiya circles, though Maharaja has not seen it in shastra): one lifetime in *sadhana*; the next lifetime in *raganuga* (spontaneous) devotional service; the third lifetime in pure devotional service; the fourth, you go to the planet where the Lord is performing His pastimes in your universe; then, trained up in a specific service, you go back home, back to Godhead. But this holds only if you are successful in *sadhana-bhakti* in one lifetime — a great challenge in itself.

## Anartha-nivritti — Have We Made Progress?

- Sadhana-bhakti brings us to the stage of **anartha-nivritti** — purifying the heart of all unwanted things (lust, anger, greed, etc.).
- A sobering self-examination: some are a quarter, some half, some at the "fag end" of life. Have you come to the stage of anartha-nivritti? Have you perfected sadhana-bhakti so that next lifetime you can take birth as a *raganuga* practitioner? Or will it be 1–10 births as a *sadhaka*, then *bhava*, then *prema*?
- **This material world is "a very nasty place"** (Prabhupada). Story: an enthusiastic disciple told Srila Prabhupada, "I love this book distribution so much, I'd like to come back birth after birth to serve you by distributing your books." Prabhupada's eyes got very big and he said in surprise, **"No, don't come back. It's a very terrible place, this material world. It's Kali Yuga."** The sentiment of serving life after life is nice, but the spiritual master's desire is that his disciple **go back to the spiritual world and engage in *nitya-seva*** — eternal service to the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna.

## The Transcendental Contract of Initiation

- At the moment of initiation there is a kind of **transcendental contract** between the spiritual master and the disciple:
  - The **disciple** promises: "My dear Guru Maharaja, I will follow the four regulative principles, chant my 16 rounds, study Prabhupada's books, and render some service."
  - The **spiritual master's** part is to **deliver the disciple all the way back home, back to Godhead** — "a veritable burden of love."
- So although the sentiment "I'll take birth again to serve you" is nice, Prabhupada said, "No, this Kali Yuga is so bad — go back in this lifetime." To do so, one must become free from all unwanted qualities in the heart *and* awaken love for God in the heart. But **time is short.** How far are we on the path?

## The Swimming Analogy — Which Lap Are We On?

- Maharaja recalls competitive swimming in high school — the **200-meter freestyle**, which he thinks was 16 laps.
- As you swim, pacing yourself but going as hard as possible, officials place a little card in the water so you can see your lap count (lap 2, lap 4, lap 6, lap 8) and gauge yourself. In the intensity of the race you might lose count, so the card is helpful.
- **Imagine** you are putting in maximum effort, three-quarters through the race, and the card says you have only done *two* laps (when you have actually done twelve or fourteen). After all that effort, only getting credit for two laps — that would be so discouraging you might climb out of the water and quit.
- Likewise: imagine practicing Krishna consciousness for 20, 30, 40, or 50 years, then looking at the gauge — *"How far am I?"* — "I'm still trying to chant 16 rounds a day, still struggling with the regulative principles, I hardly ever read." **Only getting credit for two laps after 50 years.** It is almost too late.

## A Time and Place for Everything

- Kali Yuga is "topsy-turvy" — you hear of an old man of 70 or 75 marrying his third or fourth wife. But marriage belongs between about 18 and 30; you don't do that at 75 or 80.
- **Ecclesiastes:** "A time to be born, a time to die, a time to laugh, a time to cry" — there is a time and a place for everything.
- **Prahlada Maharaja taught that Krishna consciousness ideally begins at the tender age of five** (*kaumara acaret prajno*). Most of us came at 25 or 35 — but that is okay, a concession is made in Kali Yuga. If we practice sincerely — rigorously chanting Hare Krishna, following the principles, associating with devotees, studying the shastras, rendering service — we should see ourselves making progressive advancement.

## Turn Up the Fire — Become More Active, Not Less

- **Cooking analogy:** if the preparation is cooking slowly and the guests arrive in half an hour, you turn up the gas, stir so it doesn't burn, and it cooks quicker. Likewise, if we see we are advancing slowly, we should "turn up the fire."
- It should *not* be that as we get older in Krishna consciousness we become *less* active. As we grow older we should become *more* active in the very activities that help us progress. We should not chant more when we joined and less as the years go on — we should chant more and more.
- Maharaja knows four or five godbrothers (whom he won't name to spare their embarrassment) who **chant 64 rounds every day** despite great managerial duties as sannyasis or gurus. So it is possible.

## Prahlada's Head Start — Hearing in the Womb

- Prahlada is our hero because he perfected Krishna consciousness even as a young boy — a **perfected stage at five years old**, purity of body — which should give us hope.
- He got a head start by practicing Krishna consciousness **in the womb.** But there is no reference to Prahlada cooking offerings, traveling to holy places, or chanting *japa* on his beads in the womb. What was his devotional service there?
- **The nine limbs of devotional service** (*navadha-bhakti*, SB 7.5.23, spoken by Prahlada himself): *shravanam, kirtanam, vishnoh smaranam, pada-sevanam, archanam, vandanam, dasyam, sakhyam, atma-nivedanam.* His service was the first — **shravanam, hearing** — just as the audience is doing right now.

## Hearing Is Devotional Service Now — Not Mere Preparation

- Do not think, "I'm hearing now *so that* I can perform devotional service later." **This hearing is itself devotional service** — one of the nine limbs, and in fact one of the more potent limbs: to sit and hear *Hari-katha.*
- Krishna stresses the hearing process with two important words in Bhagavad-gita: **"Tat srinu"** — "O Arjuna, listen to this message that I am speaking." Because the mind tends to wander and attention spans are short (and "elegance is truth spoken concisely"), Krishna says, in effect, "This message is so important, there's so little time — listen, I'm speaking to you." (Like Maharaja's own mother: "Son, I'm speaking to you — listen to me.")
- **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakur**, considering the young Srila Prabhupada as a candidate for initiation, accepted him, saying: **"He hears nicely."**
- So Prahlada got his head start hearing about Krishna consciousness from his spiritual master (Narada Muni) in the womb of his mother.
- **"But he's just an embryo — what can an embryo understand?"** — It is *shabda-brahma*, transcendental sound. As Gopiparana-dhana Prabhu said, it enters the ear, goes to the heart, and **cleanses** — sound cleanses, like medicine.
  - **Medicine analogy:** when you take medicine, do you know *how* it works? No — you have faith in the prescription and in the doctor, take it religiously, and it cures you. So even if we don't yet have a taste for Hari-katha, we still come and hear; even if we don't have a taste for the holy name, we still chant — understanding it is like medicine, and gradually the *ruchi* (taste) will come.

## The Prayer of the Child in the Womb

- From Srimad Bhagavatam: the child in the womb, around seven months old, becomes conscious and prays to the Lord: *"My dear Lord, here I am again in the womb of another mother. I promise You, this time when I get out, I'll take up devotional service and finish up this material [existence]."*
- **That was your first prayer.** You have said many prayers since, but do you remember your first? As soon as we took birth, the allurement of the material energy overwhelmed us with the false promise of material happiness, and we forgot the prayer.
- The point: an advanced level of consciousness is present even at seven months — enough to pray to God. And if you could compose such a prayer from your heart, it is equally conceivable that you could *hear* the message of Godhead from the Lord's representative. It is beyond logic and reasoning how these things work, but we all have experience of the potency of transcendence to elevate us beyond the modes of nature.

## Prahlada's Symptom of Advancement — Compassion

- One of Prahlada's symptoms of advancement (as discussed that afternoon at the **Sankirtan seminar**) was that he developed a **natural compassion for the ignorant state of all other living entities** in this material world.
- Two signs one is making genuine advancement before death: (1) becoming free from unwanted qualities in the heart, and (2) developing love of God. Here is one more symptom: **our heart bleeds to see the sufferings of others devoid of Krishna consciousness.**

## Prabhupada's Analogy — The Poor Man Who Becomes Rich

- Prabhupada's analogy: a man who has experienced extreme **poverty**, then by destiny (by *karma* — some pious activity in a distant past life, not by devotional service) becomes extremely **wealthy.** Because he himself knew the misery of poverty, he has a natural sympathy for those in the same condition and liberally gives his money to them.
  - **Statistic:** Maharaja read that 68% of the world's population goes to bed hungry every night. Most of us have probably never experienced the pangs of hunger (the pain when the muscles and secretions get no nourishment), yet over half the world does.
  - **Krishna's protection — BG 9.22** (*"yoga-kshemam vahamy aham"*): "For My devotee, I protect what he has and provide whatever he needs, so he can go on with his devotional service." In our movement we are fortunate from every point of view — physical, emotional, intellectual, and spiritual.
- The analogy does not say *every* wealthy person is compassionate — but generally those who themselves suffered and are now rich have a magnanimous nature to uplift others.

## Everyone in Krishna Consciousness Was Destitute

- In Krishna consciousness, *everyone* should have that magnanimous nature, because **all of us were destitute in this material world** — whether we were rich or poor, famous or infamous, sick or healthy. It is all bad.
- **Kaviraja Gosvami:** the very concept of "good and bad" in the material world is wrong — it is all bad. Even what you think is good is still within the material world, which means birth, disease, old age, and death. What can be good about that?
- **Ice cream / axe analogy:** material life is like enjoying a big dish of ice cream with caramel, whipped cream, nuts, and a cherry — while a heavy axe, sharp as a razor, hangs directly over your head on a single thin string tied to the ceiling. Would you feel comfortable? That is material life: death can come at any moment.
- **Narottama Das Thakur:** "Here I am in the middle of a holocaust, trying to enjoy myself."

## The Greatest Wealth — and What Money Cannot Buy

- We have come to Krishna consciousness and possess the greatest wealth. Our wealth is our **eagerness to chant Hare Krishna** — and with it we can obtain *prema*, pure love of God.
- But even **Bill Gates**, number one on the list of the wealthy, cannot buy love. Even the **Beatles**, so wealthy in their youth, sang **"Money can't buy me love."** (Maharaja jokes that one of the *anarthas* we have to get out of the heart is the words to all the old Beatles songs.)
- Because all of us were destitute in material life — rich or poor, intelligent or not — we all realized our fallen condition. Now, possessing this greatest wealth (the holy name), we should feel compassion for everyone who lacks Krishna consciousness.

## Every Vaishnava Should Have a Bleeding Heart

- Not just a few of us, like a few wealthy people feeling compassion for the poor. **Every Vaishnava should have a bleeding heart.**
- We are all **followers of Prahlada Maharaja, the *acharya*** — and *acharya* means "one who teaches by example." Every single detail of his life we are meant to imbibe in our own lives.
- **Prabhupada summarized Prahlada's mood:** a Vaishnava's despair (if a transcendental person can feel any despair) is to see other living entities bereft of Krishna consciousness; and his joy is to see other people taking their first steps on the path of Krishna consciousness. **That is the highest pleasure in Krishna consciousness.**
- Even **Rasika-shekhara** — Krishna Himself, who relishes the mellows (*rasa*) of devotional service — comes as **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** to relish the distribution of the holy names in Kali Yuga. **If it is good enough for God, it should be good enough for us.**

## "What Kind of Vaishnava Are You?" — Preaching Develops the Qualities

- **Bhaktivinoda Thakur's essay, "What Kind of Vaishnava Are You?"** — am I a beginner, intermediate, or advanced? "To be honest, Maharaja, I don't have any compassion for all those rascals out there." — **It's okay, you still preach — because by preaching, the qualifications come.**
- Story from the early 1970s in America: a new devotee told the temple president, "I'm not advanced enough to go out and preach Krishna consciousness." The temple president's memorable answer: **"No — that's how you *become* advanced: by preaching."**
- **Along with the instruction of the spiritual master comes the ability to fulfill it.** So no one should be shy in the business of preaching Krishna consciousness.

## Don't Be Shy — In Prasadam, in Business, in Sharing Good Fortune

- Prabhupada (quoting traditional wisdom): **one should never be shy in taking prasadam or in doing business.**
  - When nice prasadam comes, don't say, "I'm too fallen to eat that prasadam" — it's *mercy.*
  - In business, don't be too shy: "This is a really nice product, please just buy it."
- Likewise, **don't be shy in sharing your good fortune with others.** Even if it is a little difficult, awkward, or inconvenient — look at the result. **Look in the mirror: you are the result of someone's attempt to share their good fortune with others.** Had that devotee, or the Sankirtan party, or the festival not gone out, you might not be sitting here today.
- It is hard, and we're shy, uncomfortable associating with non-devotees — but make the effort anyway; the result is well worth it.

## This Is a Missionary Movement

- We must keep preaching, otherwise the spirit of the movement dies. **This is a missionary movement; the missionary spirit is the fire that keeps everything going**, and everyone must accept the responsibility to expand it — the spiritual principle that gives life, joy, and happiness to us, our family, the community, the country, and the world.
- Story: a Sankirtan devotee told Prabhupada, "It's just so hellish out there today." Prabhupada replied, **"If it wasn't for this Krishna conscious movement, you can't imagine how hellish it would be out there."** Directly or indirectly, everyone is being touched and influenced by the mercy of the Sankirtan movement. Have faith in that.

## Leaders of Society Taking Note

- **Mr. Putin:** offered a glass of wine at a political event, he humorously said, "No, I'm a Krishnaite — I don't drink, I don't take intoxication." Though joking, he respected the principles and knew about Krishna. ("Yes, we'll make you an honorary Krishnaite — that is your most important title.")
- **Mr. Tony Blair** (Prime Minister of England): when the difficulties devotees faced in **Kazakhstan** were brought to his attention, he sent a personal note to the president of Kazakhstan saying the situation should be handled in a better way. He spoke up.
- **Nelson Mandela:** when he was president of South Africa (still one of the five most respected leaders in society), he came as special guest to our **Durban festival for 60,000 children.** Scheduled for 20 minutes, he spent **four hours** with us, and on leaving told Maharaja, **"Maharaja, this has been the very best day of my life."**
- **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati's desire:** "One day we will see that Krishna consciousness is successful when the judges in the high courts are wearing Vaishnava *tilak*." That was his way, in his time, of saying that when the leaders of society appreciate and assist our movement, it is a sign of advancement. This desire of our Param Guru is being fulfilled — even in small ways, the leaders are taking note, and that is significant.

## Many Hands, and the Dripping Water

- How does all this happen? **When "little old you and me" play our humble part** in helping to spread the Sankirtan movement. "Many hands make light work."
- It is difficult to preach; we have good opposition. But like a **hard stone worn through by water:** a little drip of water splashing on the same spot over hundreds of thousands or millions of years wears a hole right through the rock. In the same way, if we keep making our effort in our own "little insignificant way," we will see changes in society and be counted as part of this great historical movement.
- Who will note that we are part of this historical movement? Not journalists or magazine writers — **we will be noticed by Guru and Krishna**, and blessed by them, because sharing one's good fortune (as Prahlada is demonstrating in this chapter) is very dear to our acharyas.

## What Liberated Souls Do When They Come Here

- The acharyas have their *nitya-lila*, their eternal pastimes with the Lord in the spiritual world. When they come to *this* world, they don't perform the same lilas — they don't stay in Vrindavan grinding sandalwood and stringing garlands for the Deity. **They are out, like you and me, preaching Krishna consciousness.** This is what liberated souls do when they come to this world. Help them in their mission, and they will be so pleased with you and shower you with blessings.
- Back to the scale — a quarter, half, three-quarters through life: "I'm only on lap two — I'm supposed to be on lap 16 — what am I going to do? **Preach.**"

## Everyone Can Preach — Ajnata-sukriti

- It is not only the ideal, "full-grown" brahmachari — shaved head, tilak, saffron, sitting straight with books in his hands — who is the preacher.
  - A **Babushka** can make lots of cookies, offer them to the Deity, and distribute them to the neighborhood children — she is also preaching.
  - Or simply get out of the house and walk around, and people will do something for you and thereby receive **ajnata-sukriti** — unknowing spiritual benefit — by helping you across the road, filling up your car with petrol ("I have a bad leg." — "Okay, I'll do it, sir."), and so on. Don't be a couch potato at home; give people an opportunity to do something for you, and they'll make some spiritual advancement. **Everyone can preach in Krishna consciousness.**

## The Champs-Élysées Story

- To illustrate, Maharaja tells of doing Sankirtan (book distribution) in the offices of the famous **Champs-Élysées** in Paris, France — a street no devotee had ever "broken through" to work.
- **How he got in:** he figured out to go to the back of the building where cars enter the underground parking, sneak in running behind a car as the garage door opened, hide, then slip through the locked inner door behind someone with a proper key. He would take the elevator to floor 13, walk out to 20 secretaries, and announce: **"Hi everybody, I'm from America, I've just come back from India, and I have all these nice books — how many of you would like to look? And I also have incense too."** It worked every time. It even helped that he didn't speak much French (they had sympathy), and he made a striking sight: freshly shaved head, big Ramanuja-style tilak, the garland from Radha-Paris-Ishvara, silk, pointed shoes, and a flashy bag.
- **The chase:** one day on floor 13, with secretaries buying packs of incense and a few Bhagavad-gitas going out, the elevator doors opened and the **chief of security** appeared, screaming in "very bad French" ("the first words I learned"). Told to get out, Maharaja said he just had to change a lady's bill and give her the book. He grabbed his things and ran; the big, fat security chief chased him around the desks. Maharaja tricked him — ran into the elevator, and as the chief ran for the closing doors, the doors caught the chief's foot; Maharaja pushed "open," the chief fell back, then pushed "close" and rode to floor 10. ("Why give up when you're ahead?")
- The chief went down to reception expecting Maharaja to flee the building. Maharaja instead got about 45 minutes on the next floor, selling almost all his books and incense — "I was in ecstasy." The chief reappeared; another chase; Maharaja went down the stairs to floor 7, selling his last Bhagavad-gita on the floor while the chief was pulling his hair out. This time the chief lifted him up, carried him, threw him in the elevator, put his foot on him, and rode him down to reception.
- **The arrest:** the chief called the police. Embarrassed at having been chased all over the building, he made a big production of it, gathering 20–30 witnesses from other floors ("Yes, this was the boy") and reporting a criminal who broke in and stole money. Three police cars pulled up.
  - Feeling uncomfortable sitting on the ground, Maharaja stood up; the chief took a big swing that grazed the top of his head; Maharaja lost his balance and fell, and the big man sat on him.
  - About 15 policemen came in, angry because the chief had hyped them up on the phone. The **local police chief** arrived first, saw the situation, paused, and stared at Maharaja — then got really angry, flipped him over, and put on handcuffs. They carried him out by his hands and feet "like an animal." The security chief demanded he get "three, four, six months in jail"; the police chief said, "Don't worry, I'll make sure that boy does one year — trespassing, stealing money, scaring the people."
- **The twist:** they threw him in a wagon truck; four police cars raced off with sirens for "little old me and my empty book bag." Then the convoy pulled over about two kilometers from the building. The police chief got out, told the other cars to go ahead to the station, and had the driver wait up front. He came around the back, opened the door, looked at Maharaja — who feared for his life — then **unlocked the handcuffs, gathered up Maharaja's scattered things (including the money), put them in the bag, helped him out of the van, and said, "You're free to go. I really appreciate the work you're doing."**
  - "You don't remember me?" The week before, Maharaja had come **door-to-door with the Bhagavad-gita** to the chief's house; the chief's wife had answered and invited him in, the chief came downstairs, they had a little talk, and the chief gave a nice donation for a Bhagavad-gita, which he was now enjoying reading ("a little hard to understand, but I'm getting the point").
  - "But you didn't tell me you were the police chief!" — "Well, don't tell everybody I'm the police chief. You came to my house, you were my guest, and I bought the book. Now go, quickly." He put out his hand: **"Good luck, young man."**
- **Worth it all:** for Maharaja, the whole ordeal was worth it just to see the chief of police express heartfelt appreciation for the work and say "good luck, young man." "I'd do it all over again just for that." So don't deny yourself this golden opportunity of going out and experiencing the mercy of Lord Chaitanya.

## The Point of the Story — Ajnata-sukriti Again

- The point: Maharaja was just out there, and *that man did devotional service.* The police chief bought a book, but he also did **ajnata-sukriti.** If you are out there doing devotional service and people assist you in any way because you are a devotee, they get some spiritual benefit.

## The Conclusion of the Story

- Maharaja walked the two kilometers back to the Champs-Élysées, dusted himself off, put his bag back on, and walked. Passing the building, he saw the security guard standing inside, looking out the window. As Maharaja walked by, the guard became furious and came running out to the door — but realizing he could not attack an innocent pedestrian on the street, he just stood there. Maharaja started **dancing down the street**, chanting: *"Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare."*

## Closing Praise

- The lecture closes with jaya: **"...Srimad Bhagavatam ki jaya! Sri Prahlada Maharaja ki jaya! Srila Prabhupada ki jaya! Sri Krishna Sankirtan Yajna ki jaya! Gaura-premanande — Hari Haribol!"**

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.44 — the verse under discussion; Prahlada Maharaja's prayer that he will not accept liberation alone but wishes to deliver the 'poor fools and rascals' back to the Lord's lotus feet.
- Bhagavad-gita 7.14 — 'daivi hy esa gunamayi mama maya duratyaya'; the divine energy of the three modes is very difficult to overcome (paraphrased as Krishna analyzing 'our opposition,' maya).
- Bhagavad-gita 7.19 — 'bahunam janmanam ante'; after many births one in full knowledge surrenders fully to Krishna.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.22 — 'yoga-kshemam vahamy aham'; for His devotee the Lord carries what he lacks and preserves what he has.
- Bhagavad-gita — 'tac chrinu / tat srinu' (rendered 'listen, I am speaking to you'), cited as Krishna urging Arjuna to hear; likely BG 7.1 ('...tac chrinu').
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23–24 — the nine processes of devotional service (navadha-bhakti): 'sravanam kirtanam vishnoh smaranam pada-sevanam / archanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam'; noted that Prahlada performed sravanam in the womb.
