# Importance of Initation

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 49 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:00:52

**Themes:** initiation · serving the guru · holy name · material world as prison · brahminical qualities · kali-yuga mercy · chanting

## Summary

Speaking at an initiation ceremony in Dallas (eight devotees receiving first initiation and eight receiving second), Indradyumna Swami builds his talk on Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.28, where Prahlada Maharaja, offered any benediction by the Lord, instead begs to keep serving his spiritual master Narada Muni. This shows the central principle that one must first serve and please the guru, through whose mercy the seed of devotion (bhakti-lata-bija) is awakened and Krishna's mercy then follows. He describes initiation as a second, spiritual birth, contrasting it with ordinary birth, which is into ignorance because the soul abandoned Krishna and the eternal spiritual world for the temporary, suffering material world, likened to Durga-dham, a prison walled in by birth, disease, old age, and death, with the material body as the prisoner's uniform. The seed of bhakti lies dormant in every heart until the guru activates it and authorizes the disciple to chant Hare Krishna; chanting even before initiation frees one from sin and material desire, but attaining prema, pure love of God, requires receiving the mantra in bona fide disciplic succession. Because a genuine brahmana cannot tolerate the pain of material existence, he seeks a solution and takes shelter of a spiritual master, holding onto the guru's lotus feet, inquiring submissively and applying his instructions (pariprasnena sevaya). Meeting such a sadhu is extremely rare, and in Kali-yuga the guru mercifully comes to find the fallen soul, as Srila Prabhupada came to the West. The most essential instruction is the chanting of the holy name, which the Swami calls Kali-yuga's special mercy, a permanent sale of the holy name that yields in this age what meditation, sacrifice, and temple worship gave in previous ages. He closes by urging the initiates never to lose sight of the goal, the day when a tear of love for Krishna flows down the cheek, quoting Rupa Goswami that the holy names create a jubilant festival in the mouth and material desires no longer appear beautiful.

## The Verse: Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.28

- This morning's reading is from **Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 7, Chapter 9** ("Prahlada Pacifies the Lord with Prayers"), text 28.
- **SB 7.9.28** — *"evaṁ janaṁ nipatitaṁ prabhavāhi-kūpe / kāmābhikāmam anu yaḥ prapatan prasaṅgāt / kṛtvātma-sāt surarṣiṇā bhagavan gṛhītaḥ / so 'haṁ kathaṁ nu visṛje tava bhṛtya-sevām"*
- **Translation:** "My dear Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, because of my association with material desires, one after another, I was gradually falling into a blind well full of snakes, following the general populace. But Your servant Narada Muni kindly accepted me as his disciple and instructed me how to achieve this transcendental position. Therefore, my first duty is to serve him. How, then, could I ever leave his service?"

## The Purport: Serve the Spiritual Master First

- Even though Prahlada Maharaj was directly offered all the benedictions he might desire, he refused to accept them from the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Instead, he asked the Lord to engage him in the service of His servant, Narada Muni. **This is the symptom of a pure devotee** — one should serve the spiritual master first, and not bypass the spiritual master to serve the Supreme Lord directly. That is not the principle for a Vaishnava.
- **Narottama Dasa Thakura** says: *"tāṅdera caraṇa sevi bhakta-sane vāsa / janame janame haya, ei abhilāṣa"* — one should not be anxious to offer direct service to the Lord.
- Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu advised that one become the servant of the servant of the servant of the Lord: **"gopī-bhartuḥ pāda-kamalayor dāsa-dāsānudāsaḥ"** — this is the process for approaching the Supreme Lord. The first service should be rendered to the spiritual master, so that by his mercy one can approach the Supreme Lord to render service.
- While teaching Rupa Goswami, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu said: **"guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja"** — one achieves the seed of devotional service by the mercy of the guru and then by the mercy of Krishna. This is the secret of success: first please the spiritual master, then attempt to please the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- Srila Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura also says: **"yasya prasādād bhagavat-prasādo…"** — one should not attempt to please the Supreme Lord by concoction; one must first be prepared to serve the spiritual master, and when qualified, is automatically offered the platform of direct service to the Lord.
- Therefore Prahlada Maharaj proposed to engage in the service of Narada Muni, never proposing to engage directly in the service of the Lord. This is the right conclusion. He said, *"so 'haṁ kathaṁ nu visṛje tava bhṛtya-sevām"* — "How can I give up the service of my spiritual master, who has favored me in such a way that I am now able to see You face to face?" He prayed to continue to engage in the service of his spiritual master, Narada Muni.

## Today's Sixteen Initiates and the "Second Birth"

- Today, eight devotees are being accepted as first initiates into the **Brahma-Gaudiya Vaishnava Sampradaya**, and second initiation is being granted to another eight devotees — sixteen devotees are taking their vows of initiation.
- After the *"ajñāna-timirāndhasya…"* guru-pranama ("I was born into the darkness of material existence, but my spiritual master has opened my eyes with the torchlight of transcendental knowledge; I offer my most respectful obeisances unto his lotus feet") and the Panca-tattva invocation:
- **Srila Prabhupada** describes this initiation ceremony as like a **second birth.**
  - Our first birth is from the womb of our mother, by dint of our father impregnating the mother. Generally, in 99.9% of cases, that is a birth into ignorance.
  - Why ignorance? Because we left Krishna to come to this material world, which is temporary, full of ignorance and suffering.

## Logic: Give Up Something Only for Something Better — and Our "Original Sin"

- Logic says you should only give up something if you get something better. That is actually a quote from the **Niti Shastra of Chanakya Pandit** — an intelligent person only gives up something if he gets something better.
  - Example: If I have a hundred dollars and you have ten, and I offer to make an exchange, you'd happily accept — I give you the hundred, you give me the ten. But if I have the ten and you have the hundred, and we switch, that's bad news for you.
- But we did the opposite in what the Christians call our "cardinal sin" / "original sin": we gave up Krishna.
  - We gave up the spiritual world (eternal, full of knowledge and bliss) for the material world (temporary, full of ignorance and suffering).
  - We gave up our freedom to serve the Lord of our heart to come to "this nasty place," the material world.
- Prabhupada was very eloquent ("Elegance is truth spoken concisely"). He said, **"This material world is no place for a lady or a gentleman."** That statement struck a chord in the speaker's heart: "Okay, I accept."

## The Material World as Durga-Dham: A Prison

- Shastra describes this material world as **Durga-Dham.** *Dham* means place; *Durga* is personified as Mayadevi (mentioned the previous day), and *durga* also means a fort/prison. So the material world is compared to a prison.
- Outside it is a beautiful day (Memorial Day weekend in Dallas, Texas). But through the eyes of Shastra — because a sadhu sees things not through his own imperfect vision, but by hearing the words of Guru, Shastra, and Sadhu — the reality is that it is a prison.
- **The four walls of the prison:** A prison is characterized by four large walls and maximum security — in old prisons so high you couldn't climb over. In this world there are four insurmountable walls that no one can overcome, even by science, technology, or advanced medicine: **birth, disease, old age, and death.** Whatever material qualification we have, no one to date, and no one in the future, will ever conquer those walls by any mundane process. "We're in jail."

## Story: The Brazilian Prison and the Prison Uniform

- On the video of the Brazilian tour, they did a program in one of the worst prisons in Brazil — about 120 of the most hardened criminals: murderers, rapists, extortionists, kidnappers, torturers. They had to pass through many gates to get in; it was maximum security, no way out.
- In the same way, we are like prisoners. Prisoners are identified by a particular dress — uniforms — and there is a psychology behind that: if they try to escape, they can be spotted easily. (In the American penitentiary system: bright orange suits now; formerly the black-and-white stripes with the ball and chain.) If a prisoner escaped and went downtown in Dallas — "whoa, it's either a Hare Krishna or a prisoner."
- When we left the spiritual world and came to the prison house of material existence, we also got a prison suit. "Just look in the mirror — there it is." It's not orange or striped; **it's the material body.**
  - The material body is the sign of our independence from God — nothing to be proud about.
  - A prisoner does not parade his orange suit with his number on the back — that would be foolish. But we foolishly parade, very proud of this material body.
  - Actually it is a sign of our independence, our rebellion against God, our rejection of God — "nothing more, nothing less."

## Honoring a Vaishnava's Spiritual Birthday, Not the Material One

- There is nothing wonderful about taking birth in this world. A Vaishnava/Vaishnavi observes their birthday with sobriety.
- In true Vedic culture, on someone's birthday they would observe the day with penance and austerity, and give charity.
- In ISKCON we do want to honor the Vaishnavas, so we observe a devotee's birthday (though "I don't really know if we did it when Prabhupada observed it" — it has become a tradition).
  - It would be better to honor a devotee on the day they take their **spiritual birth.** Prabhupada gave a lecture entitled **"Deathlessness Begins at Initiation."**
  - This is the day to honor a Vaishnava — when they take that determined vow to give up material existence altogether and become fully surrendered devotees of the Lord. "This is my birthday."
- So those taking initiation today: tomorrow, if someone asks your age, you can say "I'm one day old" — or better, "one day young; one day on the path to eternity."

## The End Result: Judging by the Fruit (Phalena Paricīyate)

- What is the end result of this second birth? Judge by the result.
- The end result of the previous (material) birth is described by the Lord in Bhagavad Gita: **"jātasya hi dhruvo mṛtyuḥ"** — "For one who takes his birth, death is certain," and for one who has died, birth is certain. This is the endless cycle of *samsara*, the repetition of birth and death. (*Phalena paricīyate* — you judge something by its result.)
- But the end result of the birth these initiates are taking today: **back home, back to Godhead.**

## The Spiritual Father Activates the Seed of Bhakti

- Bengali proverb: **"janame janame sabe pitā mātā pāya, kṛṣṇa guru nāhi mile bhaja hari ei"** — everyone in this world gets a mom and a dad, but if one gets the benediction of Krishna and Guru, one can conquer the material energy and return back home, back to Godhead, by serving the Lord.
- Just as the material father places the seed of life into the womb of the mother, so the spiritual father gives us the opportunity for spiritual life by planting — or, more precisely, **activating** — the seed of devotional service in our hearts at the moment of initiation.
  - The **bhakti-lata-bija** is there eternally within our hearts; it just becomes covered by many layers of material consciousness. The spiritual master gives the process to awaken the seed through the watering process of chanting Hare Krishna.
  - **"jīvera svarūpa haya kṛṣṇera nitya-dāsa"** — everyone is an eternal servant of Krishna. Sometimes you can't appreciate it in a germ, a bug, a wolf, or a demoniac person, but it's there; it just has to be activated. It is dormant.

## Analogy: Dormant Seeds in the Store

- Seeds sit in the warehouse or store in little cute packets. When spring comes and you want to plant a garden, you buy a packet. Those seeds may have been sitting there for years — nothing happens, they don't grow.
- But by nature's way, if you follow the authorized process — plant them in the ground in spring and water them — lo and behold, within a few weeks or months the seed germinates.
- So the seed of bhakti is there in our hearts but dormant. At the moment of initiation, the spiritual master blesses us and authorizes us to chant Hare Krishna and get the full fruit of Krishna consciousness.

## Why Initiation Is Essential: From Freedom-from-Sin to Prema

- Before initiation, one can chant Hare Krishna and get many benefits: freedom from sinful reactions; material desires can be subdued or even destroyed to the root.
- But to get **prema**, love of God — the ultimate goal of life — you must receive the mantra from a spiritual master in proper disciplic succession. Therefore initiation is essential.
- Getting free from sinful reactions and uprooting material desires is like getting rid of a disease. **More important is the active life after the hospital:** you get free from the disease in the hospital — that's something — but now leave and live the real life.
- All glory to the holy name, which destroys sinful karma and uproots material desires. But the real glory (discussed in Chaitanya-caritamrita, in reference to **Haridasa Thakura**) is that the holy name **awakens ecstatic love for God.** To get that, you have to get the mantra at your initiation. It is a transcendental phenomenon — you may not see it during the whole process, but an amazing transformation is taking place in the heart. You are authorized to chant Hare Krishna to achieve the ultimate goal of life: prema.

## The Goal Never to Be Forgotten: The Tears of Separation

- **Shikshashtaka 7** — *"yugāyitaṁ nimeṣeṇa cakṣuṣā prāvṛṣāyitam / śūnyāyitaṁ jagat sarvaṁ govinda-viraheṇa me"* — "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain. I am feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence." **This is the goal.**
- We should never lose sight of the goal of Krishna consciousness amid all the various aspects of the process: that one day we will sit down and chant, and a tear will manifest from our eye due to the purification of the heart, and roll down our cheek. This is the perfection of life — "nothing more, nothing less."

## Wandering the Universes Until Causeless Mercy

- **"brahmāṇḍa bhramite kona bhāgyavān jīva, guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja"** — since the living entity left the spiritual world, he has been aimlessly wandering through material existence from one *brahmanda* (universe) to another, one species of life, one body to another.
- But if somehow he gets causeless mercy and comes in contact with Guru and Krishna, he can activate the seed of devotional service and go back home, back to Godhead. **That is the greatest fortune.**

## In Kali-Yuga, the Guru Finds Us

- Knowing this, intelligent people in Vedic culture would naturally search out a bona fide spiritual master. Prabhupada said that in a civilized (Aryan) culture, everyone has a bona fide spiritual master — everyone has a guru, knowing the ultimate goal of life. Parents would direct the student to the gurukula; and if that were absent at some point, one would make the effort to find the shelter of a bona fide Vaishnava guru.
- But this is Kali-yuga, and we have forgotten all these things — so the spiritual master very kindly finds us. No one asked Srila Prabhupada to come to the West; on the order of his guru and out of his infinite compassion, he came looking for us.
- **Gurangi Dasi** (a disciple of Sivarama Maharaj), realizing how everything is "vice versa, topsy-turvy, upside down" in Kali-yuga, said in her Vyasa-puja offering: "Guru Maharaj, it's taken you so long to find me. Please never kick me away." The speaker loved it and learned something very deep from that Vaishnavi.
- It is so rare to come in contact with a devotee who knows our real interest — the real interest of our heart — and will do anything and everything: tolerate us, wait patiently for us, prod us, chastise us, bless us, even birth after birth if required, to fulfill the guru's responsibility (undertaken at the moment of initiation) to take the disciple back home, back to Godhead.
- It is a contract. It evolves into a very intimate, loving relationship, but Prabhupada said in Bhagavad Gita that "friendly talks are discontinued" — it is a formal relationship between guru and disciple.

## Chanakya Pandit: A Sadhu Is Rarer Than a Ruby or a Pearl

- **Chanakya Niti** — "There does not exist a ruby in every mountain, nor a pearl on the head of every elephant, nor are there sandalwood trees in every forest, nor will you meet a sadhu in every birth." The speaker loves this verse and quotes it often — it is ever-fresh.
  - If you find a ruby in the mountain, take it.
  - If you're riding an elephant and one of those special pearls manifests from his head at the time of rutting, take it. (The speaker saw one such pearl once, in South India at Tirupati — a sadhu showed it; getting such a pearl is said to make your wealth equal to that of Indra, king of heaven.)
  - Sandalwood trees hardly exist anymore because they're not profitable; in places like Malaysia where there used to be many, they're being cut down for rubber trees.
  - And how much less chance one has to meet a sadhu, because generally one must perform *punya* (pious activity) to come in contact with a sadhu. So if one meets a sadhu, one should consider oneself very fortunate.

## The Two Subjects of All Krishna Conscious Teaching

- What is the good fortune we obtain from a spiritual master? Essentially, the mercy we receive from a Vaishnava is that he teaches us **how to get out of the material world and into the spiritual world.**
- If you hear all the devotees who lecture in Krishna consciousness, it comes down to just two subjects, however varied their verses (shlokas), analogies, stories, and presentations:
  - What is material life? What is spiritual life?
  - How to get out of material life, and how to get back to the spiritual world.
- These are the things we learn from our spiritual master.

## Rupa Goswami's Prayer for the Lord's Extended Hand

- **Padyavali** (Rupa Goswami) — "O Lord, I am drowning in the painful, fathomless whirlpool of material existence. O Lord, O friend of those who have no shelter, O effulgent moon of mercy, please, just this one time, won't you extend Your hand to save me?"
  - When the speaker first read this verse many years ago, a beautiful mental picture of Prabhupada came into his mind.
- What is the extension of the Lord's mercy for the devotee crying for freedom from material existence and for eternal servitorship in the spiritual world? Krishna says (Bhagavad Gita 12.6–7): for My devotee, **"teṣām ahaṁ samuddhartā mṛtyu-saṁsāra-sāgarāt"** — "I am the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death."
  - There is a picture of Vishnu coming down on Garuda while the devotee — shaven head and sikha — drowns in the ocean of material existence, reaching up as Krishna reaches down.
- Taken more literally: What is the extension of the Lord's hand that takes the devotee out of material existence? It is His representative, the spiritual master — **sakshad-hari**, Krishna's direct representative. If you wonder, "Does Krishna know I exist?" — yes; if you have a bona fide spiritual master, Krishna sent you that guru to deliver you from material existence.

## Only a Madman Is Satisfied to Remain in Suffering

- We are suffering in this painful material world, and the only solution is to meet Krishna's representative, follow his instructions, and go back home, back to Godhead. A person satisfied to remain in this painful condition is mad. There is more pain than pleasure here.

## The "50-50" of Public Lectures vs. the Acharya's Conclusion

- In public introductory lectures (to free people from the illusion that they are happy here), we say: "Ladies and gentlemen, let us consider the real nature of this world. With all your plans, it is, after all, a world of duality — up and down, left and right, boy and girl, black and white, heat and cold, happiness and distress."
  - In Brazil the speaker noted: "You've got a lot of happiness — 8,000 miles of beaches — but it's also distress." Like two sides of a coin: you can't have heads without tails, happiness without distress.
  - Happiness is temporary; its end result is distress. It may last a minute or a hundred years, but it will be finished at the moment of death. As much as you are attached to something, you will experience the anxiety of separation when it leaves you. So in public we say "50-50."
- But one time Prabhupada said, "The conception of good and bad is wrong — it's all bad." This is an acharya speaking. In our public Bhagavad-gita classes we say 50-50, but amongst ourselves we know that real happiness begins only when one takes to spiritual life, to Krishna consciousness. In fact, there are more ways to suffer than to enjoy in this world. This makes you think: "Why maintain my material attachments? Been there, done that; didn't work. Let me fully surrender to the process of Krishna consciousness."

## Analogy: How Many Ways Can the Hand Enjoy vs. Suffer?

- How many ways can your hand enjoy? Not many — maybe picking up a gulab (sweet).
- How many ways can the hand suffer? Someone can slam the door on it, burn it, freeze it, rip the skin, bruise it.
- And what is the relief? Put cream on the burn, warm the cold hand, stitch up the cut. That is what we call "pleasure."
- Therefore Prabhupada concluded that what we take as pleasure in this world is merely **a temporary relief from a painful condition.** ("You're hitting some… ah, ah, stop… ah, ecstasy — hello? Not ecstasy. It's just temporary relief from a painful condition.")

## Two Things a Brahmana Cannot Tolerate — (1) A Place Without Water

- An intelligent man and woman — Brahmana and Brahmani — cannot tolerate two things. The first: a place where there is no water.
  - Therefore Brahmanas don't take birth in the deserts; only sinful people take birth in deserts as a result of past activities. A Brahmana cannot be peaceful or comfortable where there is no water, or in a situation where he must endure pain, because he is in the mode of goodness and is very sensitive. (This is why "devotees don't like to go to the dentist.")

## Cleanliness (Suchinam) as the Platform for Thinking of Krishna

- Why no place without water? Because *suchinam* — Brahmanas are always very clean. You cannot think of Krishna unless you live in a clean situation, unless your heart is clean, and, for all practical matters, unless your environment is clean.
- Prabhupada said, in the house of a Brahmana: **"A place for everything, and everything in its place."**
- The speaker's practice: When disciples request second initiation, he secretly invites himself (or Prahlad) to their house — not to eat, but to see if they are Brahmanas: whether there is a place for everything and everything in its place. He runs his finger along the windowsills. "'Gurudev, what is the qualification for Brahmana initiation?' No dust on the windowsill."
- **Story: Prabhupada's sister, Pishima.** She was a Brahmini Vaishnavi who carried a little bottle of Ganges water. The first time the speaker saw her (a big woman), she put the Ganges water down and cleansed the place before sitting while Prabhupada was giving darshan; afterwards she got up and again cleansed the place before leaving. Such a humble Vaishnavi — creating an atmosphere of auspiciousness with the Ganga-jal, and embodying the Brahminical principle, *suchinam*, of being very clean.
- **Story: Prabhupada's letter to Sri Govinda.** In the early years, Prabhupada wrote to one of the speaker's godbrothers, Sri Govinda in Chicago: "A Brahmana is so clean that after drinking water, he washes his hands." The speaker learned a lot from that.
- **Learning cleanliness from Bhakti Brhingya Govinda Maharaj.** The speaker says he learned cleanliness from his dear godbrother Bhakti Brhingya Govinda Maharaj — "he's a fanatic. And it's okay to be a fanatic in Krishna consciousness." The saying "too much of a good thing is not good" does not apply in Krishna consciousness — you can't say too much chanting of Hare Krishna is not good; no, do it unlimitedly. Govinda Maharaj would "get on his case," and the speaker learned to keep everything clean: clothes, possessions, room. When he arrives he spends a long time cleaning, and before he leaves he also cleans; always organized. In that atmosphere of goodness — a stepping stone to transcendence — you can think of Krishna, **shuddha-sattva.**

## From the Modes to Pure Goodness (Shuddha-Sattva)

- Krishna consciousness is not just about *sattva* (goodness), but goodness is essential as a platform for coming to *shuddha-sattva*, pure goodness.
- As we rise from *tamas* (ignorance) to *rajas* (passion) to *sattva* (goodness), we are also meant to step into *shuddha-sattva* (pure goodness) by becoming devotees of God and living in a godly atmosphere — like this temple.

## Two Things a Brahmana Cannot Tolerate — (2) Pain (Contrasted with the Kshatriya)

- A Brahmana is also known by the fact that he cannot tolerate pain — meaning, ultimately, he cannot tolerate the painful condition of material existence, so he is always looking for a solution: how to get out.
- Kshatriyas, by contrast, can tolerate pain. On the battlefield they can't be wimps; even with a bruise, a cut, or a broken arm, they must go on fighting — that's their dharma.
  - Just as devotees have their emblems (kunti-mala, tilaka, sikhas, hair tied back for matajis), a Kshatriya is only considered a Kshatriya if he has scars — proof that he was in battle defending dharma.
- A Brahmana can't tolerate this painful material existence; that is a symptom. He wants relief, liberation (*mukti*); he wants to go back home, back to Godhead.

## Story: Karna and Parasurama

- Told by Prabhupada. **Karna** was one of the greatest Kshatriyas who ever lived — a great challenge even to Arjuna. In the guise of a Brahmana, he went to **Parasurama** (an incarnation of the Lord) to learn the art of warfare.
- Why the disguise? Parasurama didn't like Kshatriyas at that time. In fact, he destroyed generations of them (the speaker says "17") because they were misbehaving like modern-day politicians — taxing the people and giving back maybe 1% while 99% goes in their pocket or a Swiss bank account, or having affairs in a hotel in New York City. The world became overburdened by these kings who were actually sudras misbehaving, so "off with their heads." But Parasurama was a powerful warrior, an incarnation of God — so to learn the art of war, go to Him, but not as a Kshatriya.
- The difficulty of the disguise: Kshatriyas are like 6'5", with big broad shoulders, thin waist, muscular legs, and scars — you can tell a Kshatriya. Brahmanas are not that tall, fair-skinned (staying home, eating the maha-prasad), with a little belly like Ganesh; they aren't into "six-packs" or their bodies — they're proud of their belly: "I am Brahmana; you feed me, you're feeding God, come, come."
- So Karna wore the robes and came to Parasurama: "I am a Brahmana, but I need to learn the art of warfare, because in extreme emergency Brahmanas may take up other occupations — specifically, they may sometimes have to become Kshatriyas (rare, but possible). These are the two higher orders of society, Kshatriyas and Brahmanas." Parasurama agreed and taught him daily, while Karna carefully kept his disguise.
- **The test — the boring insect:** One day, after intense martial-arts training, Parasurama said He was tired and needed rest: "You are my humble disciple — sit there, and I will put My head on your lap and take a nap." (Note the intimacy: the master putting his head on the disciple's lap.) As the Lord slept, a very dangerous insect landed on Karna's thigh and began boring through it, eating his flesh through, with blood pouring from his leg.
  - Karna could tolerate it, because he was a Kshatriya — such things are not unknown to Kshatriyas, who tolerate the pain of battle for the higher purpose of protecting the innocent (real Kshatriyas, not like the boys we send into battle now). He bore the pain and did not move, not wanting to disturb his Guru Maharaj; the insect ate all the way through his flesh and flew away. He thought, "I'm doing the right thing."
- **Discovery:** When Parasurama woke and saw the blood flowing and the hole in Karna's leg, He took out His chopper: "Off with your head, you rascal!"
