# Importance of Initation

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 49 ·  · 1:00:52

## Summary

Speaking at an initiation ceremony in Dallas (eight devotees receiving first initiation and eight receiving second), Indradyumna Swami builds his talk on Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.28, where Prahlada Maharaja, offered any benediction by the Lord, instead begs to keep serving his spiritual master Narada Muni. This shows the central principle that one must first serve and please the guru, through whose mercy the seed of devotion (bhakti-lata-bija) is awakened and Krishna's mercy then follows. He describes initiation as a second, spiritual birth, contrasting it with ordinary birth, which is into ignorance because the soul abandoned Krishna and the eternal spiritual world for the temporary, suffering material world, likened to Durga-dham, a prison walled in by birth, disease, old age, and death, with the material body as the prisoner's uniform. The seed of bhakti lies dormant in every heart until the guru activates it and authorizes the disciple to chant Hare Krishna; chanting even before initiation frees one from sin and material desire, but attaining prema, pure love of God, requires receiving the mantra in bona fide disciplic succession. Because a genuine brahmana cannot tolerate the pain of material existence, he seeks a solution and takes shelter of a spiritual master, holding onto the guru's lotus feet, inquiring submissively and applying his instructions (pariprasnena sevaya). Meeting such a sadhu is extremely rare, and in Kali-yuga the guru mercifully comes to find the fallen soul, as Srila Prabhupada came to the West. The most essential instruction is the chanting of the holy name, which the Swami calls Kali-yuga's special mercy, a permanent sale of the holy name that yields in this age what meditation, sacrifice, and temple worship gave in previous ages. He closes by urging the initiates never to lose sight of the goal, the day when a tear of love for Krishna flows down the cheek, quoting Rupa Goswami that the holy names create a jubilant festival in the mouth and material desires no longer appear beautiful.

## Key Points

- The lecture is delivered at an initiation ceremony (8 first initiates, 8 second initiates) based on SB 7.9.28, where Prahlada asks to serve his guru Narada Muni rather than accept benedictions, teaching that one must serve and please the spiritual master first (guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija).
- Initiation is a second, spiritual birth ('deathlessness begins at initiation'); ordinary birth is into ignorance because the soul left Krishna and the eternal spiritual world for the temporary material world of suffering.
- The material world is Durga-dham, a prison bounded by four insurmountable walls that no science can conquer: birth, disease, old age, and death; the material body is the 'prison uniform,' a mark of rebellion against God rather than something to be proud of.
- The seed of devotion (bhakti-lata-bija) is dormant in every heart, like a stored seed; the guru activates it and authorizes chanting Hare Krishna. Chanting before initiation removes sinful reactions and material desire, but prema (love of God) requires receiving the mantra in genuine disciplic succession.
- A brahmana cannot tolerate the pain of material existence and therefore seeks liberation, illustrated by the story of Karna disguised as a brahmana studying under Parasurama, exposed when he silently endured an insect boring through his thigh (a ksatriya's tolerance, not a brahmana's).
- One must take shelter of a bona fide guru and hold on to his lotus feet (guru, not gauru/cow), inquiring submissively and applying his teachings (pariprasnena sevaya); meeting a sadhu is as rare as a ruby in every mountain, a pearl on every elephant's head, or sandalwood in every forest (Chanakya).
- Chanting Hare Krishna is the most essential instruction and Kali-yuga's special mercy, a 'permanent sale of the holy name,' delivering the same result that meditation (Satya), sacrifice (Treta), and deity worship (Dvapara) gave in earlier ages; the goal is a tear of love for Krishna.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.28 — the class verse; Prahlada, falling into a 'blind well full of snakes,' says that since Narada Muni accepted him as a disciple his first duty is to serve the guru, so how could he ever leave that service.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.34 — 'pariprasnena sevaya' (heard as 'paripashana sevaya'); one must approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively, and render service.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 19.151 — 'guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija'; the seed of devotional service comes by the mercy of guru and then Krishna (Sri Chaitanya instructing Rupa Goswami).
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 20.108 — 'jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya dasa'; every living entity is eternally a servant of Krishna, the seed always present within.
- Gurvastaka (Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura), verse 8 — 'yasya prasadad bhagavat-prasado'; by the guru's grace one obtains Krishna's grace, so one should not try to please the Lord by concoction.
- Guru-vandana prayer — 'om ajnana-timirandhasya... caksur unmilitam yena tasmai sri-gurave namah'; born in the darkness of ignorance, the disciple's eyes are opened by the guru's torchlight of transcendental knowledge (recited and translated before the talk).
- Narottama dasa Thakura, Prarthana — 'tandera carana sevi bhakta-sane vasa, janame janame haya ei abhilasa'; the desire, birth after birth, to serve the associates of the Lord rather than seek direct service.
- Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's prayer — 'gopi-bhartuh pada-kamalayor dasa-dasanudasah'; one should become the servant of the servant of the servant of the Lord.
- Bhagavad-gita 2.27 — 'for one who takes birth, death is certain, and for one who dies, birth is certain'; the endless cycle of samsara, judged by its result (phalena pariciyate).
- Bhagavad-gita 13.8-12 — 'janma-mrtyu-jara-vyadhi-duhkha-dosanudarsanam'; awareness of the fault and fragility of birth, disease, old age, and death is a sign of intelligence and real knowledge.
- Bhagavad-gita 12.6-7 — 'tesam aham samuddharta mrtyu-samsara-sagarat'; for His devotee Krishna is the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death (depicted as Vishnu descending on Garuda to lift the drowning devotee).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.1.22 — the Naimisaranya sages to Suta Goswami: 'kalim sattva-haram...'; they accept him as the captain of the ship for those wishing to cross the difficult ocean of Kali, which destroys all good qualities (cited as the first verse the speaker ever heard in Krishna consciousness).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.3.52 — 'krte yad dhyayato visnum...'; whatever was achieved in Satya-yuga by meditation on Vishnu, in Treta by sacrifice, and in Dvapara by serving the Lord's lotus feet, is obtained in Kali-yuga simply by chanting the holy name.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 3.33.7 — 'aho bata svapaco 'to gariyan...'; even a lowborn dog-eater becomes an exalted, worshipable Vaishnava by the potency of the holy name on the tongue; to doubt this is to doubt the Lord, since Krishna and His name are non-different.
- Chanakya-niti (Niti-shastra) — 'there is not a ruby in every mountain, nor a pearl on the head of every elephant, nor sandalwood in every forest, nor a sadhu in every birth'; also the maxim that an intelligent person gives up something only to gain something better (referenced against the soul's original 'sin' of giving up Krishna).
- Rupa Goswami, Padyavali — (1) 'I am drowning in the painful, fathomless whirlpool of material existence... O effulgent moon of mercy, just this once extend your hand to save me'; (2) concluding verse: 'now that we are your devotees, your holy names have created a jubilant festival in our mouths... material desires no longer appear beautiful to us.'
- Gurvastaka, verse 7 — 'saksad-dharitvena samasta-sastraih' (spoken as 'saksad-hari'); the spiritual master is honored as the direct representative of Krishna, the Lord's extended hand that lifts the devotee from material existence.
- Bengali proverb / Vaishnava saying — 'janame janame sabe pita mata paya, krsna guru nahi mile bhaja hari ei'; everyone obtains a mother and father in every life, but only by the benediction of Krishna and guru can one return home, back to Godhead.
