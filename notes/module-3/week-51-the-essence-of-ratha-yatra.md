# The Essence of Ratha Yatra

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 51 · Festivals and Kirtans · 48:17

## Summary

Speaking at the Ratha-yatra festival in Belfast (following the Dublin festival), Indradyumna Swami argues that an event's success is judged not by quantity but by quality of who attends, and the supreme guest was Lord Jagannatha himself, with Baladeva and Subhadra and all his eternal associates. He explains that the material world is a place souls come to forget God, yet out of causeless compassion the Lord descends again and again to reclaim his forgetful servants, and Jagannatha, as Patita-pavana, is especially merciful. The heart of his message is that Ratha-yatra reverses the usual dynamic: because people no longer come to the temple to see God, God leaves the temple on his chariot and comes into the streets to see us, and even standing up in respect as the chariot passes is said to make one an eternal liberated associate. He warns that identifying with the temporary body and its bodily/national labels is a form of madness, since we are eternally the servants of Krishna, and stresses that the next birth is not under our control. Drawing many analogies, he presents the festival as a preview or movie-trailer of the eternal, blissful spiritual world, contrasting its permanence with the fleeting happiness of the material world. He teaches that the truth is non-sectarian and available in every bona fide tradition, and that the practical way to sustain the festival's spiritual experience is the chanting of the Lord's holy names, the sound incarnation of God who is also Patita-pavana. He closes by correcting the popular slogan 'chant and be happy' with what he says Prabhupada originally taught in the early London days: 'chant, and your life will be sublime.'

## Key Points

- Success of a festival is measured by the quality of who comes, not the quantity; the highest guest at Ratha-yatra is Lord Jagannatha himself, who never appears alone but with all his devotees and energies (Prabhupada: you never see a picture of Krishna alone; that is Mayavada).
- The material world is where souls come to forget God; out of compassion the Lord descends 'once in a day of Brahma' and continually sends his representatives (saints, prophets, messiahs), so genuine dharma is always available to the sincere seeker.
- Ratha-yatra's deeper meaning: since people no longer come to the house of God, the Lord comes out to see them, riding majestically through the town so that even the forgetful are forced to behold his beautiful form and be purified.
- Jagannatha and the holy name are both called Patita-pavana, deliverer of the most fallen; even standing up out of respect as the chariot passes, or pulling the rope, or taking prasadam, purifies the heart regardless of one's knowledge, likened to how a virus/flu enters and acts on the body unseen (he had just recovered from a bad flu before coming to Ireland).
- Identifying with the body and its labels (man/woman, white/black, Irish/English/American) is a form of madness, like a common man in an asylum insisting he is Shakespeare, Julius Caesar, Winston Churchill, or Michael Jackson; our true identity is eternal servant of Krishna, Lord of the Universe.
- The next birth is not our choice, illustrated by university admission by grades (Oxford, Cambridge, Harvard) and Prabhupada's account of a famous Indian politician who died in the 1940s reportedly taking birth as a dog in a Swedish family.
- Ratha-yatra is a 'preview' or movie-trailer of the eternal, blissful spiritual world; material happiness is real but fleeting (a drop of water on a parched tongue, water on a lotus leaf / off a duck's back), and the sustaining practice is chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra — 'chant, and your life will be sublime.'

## Scriptural References

- Katha Upanishad 2.2.13 — 'nityo nityanam chetanas chetananam eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman' — among all eternal living beings, the one (Krishna) who fulfills the desires of everyone.
- Brahma-samhita 5.1 — 'ishvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam' — Krishna is the supreme controller, cause of all causes, superior even to Brahma and Shiva.
- Bhagavad-gita 7.14 — 'daivi hy esa gunamayi mama maya duratyaya' — the divine energy of the three modes is very difficult to overcome; the Lord himself admits this.
- Bhagavad-gita 14.4 — Krishna is the seed-giving father of all living entities ('aham bija-pradah pita'); therefore the festival is for everyone, not only Indians.
- Bhagavad-gita 4.7-4.8 — 'paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya cha dushkritam / dharma-samsthapanartham... sambhavami yuge yuge' — the Lord descends to deliver the sadhus, annihilate the miscreants, and reestablish religious principles.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.3.28 — 'ete camsa-kalah pumsah krishnas tu bhagavan svayam' — all incarnations are plenary portions or portions of portions, but Krishna is the original Personality of Godhead; Jagannatha is an expansion of Krishna.
- Bhagavad-gita 8.16 — 'abrahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna' — from the highest planet to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death occur; 'Belfast is included there somewhere.'
- Bhagavad-gita 8.15/8.21 — one who attains the Lord's supreme abode never takes birth again ('yam prapya na nivartante') — 'Krishna's guarantee.'
- Bhagavad-gita 2.13 — 'dehino 'smin yatha dehe kaumaram yauvanam jara' — as the soul passes from childhood to youth to old age in one body, it passes to another body at death.
- Bhagavad-gita 16.1-3 (divine qualities) — cleanliness, truthfulness, mercy, austerity, tolerance, forgiveness, love for all living entities, having no enemy — the natural qualities of the saintly person.
- The Nectar of Devotion (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu) — one who stands up out of respect as the Ratha-yatra chariot passes is from that time to be considered an eternally liberated associate of the Lord (basis of the godbrother's question to Prabhupada, answered 'Is it in shastra? Then it must be so').
- Chanakya Pandita's Niti-shastra — practical counsel not to settle where there is no good physician, no pious king, no flowing river, or where people are inimical; and the verse that emeralds are not in every mountain, sandalwood not in every forest, pearls not on every elephant's head, and genuine sadhus not found in every birth.
- Bible, Matthew 6:10 — 'Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven' — cited to show the kingdom of God can be realized even here.
- Milton (Paradise Lost) — the material world compared to a 'fool's paradise'; also alludes to Jean-Paul Sartre, who saw the miseries of material existence but, lacking knowledge of spiritual life, could offer only suicide as a solution.
