# The Essence of Ratha Yatra

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 51 · Festivals and Kirtans · 48:17

**Themes:** ratha-yatra · jagannatha · forgetfulness of god · deity worship · holy name · material vs spiritual

## Summary

Speaking at the Ratha-yatra festival in Belfast (following the Dublin festival), Indradyumna Swami argues that an event's success is judged not by quantity but by quality of who attends, and the supreme guest was Lord Jagannatha himself, with Baladeva and Subhadra and all his eternal associates. He explains that the material world is a place souls come to forget God, yet out of causeless compassion the Lord descends again and again to reclaim his forgetful servants, and Jagannatha, as Patita-pavana, is especially merciful. The heart of his message is that Ratha-yatra reverses the usual dynamic: because people no longer come to the temple to see God, God leaves the temple on his chariot and comes into the streets to see us, and even standing up in respect as the chariot passes is said to make one an eternal liberated associate. He warns that identifying with the temporary body and its bodily/national labels is a form of madness, since we are eternally the servants of Krishna, and stresses that the next birth is not under our control. Drawing many analogies, he presents the festival as a preview or movie-trailer of the eternal, blissful spiritual world, contrasting its permanence with the fleeting happiness of the material world. He teaches that the truth is non-sectarian and available in every bona fide tradition, and that the practical way to sustain the festival's spiritual experience is the chanting of the Lord's holy names, the sound incarnation of God who is also Patita-pavana. He closes by correcting the popular slogan 'chant and be happy' with what he says Prabhupada originally taught in the early London days: 'chant, and your life will be sublime.'

## Congratulations and the True Measure of a Festival's Success

- Opening congratulations to the GBC (Governing Board Commissioner), Praghosa Prabhu, for a successful Dublin Ratha Yatra and an equally successful Belfast Ratha Yatra; and to the local temple president, Karaka Prabhu, for such efficient organization, giving everyone a chance to serve Sri Jagannatha, Baladeva, and Subhadra through this festival.
- Objection acknowledged: it was a Sunday, and there weren't many people out — perhaps several hundred to a couple thousand saw the festival. Yet the festival was a complete success.
- **Quantity vs. quality:** One way to judge an event is by *how many* come; another is by *who* comes. We may have lacked quantity, but no one can doubt the quality of who came.
- Who was the most important guest? The Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself — Lord Jagannatha, Lady Subhadra.
  - **Brahma-samhita 5.1** — *isvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam* — Krishna is the supreme controller, the cause of all causes.
  - **Katha Upanishad (2.2.13)** — *nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam / eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman* — among all the eternal living beings, there is one, *eka*, who fulfills the desires of everyone; that is Krishna, the chief *isvara*, superior even to great devatas like Indra, Brahma, Shiva, and Ganesha, who derive their power from Him.
- Wherever the Lord is personally present, all His associates, energies, and servants are also there, whether we can see them or not. Prabhupada once said "you'll never see a picture of Krishna alone — that's Mayavada"; the Lord is always there with His devotees, and devotional service is always taking place.
- So it *was* a successful festival: the Lord was there with His transcendental associates, and by their mercy (and the organizers' mercy) some few souls received mercy.

## One Soul Awakened Is Success

- **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's standard:** Our *parama-guru* said he would consider his whole mission — a great preaching mission he established single-handedly in the subcontinent — a success if just one single person became Krishna conscious. So rare is it in this world.
- People do not come to the material world to render service to God; they come to *forget* God, and that forgetfulness is very deep in the heart of the conditioned soul.
- **Bhagavad-gita 7.14** — *daivi hy esa guna-mayi mama maya duratyaya* — this divine energy of the Lord, consisting of the three modes of material nature, is very difficult to overcome. The Lord Himself admits this.
- When the Lord or His representatives come, they capture someone and remind him: "My dear eternal servant of Krishna, by the force of this Maya you have forgotten; please again take up Krishna consciousness." Today, some souls on the streets of Belfast were awakened to their original constitutional position as devotees of the Lord — that is the purpose of Ratha Yatra.

## The Deeper Meaning Behind the Festival

- This festival has been going on in Jagannatha Puri (Purushottama-kshetra) for thousands of years. It was Srila Prabhupada's desire to bring it to the West — Ireland, England, Scotland, Wales, France, Germany, Russia, Australia, China — so people could appreciate the beauty of Vedic culture.
- But the meaning is far more profound than colors, singing, dancing, and festivity: through this Ratha Yatra (the "chariot of grain"/chariot journey), the Lord Himself hopes to fulfill one of His main objectives in coming to this world.
- "The Lord has no business in this world" — again, this is a place where people come to forget God.

## Chanakya Pandit's Common Sense — Where Not to Live

- **Chanakya Pandit's Niti-shastra:** He advises not to go to a place lacking (1) a good physician/doctor, (2) a pious king, (3) a clean flowing river, and (4) where people are inimical to us.
  - Reasoning ("Niti-shastra means common sense"): if you fall ill in a foreign place with no doctor, who will care for you? An impious king may imprison the foreigner. Without a clean river, how will you get water to drink, cook, wash clothes, or bathe? And going where people are inimical means only trouble — "you go on vacation there, you won't have a vacation."
- Apply the same logic to this world: it is full of forgetful souls. From Brahma down to the insignificant ant, everyone is forgetful of God to greater or lesser degrees.

## Even the Demigods Are Slightly Forgetful — So Why Does God Come?

- Even the great devatas, whom we must respect as great devotees, are "not in the spiritual world yet"; they still have some material desires keeping them in material existence. A little more cleansing of the heart, then back home to Godhead — but for now, some slight forgetfulness remains, so they too undergo birth, disease, old age, and death (though their lifespans are very long). If they are Krishna conscious at death, they immediately go to Vaikuntha.
- Everyone is forgetful of God to some degree. So why would God come here? He lives in the spiritual world enjoying loving pastimes with His devotees 24 hours of all eternity — "the spiritual world is simply loving sentiments, loving relationships with Him."
- He comes not to enjoy the material energy (as we do) but **out of compassion.** Just as it pains a father's heart when his son or daughter leaves home, how much more true for the Supreme Father.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 14.4** — He is the seed-giving father of all living entities. If even one of His children leaves the kingdom of God for the material world, it causes Him pain, and out of His infinite compassion He comes.

## Why the Lord Descends — Bhagavad-gita 4.7–8

- **Bhagavad-gita 4.7–8** — the famous verses: the Lord comes to deliver the *sadhus* (*paritranaya sadhunam*), to annihilate the miscreants/*asuras* who imbibe demoniac principles (*vinashaya ca dushkritam*), and to reestablish the principles of religion (*dharma-samsthapanarthaya*).
  - Examples of *asuras*: Ravana, his sister Surpanakha — their pleasure is giving pain to devotees, whereas *sadhus* give pleasure to others by enlightening them spiritually. Such persons do exist in this world, hard as it is to understand.
  - Everything here is temporary and erodes in time, even the principles of religion; therefore the Lord (or His representative) must come again and again.
- **Once in a day of Brahma** — described in Gita — the Lord comes; but by the calculation of the demigods (who are more important than us), "every day" the Lord is coming, or sending His representative — a saint, a rishi, a prophet, a messenger, a Messiah.
  - Image: someone taking his valuable time to knock on your door every day at 12 noon with an important message. In different times and parts of the world, there is always some form of *dharma* available for the sincere seeker. "No one can say we don't have the chance."

## Jagannatha Is Krishna, and Especially Merciful

- Jagannatha Swami is not different from Sri Krishna. **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.3.28** — *ete camsa-kalah pumsah krishnas tu bhagavan svayam* — all the listed incarnations are plenary portions or portions of plenary portions, but Krishna is the original Personality of Godhead; the expansions appear whenever the demons get the upper hand.
- Jagannatha Swami is one such expansion. With spiritual eyes one would see His original form: Jagannatha *is* Vrindavan Krishna, in His sweet loving pastimes in His own abode of Sri Vrindavan, with His brother Balarama and sister Subhadra.
- All forms of the Lord are merciful, but Jagannatha is *especially* merciful; therefore He is called **Patita-pavana** — the deliverer of the most fallen. (We more often apply this title to Lord Chaitanya, the most recent incarnation, but Jagannatha has been Patita-pavana for thousands of years.)

## The Deity Is Not a Statue — the House of God

- In our tradition, the Lord is worshipped as the *arca-vigraha*, the deity form in the temple. "He is not a statue... not simply wood." By His mercy He comes in this form: God can turn matter into spirit and spirit into matter. Being absolute, out of causeless mercy He appears as the deity so devotees can daily behold His beautiful form and become purified.
- Because the form of the Lord is absolute, simply gazing upon it washes away all impurities in the heart — lust, anger, grief.
- This is why India is full of temples "from the Himalayas down to the tip of India" — temple after temple — so devotees can grow daily and purify their hearts by seeing the Lord. "Probably more temples in India than there are churches, mosques, and synagogues combined in the entire world."
- **Bharata-varsha:** *para* can be translated as "light"; Bharata-varsha means "the land of light" — not merely tropical light but the light of truth revealed. Naturally, people in India are mostly interested in spiritual life — though this is changing in Kali-yuga as they turn to Western values.
- Church, mosque, temple, synagogue are all "houses of God" where the Lord is worshipped in the form of that tradition. Because it is God's house, it is always "open house" — "the door back to the kingdom of God." The Lord invites: "Come see Me, hear My glories, chant My glories, associate with My devotees, partake of foodstuff offered to Me." This is how we become purified — temple worship being especially the process of the previous age (Treta/Dvapara).

## Kali-yuga: Houses of God Becoming Supermarkets

- As Kali-yuga advances, fewer and fewer people come to the mosque, church, synagogue, and temple; so they sell the houses of God and make supermarkets out of them.
- **Belfast Presbyterian church:** During the Ratha Yatra parade with kirtan, the speaker passed a very old Presbyterian church, its stone dated 1905 — now a supermarket. He was shocked. In some countries churches are turned into pubs, and "no one is objecting" — 20–30 years ago people would object, but now Kali-yuga advances so fast that people are not objecting. "A very sad state."
- We are enamored by the false hope of material pleasure through gadgets — the iPhone (which "does everything," zoom-in cameras, digital, computer transfer), iPods, televisions, cars — all offering the promise of happiness. But it doesn't work "because it doesn't touch the soul." It may satisfy the senses temporarily but cannot touch the soul.
- The first step in self-realization: "I am not the body, I am the soul inside." So one misses the point in thinking material enjoyment gives lasting pleasure — it gives "some cheap thrill" but cannot satisfy the soul's hankering for unending happiness.

## The Lord Comes Looking for Us — the Chariot in Belfast

- Because people run to the stores rather than the house of God, the Lord — present everywhere (in the spiritual sky, in every atom, and in the heart as Paramatma/Supersoul) — understands this mentality and expands His compassion.
- How? He leaves the temple, not the *ashram*. Knowing people won't come to see Him, "He comes to see you." "Because we're not looking for God, He comes looking for us."
- He inspires His devotee to build a beautiful chariot, is taken from the temple, placed on the majestic Vaikuntha chariot, and rides through the town, "and you're forced to see Him, because nothing is more beautiful than that display of the spiritual."
- **Belfast observed:** The town, per its sorrowful history (hopefully improving), is less developed than other European cities — many places not rented, empty land, visible destruction, "gray, dark, somewhat gloomy... pretty grim looking." But when the Lord's chariot came through the streets, it was "advertising the spiritual world" — "don't remain here."
- **Bhagavad-gita 8.16** — from the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death take place. "Belfast is included there somewhere."
- But **Bhagavad-gita** promise (8.15–16): one fortunate enough to turn from the false promise of material happiness to loving devotional service, giving his life to Krishna, purifies his heart and awakens ecstatic love of God; at the moment of death he goes not to another birth but immediately to Krishna's abode — the spiritual world — and, "having once gone there, you never come back." That is Krishna's guarantee.
- **Spiritual master on remembering the material world:** Asked whether, back in the spiritual world, we remember the material world, the spiritual master said yes — but you remember it "like a very bad dream": you know you had a bad dream, and sometimes it is so bad you don't want to go back to sleep. So the comparison between material and spiritual life is not a difficult choice.

## Jean-Paul Sartre — Diagnosis Without a Cure

- Those who know only material life find it frustrating. **Jean-Paul Sartre**, the French philosopher, "could speak like me about the miseries of material existence" — without knowing the Sanskrit shlokas, he was intelligent enough to see things in proper perspective, to see that the suffering far outweighs the happiness. But he had no alternative — he didn't know about spiritual life, Krishna consciousness, or Krishna, so his only solution to the problem was to take his life.
- Our solution to the same problem is to give real life — spiritual life, Krishna consciousness. That too is a reality, "not a figment of someone's imagination... not some poet's description or philosopher's dream. It's a fact."

## Real People from the Spiritual World and the Rarity of Saints

- We know it is a fact because real people come from the spiritual world to remind us of our real home: Krishna came 5,000 years ago; Jesus Christ 2,000 years ago; Muhammad 1,500 years ago; Buddha also came. These persons do not display the mentality, desires, and characteristics of conditioned souls.
- The saintly person naturally manifests all wonderful spiritual qualities from the heart: cleanliness, truthfulness, mercy, austerity, tolerance, forgiveness, love for all living entities. "He has no enemy; he sees everyone as his friend." Rare, but once in a while one stumbles across such a personality.
- **Chanakya Pandit again:** You will not find emeralds in every mountain, nor sandalwood in every forest, nor a pearl on the head of every elephant — and you will not find genuine sadhus, rishis, or yogis in every birth. When you do find one, take lessons from him on how to get out of the material world and go back home, back to Godhead.
- The Lord Himself takes up this mission — not only the traveling rishis, sadhus, and yogis. He inspires: "Take me from this dark temple where no one comes to see me; take me onto the streets so I can see them."

## The Potency of Ratha Yatra — Standing Up for the Lord

- The Lord is so kind, He doesn't miss even one conditioned soul.
- **Nectar of Devotion:** If someone, merely out of respect, stands up when the Ratha Yatra chariot passes by, that person is from that point on to be considered an eternal associate of the Lord.
- **Godbrother's question to Prabhupada:** One godbrother, reading this, went to the spiritual master: "I've been practicing Krishna consciousness for seven years, and there's no guarantee I'll make it back to Godhead because I'm a fallen person — yet it says here that someone who stands up to receive the chariot of Lord Jagannatha is considered an eternally liberated servant of Krishna. How is that possible when I'm still struggling?" Prabhupada asked, "Where does it say that?" — "In the Nectar of Devotion." Seeing that he had read it correctly, Prabhupada asked, "Is it in shastra? Then it must be so. It must be so."
- So potent is the activity of receiving the Lord, pulling the chariot, coming to His festival, and taking prasada that the heart becomes purified — even if one shows respect not knowing all His glories: "Sir, I will stand for you; I will help your devotees pull this."

## The Festival Works Like a Virus — For Everyone

- Therefore Srila Prabhupada wanted a Ratha Yatra in every town and village of the world.
- Objection: they don't know what's happening when they stand, pull the rope, eat the food, or hear the mantra Hare Krishna. Answer: "It does not matter. It will work."
- **Virus/flu analogy:** A virus enters your body and you get a cold or flu whether or not you understand it. Just before coming to Ireland, the speaker caught a very bad flu; friends said "don't go, it'll go away," but it stayed for several days, working in his body and causing distress without his knowing how. Conversely, when the "virus" of Krishna consciousness enters the heart through oral reception (Hare Krishna), food, visualizing the Lord's form, and seeing His devotees, "in a good way it purifies your heart and awakens your Krishna consciousness."
- So these beautiful, colorful, ecstatic festivals go to the "dreary" towns — Belfast, Dublin, London, Paris — all dreary from the ultimate viewpoint compared to the spiritual world, which is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss.
- This is not just for Indian people. **Bhagavad-gita** — Krishna is the seed-giving father of every living entity; the festival is for everybody.
- **Jagannatha** means "Lord of the Universe." Everyone is His servant. To think otherwise is a form of madness.

## Madness — Thinking You Are the Body

- One definition of madness is thinking you are someone you are not. In a mental institution you can meet a common man who says he is Shakespeare, Julius Caesar, Tony Blair, Winston Churchill, or Michael Jackson. We laugh — but "we're really laughing at ourselves."
- Because if you think for one moment that you are this material body — accepting any of its nomenclatures: "I'm a man," "she's a woman," "I'm white," "she's black," "I'm American," "you're English, Irish, German, Russian" — it is the same type of madness.
- What are you really? "Only the eternal servant of Krishna." Having fallen into the material creation, Maya covers us and makes us think "I'm Irish, I'm English, I'm American, I'm German, I'm Japanese, I'm white, I'm black, I'm blue, I'm green." We become so attached to bodily identification that we're ready to fight for it — "the kingdom of illusion." We are none of these things; we are temporarily identifying with bodies "here today and gone tomorrow."

## The Uncertainty of the Next Birth

- No one can say for certain he will live until tomorrow night. Time waits for no man.
- **Bhagavad-gita 2.13** — the embodied soul passes from the body of a baby to a boy, to a youth, to an old man, and at death passes into another body.
- What determines the next body? It may not be your choice. **University analogy:** When you graduate from high school you can go only to the university you are qualified for; everyone wants Oxford, Cambridge, or Harvard, but few go — you go according to your grades. Don't think that when you leave this body you'll simply be born in Delhi or New York.
- **The famous politician reborn as a dog:** The spiritual master said very clearly that when one very famous Indian politician died in the 1940s (name withheld), an astrologer said he had taken birth as one of two dogs in a Swedish family. It sounds preposterous — "but what do we know?" We hardly know how our own bodies work or how the world around us works. These things must be understood from *shastra*, from the Lord Himself and from self-realized saints — "through enlightened beings, not conditioned souls."
- There is no guarantee where you'll take your next birth. If we think we are anything but Krishna's servant, we are in illusion, and as long as we're in illusion we stay in the material world — meaning birth, disease, old age, and death. "Not a very pretty picture." The spiritual master said "this material world is no place for a lady or for a gentleman."

## Glimpses of Truth Through the Clouds

- We are so conditioned and covered that we can't see. Once in a while we get a glimpse of the truth — like the clouds in that part of the world, where a little ray of sunshine breaks through ("Wow, the sun!") and then it's gone. So in life certain impetuses give us a glimpse of truth before we become covered over again. "We're very unfortunate." Therefore the Lord comes again and again, and sends His representative again and again, to remind us that we are not of this world — and that we should not *want* to be, because at best it all ends in death.

## The Positive Alternative — God Is a Person

- "I hate to be the bearer of bad news, but that is the fact of life" — yet we have the positive alternative of real life. Taking up Krishna consciousness as seen in this hall, there is every chance of reviving one's eternal nature as God's devotee.
- Ultimately, God is Krishna — not just a word, a vague impression, or a "supreme being." He may be that to the neophyte, but to the advanced devotee He is a person. He is the Absolute Truth, and since everything comes from the Absolute Truth, He too must be a person — but not a person like us. He has no material body that gets diseased and old; He has an eternal, beautiful, spiritual form, ever-youthful (*nava-yauvanam*).
- He invites us to the spiritual world to play, dance, eat, and be with Him in deep loving exchanges we could not find in this material world.

## Ratha Yatra as a Preview of the Spiritual World

- To give us a clue, He comes out in the Ratha Yatra parade — devotees singing, dancing, and laughing — "like a little glimpse."
- **Movie-preview analogy:** Just as a new movie has a preview before it shows at the cinema ("Hey Mom, I want to go" — "It's coming out in two weeks"), you start thinking, "that's what I want." This Ratha Yatra festival is a preview of what the spiritual world is like.
- What intelligent man or woman would not take up this process? Make the comparison: the spiritual world is eternal, full of knowledge, and full of bliss; this material world — as Milton said, a "fool's paradise" — is temporary, not full of knowledge (even material knowledge takes 25 years to learn, what to speak of spiritual knowledge), and not full of bliss.
- There *is* some happiness — but not enough to satisfy your thirst. **Desert analogy:** In the desert, thirsty, someone says "I have water," then pours a single drop from a teaspoon onto your tongue. "Is that enough?"
- It would be ignorant to say there is no happiness here: blue skies, smiling babies, handsome boys, pretty girls, flowers, poetry, music. But the whole show is temporary; it comes and goes.
  - The happiness is like water that falls on a lotus flower and stays only a moment, or (Western analogy) "water off a duck's back." You work hard for so long to get it, and in a moment it's gone, and you start the process all over.

## The Soul's Hankering for Constant Happiness

- The soul hankers for something constant. Just as sugar is sweet, salt is salty, and water is wet, the soul — you, inside the temporary body, Krishna's servant — has the intrinsic nature "to always be happy, without cessation."
- It seems impossible because we know only this world. But there are souls even in this world, now, who always experience that *ananda*, because even here they don't forget God even for a moment — they are always in His association. So even in this world you can be happy.
- Christian brothers' prayer: "Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven." Real religion is not sectarian; the truth can be found in any bona-fide spiritual tradition. "We're just trying in our own humble way to present that truth."
- We can have a version of the kingdom of God here in the material world if we set our life around Jagannatha, around Krishna — happy here, but supremely happy by going back to the spiritual world "where happiness has no beginning and no end."

## The Guarantee — a Taste Superior to Anything Material

- This whole Ratha Yatra festival is to give that experience. If you really embrace it and participate, "you will experience something superior to anything you ever tried to enjoy in this material world. That we can guarantee" — a taste better than what the material world offers at its best.
- Proof: how else have all these boys and girls renounced the materialistic way of life and taken up a set of disciplined rules and regulations "so disciplined that it makes the materialist shake in fear" — yet remain always happy in Krishna consciousness?
- "The smiling religion": One man told the speaker, "I know your religion — you're the smiling religion." The four regulative principles — no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling — are very difficult for most people, but easy for devotees "because we have that higher taste, that deeper spiritualization, that joy which comes from being Krishna conscious."

## How to Maintain the Experience — the Holy Name

- New people will experience a special atmosphere here and then leave. How to keep it, since Ratha Yatra comes only once a year?
- Answer: another incarnation of the Lord, also **Patita-pavana**, is the Lord's holy name. Out of infinite mercy the Lord has appeared in the form of sound vibration. The Lord's incarnations are endless, and one is through sound.
- It is not like material sound: if you're thirsty and chant "water, water, water," you don't quench your thirst, because the word "water" and the object are different. But God is absolute — He is fully present in the sound of His name, the reservoir of all pleasure.
- Whether you say Hallelujah, Jah, Yahweh, Allah Akbar, Buddha, or Krishna, "the Lord is dancing on the tip of the tongue," and you can always be happy, always in contact with the Lord, always in the spiritual world — even here in the material world, even in "this dreary town of Belfast" — if you remain in contact through the singing of holy names: Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama, Hare Rama.

## The Greatest Gift and the Essence

- This is the greatest gift we can give. "This movement is just about giving." **Spiritual mathematics:** one minus one equals a thousand. In material life, if you give something away it is lost forever; but in spiritual life, if you give something, you gain even more by the Lord.
- So the speaker is happy to come to Belfast and give this nectarean process. He must leave in a couple of hours, but you can remain in blissful Krishna consciousness by taking one thing home. If nothing else — no gift shop, no book display seen — "just take this one chant, this one mantra, this one psalm with you": Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.
- Commonly said in ISKCON today: "Chant and be happy." But what Prabhupada originally said (the speaker was there in London in the early days) was "Chant and your life will be sublime" — preferred, "because happy is one thing, but sublime is refined happiness, spiritual happiness — the happiness of being reinstated in our original loving relationship with Jagannatha, with the Lord of the universe, with Krishna, with God."
- **The essence of Ratha Yatra** — the essence of Indian culture, of Vedic culture, of the Lord's coming into this material world — "is: chant Hare Krishna and your lives will be sublime." Thank you very much. Hare Krishna.

## Glossary

- **Jagannatha** — 'Lord of the Universe'; the deity form of Krishna worshipped at Puri
- **Ratha-yatra** — the festival of the chariots in which Jagannath is drawn through the streets
- **Patita-pavana** — 'deliverer of the most fallen,' a title of Jagannath, Lord Chaitanya, and the holy name
- **Subhadra** — Krishna's sister, worshipped alongside Jagannath and Baladeva
- **arca-vigraha** — the authorized deity form in which the Lord accepts worship in the temple
- **dharma** — religious and spiritual principles; the eternal duty of the soul to serve God
- **Kali-yuga** — the present age of quarrel and hypocrisy
- **maha-mantra** — the great chant, Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama...
- **Bharata-varsha** — India; 'the land of light,' the seat of spiritual culture
