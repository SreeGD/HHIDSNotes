# In Search of Humility (Developing Humility(

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 53 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:09:40

**Themes:** humility · pride and false ego · chanting hare krishna · being an instrument · sankirtana · serving vaishnavas

## Summary

Speaking at New Raman Reti (Alachua) from Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.5.11, Prahlada Maharaja's prayer, Indradyumna Swami chooses to go back to basics and examine the foundation of devotional life, chanting Hare Krishna, arguing that humility is its most essential ingredient, the sugar in the cake without which the chanting cannot yield its full fruit. He reviews the stages of chanting (offensive, clearing, and offenseless or pure) and of bhakti (from sraddha through bhava and prema), noting that the ultimate fruit of the holy name is prema and even vipralambha-bhava, the feeling of separation that Haridasa Thakura and Mahaprabhu express in the Siksastakam. The heart of the talk is Siksastakam verse 3 (trinad api sunicena), to chant thinking oneself lower than a straw, more tolerant than a tree, free of false prestige and offering all respect to others, which he calls Prabhupada's formula for love of God, illustrated by Prabhupada telling his godbrother Dr. O.B.L. Kapoor in his chemical lab that he knew the formula but did not know how to apply it, and which Mahaprabhu personally gave both to the assembled devotees in Adi-lila 17 and to Raghunatha dasa Goswami in Antya-lila. Because humility's opposite is pride, he analyzes what devotees are falsely proud of, the body (a prisoner's stripes, a sign of independence from Krishna), possessions, and above all their own achievements in devotional service, warning that attachment to fame is the hardest thing to renounce, shown by his Kumbha Mela account of renunciate sadhus parading proudly on elephants and a Shankaracharya-follower's beautiful Krishna-katha spoiled by the impersonal conclusion tat tvam asi. He insists devotees can take no credit because they are only instruments in the hands of guru and Krishna (the dull-versus-sharp-knife analogy, Prabhupada blushing and crediting his Guru Maharaja, and the punar musiko bhava story of a mouse turned into cat, dog, and tiger and then back into a mouse), and that the Lord even strips away opulence to keep His devotees humble, as when Arjuna lost his weapons and was defeated by cowherds after Krishna's departure, and as Kunti prays that the Lord is approached only by the materially exhausted. He closes with practical means to uproot pride: living simply through yukta-vairagya (taking by need not greed, and the reminder that even the body belongs to Krishna, as Mahaprabhu forbade Sanatana Goswami to end his life), serving other Vaishnavas menially (King Prataparudra sweeping the road with a golden broom to win Mahaprabhu's embrace), voluntarily accepting a lowly position, and going out on sankirtana where no one offers you guru-puja, the natural school of humility, recalling Prabhupada chanting and selling fifty copies of Easy Journey to Other Planets in a Mexico City park in 1973.

## The Verse: Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.11 (Prahlada Maharaja)

- Speaking from the **Seventh Canto**, one of Prabhupada's favorite cantos — most of his lectures came from the Seventh Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam ("like father, like son"). Chapter Five: Prahlada, the saintly son of Hiranyakashipu. **Text 11.**
- First invokes auspiciousness: **Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya** (twice).
- Word-for-word (from the board): *ari* — enemy; *sva* — a kinsman or friend; *cha* — also; *iti* — thus; *asad-graha* — material conception of life; *pumsam* — of persons; *yat* — of whom; *mayaya* — by the external energy; *krita* — created; *vimohita* — bewildered; *dhiyam* — of those whose intelligence; *drishta* — practically experienced; *tasmai* — unto him; *bhagavate* — the Supreme Personality of Godhead; *namah* — my respectful obeisances.
- **Translation (SB 7.5.11):** *"Sri Prahlada Maharaja replied: Let me offer my respectful obeisances unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead, whose external energy has created the distinctions of 'my friend' and 'my enemy' by deluding the intelligence of men. Indeed, I am now actually experiencing this, although I have previously heard of it from authoritative sources."*

## Prabhupada's Purport: Equal Vision and Vaishnava Humility

- Cites **Bhagavad-gita 5.18** — *"vidya-vinaya-sampanne brahmane gavi hastini / shuni chaiva shvapake cha panditah sama-darshinah"* — "The humble sage, by virtue of true knowledge, sees with equal vision a learned and gentle brahmana, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and a dog-eater (outcaste)."
- The **panditas** — those actually learned, equipoised, advanced devotees with full knowledge — do not see any living entity as enemy or friend. With broader vision they see that everyone is part of Krishna, as confirmed by Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu: **"jivera svarupa haya krishnera nitya-dasa"** — every living entity, being part of the Supreme Lord, is meant to serve Him, just as every part of the body serves the whole body. As servants of the Supreme Lord, all living entities are one.
- But a Vaishnava, because of his natural humility, addresses every other living being as **"Prabhu"** (master). A Vaishnava sees other servants as so advanced that he has much to learn from them; thus he accepts all other devotees as Prabhus. Although everyone is a servant of the Lord, one Vaishnava servant, out of humility, sees another as his master.
- Understanding of the master begins from understanding of the spiritual master. By the mercy of the spiritual master one receives the benediction of Krishna; without the grace of the spiritual master one cannot make any advancement.
- (Translation read again in full.)

## Choosing the Topic: Back to Basics — Chanting Hare Krishna

- At a Sunday feast the speaker is generally allowed to choose his own topic, and in Krishna consciousness there are unlimited transcendental topics. Today, though, "go back to basics" — the very common but essential theme of chanting Hare Krishna, which we were just doing.
- Chanting Hare Krishna is the basic activity in devotional service. Mahaprabhu encourages us to gradually wind down all our other activities to **Kirtanad** — as in the third verse of the Shikshashtakam, **"kirtaniyah sada harih"** — to chant 24 hours a day, if we could.
- **Story of Malati in the hospital (Bombay):** She had jaundice/hepatitis. When Prabhupada came to see her, she was sitting up chanting japa and, surprised, apologized: "Prabhupada, I didn't do any service today, I just chanted Hare Krishna." Prabhupada said: "No, that is the goal. Because you Western boys and girls are so restless, I have to think of so many engagements for you. But one day, when we are old, we can just sit and chant."
- Prabhupada writes in a Bhagavatam purport that one idea behind establishing the **Krishna-Balaram Mandir** in Vrindavan was that in old age the members of this movement could retire to Vrindavan and chant Hare Krishna. We all look forward to that.

## Repairing the Foundation of Our Chanting

- We are **sadhakas**, practicing the chanting now — it is the very foundation of our Krishna consciousness. Sometimes we have to look back and see if the foundation needs repair work.
- **Story (Dharma Prabhu's house):** In anticipation of last night's program and kirtan, Dharma Prabhu redid the foundation of his home, spending $1,000 for one kirtan — "a good investment." Had he not put in the new foundation, the walls would have crumbled.
- Just as we repair a physical foundation, we speak of another repair: seeing whether we are chanting purely, to get the maximum result.
- There are three stages: offensive chanting, the clearing stage, and **shuddha-nama** (chanting without offenses). We must sometimes analyze where we are so we know the next step and how far we have to go. We cannot just "chant, chant, chant" — this is **buddhi-yoga**, using our intelligence analytically: "Where am I now on the path back home, back to Godhead? Am I at step one, two, or three?" so we can feel confident of some advancement.
- Cites the first half of Rupa Goswami's verse on the progression of bhakti (**"adau shraddha tatah sadhu-sango 'tha bhajana-kriya / tato 'nartha-nivrittih syat..."** — from faith, to association with devotees, to devotional practice, to the clearing of unwanted things): gaining faith by associating with devotees; taking up **bhajana-kriya** (the regulative principles, getting initiated, serving the spiritual master, accomplishing something on his behalf, purifying the heart). After a lifetime of devotional service comes steadiness (**nishtha**), then **ruchi** (actual taste), letting go of material attachments in favor of the taste from chanting and serving the holy name; then maybe some **bhava** — a little tear from the eye when we chant — and maybe some **prema**. We must analyze where we are.

## Chanting Is a Question of the Heart

- Wherever we are, we must chant the holy name purely. It is not just repeating the mantra — it is a question of the heart.
- **Prabhupada pastime (one of the speaker's favorites):** A guest asked Prabhupada, "What is the meaning of this mantra you're chanting over and over?" Prabhupada could have given his standard authoritative explanation: *"My dear Lord, O energy of the Lord (Srimati Radhika), please engage me in Your devotional service again."*
  - Technically it is not really a mantra, because a mantra has a **bija** (seed) — like *klim, shrim, aim* — from which the potency comes, and you say the bija before the line. But there is no bija here; we don't say "Om, Hare Krishna." It is more like a **prayer**, and a prayer is from the heart.
  - So how did Prabhupada explain it to that boy? He gave a very special answer: *"It means, my Lord, my friend, my friend, my friend. We are calling on the mercy of the Lord: Please pick me up from this ocean of death, and fix me as an atom at Your lotus feet."*

## The Fire of Digestion: Getting the Full Benefit

- It is important to discuss this chanting again and again — not just to chant, but to discuss how to chant purely.
- **Analogy:** If the fire of digestion is not strong, no matter how much food you eat, the nourishment will not be distributed throughout the body. Ayurveda is all about the fire of digestion — stoking it, getting it working — so that when you eat, the food is distributed nicely.
- **Story (the Ayurvedic doctor):** The speaker visited an Ayurvedic doctor who was a bit sleepy from seeing many patients. Taking his hand, the doctor asked his age. "I'm almost 60." The doctor said: "Oh, you have the digestion of an 18-year-old boy — in modern terms, you have a tiger in your tank."
- In the same way, we must chant so as to get the full benefit of the chanting.

## The Benefits of Chanting — Up to the Ultimate Benefit

- Prabhupada said there are many benefits from chanting Hare Krishna:
  - Even a fallen person can chant. In Islam, **"Haram! Haram!"** means "how horrible" — a man being killed by a boar while hunting in the forest cries out "Haram, haram!" Because those two syllables (*Ha, Ra*) are found in the Maha-mantra, he gets purified — so much benefit, even from unconsciously saying the name of the Lord while being killed.
  - The holy names are powerful enough to uproot the strong material desires that have kept us bound to material existence "like ropes tied to a stake," life after life after life. It unleashes those desires.
- **The ultimate benefit** is explained by the greatest chanter of the holy names, **Haridasa Thakura, the Namacharya**, who told the august assembly of brahmanas that the ultimate benefit is that by chanting you get **prema**.
  - Cites the seventh verse of the **Shikshashtakam** (Lord Chaitanya's words): *"O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain. I am feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence."*
  - This exalted state of **vipralambha-bhava** (separation from Krishna) is the goal of chanting the holy names.
- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** says: upon chanting Hare Krishna, if you don't experience some of these symptoms, then there is something not perfect (not "wrong") with the chanting. No one is "wrong" in chanting — you can only be right, absolutely right, no matter how you chant — but the full benefit requires pure chanting.

## The Most Important Ingredient: Humility

- **Analogy (the birthday cake):** Last night there was a program for a little girl's 12th birthday, and a sweet devotee made her a nice cake. A cake has many ingredients, but one of the more important is the **sugar**. Imagine a beautiful cake with thick, soft icing — (aside: in Russia they call them "torts," but the speaker, raised in America with apple pie and ice cream, is never really satisfied; "they don't know how to make a cake") — but you only have a real cake if it has the sugar in it.
- So what could be the most important ingredient in our chanting? **Our humility.** What does humility have to do with chanting? According to Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, it has everything to do with chanting.

## The Famous Verse: Trinad api Sunichena

- The famous verse: *"one should chant the holy name of the Lord in a humble state of mind, thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street; one should be more tolerant than a tree, devoid of all sense of false prestige, and ready to offer all respect to others. In such a state of mind one can chant constantly."* (Shikshashtakam, verse 3.)
- Researching this for his own purification and understanding of chanting, the speaker discovered it is one of the most important verses in the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition.
- **The "formula for love of God" story** (from the book related by **Mula Prakriti Mataji**): As a grihastha, Prabhupada (Abhay) worked in the pharmaceutical business in his chemical laboratory. One day, while mixing chemicals, his godbrother **Dr. O.B.L. Kapoor** stopped by: "Abhay, how are you doing? What's going on down there in the laboratory?" Prabhupada said, "Oh, I have just discovered the formula for love of God." His godbrother said, "Wow, I'm coming right down." He came down with paper and pen: "What is it? Some potassium mixed with a little of this or that? Quick — which ingredients, and how much of each?" Prabhupada said, "Well, I know the formula, but I don't know how to apply it." Kapoor pressed: "Just give me the formula — it's so rare, we can drink it and have love of God! Hurry! How much potassium?"
  - Prabhupada said: *"Here it is — Trinad api sunichena, taror api sahishnuna, amanina manadena, kirtaniyah sada harih. This is the formula for love of God."* But in his humility Prabhupada added, "I don't know how to apply it."

## Lord Chaitanya Personally Teaches This Verse — Twice in the Chaitanya-charitamrita

- Besides being part of Lord Chaitanya's immortal **Shikshashtakam** prayers, we find Lord Chaitanya personally instructing His devotees in this verse at least twice in the **Chaitanya-charitamrita**.

### First: Adi-lila, Chapter 17

- After Lord Chaitanya takes prasadam prepared by a devotee called **Shuklambara Brahmachari**, He calls all the devotees over — just like in kirtan, calling everyone from the far end of the temple room: "Let's get together and chant Hare Krishna."
- He stressed this particular shloka because He was the **Yuga-avatar** introducing the Yuga-dharma (chanting Hare Krishna), so He quoted from Shastra — "God Himself quotes from His own Shastra."
- Towards the end of the chapter He instructs them (then simply speaking, now recorded as a verse): "My dear devotees, I want you all to chant the holy name of the Lord in a very humble state of mind. Feel yourself lower than the straw in the street; be more tolerant than a tree; be devoid of a sense of false prestige; and be ready to offer respects to everyone else. If you do this, I promise you will be able to chant Hare Krishna 24 hours."
- Then, raising His hands, Mahaprabhu declared: *"Everyone please hear me — string this verse on the thread of the holy name and wear it on your neck for continuous remembrance."*
- These are Mahaprabhu's instructions — very important; we should open our hearts and etch them in. That is God speaking.
  - **Illustration:** When any important person enters an assembly, everyone listens. If President Bush came here and stood up, nobody would listen, because he's not very important (aside: "I hope there are no CIA members here"). But if our GBC Shesha Prabhu walked in and said, "I have something to say," everyone would respond, "Yes sir, what is it?" So who is the most important person? **"ishvarah paramah krishnah sat-chid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam"** (Brahma-samhita 5.1). When He walks in, or when His words are repeated by the speaker, all our ears should be open and we should absorb.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** paraphrased "wear it on your neck" as: we should wear this verse **like a garland around our mind**.
  - On garlands: Prabhupada told us in Detroit that "the only opulence left in Kali Yuga is flowers." A garland worn by **Sri Sri Radha-Shyamasundara** is very special — it is part of the process of deity worship to wear a garland worn by the Lord. Sometimes devotees make them small and put them around the forehead; "What are you doing? That looks so funny — just wear it." But no, they are doing the right thing: you can take the garland and wear it around your forehead — even the men can do it — following the instructions of Bhaktisiddhanta to wear this devotional paraphernalia, a garland around our mind.

### Second: Antya-lila — Instructions to Raghunatha Dasa Goswami

- We should know these books like a lawyer knows his law books — knowing so many previous cases, details, and intricacies in order to win. So to win the hard struggle for material existence, gain victory in chanting, and go back to Godhead, we must know these books. This verse is repeated again in the **Antya-lila**, in the instructions to Raghunatha Dasa Goswami — "maybe my favorite part" of the Chaitanya-charitamrita.
- Two very important verses Mahaprabhu instructs him: *"Do not talk like people in general or hear what they have to say (the internet); do not eat very palatable food, nor should you dress very nicely; do not expect honor, but offer respect to others; always chant the holy names, and within your mind render service to Radha and Krishna in Vrindavan."* These two verses are the essence of our Gaudiya philosophy.
- **Why so few verses:** They were really the only verses Mahaprabhu gave Raghunatha Dasa Goswami, because when Raghunatha Dasa came to Jagannatha Puri, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu immediately placed him in the care of **Svarupa Damodara Goswami** — His very personal, intimate servant, who was **Lalita** in Krishna-lila. Lalita is the guru of the gopis, so he knew all the intricacies; he brought forth the **Radha-bhava** of Gauranga.
  - **Two reasons for Mahaprabhu's appearance:** (1) As the Yuga-avatar, to introduce the chanting; (2) because Krishna wanted to experience the loving sentiments of Radharani — what is the nature of this love, how does it work, what is its sweetness? The two became one in the personality of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Those spiritual emotions were experienced primarily in His Antya-lila, after His childhood pastimes and after He spread the Sankirtan movement all over India; He then retired to Jagannatha Puri and accomplished the second purpose of His appearance — to experience Radha-bhava. This was helped by Svarupa Damodara Goswami, who would sing various verses from the Tenth Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam to bring the rasa — the feelings of love for Krishna that Radha had — out in the heart of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
  - So generally it was Svarupa Damodara Goswami who instructed Raghunatha Dasa, but on this occasion Raghunatha Dasa got some **personal** instructions directly from Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and we can understand how he was hankering for that.
- **General and personal instructions:** All of us would like personal instructions from our spiritual master. We have the general instruction, but we consider ourselves particularly blessed if our spiritual master gives us a particular service to make our lifetime mission.
  - **Example — Prabhupada:** He received the general instructions in class, but what did Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati personally tell him? "If you ever get money, print books." At the Bhagbazar temple in Calcutta, when disciples were quarreling over rooms and the preaching spirit was going down, Bhaktisiddhanta said, "I would sell all the marble in this new temple if I could, to buy a printing press to print books." And in the very beginning he told Prabhupada, "You look like an educated gentleman; why don't you preach Krishna consciousness in the English language?" Prabhupada took those few sentences — preach in English, and if you ever get money, print books — and based the rest of his life on those instructions. It is very nice to get instructions from your guru, both general and personal, that you can think about your whole life.

## Renunciation Runs Through Every Ashram

- These instructions were to Raghunatha, but to all of us as well, because although we may not be sannyasis, the thread of renunciation runs through our entire lives.
- Even grihasthas are meant to be renounced — not living like **grihamedhis**. The grihastha is an ashram of mind control and sense control, and they give **dana** (charity). It hurts to give away hard-earned money — you want to keep it, utilize it, save it for the future — but householders remain renounced by giving charity.
- Grihasthas actually support all the other ashrams: the brahmacharis, the vanaprasthas, the sannyasis. They have that burden of love upon them, and they give.
- When you give in Krishna consciousness, it should pinch, hurt a little — love means you give something significant. When we give presents to the deities, give something valuable (as the **Nectar of Devotion** says). So the grihasthas give for the propagation of Krishna consciousness.
- **The last instruction and embrace:** After His few instructions to Raghunatha Goswami, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's last instruction was **Trinad api sunichena...** — and then He embraced him. Again, that is the essence: we should chant in a humble state of mind.

## Pride Is the Opposite of Humility — What Are We Proud Of?

- The opposite of humility is **pride** — two sides of the same coin, heads and tails. You have to get rid of the pride in order to be humble. So what are we proud of?

### Pride in the Body

- "Mirror, mirror on the wall — who is the most beautiful of them all?" "You are." "Very intelligent reflection, thank you." We can be proud of our bodies — that's the problem. All bodies are the same, made of earth, air, fire, water, mind, intelligence, and false ego. But because one is shaped an inch differently, a different color, hair a little softer, a different shape, we think "that one's beautiful, that one's not." This is **maya**, illusion.
- The body is the sign of our independence from Krishna. When you fall down into the prison house of material existence, you get a material body. You should think of it like the black-and-white **striped uniform** prisoners used to wear, by which they could be identified if they escaped. Prisoners weren't proud of the stripes — they wanted to get rid of them and put on civilian clothes. But we look at our stripes and think, "Yes, beautiful — if only everyone else could appreciate." Maya means "that which is not": we are proud of something we shouldn't be proud of.
- **Real birthday:** We celebrate birthdays, but the real birthday is the day of initiation. There is a Prabhupada lecture: "Deathlessness begins at the moment of initiation." That is our second birth. We shouldn't celebrate our birth into ignorance — all living entities are born into ignorance, overcome by desire and hatred (the exact Gita verse escapes him). Not "I was born into ignorance 25 years ago" — rather, celebrate the day you got initiation, going back home, back to Godhead.

### Pride in Possessions

- Cites Srimad Bhagavatam (5.5.8): *"The attraction between male and female is the basic principle of material existence. On the basis of this misconception, which ties together the hearts of the male and the female, one becomes attracted to his body, wife, husband, children, house, household paraphernalia, village, country, and universe as 'I, me, and mine.' Thus one increases life's illusions and thinks in terms of 'I and mine.'"*
- So we can be attached and proud of our possessions — "my this and my that." Even a beggar could be proud of his penny.

### Pride in Our Deeds (the Greatest Danger)

- We can be proud of our deeds — what I have done and accomplished as a devotee. Because devotees generally live simply, we don't have a surplus of millionaires; perhaps the greatest problem we have is being proud of our **achievements in devotional service**.
- This is what Mahaprabhu means (Shikshashtakam 4): *"I have no desire for wealth, no desire for beautiful ladies, no desire for name, fame, or prestige. All that I want is that I might have Your causeless devotional service in my life, birth after birth."*

## The Hardest Attachment to Give Up: Fame

- Transcendentalists can give up material opulence and the opposite sex, but it is often very hard to give up the attachment to being known as a famous transcendentalist.
- **Story (Kumbha Mela):** Years ago the speaker was impressed at first by the renunciation of some sadhus — living with the bare necessities, and, as renunciates, freed from association with the opposite sex ("for lust alone, O Arjuna, is the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world"). But later he saw them on parade on big elephants, waving and clearly enjoying being famous. They had renounced women and money, but seemed to relish being famous.
- Then he went to one of their lectures — a big Shankaracharya (a follower of Shankaracharya): skinny, long hair, austere, powerful. He spoke so nicely about Krishna — Bal Krishna, baby Krishna, how cute He is, teasing the gopis, the demon pastimes, bringing down Trinavarta, getting bound by the ropes of Mother Yashoda. Then he said, **"Tat tvam asi — thou art that Krishna."** The speaker thought, "Oh my God, I'm getting out of here," and had to go bathe and jump in the Triveni and scrub it off. Such a person is very proud.

## We Are Only Instruments — Not Credit-Takers

- So we have to deal with pride — pride of what we've done, or think we've done.
- **Raghunatha Dasa Goswami** says (from his prayers): *"O mind, a shameless, low-born prostitute who eats dog meat is dancing in my heart in the form of desire for fame. How can pure love for Krishna ever find a place in my heart?"* He is the greatest devotee, but the great devotees teach us by example.
- We must always remind ourselves that we can't take credit for anything we've done. How are we able to do it at all? Because **we are instruments in the hands of Guru and Krishna.** We want to make advancement, become advanced — not so that we'll become famous, but so we can be a valuable instrument in the hands of Guru and Krishna to spread the Sankirtan movement all over the world.
- **Analogy (the dull knife):** Our ladies will say it's very frustrating to cut vegetables with a dull knife — really hard work — but with a sharp knife it's very easy. If we are advanced, if our heart is cleansed of material desires and we have some attachment to chanting Hare Krishna, then our Gurudev can use us very nicely in the mission of Krishna consciousness. This is why we want to be advanced.
- Cites **Bhagavad-gita 11.33**: *"Therefore get up, Arjuna. Prepare to fight and win glory. Conquer your enemies and enjoy a flourishing kingdom. They are already put to death by My arrangement, and you, O Savyasachi, can be but an instrument in the fight."*

## There Is a Lot of Work to Be Done — "You Are Not Ready Yet"

- Prabhupada told the GBC: "I have a plan to take over the world in 18 days, like the Battle of Kurukshetra." "Wow, Prabhupada, what's the plan?" Prabhupada said: "But you're not ready yet."
- **Story (Mayapur lawn):** Prabhupada was lecturing on the lawn outside "the castle" (his house), speaking about how a devotee sees Krishna everywhere. He asked for questions, and the speaker raised his hand: "Srila Prabhupada, you say the devotee sees Krishna everywhere, but in the Shikshashtakam prayers, Lord Chaitanya — in the mood of a devotee — feels so much separation from Krishna. It appears contradictory: how can one see Krishna but at the same time feel separation from Krishna?" (He thought it was a very intelligent question.) Prabhupada thought, then looked him straight in the eyes; embarrassed, the speaker had to look down. Prabhupada said: **"You will understand the answer to that question when you are very advanced."** Not now.
- So Prabhupada said he could take the world, but he didn't have the instruments to "prepare the feast." He is waiting for us. This movement could spread like wildfire.

## Our Heritage of Great Vaishnavas

- We have inherited the legacy of so many wonderful Vaishnavas — our heroes: Srila Prabhupada, Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, Gaura Kishora Dasa Babaji, Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, Baladeva Vidyabhushana, Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura, Narottama Dasa Thakura, Shyamananda Prabhu, Srinivasa Acharya, the Six Goswamis, Lord Chaitanya, Radha-Krishna.
- Prabhupada said: "Lord Ramachandra conquered Lanka with monkeys and bears. So I am also conquering the world with my disciples." But we don't want to be monkeys and bears our whole life. Children are cute, but we don't want them to stay little children forever — we want them to grow up into mature Vaishnavas, come on the Polish tour, and dance their Bharatanatyam. It would be weird if they stayed five years old for life. We expect our children to grow up.

## The Best Gift to the Spiritual Master: Our Advancement

- The best gift we can give our spiritual master is our progress in Krishna consciousness. On his Vyasa-puja we give flowers and a nice feast symbolically, but the best thing we can give guru is to relinquish material desires, let go of our independence from Krishna, embrace Guru and Krishna's lotus feet and their service, and become full of transcendental qualities they can use in spreading the movement.
- When you look at a devotee, you see a repository of all beautiful qualities.
- **Story (London policeman):** Prabhupada was walking across a street in London when a policeman grabbed Shyamasundara Prabhu, his secretary, and said: "Look, your teacher's glowing like the sun." When people come in contact with a Vaishnava, they should feel, "This is something special — I can't put my finger on it, but there's something special here." From that association, they become determined: "I want to be like them. I'm tired of being depressed and stressed and anxious and unhappy. I want to be like these people who have happy faces shining with joy, who live a pure life, who are going back home, back to Godhead. Enough of this material existence." That conviction comes from associating with devotees who possess transcendental qualities.
- Such devotees are rare. **Chanakya Pandita** says: "You won't find rubies in every mountain, you won't find sandalwood trees in every forest [joking, 'in Alachua'], and you won't find genuine sadhus in every birth. If you find any of those things — grab them."
- So we want to become advanced so Gurudev can engage us nicely in preaching Krishna consciousness — we must be the instruments. So we have to overcome this pride very quickly, remembering that by the mercy of our spiritual master we are able to do anything and everything in Krishna consciousness.

## Learning Humility from Brahma

- How do we develop that mood? By looking toward advanced devotees who have developed it — like **Brahma**, the first disciple, who was humble; he is the head of our **Brahma-Gaudiya-Madhva-sampradaya**.
- Cites Brahma's prayer (Srimad Bhagavatam): *"I pray to engage in the Lord's service in the creation of the material world and not to be materially affected by my work. Thus I may be able to give up the false prestige of being the creator."*
- This was the challenge for Brahma. Look what he accomplished — study the world around us and appreciate what he put together. He is the engineer of this universe. Even the most intelligent scientists are baffled by the simplest aspects of this creation; they have only discovered "the tip of the iceberg." All that we see took **four heads** to create — something quite amazing. But he doesn't want to take any credit.
- **Story (Prabhupada in Paris):** On his Vyasa-puja we glorified Prabhupada: "Prabhupada, you've done so much spreading this movement." He blushed — literally, his cheeks went pink — looked down, and said: "No, no, it is all the mercy of my Guru Maharaj." He followed in the footsteps of such devotees.
- In the **Brahma-vimohana-lila**, after Brahma stole all of Krishna's cows and cowherd boys, he witnessed the Lord's glory and prayed (cites *"jananta eva janantu..."*): *"There are people who say, 'I know everything about Krishna.' Let them think that way. As far as I am concerned, I do not wish to speak very much about this matter. O my Lord, let me say this much: as far as Your opulences are concerned, they are beyond the reach of my mind, body, and words."* Everything is the mercy of the Lord.

## Punar Mushiko Bhava: The Story of the Mouse

- We should never think otherwise, or we risk **"punar mushiko bhava"** — again becoming a mouse.
- **The story** (told "as if new"): There was a mouse, and there was a cat in the neighborhood. This mouse had a little intelligence (buddhi) and thought, "Let me go to a yogi — yogis have mystic powers — and ask him to turn me into a cat so the cat won't bother me." He went to the yogi: "Please turn me into a cat." "Yes."
  - In due course a dog came — and dogs and cats are natural enemies, so the dog chased the poor cat in circles around the town. The cat ran to the yogi: "Quick, kindly sir, turn me into a dog." Done; the other dog ran off with its tail between its legs.
  - The dog thought everything was fine — until suddenly a Bengali tiger (the royal tiger of Bengal) came to town. (Aside: "I always wondered how a tiger came to town — it's a village.") The tiger began to chase the dog, who very quickly went back to the "jogi": "Quick — tiger!" "Okay." The tiger looked at the yogi, thinking, "He's a little skinny, but he'll be nice and crunchy."
  - The yogi understood what was happening, so he said: **"You rascal — again become a mouse!"** Squeak, squeak, squeak.
- So we are what we are only by the mercy of guru. Therefore Bhaktivinoda Thakura sings, "I am running behind you, guru."

## The Art of Devotional Service: Advance Yet Remain Humble

- This is the trick, the art of devotional service — to be able to advance and accomplish a lot, yet remain humble.
- How to do it: Remember Gurudeva at least three times a day. Give credit where credit is due.

## The Lord Removes Pride from His Devotees — Arjuna's Lost Powers

- The Lord doesn't want to see any pride in His devotees; He wants them to advance, so sometimes He arranges to remove it. Just as with Arjuna — though these are great personalities, they are put into illusion to teach us a lesson.
- **The pastime:** Once, after the Battle of Kurukshetra had been fought and won, and Krishna returned to the spiritual world, the mighty Arjuna was escorting some cowherd girls (gopis) through a forest with all his divine weapons. They were attacked by a group of forest dwellers with sticks and stones — and, astonishingly, these dwellers defeated Arjuna and all his divine weapons, and kidnapped the gopis. Previously Arjuna could fight all the Kuru warriors; now suddenly all his powers were gone.
- **Why:** These powers had been required for fighting the Battle of Kurukshetra, because Krishna was arranging the killing of so many demons, some more powerful than Arjuna. So Krishna arranged for Arjuna to receive fabulous weapons — the **Gandiva bow** (from Lord Shiva, after defeating him in battle), his chariot, his quiver, and his **inexhaustible arrows** (a modern-day comparison: the cartridge in a .38 has nine rounds — but this was an inexhaustible gun); he got the quiver from **Agni**. But when those powers were no longer required, Krishna took them away, because He wants His devotee to remain meek and humble. "The Lord giveth and the Lord taketh away," according to Kunti.
- **Kunti's prayers** on opulence as a source of trouble (Srimad Bhagavatam 1.8.26): *"My Lord, You can be easily approached, but only by those who are materially exhausted. One who is on the path of material progress — trying to improve himself with respectable parentage, great opulence, high education, and bodily beauty — cannot approach You with sincere feeling."*

## Yukta-vairagya: Using Everything in Krishna's Service

- So how are we approaching Krishna? (Chants the Maha-mantra.) You can approach Krishna with opulence — it doesn't mean you have to give everything away and live like a beggar. **Yukta-vairagya** — real renunciation — means to see everything as belonging to Krishna and to use it in His service.
- **Story (the gold coin):** People walking down the street see a gold coin. The **karmi** picks it up: "Ah, I can buy something for my girlfriend." The **impersonalist** walks by: "Oh, it's maya," and crosses to the other side of the street. The **devotee** picks it up: "Ah, I can buy something nice for Radha-Shyamasundara." So you can be rich — it's a question of yukta-vairagya, realizing that everything belongs to Krishna.
- Even this body — I don't have a right to utilize this body for my own sense gratification.
- **Story (Sanatana Goswami):** Sanatana Goswami was going to commit suicide, feeling so fallen because he had sores all over his body from walking through a forest and drinking contaminated water. Mahaprabhu would come every day and embrace him, and the sores would ooze onto the divine body of Mahaprabhu. Sanatana decided to give up his body under the wheels of the Ratha-yatra chariot. Mahaprabhu found out and came to him: "I know what you want to do — you want to give up your body. But you don't have a right to give up that body; it belongs to Me, and I have many plans for how I will utilize that body in My mission. So don't kill yourself."
- So we don't have a right to enjoy what is not ours. Did you create this body? Are you maintaining it? Do you ultimately have the power to destroy or dissolve it? No — it is all being done by God. We have to use everything in His service.
- So when the Lord's mission was finished, Arjuna's mission was finished, and He took away the weapons.
- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's comment** on that pastime: the dacoits who defeated Arjuna were actually Krishna Himself — for who else could touch the gopis? Who could touch Sita? (Ravana only took the **Maya-Sita**; Agni kept the real Sita and returned her eventually in the fire pastime.)

## Conclusion: Three Ways to Get Rid of Pride

### 1. Live a Simple Life

- Cites **Sri Ishopanishad, mantra 1**: *"Everything animate or inanimate within the universe is controlled and owned by the Lord. One should therefore accept only those things necessary for himself, set aside as his quota, and one should not accept other things, knowing well to whom they belong."* The quota differs for the brahmachari, grihastha, vanaprastha, and sannyasi.
- As Bhaktivinoda Thakura said, "man's glory lies in his common sense." Take according to need, not greed (as Gandhi said). "If you ain't got nothing, you ain't got nothing to lose" — "I just got a loincloth and it's torn; nobody wants it." If you don't have too many material possessions, you don't have too much to be proud about.
- **Story (Prabhupada's marriage):** Prabhupada's father, Gour Mohan, married him to a girl who wasn't very pretty. Prabhupada later understood why and thanked his father: he did not become overly attached, and became attached to his wife because of her devotional qualities, not her external beauty.
- So we don't mind being deficient in something material — we think, "This is a blessing." We get diseased, sick, old, and ugly, and we think, "Finally, now I can really focus on Krishna. Who's going to look at my ugly face anymore? But I can look at the beautiful face of Radha-Shyamasundara." Anything that happens to a devotee, he considers his good fortune. Simple living, high thinking — that helps with pride.

### 2. Serve Other Devotees

- Cites the medicine for the age of Kali: for those bitten by the serpent of Kali/samsara, relief from the pain comes through the "medicinal herb" of **dancing in kirtan** — not being a wallflower but coming into the middle, lifting the arms in the air, dancing and singing — and through **"Srimad Vaishnava-sevanam,"** serving the Vaishnavas.
- What kind of service? Menial, humble service: "Can I wash your clothes today? Can I help you with the dishes? Do you need a little extra money for your petrol?" We can serve the Vaishnavas.
- Cites the Lord's statement (Adi Purana): *"One who says he is My devotee is not My devotee; but one who is the devotee of My devotee — he is My devotee."*
- Don't act in such a way as to see God; act in such a way that God will see you.
- **Story (Maharaja Prataparudra):** Why did Lord Chaitanya embrace Maharaja Prataparudra in the garden after the Ratha-yatra festival? Not because he was a king — sannyasis should not associate with kings, who are very worldly. For years Prataparudra tried to gain the favor of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Why did Mahaprabhu finally accept him? Because Prataparudra did some **menial service** for God: though he was king, he went down to the ground in front of Jagannatha, took a broom, and swept the dirty street in front of the Lord. Mahaprabhu caught that service out of the corner of His eye. You take one step towards God, He takes a thousand steps. Who has the credit, the adhikara, to get the embrace of God? We don't — but Prataparudra did some little service and got the mercy.
- So we can serve the Lord and serve the devotees; this helps us become free from false ego. We want people to serve us — instead, spend most of your time serving others. As President Kennedy said, "Ask not what your country can do for you; ask what you can do for your country." He almost had it — it should be, "Ask not what God can do for you; ask what you can do for God." Always be engaged in service.
- If somebody wants to serve you, you feel very uncomfortable, but you have to let them do it — otherwise how will they get purified? This too teaches us humility: "No, not me... no, please."

### 3. Put Yourself Where People See You as Less — Go on Sankirtan

- Voluntarily put yourself in a position where people see you as less than them, not greater. Not in the temple — in the temple we are taught to glorify everyone else. So where in this world will people look down on you, or see you as equal or less? On **Sankirtan** (on the streets).
- Some devotees, knowing this, feel more comfortable on the street doing Sankirtan than in the temple. It works both ways: come to the temple, get inspired, go on Sankirtan to preach; and by preaching become inspired to see the deity — back and forth. But out on Sankirtan it's hard to be proud, because no one is offering you guru-puja: "Hey, get out of the way, buddy." A good place to practice humility.
- Pride causes one to think he is better than others, but as devotees we are not better than others. Where have we come from? We have crawled out of the thick, fetid mud of material existence just "a few moments ago" in cosmic time. Where have we been most of the time? Cites the Chaitanya-charitamrita verse on wandering through the universe (**"brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krishna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija"** — the fortunate soul, wandering through the universe, by the mercy of guru and Krishna receives the seed of the creeper of devotion). Since time immemorial we have been transmigrating from one universe, one species, one body to the next; and just a few short years ago our spiritual master, by his grace alone, convinced us to take a few cautious steps in devotional service.
- So we are not better than others — but we are certainly more fortunate than them: we can see the beautiful form of Radha-Shyamasundara. So we should share our good fortune with them.
- Lord Chaitanya will purge our heart of all bad qualities (pride) and bestow all good qualities (humility) if we go out and preach the divine command. Bhaktivinoda Thakura used to pray: *"When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest, and, with a lowly heart, I will go out to preach the divine command?"*
- On Sankirtan you learn many good lessons: you are reminded of the temporary, miserable nature of this world; you witness firsthand the miraculous pastimes of Lord Chaitanya delivering conditioned souls; you become renounced from material life and attached to Krishna.
- **Story (Mexico):** Last year the speaker went on harinama in an isolated village outside Mexico City. During the kirtan, one devotee said: "Maharaj, Prabhupada came here when he visited Mexico City in 1973 and did Sankirtan — he chanted Hare Krishna in this same park, right where you're standing." The speaker immediately jumped away from that spot — one should not sit on the "vyasasana," the sacred place where the guru preached the divine command. The devotee continued: "Prabhupada led a kirtan right there, to all these people in the park who were throwing frisbees, had their dogs, and were drinking wine. Then somebody came up just at that moment with a newly printed book in Spanish — *Easy Journey to Other Planets* — and Prabhupada called the crowd forward and sold 50 copies of *Easy Journey to Other Planets* (cinco pesos each), signing all the copies." The speaker thought, "Wow — let's stay here all night, just chant and dance."
- So this activity of Sankirtan is not given up even by great souls, because great souls relish the opportunity to serve the Lord. When they get their choice of what to do, they want to preach — just as a shudra likes to work with his hands, a vaishya likes to milk cows, a kshatriya likes to go to battle, so brahmins like to preach, to destroy ignorance and reveal the absolute truth.
- So we should go out and preach, because it is very healthy for us — it helps develop one of the most important qualities in devotional service, **humility**, by which we can chant and one day see, with our spiritual eyes, the divine form of Sri Sri Radha-Shyamasundara.
