# In Search of Humility (Developing Humility(

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 53 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:09:40

## Summary

Speaking at New Raman Reti (Alachua) from Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.5.11, Prahlada Maharaja's prayer, Indradyumna Swami chooses to go back to basics and examine the foundation of devotional life, chanting Hare Krishna, arguing that humility is its most essential ingredient, the sugar in the cake without which the chanting cannot yield its full fruit. He reviews the stages of chanting (offensive, clearing, and offenseless or pure) and of bhakti (from sraddha through bhava and prema), noting that the ultimate fruit of the holy name is prema and even vipralambha-bhava, the feeling of separation that Haridasa Thakura and Mahaprabhu express in the Siksastakam. The heart of the talk is Siksastakam verse 3 (trinad api sunicena), to chant thinking oneself lower than a straw, more tolerant than a tree, free of false prestige and offering all respect to others, which he calls Prabhupada's formula for love of God, illustrated by Prabhupada telling his godbrother Dr. O.B.L. Kapoor in his chemical lab that he knew the formula but did not know how to apply it, and which Mahaprabhu personally gave both to the assembled devotees in Adi-lila 17 and to Raghunatha dasa Goswami in Antya-lila. Because humility's opposite is pride, he analyzes what devotees are falsely proud of, the body (a prisoner's stripes, a sign of independence from Krishna), possessions, and above all their own achievements in devotional service, warning that attachment to fame is the hardest thing to renounce, shown by his Kumbha Mela account of renunciate sadhus parading proudly on elephants and a Shankaracharya-follower's beautiful Krishna-katha spoiled by the impersonal conclusion tat tvam asi. He insists devotees can take no credit because they are only instruments in the hands of guru and Krishna (the dull-versus-sharp-knife analogy, Prabhupada blushing and crediting his Guru Maharaja, and the punar musiko bhava story of a mouse turned into cat, dog, and tiger and then back into a mouse), and that the Lord even strips away opulence to keep His devotees humble, as when Arjuna lost his weapons and was defeated by cowherds after Krishna's departure, and as Kunti prays that the Lord is approached only by the materially exhausted. He closes with practical means to uproot pride: living simply through yukta-vairagya (taking by need not greed, and the reminder that even the body belongs to Krishna, as Mahaprabhu forbade Sanatana Goswami to end his life), serving other Vaishnavas menially (King Prataparudra sweeping the road with a golden broom to win Mahaprabhu's embrace), voluntarily accepting a lowly position, and going out on sankirtana where no one offers you guru-puja, the natural school of humility, recalling Prabhupada chanting and selling fifty copies of Easy Journey to Other Planets in a Mexico City park in 1973.

## Key Points

- Humility is the essential ingredient of pure chanting, the sugar in the cake; its opposite, pride, must be removed to chant the holy name effectively and progress toward prema.
- Siksastakam verse 3 (trinad api sunicena) is the central formula for love of God, given by Mahaprabhu to all devotees (CC Adi 17) and to Raghunatha dasa Goswami (CC Antya); Prabhupada humbly added that he knew the formula but not how to apply it.
- Devotees falsely take pride in the body, possessions, and especially their own devotional achievements and fame; fame is the hardest attachment to give up (Kumbha Mela sadhus on elephants, and the Shankaracharya-follower's tat tvam asi).
- We can claim no credit; we are mere instruments of guru and Krishna (dull vs. sharp knife, BG 11.34 be but an instrument), and Krishna even removes powers and opulence to keep devotees humble (Arjuna defeated by cowherds after losing his weapons).
- Practical antidotes to pride include simple living and yukta-vairagya (take by need not greed, everything belongs to Krishna) and menial service to Vaishnavas (Prataparudra sweeping the road with a golden broom to earn Mahaprabhu's embrace).
- Street sankirtana is the natural training ground for humility, since no one offers you guru-puja there, and it reminds us we are not better than others, only more fortunate (CC Madhya 19.151, brahmanda bhramite).
- The goal of humble chanting is one day to see, with spiritual eyes, the divine form of Sri Sri Radha-Shyamasundara.
