# The Sacred Abode of jagannath Puri

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 51 · Foundations in Bhakti · 2:31:29

**Themes:** jagannath puri · ratha-yatra · chaitanya mahaprabhu · love in separation · deity worship · prabhupada's mission

## Summary

Indradyumna Swami devotes the third talk of his Char Dham webinar series to Jagannath Puri, the abode of Purushottama dear to Gaudiya Vaishnavas and indeed to all classes of transcendentalists, citing the Skanda Purana's promise that merely seeing Jagannath grants automatic liberation and noting that even Lord Rama instructed Vibhishana to worship Him. He recounts the founding pastime of King Indradyumna, who longed to see a particular form of the Lord: guided by a sadhu's description of Nila Madhava, the brahmana Vidyapati found the deity in the jungle, witnessed a crow drown and rise in a four-armed Vaikuntha form, and the Lord ultimately manifested as the wooden Daru-brahman forms of Jagannath, Baladeva and Subhadra carved by Vishvakarma disguised as a humble carpenter. He traces the temple's turbulent history of Buddhist and Shankarite claims, eighteen invasions, Aurangzeb's fifteen-year closure (which he likens to the COVID lockdown), and Kalapahara's attempt to burn the deities, thwarted when the brahmana Shibeshwara Mohanti rescued the Brahma-padartha from their navels. The heart of the lecture is Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who spent his final twenty-four post-sannyasa years in Puri, externally spreading the yuga-dharma of harinama through Ratha Yatra and internally relishing Radharani's mahabhava of separation in the Gambhira. Indradyumna Swami explains that Ratha Yatra re-enacts drawing Krishna back from Kurukshetra to Vrindavan, illustrating with the Sahitya-darpana verse that Chaitanya sang and Rupa Goswami's transcendental recomposition of it, and with Chaitanya simultaneously dancing in all seven kirtan groups just as Krishna danced with every gopi and every Dvarka queen. He glorifies Jaya Dev Goswami's Gita Govinda, vindicated when Jagannath tore up the king's rival composition and embraced Jaya Dev's, beloved of a gardener for whom the Lord Himself came to listen, and honored by King Prataparudra's decree that only Gita Govinda be sung before the deity. Turning to ISKCON, he shows Jagannath as Vrindavan Krishna longing for home (the Haridasa deities now on the Vrindavan Parikrama Marg) and as Patita-pavana who expands worldwide because mlecchas are barred from the Puri temple, and portrays Srila Prabhupada, who as the boy Abhay was given a Ratha cart by a passing woman and vowed to spread the festival everywhere, fulfilling that vow even by walking London's four-hour parade while gravely ill. He closes by urging the second and third generations to carry the movement forward for ten thousand years, reading Prabhupada's 1976 letter to Jayananda Prabhu and a verse of the Jagannath Ashtakam.

## The Char Dhams and the Place of Jagannath Puri

- This is the third talk in a series on the *Char Dhams* — the four main holy places of India — in accordance with Lord Chaitanya's instruction to Rupa Goswami to either live in or visit a holy place in India (or ISKCON temples worldwide).
- Today's subject: **Jagannath Puri**, very dear to Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
- The four dhams: **Badrinath, Dvaraka, Rameshwaram, and Jagannath Puri.**
- Quaint saying about how the Supreme Lord uses the four dhams: "He takes bath in Badrinath, He dresses Himself in Dvaraka, He eats in Puri fifty-two times a day, and He goes to sleep in Rameshwaram."
- Puri is dear not only to Gaudiya Vaishnavas but to all classes of transcendentalists. How do we know? *Guru-shastra-sadhu* — we know everything from guru, shastra, and sadhu.
  - **Skanda Purana** — "Whoever sees Jagannath, the holder of the conch, chakra, and mace, by His mercy is automatically liberated." All classes of transcendentalists desire liberation (though their conception of liberation may vary), and anyone who goes to Jagannath Puri is automatically liberated by Jagannath's mercy.
- The word **Jagannath** means "Lord of the Universe," eternally present on this earth, attractive to all who live there.

## Rameshwaram Connection — Lord Rama Advises Vibhishana to Worship Jagannath

- Monday's webinar was on Rameshwaram and mentioned **Vibhishana**, brother of Ravana. Like Ravana he was a demon, but he had a change of heart, left Ravana, and joined Rama.
- After Lord Rama conquered Ravana, his armies, and Lanka, He made Vibhishana the king of Lanka and gave him an interesting instruction.
- **Ramayana, Uttara-khanda, chapter 108, verse 30** — *kim ca adya vaktum icchami rakshasendra maha-bala / aradhaya jagannatham ikshvaku-kula-daivatam* — "O Vibhishana, ruler of the demons, I advise you to worship Lord Jagannath, the Lord of the Ikshvaku dynasty."
- Significance: it shows how popular and exalted the worship of Jagannath is — Lord Ramachandra Himself instructed even Vibhishana to worship Lord Jagannath.

## The Benefits of Living in or Visiting Jagannath Puri

- The sadhus say of Jagannath Puri:
  - Sleeping is considered offering prostrated obeisances.
  - Speaking is considered offering prayer.
  - Singing is considered hymns of glorification.
  - Walking is taken as *parikrama*.
  - Each breath one takes is equal to performing an **Ashvamedha sacrifice.**
- Jagannath Puri is so exalted that **Jiva Goswami** mentions it in his **Bhakti-sandarbha**, quoting the **Brahma Purana**: the 80 square miles of **Purushottama-kshetra** (Jagannath Puri) are glorious — the demigods in the heavenly planets see all its inhabitants (whether human, animal, or otherwise) as all having four arms and hands, exactly like the eternally liberated beings in Vaikuntha.
- "From all angles, Jagannath Puri is an amazing place."

## Special Significance for Gaudiya Vaishnavas

- For Gaudiya Vaishnavas / ISKCON devotees, Puri has special appeal because many of our acharyas visited there.
- In particular, **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura** lived in Jagannath Puri for some time and oversaw the management of the temple.
- **Srila Prabhupada** immortalized the divine appearance of his own spiritual master (who appeared at Puri) in his 1936 Vyasa-puja offering:
  - "*Adore, adore ye all the happy day, blessed than heaven, sweeter than May. / When he appeared at Puri, the holy place, my lord and master, His Divine Grace. / O my master, the evangelic angel, give us thy light, light up thy candle. / Struggle for existence, the human race, [their] only hope, His Divine Grace.*"

## The History of Jagannath Puri — King Indradyumna's Longing

- The history goes back to the beginning of time; it is so vast that one could speak for more than four hours (as with the story of Rameshwaram). Told here briefly.
- There was a king named **Indradyumna Maharaj**, a great devotee of Lord Vishnu. Because he had *bhakti*, great devotion, he hankered to see a particular form of the Lord.
  - There are so many forms and incarnations coming from Krishna, and every devotee has, deep in the heart, a desire to see a particular form of Krishna with particular pastimes, atmosphere, and devotees.
- The king did not know where to go or how, but **Bhagavad-gita 18.61** — the Lord is seated in everyone's heart, directing the wanderings of all living entities. If you want to wander aimlessly in material existence, He will guide you to that; but if a soul develops the desire to advance spiritually, the Lord within the heart directs him toward a bona-fide spiritual master. "It's not anything material — it's a transcendental phenomenon."
- **Gauranga dasi's Vyasa-puja offering** (a disciple of His Holiness Sivarama Maharaj): "Guru Maharaj, it took you so long to find me — please never kick me away." Every disciple can relate: often we were not looking for Krishna but were engaged in materialistic activities, and the Lord intervened and took charge.
- Lesson: cultivate such desires — not the nasty material desires that all have a bad ending, but, with the help of the spiritual master and devotees, discover and work on our real spiritual desires. Then "there'll be eternal bliss."

## The Sadhu at Court and the Deity Nila Madhava

- How did Krishna know the king's heart? Because He is the Paramatma in the heart. Prabhupada said the Supersoul and the living entity are very close, but through ignorance we cannot see the Lord or understand He is there.
- The Lord arranged for a sadhu to come to the king's court one day — not to talk politics. Generally sadhus don't go to kings' courts, because kings tend to be materialistic, dealing with politics, division, and competition — but this king was different.
- **Vedic culture:** When a sadhu came to court, all activity would stop, the sadhu would sit, and he would speak *Krishna-katha*.
- The sadhu spoke of Krishna's pastimes, qualities, and devotees, and mentioned one form of Krishna, a deity named **Nila Madhava.** Just hearing of this form, the king became very interested — "It's the Lord whom I am so looking for." It resonated with his heart. (Certain things resonate with our hearts — a certain type of prasada, a certain holy place; we have our Vrindavan-vasis and our Mayapur-vasis.)

## Vidyapati Finds Nila Madhava in the Jungle

- The king sent out devotees to find Nila Madhava. One particular brahmana named **Vidyapati**, after an arduous journey through the jungle, found Nila Madhava being worshipped by the inhabitants of the jungle (the Sabaras).
  - First and most important, he was happy because he had executed his service to guru and Krishna; as a result, the blessing was that he had *darshan* of Nila Madhava, an exquisitely beautiful deity, in the middle of the jungle.
- In the middle of the jungle, Vidyapati held a kirtan and danced in ecstasy.
  - **How to express devotion:** we chant and dance in ecstasy for the Lord's pleasure — the natural symptom of spiritual advancement, "no wallflowers."
  - **Vishnujana Maharaj's question to Prabhupada:** He asked whether, on Sankirtan in public, devotees should dance in a sober way or dance to their heart's content. Prabhupada answered: "Don't be so concerned with the public. You are dancing for the pleasure of Krishna. Dance to your heart's content. Because it's real, the public will appreciate."
  - **The speaker's own book-distribution story (Paris, in his 20s):** As a grihastha, he dressed every day in clean white cloth (dhoti and kurta), shaved his head, wore big tilak (almost like Ramanuja tilak), and the lady pujaris gave him a garland from Jagannath, Balarama, or Subhadra every morning before he went out. He carried a beautiful multicolored book bag with little mirrors (from India) that had a hole in the middle where he kept a stick of burning incense, so wherever he went the scent of incense followed. Going to shops, butcher shops, cafes, and offices, "it was almost like Yoga-maya took over" — people were stunned and captivated by this smiling American boy who spoke no French, "a person from another dimension, from the spiritual world," and he distributed so many books. Even the envious and the *asuric* never harmed him; they would just say, "All right, please get out." Conclusion: "Spirit is much stronger than matter... we can just be who we are."

## The Crow and the Transcendental Water

- After his darshan, Vidyapati sat looking around the jungle and noticed a crow in a tree. The crow fell asleep, fell off the branch into a nearby lake, and drowned.
- The idea: wherever the Lord is present, that whole area becomes transcendental — it becomes like Vrindavan.
- Suddenly the crow rose out of the water in a four-armed Vaikuntha (spiritual) form and ascended to the spiritual world — liberated because of the transcendental nature of that water and the spiritual atmosphere of that holy place.

## The Voice from the Sky and the Command to Build a Temple

- Vidyapati, amazed, climbed the tree and was about to jump in to repeat the crow's feat when he heard a voice from the sky: "O brahmana, wait. Since you have been able to see Nila Madhava, you now have the duty to inform King Indradyumna."
- Vidyapati climbed down, went back through the jungle, and came to King Indradyumna.
- The king gathered all his ministers, soldiers, warriors, and advisors and went into the jungle to the place where Nila Madhava was — but by the time they arrived, Nila Madhava was gone. ("The ways of the Lord are inconceivable.")
- The king was very upset, and again a voice from the heavens sounded: "O king, you must build a temple. There and there the Absolute Truth will manifest in a wooden form (*daru-brahma*), and you will see Me — you will not see Me as Nila Madhava. I will now appear in the form of Jagannath."

## Vishvakarma the Carpenter and the Carving of the Deities

- The king built a very beautiful temple, then asked for **Vishvakarma**, the architect of the demigods, who appeared as a humble carpenter, to carve the beautiful wooden deities.
- The carpenter's condition: it would take him 21 days; he would work alone and undisturbed, and if anyone disturbed him he would leave the work unfinished.
- **Laulyam** — greed for Krishna. The king was very anxious.
  - **Rupa Goswami (Upadeshamrita):** "As young boys are attracted to young girls, and young girls to young boys, with the same intensity but in a transcendental way — my dear Lord, let me be attracted to You, let me be greedy for You."
  - This enthusiasm is an important principle in **Upadeshamrita.** How to maintain enthusiasm and keep in touch with the spiritual energy? Chant Hare Krishna. How to *lose* enthusiasm? Associate with the material energy. "Krishna consciousness is quite simple."

## The Unfinished-Looking Forms Are Perfection

- The king couldn't contain himself; after fourteen days he opened the door where the carpenter was carving. The carpenter (Vishvakarma) had disappeared, leaving three forms of the Lord.
- They look unfinished, "but they are perfection, because the Lord appears as He wants to appear before His devotee." When gifted with a deity, one should think: "This is how the Lord wanted to appear before me in my neophyte state, so I could worship Him in the deity form."
- We should not make material calculations ("I don't like the size of that; a little smaller here, a little bigger there"). The Lord appeared in the form He wanted, to accept our worship.
- The king was very glad and performed the installation ceremony (*pratistha*). This happened "millenniums and millenniums ago," yet Puri, the temple, the deity, and the Seva are all still there.

## Prayer for ISKCON's Stability

- The speaker often prays that ISKCON will remain (as they say in French, "*tel quel*" — as it is), exactly as Prabhupada designed and instructed, for many millenniums to come.
- If our devotees can be faithful, and if the movement maintains such stability — remaining unchanged from what Prabhupada gave — it could go on for hundreds or thousands of years, at least 10,000 years: the golden age of Lord Chaitanya, "at least to the end of Kali-yuga."

## Other Traditions' Claims on Jagannath; The 18 Plunderings

- Studying the history, one finds that many spiritual traditions came to Jagannath Puri and claimed Jagannath as their own, giving Him different identities.
  - **Buddhism** was very popular and all-pervading in India for about 1,500 years. Although the brahmanas worshipped Jagannath, the Buddhists were much in control of that region and considered Jagannath their deity — they even have a scripture claiming they found the Jagannath deity.
  - **Shankaracharya** also established a *matha* (monastery) in Puri, seeing it as one of the main spiritual places in India. The impersonalists saw Jagannath in their way — since their philosophy is to merge with God, they wanted to merge with Jagannath. Many yogis also came and spoke of Jagannath according to their own perception.
- These are peaceful times now, but there were many changes through the eons. History shows Jagannath Puri was invaded and plundered **18 times.**
- **Aurangzeb (1692):** The tyrant Muslim ruler ordered the temple closed, prepared to destroy it if the pujaris did not comply. The temple was closed until his death, 15 years later — no one could go to the temple for 15 years.
  - Comparison: we are uncomfortable on lockdown for two weeks or a few months (the webinar is during the COVID-19 quarantine) — imagine a 15-year quarantine in Jagannath Puri, when no one could come to the temple.

## The Flame of Bhakti That Never Went Out

- Who is Jagannath? The deity that King Indradyumna Maharaj so desired to see and love with *bhakti* — and the Lord responded according to the bhakti in the king's heart: "O Lord of the Universe, please reveal Yourself to Me."
- That mood of loving service never went out — "like the flame, the pilot light in your furnace, never goes out." No matter what happened in Puri, the mood of serving Jagannath with love and devotion was always there in the temple and in the hearts of the devotees, always burning.

## Lord Chaitanya — the Volcano of Bhakti

- That bhakti became a volcano — blowing with lava and fire — with the appearance of **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu 500 years ago.**
- Lord Chaitanya made an immense contribution to bhakti: He revealed the perfect way to worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead and what is the perfection of love of God — the love of the Vrindavan gopis for Krishna.
- **Upadeshamrita 9** (paraphrased) — the most worshipful form of the Lord is Sri Krishna, the son of Maharaj Nanda, and Vrindavan is the topmost worshipful abode. (Often quoted in the webinar, "rightfully so, because the truth must be spoken.")
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita:** On His second (main) visit to Puri, Lord Chaitanya stayed 24 years. **CC Madhya-lila** — *chabbisa vatsara chila kariya sannyasa / bhakta-gana lana kaila nilacale vasa* — "For His remaining twenty-four years, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, after accepting the renounced order of life, stayed at Jagannath Puri with His devotees."
- From an external point of view, Puri had high moments and low moments (though it is always transcendental). Even in the times when the Buddhists were there and Shankaracharya established a matha, there were many sincere, dedicated devotees of Lord Jagannath.

## Kala Pahara and the Rescue of the Deities' Essence

- A famous pastime the people of Jagannath Puri like to relate: In the year 1568 (the transcript says 1658), another tyrant Muslim ruler named **Kala Pahara** marched into Puri with a large army to ransack the temple and take its wealth.
- The pujaris, warned ahead of time, took the deities — Jagannath, Subhadra, and Baladeva (very large deities) — to an island in the middle of a lake outside Puri. But Kala Pahara's spies learned of it, intercepted the caravan, and stole the deities.
- They put them on the backs of elephants, took them to the bank of the Ganges, poured liquid on them, and tried to burn the deities, then left. "Who can imagine — the deities were on flame on the bank of the Ganges."
- A very devout brahmana named **Bisar Mohanti** had secretly followed the Muslims from the island to the Ganges. Watching from a distance as they set the deity on fire, he ran as soon as they left and pushed the deity into the Ganges.
- Most importantly, he retrieved the sacred **Brahma-padartha** ("Brahma-vastu") — the essence hidden in the navels of Jagannath, Subhadra, and Baladeva (said to be a **shalagram-shila**). As the deity began to float away, he took those three shalagram-shilas in his hand, placed them inside a mridanga, and walked as a humble pilgrim back to Puri, giving the essence of the deities to the pujaris, who later placed them in the navels of the new Jagannath, Subhadra, and Baladeva. "He became a great hero."
- Reflection: so many great devotees — wonderful to hear of their sacrifices, surrender, and service.

## Chaitanya's Twofold Contribution to Puri

- It was Lord Chaitanya who made a monumental contribution to the world of bhakti in Jagannath Puri. He is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, but He came as a devotee to taste the essence of pure bhakti — the *bhakti* Srimati Radharani has for Krishna — and He brought that mood to Puri in two ways:
  - **Externally** — by participating in the Ratha Yatra festivals every year (chanting and dancing) while introducing the *yuga-dharma* of chanting Hare Krishna.
  - **Internally** — by experiencing the deeper mellows of *rasa* (devotional service) while living in the **Gambhira** temple (His deities there called Radha-Kanta). In a small room, in the association of very confidential devotees, Mahaprabhu experienced the deeper sentiments Radharani has for Krishna.
- This **Mahabhava** is a special thing that we worship from a distance. We can understand it to some extent by the grace of three personalities: **Radharani, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and Madhavendra Puri.** We appreciate, adore, and worship it from a distance.
- We can experience it to a certain degree through **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6** — *sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokshaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati* — unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service to the Lord, which brings full satisfaction to the heart. "That's real *prema*." There are different levels of love of God, but that is the highest prema, given by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who demonstrated it in Jagannath Puri.

## Madhavendra Puri's Prayer of Separation

- Puri became more blessed by the Lord who introduced the yuga-dharma (by which we get Krishna-prema) and who Himself experienced Radharani's love in the intimate moments in the Gambhira temple.
- This is the mood of **vipralambha** — separation. Madhavendra Puri also began to appreciate this love and prayed to the Lord in this mood:
  - **Madhavendra Puri's verse** (*ayi dina-dayardra natha he mathura-natha kadavalokyase*) — "O my Lord, O most merciful master, O Master of Mathura, when shall I see You again? Because of my not seeing You, my agitated heart has become unsteady. O most beloved one, what shall I do now?" This is how the gopis pray when Krishna is in Mathura.
  - "That's real prayer" — contrasted with "help me pass my exams, help me get over this sickness, can I have a new car?" It is good to know the highest standard.

## Hippies Singing Madhavendra Puri's Prayer on the Streets of America

- When they were new devotees in America in the 1970s, they used to sing that prayer in a paraphrased way on the streets of Detroit, Washington, and San Francisco.
- The amazing thing about Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement: American boys and girls just out of the hippie era were singing the song of Madhavendra Puri on the concrete streets, "not even knowing so much themselves" — but "the truth must be spoken."
- Connected to **Rupa Goswami's glorification of Lord Chaitanya:** When Rupa Goswami first saw Lord Chaitanya, he recognized who He was and said, "You are the most magnificent incarnation — because You are giving the highest understanding of love of Godhead to the most fallen people of Kali-yuga and elevating them. What could be more amazing than You?"
- The paraphrase they sang on the streets, in the mood of Rupa Goswami and of separation: "O darling mind, my heart is burning in pains of separation. O, what shall I do? O, what shall I do?" — pouring out to Krishna, because a brahmana always speaks the truth.

## The Power of Transcendental Sound

- The devotees stayed and heard how the Lord is glorified, and how the scriptures glorify Him — just as we hear the Bhagavad-gita and hear the same scriptures we ourselves study.
- The process: *shravanam, kirtanam, vishnu-smaranam* (hearing, chanting, remembering Vishnu). The transcendental sound vibration is so powerful — "it's all that you need": the Hare Krishna mantra, or the reading of the books Srila Prabhupada has given us.

## The Gita Govinda and the Lord's Judgment Between Two Books

- One book Lord Chaitanya especially enjoyed, which brought forth deep feelings of love for Krishna, was **Jayadeva Goswami's Gita Govinda**, compiled in the 12th century (before Mahaprabhu). Jayadeva Goswami composed it particularly for **Lord Jagannath** — just as, in this world, people compose songs and poems about their beloved.
- **The contest of the two Gita Govindas:** A king of Puri at that time, named **Purushottama Deva**, also composed a work glorifying Govinda, calling it the **Abhinava Gita Govinda**. He considered it superior to Jayadeva Goswami's Gita Govinda and decreed that Jayadeva's should not be read to the deities and that only his own should be read.
  - This caused a great uproar — "a spiritual revolution" — in Jagannath Puri: Jayadeva composed the Gita Govinda for Jagannath, and now the king says only *his* version should be heard.
- Given the sensitive nature of the challenge, the brahmanas decided to let **Lord Jagannath decide for Himself** — let the Lord be the judge.
  - They placed both books before the deity of Jagannath in the evening — the king's *Abhinava Gita Govinda* on one table and Jayadeva Goswami's *Gita Govinda* on the other — then put the deity to sleep and closed the doors.
- The next morning, at the time to open the deity's doors and greet Him, the brahmanas, ministers, people, and the king gathered, blew the conches, and opened the altar. They found the king's *Abhinava Gita Govinda* with its pages ripped out, torn, and strewn all over the floor, while Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda* was nestled under Lord Jagannath's arm, very close to His heart. "It was clear that the Lord had spoken."
- Conclusion: Is the Lord a statue? No. "My dear Lord, You are not a statue; You are the son of Maharaj Nanda." When the devotee is purified, there is a relationship between the Lord and His devotee. The deity is our hope — of everything past, present, and future — while the humble, bumbling servant tries to serve; the deity is very tolerant and patient. We worship very seriously, following all the rules and regulations of deity worship, always conscious that the deity is a person — Krishna, the Supreme Person — with no room for error.

## The Speaker's Own Jagannath Deities

- The speaker was once in Puri and was blessed by a sadhu who gave him deities of Jagannath, Baladeva, and Subhadra — ancient household deities, over 200 years old, made of bronze (as was the tradition among sadhus and households many centuries ago).
- It has become part of these webinars to share his deities with the audience. He introduces his Jagannath deities: very small, over 200 years old, beautifully painted by his disciple Vrindarani; "standing so sweetly, so nicely," a perfect size for his altar.
- His other altar deities: Radha-Rasika-roy (Radha-Krishna, a bit taller), Nrisimhadeva, Govardhan, and the Gaudiya deities. "Thank you, Jagannath, for coming into my life" (his disciple spent many months polishing and painting the metal).
- Time check: two and a half hours in. He asks whether the audience is tired and, seeing "yes, keep going" in the comments, continues — "I've got a lot more nectar."

## The Gardener Who Sang the Gita Govinda

- More on the Gita Govinda, because it gives so much pleasure to Jagannath. One time, the devotees put Lord Jagannath to rest in His temple.
- There was a gardener in one of the many flower/vegetable/fruit gardens of Jagannath Puri who had memorized the entire Gita Govinda and was singing it from his heart for the pleasure of Lord Jagannath while collecting flowers and fruits.
- "If You can talk, Lord, You can also walk" — Lord Jagannath came to hear him and hid behind a thorn bush; the thorns ripped His cloth and scratched His body.
- Suddenly the conch blew for the opening of the deity doors for the afternoon *darshan*, and Lord Jagannath had to run back, get on the altar, and stand there quickly. But when the pujaris saw His state — ripped clothes and a scratched body — they were alarmed: "What happened to Jagannath?" Fearing the king would become angry, they kept the doors closed ("an emergency"), no darshan, and went to explain to the king that they could not understand how the Lord got scratches everywhere.
- That night, the king had a dream (revealing what had happened).

## King Prataparudra's Royal Decree

- At that time there were many different songs, poems, and performances being done before the Jagannath deity day and night. But because Jagannath revealed His attachment to the Gita Govinda, this king — **King Prataparudra** — issued a royal decree, ten years before Lord Chaitanya came to Puri.
- The decree (paraphrased): "From this day forward, the performance of songs and dances for Jagannath and Balaram will consist exclusively of the Gita Govinda and no other work. The performers are instructed to learn the songs of the Gita Govinda exclusively. Four musicians will accompany the dancers who enact the Gita Govinda, and others may follow as a chorus. Temple servants who allow any other song to be performed will be deemed enemies of Lord Jagannath."
- Comment: no present-day ruler, dictator, or president issues such decrees, "but it would actually be worth issuing such decrees even now."
- This is the very Gita Govinda that Lord Chaitanya so wanted to hear when He went to Jagannath Puri — the *vipralambha-dham*, the dham of intense separation.

## Ratha Yatra — Chaitanya's Internal Experience of Radha's Love

- Even among the associates of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, not everyone understood the Lord's deeper, ecstatic mood. But that special love He revealed at the Ratha Yatra.
- Ratha Yatra is integral to Lord Jagannath's pastimes in Jagannath Puri: it symbolically represents taking Krishna, Balarama, and Subhadra back from Kurukshetra to Vrindavan, where they had left many years before.
- So the experience Lord Chaitanya was having of Radharani's ecstatic love for Krishna, He experienced during the Ratha Yatra. Coming in the mood of Radharani, with Jagannath-Krishna on the Ratha Yatra cart, by chanting and dancing in front of the cart, Mahaprabhu poured out His heart — "how much He loves Jagannath, how much Radharani loves Krishna."

## The Song from the Sahitya Darpana and Rupa Goswami's Recomposition

- Referenced from the earlier lecture on Kurukshetra: a love song from the **Sahitya Darpana**, sung in Sanskrit — outwardly a love song between a boy and a girl, but Lord Chaitanya sang it pouring His heart out to Krishna:
  - "That very personality who stole my heart during my youth is now again my master. These are the same moonlit nights of the month of Chaitra; the same fragrance of *malati* flowers is there; the same sweet breezes are blowing from the Kadamba forest. In our intimate relationship, I am also the same lover, yet my mind is not happy here. I am eager to go back to that place on the banks of the Reva, under the Vetasi tree. That is my desire."
- Who understood what Lord Chaitanya really meant? **Rupa Goswami** — not merely because he was a sannyasi, but because he was **Rupa Manjari** in the spiritual world, who intimately serves Radha and Krishna.
- Spontaneously, Rupa Goswami recomposed that mundane love song into a transcendental one, as if Radharani were pouring her heart out to Krishna. He wrote it on a palm leaf, folded it, and hid it; Lord Chaitanya later found it and, reading it, said, "Ah, Rupa Goswami understands My inner heart."
- **Rupa Goswami's recomposition** — Radharani speaking to Lalita: "My dear friend, now I have met My very old and dear friend Krishna on this battlefield of Kurukshetra. I am the same Radharani, and now we are meeting together. It is very pleasant, but I would still like to go to the bank of the Yamuna, beneath the trees of the forest there. I wish to hear the vibration of His sweet flute, playing the fifth note within the forest of Vrindavan." ("For myself and my existence, I bathe in the nectar of the words of Sri Rupa Goswami.")
- So although Ratha Yatra in Puri is a very public event, Lord Chaitanya experienced it as an internal affair. One of the principal reasons Krishna came to this world was to introduce Sankirtan and to experience Radha's love — and He did both at Ratha Yatra: everyone chanting Hare Krishna, and He experiencing that love. Ratha Yatra is the perfect festival — for the benefit of the most fallen and of the most exalted *paramahamsas*.

## Chaitanya Dancing in Seven Groups at Once

- The Lord was also very practical: before the Ratha Yatra parade, He organized **seven kirtan groups** (*sankirtan* parties), each with one singer, one dancer, and two mridangas — thus seven dancers and fourteen mridanga players surrounding Lord Jagannath's cart. They were all experts, but most importantly they sang from the heart, with love and devotion to Jagannath. That caused Lord Chaitanya to dance.
- The way He danced was amazing: He went to each group and danced with that group — moving so quickly that each group thought He was dancing only with them. In fact, He was in each group at the same time.
- **Two parallels in Krishna's pastimes:**
  - In the **Rasa-lila**, all the gopis thought "Krishna is dancing only with me" — He expanded Himself.
  - In **Dvaraka**, in the palaces of the 16,108 queens, He was literally in each palace, and each queen thought "Krishna is with me."
- He showed that same divine pastime at Ratha Yatra. "Ratha Yatra is such a deep thing."
- Question: How can Chaitanya do what Krishna did? Answer: because Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is the **combined form of Radha and Krishna.** And who is Jagannath? Vrindavan Krishna.

## The Jagannatha Ashtakam and the Haridasa Pastime

- The **Jagannatha Ashtakam** clearly reveals that Jagannath is Vrindavan Krishna. (The speaker sends for the book on Puri from the next room.)
- **The Haridasa pastime:** In Puri there was a very devout Vaishnava named **Haridasa** who actually lived in Vrindavan. One night Lord Jagannath told Haridasa: "You are a very nice devotee. I am here in Jagannath Puri in Orissa, but I want to come live in Vrindavan." Haridasa lost his speech on hearing that the Lord wanted to come from Puri to live in Vrindavan.
- Lord Jagannath explained that since it was the time of **Nava-kalevara** (the changing of His body in Puri), Haridasa should go there and bring His old body to be worshipped in Vrindavan.
  - **Nava-kalevara:** the essence of the deity is that they take the shalagram-shila out of the old deity and put it into the new deity — this is when Lord Jagannath gets a new body. The old bodies are normally placed in the ocean or buried in a particular garden. Jagannath said, "I don't want My old body buried under the ground; I want it brought to Vrindavan and worshipped."
- As soon as Haridasa arrived, they had taken the shalagram from the old deity and placed it in the navel of the new deity, and were about to bury the older form of Jagannath in a garden. Haridasa rushed to the priests: "Wait, don't put that body of Jagannath in the ground. I received an order from Lord Jagannath in a dream in Vrindavan — He wants me to take Him to Vrindavan."
- The pujari answered something interesting: "Dear sir, we cannot change our spiritual traditions every time the Lord appears in a dream to somebody. But if you wish to pursue the matter, you must go and meet the king."
  - In Puri, the king is the chief manager of the temple. When Haridasa met the king, the king did not want to violate the advice of the priests: "We don't want to stop these traditions; we will entrust the deity to *samadhi* (burial)."
- Haridasa, knowing the Lord had given him a command, fasted and prayed to the Lord to resolve the situation, sitting for a long time (on the long beach/river at Puri).
- That night, Lord Jagannath appeared in the **king's dream** and said, "Why have you neglected My order? Call for Haridasa at once and make arrangements for Me to go to My home in Vrindavan."
- The king woke with a start, personally went out, found the saint Haridasa on the beach, bowed down, and asked forgiveness. They shared their dreams (Haridasa had also had a dream of the Lord). The king then went to the temple priests, revealed his dream, and everyone made arrangements for the deity to go to Vrindavan.
- Instead of being placed in samadhi in the sacred garden behind the temple, Jagannath, Baladeva, and Subhadra were seated on a royal chariot drawn by horses, with soldiers and priests, and taken all the way to Vrindavan, where they were installed in a temple by the Yamuna shore — the place now called **Jagannath Ghat** on the Parikrama Marg. "The deities are still there," behind a big wall on Parikrama Road.
- Meaning: Jagannath is Vrindavan Krishna. Longing to enjoy in Vrindavan, tired of separation from Vrindavan, He decided to return home to enjoy the beautiful groves by the Yamuna — "a wonderful, amazing, wild atmosphere so different from Jagannath Puri."

## The Chain of Bhakti — Bhaktisiddhanta and Prabhupada

- There are many examples of how Chaitanya Mahaprabhu infused Puri with the highest bhakti. **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura** took that same exalted bhakti to the four corners of India (like the Gaudiya Math), and one of his students — **Srila Prabhupada** — took the same bhakti and spread it all over the world.
- Where did Prabhupada get that bhakti? He once said that in his life he never knew what is sinful activity and never for a moment forgot the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna. But in the proper mood, Prabhupada attributed his bhakti to the mercy of his spiritual preceptors — "the proper attitude; he taught us by example, *acharya*."
- How do we know Prabhupada had that mood — the mood of any Vaishnava, "that your mercy is all that I am made of"? Because he wrote it down.
  - Prabhupada said his purports in all his books "were his ecstasies," and he attributed the ecstasy he felt in Krishna consciousness — the highest ecstasy — to **two** spiritual preceptors.

## The Dedication of the Krishna Book

- Where is this? In the **Krishna book** ("summary study of the Tenth Canto" of Srimad-Bhagavatam — Krishna's Vrindavan pastimes — which Prabhupada gave us before he left this world; it should be read all the time, not forgotten).
- The dedication is to his two spiritual preceptors:
  - "*To my father, Gour Mohan De, 1849–1930, a devotee of Krishna who raised me as a Krishna conscious child from the beginning of my life. In my childhood ages he instructed me how to play the mridanga. He gave me Radha-Krishna vigraha to worship, and he gave me Jagannath Ratha Yatra to duly observe the festival as my childhood play. He was kind to me, and I imbibed from him. ... The idea was later on solidified by my spiritual master, my eternal father, [Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura].*"
- So Prabhupada had two spiritual preceptors — the father who gave him birth and the father who gave him spiritual birth (both gave spiritual birth). Note how Prabhupada attributed his ecstasy to his spiritual preceptors.
- Principle: We progress mostly based on our blessings. Yes, we follow the rules, rituals, and the 64 items of devotional service, "but that alone is not enough. We require the blessings of great souls, Mahatmas." It cannot be disputed that the key to success in spiritual life is the blessings of our spiritual preceptors; we must remember them and express our gratitude.

## Prabhupada's Childhood Ratha Yatra Cart

- With that bhakti planted in his heart, Prabhupada naturally became attracted to Ratha Yatra — the medium by which Chaitanya Mahaprabhu introduced the yuga-dharma and Himself experienced Radha's love for Krishna. Prabhupada took it up as a small child.
- **The story:** Young Abhay (Prabhupada) told his father, "I want to do Ratha Yatra," but a cart was needed. He and his father went to a carpenter, who quoted a price. Gour Mohan De, who worked at/ran a cloth shop, didn't have much money and said, "I'm sorry, Abhay, we don't have enough money to build a cart."
- Prabhupada started to cry — "crying for Krishna at such a young age," while "we've been trying to cry for Krishna for so many years [and] couldn't make a lot of tears... in many lives."
- A Bengali woman passing by asked why the boy was crying. Told he wanted a Ratha cart but they were too expensive, she said, "I have a little Ratha cart. You just take it."
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.22** — *yoga-kshemam vahamy aham* — "I protect what My devotee has and provide what he needs for devotional service." Krishna's words jump off the page when we examine the life of a pure devotee.
  - Was it just chance that the lady came along? No — "not a blade of grass moves without the will of the Lord." Krishna, in her heart, sent her there ("How many people have a small Ratha Yatra cart?").
- There is the **Bhagavata** (the scripture) and the **person Bhagavata** (the person who exemplifies the scripture) — "that's why we need pure devotees like Prabhupada."
- Is Prabhupada gone? No. **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati** said of pure devotees, "He lives forever by his divine instructions, and the follower lives with him."
  - **The epitaph Bhaktivinoda Thakura placed on the samadhi of Haridasa Thakura in Puri:** "He reasons ill who tells that Vaishnavas die, when thou art living still in sound. The Vaishnava dies to live, and living tries to spread the holy name around."
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.22** again — "For My devotee, I protect what he has and provide what he needs." Jagannath need not be in Puri only — He is Lord of the Universe and can be worshipped anywhere in the world, and He was worshipped as a childhood cart by His pure devotee's desire. ("Jaya Srila Prabhupada, Jaya Jagannath, Jaya Ratha Yatra.")

## Prabhupada's Boyhood Vision

- Prabhupada painted the cart himself and decorated it exactly as he had heard the cart is decorated in Puri.
- Later, preaching in the West, Prabhupada told one of the speaker's godbrothers in America that as a boy he used to wake up in the middle of the night, go out, and stare at the cart, dreaming — thinking, "One day I will do this all over the world." He was seven years old.
- And he did. "How blessed are we that somehow we are caught up in this tsunami of his mercy, taking Jagannath and the Ratha Yatra festival over the world." How did it happen? "It was his desire."

## Darshan Everywhere — Jagannath Sees Into Our Hearts

- Prabhupada took Ratha Yatra everywhere (or, by his potency, went everywhere), so that everyone could see Jagannath. Since He is Lord of the Universe, why should He come out only one day and then go back inside for the whole year? Let the whole universe see Him, and let Him see them.
- *Darshan* does not merely mean we see the Lord; the real potency of darshan is that the deity sees into our heart. **"Ceto-darpana-marjanam"** (Sikshashtaka 1) — this purifies our heart of lust, anger, and greed, all obstacles on the path to *bhakti*. So Ratha Yatra should be held in all cities and places, giving the opportunity for the Lord to see us and cleanse our hearts.

## Jagannath Expanded Worldwide — Deliverer of the Most Fallen

- In Puri it is a long-standing tradition that unqualified people cannot enter the temple — a restriction found in the Vedic law book **Niladri Mahodaya, chapter 37, text 137:** "*mlecchas* (non-Vedic persons), dogs, and inauspicious birds like vultures are never to be allowed inside Jagannath's temple."
- Who are *mlecchas*? Described in the **Mahabharata (1.79.13)** as someone degraded, polluted, and performing despicable acts like killing animals. (This does not refer to ISKCON and other Gaudiya Math groups, but various groups in the West are not allowed in.)
- Therefore Jagannath has expanded Himself all over the world — in as many temples as are built for His glorification and in as many homes as grihasthas want to worship Him (just as one Orissan family worshipped the deities the speaker now has for 200 years). Anyone is entitled to the darshan of Jagannath, because He is **Patita-pavana**, the deliverer of the most fallen — "not just one day a year, but as many days as possible, come to the temple and see Him."
- We are grateful and give respect to the **Pandas** (priests) and brahmanas of the Jagannath temple for keeping the tradition of giving everyone the Lord's darshan at the Ratha Yatra once a year in Puri. But because He is Lord of the Universe, we take Him all over the universe. So even the most fallen person can say, **"Jagannatha Swami nayana-patha-gami bhavatu me"** — "O Lord of the Universe, kindly be visible to me."

## The Eternal Associate; Prabhupada's Insistence at the London Ratha Yatra

- **The godbrother's question repeated:** A devotee asked Prabhupada whether it is true that any person who stands up out of respect when the Ratha Yatra cart passes by is to be considered an eternal associate of the Lord. Told it was in the Nectar of Devotion, Prabhupada said, "Then it must be true." ("Jaya Lady Subhadra, Jaya Balaram.") Anyone who comes with respect behind the Ratha Yatra is considered an eternal associate of God.
- Devotees who remember Srila Prabhupada at Ratha Yatra know that nothing pleased him more than to see a beautiful Ratha Yatra festival in the Western cities and in India.
- **Prabhupada's insistence (London):** Srila Prabhupada was very sick in Calcutta and was supposed to fly to London to participate in a Ratha Yatra. Advised not to go ("It's a long flight; you're in poor condition"), Prabhupada said, "I'm going" — so important was it to him, especially in London.
  - He flew with disciples caring for him; on arrival he was "decimated," in really bad condition. He was airlifted by helicopter from the airport to the start of the Ratha Yatra, then by car to the parade.
  - When he got out, the devotees practically carried him to Lady Subhadra's cart, intending to help him up the ladder to sit for the four-hour parade. But Prabhupada looked at the devotees and at Subhadra and said, "No, I'll walk." The astonished devotees could not argue. Prabhupada walked the four hours, sometimes dancing during the Ratha Yatra. How did he do it? "By bhakti" — a manifestation of his love, the love of God, to spread it all over the world.
