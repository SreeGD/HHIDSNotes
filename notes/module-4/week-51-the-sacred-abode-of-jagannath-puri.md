# The Sacred Abode of jagannath Puri

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 51 · Foundations in Bhakti · 2:31:29

## Summary

Indradyumna Swami devotes the third talk of his Char Dham webinar series to Jagannath Puri, the abode of Purushottama dear to Gaudiya Vaishnavas and indeed to all classes of transcendentalists, citing the Skanda Purana's promise that merely seeing Jagannath grants automatic liberation and noting that even Lord Rama instructed Vibhishana to worship Him. He recounts the founding pastime of King Indradyumna, who longed to see a particular form of the Lord: guided by a sadhu's description of Nila Madhava, the brahmana Vidyapati found the deity in the jungle, witnessed a crow drown and rise in a four-armed Vaikuntha form, and the Lord ultimately manifested as the wooden Daru-brahman forms of Jagannath, Baladeva and Subhadra carved by Vishvakarma disguised as a humble carpenter. He traces the temple's turbulent history of Buddhist and Shankarite claims, eighteen invasions, Aurangzeb's fifteen-year closure (which he likens to the COVID lockdown), and Kalapahara's attempt to burn the deities, thwarted when the brahmana Shibeshwara Mohanti rescued the Brahma-padartha from their navels. The heart of the lecture is Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who spent his final twenty-four post-sannyasa years in Puri, externally spreading the yuga-dharma of harinama through Ratha Yatra and internally relishing Radharani's mahabhava of separation in the Gambhira. Indradyumna Swami explains that Ratha Yatra re-enacts drawing Krishna back from Kurukshetra to Vrindavan, illustrating with the Sahitya-darpana verse that Chaitanya sang and Rupa Goswami's transcendental recomposition of it, and with Chaitanya simultaneously dancing in all seven kirtan groups just as Krishna danced with every gopi and every Dvarka queen. He glorifies Jaya Dev Goswami's Gita Govinda, vindicated when Jagannath tore up the king's rival composition and embraced Jaya Dev's, beloved of a gardener for whom the Lord Himself came to listen, and honored by King Prataparudra's decree that only Gita Govinda be sung before the deity. Turning to ISKCON, he shows Jagannath as Vrindavan Krishna longing for home (the Haridasa deities now on the Vrindavan Parikrama Marg) and as Patita-pavana who expands worldwide because mlecchas are barred from the Puri temple, and portrays Srila Prabhupada, who as the boy Abhay was given a Ratha cart by a passing woman and vowed to spread the festival everywhere, fulfilling that vow even by walking London's four-hour parade while gravely ill. He closes by urging the second and third generations to carry the movement forward for ten thousand years, reading Prabhupada's 1976 letter to Jayananda Prabhu and a verse of the Jagannath Ashtakam.

## Key Points

- Glories of Jagannath and His darshan: the Skanda Purana promises automatic liberation to whoever sees Jagannath, holder of conch, chakra and mace; Lord Rama told Vibhishana to worship Jagannath, deity of the Ikshvaku dynasty; and the Brahma Purana (quoted by Jiva Goswami in Bhakti Sandarbha) says all inhabitants of the 80-square-mile Purushottama-kshetra are seen as four-armed like the liberated souls of Vaikuntha. In Puri, sleeping counts as offering obeisances, speaking as prayer, singing as glorification, walking as parikrama, and each breath as an Ashvamedha sacrifice.
- The founding pastime: King Indradyumna's longing to see the Lord led the brahmana Vidyapati to find Nila Madhava in the jungle; a crow that drowned in the sacred lake rose in a four-armed Vaikuntha form; and when Nila Madhava vanished, the Lord had Vishvakarma (disguised as a carpenter) carve Jagannath, Baladeva and Subhadra, whose 'unfinished' forms the impatient king received after breaking the 21-day seclusion on the 14th day, teaching that the Lord appears exactly as He wishes for His devotee.
- Turbulent external history: Buddhists and Shankarites each claimed Jagannath; the temple was plundered 18 times; Aurangzeb closed it for 15 years until his death (compared to the COVID lockdown/quarantine); and when Kalapahara stole the deities and tried to burn them on the Ganges bank, the devout brahmana Shibeshwara Mohanti rescued the Brahma-padartha (said to be shalagram-shilas) from their navels, carried them back hidden in a mridanga, and had them installed in the new deities.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu spent his last 24 years after sannyasa in Puri, contributing to bhakti in two ways: externally through Ratha Yatra sankirtana, introducing the yuga-dharma of chanting Hare Krishna, and internally by relishing the deep mellows of Radharani's love-in-separation (mahabhava) in the Gambhira, worshiping His deity Radha-Kanta. This highest prema is appreciated by the grace of Radharani, Chaitanya, and Madhavendra Puri.
- Ratha Yatra symbolizes bringing Krishna, Balarama and Subhadra back from Kurukshetra to Vrindavan; Chaitanya rode it in Radharani's mood, singing a worldly love-song from the Sahitya-darpana that Rupa Goswami recomposed into a transcendental one (Radharani speaking to Lalita), and He danced in all seven kirtan parties at once so that each thought He was only with them, paralleling the rasa-lila gopis and the 16,108 Dvarka queens.
- Gita Govinda pastimes: Jaya Dev Goswami composed it in the 12th century for Jagannath; when King Purushottama Deva promoted his own rival Abhinava Gita Govinda, the deity settled the dispute overnight by scattering the king's torn pages while cradling Jaya Dev's book at His heart; a gardener's heartfelt singing drew Jagannath into thorn bushes (leaving Him scratched); and King Prataparudra decreed that only Gita Govinda be performed before Jagannath and Baladeva, calling violators enemies of the Lord.
- Jagannath is Vrindavan Krishna: the Jagannath Ashtakam reveals this, and in the Haridasa pastime the Lord asked in a dream to leave Puri during Nava-kalevara to be worshiped in Vrindavan, His old form now installed at Jagannath Ghat on the Parikrama Marg. As Patita-pavana barred to no one, and since mlecchas cannot enter the Puri temple (Niladri Mahodaya), Jagannath expands into temples and homes worldwide; anyone who merely stands in respect as the Ratha Yatra passes becomes His eternal associate.
- Srila Prabhupada carried this bhakti worldwide, attributing his own devotion to two preceptors, his father Gour Mohan De (who gave him a childhood Ratha Yatra) and his guru Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati; as the boy Abhay he was given a small Ratha cart by a passing Bengali woman (illustrating yoga-kshemam vahamy aham) and vowed to spread it everywhere, later insisting on walking London's four-hour Ratha Yatra despite grave illness. Indradyumna Swami urges the younger generations to sustain the movement for the 10,000-year golden age of Lord Chaitanya, closing with Prabhupada's 1976 letter to Jayananda Prabhu.

## Scriptural References

- Skanda Purana — 'Whoever sees Jagannath, the holder of the conch, chakra and mace, by his mercy is automatically liberated' (glory of Jagannath's darshan to all transcendentalists).
- Ramayana, Uttara-khanda, chapter 108 (verse ~30) — Lord Rama instructs Vibhishana ('ruler of the demons') to worship Lord Jagannath, deity of the Ikshvaku dynasty.
- Brahma Purana, cited by Jiva Goswami in Bhakti Sandarbha — the 80 square miles of Purushottama-kshetra; the demigods see all its inhabitants (human, animal or otherwise) as four-armed, like the liberated beings of Vaikuntha.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6 — 'sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokshaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati': unmotivated and uninterrupted devotional service that fully satisfies the heart (invoked on the nature of pure prema).
- Siksastakam, verse 1 — 'ceto-darpana-marjanam': cleansing the mirror of the heart, cited on the purifying potency of Jagannath's darshan.
- Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya-lila — 'chabbisa vatsara chila kariya sannyasa...': after accepting sannyasa, Shri Chaitanya stayed at Jagannath Puri with His devotees for His remaining 24 years.
- Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya-lila 1 — the Sahitya-darpana verse Chaitanya sang, 'That very personality who stole my heart in my youth is again my master... the same moonlit nights of Chaitra... the same malati fragrance... yet my mind longs for the bank of the Reva under the Vetasi tree'; and Rupa Goswami's transcendental recomposition (Radharani to Lalita), 'Now I have met My old friend Krishna on this Kurukshetra battlefield... yet I long to hear His flute beneath the trees of the Vrindavan forest.'
- Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya-lila 19.53 — Rupa Goswami's glorification of Chaitanya as the most magnificent and merciful incarnation for giving krishna-prema to the most fallen ('namo maha-vadanyaya krishna-prema-pradaya te').
- Madhavendra Puri's prayer (Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya-lila 4) — 'O merciful Lord, O master of Mathura, when shall I see You again? Because of not seeing You my agitated heart has become unsteady; O most beloved, what shall I do?' — the gopis' mood in Krishna's absence, paraphrased and sung by early ISKCON devotees on American streets.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.22 — 'yoga-kshemam vahamy aham': the Lord carries what His devotee lacks and preserves what he has, illustrated by the woman who gave young Abhay a Ratha cart.
- Jagannath Ashtakam — verse 1 ('kadachit kalindi-tata...'), worshiping Jagannath who plays His flute on the Yamuna's bank like a bumblebee at the lotus faces of Vraja's gopis, His feet honored by Lakshmi, Brahma, Shiva, Indra and Ganesha; refrain 'Jagannatha-svami nayana-patha-gami bhavatu me' (may the Lord of the universe be the object of my vision).
- Niladri Mahodaya, chapter 37, text 137 — non-Vedic persons, dogs and inauspicious birds like vultures are never allowed inside Jagannath's temple; with the definition of 'mleccha' from Mahabharata (Adi Parva ~1.79.13) as one degraded by acts like killing animals.
- Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's verse — 'tilaka vimala mala nama yukta pavitraha / hari hari kali-madhye...': from when the holy names of Hari appeared on earth, Vaishnavas adorned with tilak and neckbeads and the maha-mantra became manifest everywhere, purifying Kali-yuga (a 500-year-old prediction of the worldwide sankirtana movement).
- Upadeshamrita (Rupa Goswami) — enthusiasm (utsaha) as a key principle of devotional life, with the analogy that as youths and maidens are mutually attracted, so may the devotee become 'greedy' (lobha) for Krishna.
- Srila Prabhupada's Krishna Book dedication — to his father Gour Mohan De (1849–1930), who raised him Krishna conscious, taught him the mridanga, gave him Radha-Krishna vigraha to worship and a Jagannath Ratha Yatra as childhood play; and Prabhupada's 1936 Vyasa-puja poem 'Adore, adore ye all the happy day' on Bhaktisiddhanta's appearance at Puri.
- Srila Prabhupada's 1976 letter to Jayananda Prabhu after the New York Ratha Yatra — 'For so long I have wanted to have Ratha Yatra in New York and now at long last you have helped me fulfill this long cherished dream...'; also the Nectar of Devotion statement that one who stands in respect as the Ratha Yatra cart passes becomes an eternal associate of the Lord.
