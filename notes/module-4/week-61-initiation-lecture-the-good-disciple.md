# Initiation Lecture – The Good Disciple

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 61 · Festivals and Kirtans · 46:50

## Summary

Speaking at an initiation ceremony in Melbourne, Indradyumna Swami bases his talk on Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.9.28, in which Prahlada Maharaja, though offered any benediction directly by Lord Nrisimhadeva, asks only to remain in the service of his spiritual master Narada Muni — the mark of a pure devotee who first serves the servant of the servant and, by the mercy of guru (guru-krsna-prasade), receives the seed of devotion. He explains that initiation is a second, auspicious birth into the Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya sampradaya: whereas material birth is a "fall down" that guarantees death and repeated rebirth, this spiritual birth offers the one door out of the cycle, so a devotee stays sober yet hopeful, remembering the flickering nature of the world. The spiritual master plants the seed of bhakti in the disciple's heart through transcendental sound, and by faithfully chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra and following the four regulative principles, even an illiterate person will have all knowledge revealed and awaken his dormant love of God. He compares the holy name to a hidden treasure that must be guarded — kept somewhat confidential (the word gupta, root of gopi), walled in by the four vows, and protected from the inner thieves of lust, anger, and greed. Using the Chaitanya-charitamrita astrologer parable, he says one must go "east" to Krishna consciousness and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's sankirtana, avoiding the wasp of karma-kanda (south), the ghost of mental speculation (west), and the snake of mystic yoga (north). A disciple inherits both the guru's great mercy and a corresponding debt, repaid by becoming fixed in Krishna consciousness and preaching, thereby giving glory to the spiritual master — since a guru is judged by the quality of his disciples. He weaves in personal stories, including his atheist scientist mother who had a change of heart on her deathbed and passed away with God's name on her lips, and his "jolly" godbrother Sridhara Swami who kept a smile to the end, and closes by urging the new initiates to stay active under their Melbourne temple president.

## Key Points

- The lecture's core verse, SB 7.9.28, shows Prahlada choosing to serve his guru Narada Muni over accepting the Lord's direct benedictions; a pure devotee first serves the servant of the servant (guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija).
- Initiation is a second birth into the Gaudiya sampradaya; material birth is a fall-down that guarantees death and rebirth, so devotees remain equipoised and sober yet hopeful, always remembering the world's temporary, flickering nature.
- The spiritual master plants the seed of bhakti through transcendental sound; chanting the maha-mantra with faith and following the four principles reveals all knowledge and awakens the soul's dormant love of God, even for the illiterate.
- The holy name is a priceless treasure to be guarded — kept somewhat confidential (gupta) and protected by the "wall" of the four regulative principles (no meat-eating, intoxication, illicit sex, or gambling) against the inner thieves of lust, anger, and greed.
- Astrologer/buried-treasure parable: avoid the south (wasp of karma-kanda), the west (ghost of mental speculation), and the north (snake of mystic yoga); go east to Krishna consciousness and Chaitanya's sankirtana movement.
- A good disciple feels indebted for the guru's mercy and repays the debt by becoming fixed up and preaching, giving glory to his spiritual master, who is judged by the quality of his disciples.
- Devotees should still respect their material parents as their first siksa-gurus — illustrated by the swami's scientist mother, who converted before death — and should be strict and sincere in their vows, picking themselves up if they fall.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.9.28 — the lecture's main verse; Prahlada prays to remain in the service of his spiritual master Narada Muni rather than accept the Lord's benedictions.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Madhya 19.151 — "guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija," Sri Chaitanya's instruction to Rupa Goswami that the seed of devotion comes by the mercy of guru and then Krishna.
- Sri Gurv-astaka, verse 8 (Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura) — "yasya prasadad bhagavat-prasado," one pleases the Lord only by first pleasing the spiritual master.
- Bhagavad-gita 2.27 — for one who is born, death is certain (jatasya hi dhruvo mrtyuh); paired with "duhkhalayam asasvatam" (BG 8.15) describing this temporary world full of miseries.
- Bhagavad-gita 18.55 — "bhaktya mam abhijanati yavan yas casmi tattvatah," one can know the Lord as He is only through devotional service.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.10 — to those who serve Him with love, Krishna gives the understanding by which they can come to Him.
- Bhagavad-gita 11.8 — "divyam dadami te caksuh," Krishna grants Arjuna divine eyes to behold the universal form (virata-rupa).
- Svetasvatara Upanisad 6.23 — to one with implicit faith in both the Lord and the guru, all Vedic knowledge is automatically revealed.
- Narottama dasa Thakura — "janame janame sabe pita mata paya," everyone gets parents birth after birth, but the mercy of Krishna and guru is rarely obtained.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Aruna-daya-kirtana — "jiva jago, jiva jago, gaura-canda bole," a call to wake the sleeping souls from the lap of the witch Maya.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Madhya 22.107 — "nitya-siddha krsna-prema...," pure love of God is eternally established in the heart and awakens when it is purified by hearing and chanting.
- Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.5.32 — "krsna-varnam tvisakrsnam sangopangastra-parsadam," the yuga-avatara who appears in a golden form performing sankirtana in Kali-yuga.
- Padma Purana / Chaitanya-charitamrita Madhya 17.133 — "nama cintamani krsnas caitanya-rasa-vigrahah," the holy name is Krishna Himself, non-different from the Lord.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Adi 17.22 — "kali-kale nama-rupe krsna-avatara," in the age of Kali the Lord incarnates as the holy name, which delivers the whole world.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita Antya-lila, chapter 6 — Mahaprabhu gives Raghunatha dasa Goswami the Govardhana-sila, saying that worshiping it in transcendental goodness will one day let him see the son of Nanda Maharaja.
- Chanakya Pandit (Niti-sastra) — give up evil association, associate with the saintly, perform pious deeds, and always remember the flickering nature of the world; also the verse that knowledge is the ornament of everything.
- Mahabharata — Devavrata (Bhishma) takes his "terrible vow" ("Pita, I shall not marry"), cited as an example of strict adherence to one's vow.
- The astrologer's buried-treasure parable (attributed to the Chaitanya-charitamrita) — the poor man is told to go east for the inherited wealth, avoiding the wasp of karma-kanda (south), the ghost of mental speculation (west), and the snake of yoga (north).
