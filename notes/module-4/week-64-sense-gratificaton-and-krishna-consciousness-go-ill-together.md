# Sense Gratificaton and Krishna consciousness go ill together

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 64 ·  · 23:13

**Themes:** material desire and purification · sense gratification vs devotion · maturity in devotion · pure devotion of the gopis · taking shelter · responsibility

## Summary

His Holiness Indradyumna Swami explains that one may approach Krishna even while full of material desires, because the association itself purifies—just as a blind man who cannot appreciate fire still benefits from its warmth. Citing Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.3.10, he says that whether one is desireless, full of material desire, or seeking liberation, one should worship the Supreme Lord, which is why even an offensive chanter of the holy name can still be purified if his desire is sincere. The goal, however, is to advance past material motivation to a higher taste (Bhagavad-gita 2.59), giving up sense gratification rather than clutching both—illustrated by Dhruva Maharaja, who after seeing the Lord regarded his hard-won kingdom as a mere pebble beside a diamond, and by the monkey that trapped its own hand by refusing to release a banana. Quoting Prabhupada's purport, he stresses that 'sense gratification and Krishna consciousness go ill together,' like oil and water that never mix. The devotee must therefore mature out of the childish 'give me, give me' stage—which the father and the spiritual master tolerate for a time—into nishtha, the steady stage of unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6), becoming responsible and a shelter for other devotees instead of lamenting a lack of association. He contrasts complaining, well-facilitated devotees with the young, persecuted devotees in Russia who have no temple and read scripture in dark rooms yet still go out to preach. The temple, Prabhupada said, is not a place for eating and sleeping but a base for preaching; one should take only what is necessary (Sri Isopanishad, mantra 1) and offer the balance in service, ultimately reaching desirelessness (nishkama) and following the gopis, who—as Prabhupada wept to recall—never asked anything of Krishna but only wished to please Him, which is the very definition of pure devotion (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 1.1.11). In the question period he adds that material facility is safe only when supervised by guru and Krishna, that one need not remember past misdeeds to surrender now (the drowning man who should board the boat rather than ask questions), and that Jaya and Vijaya fell to the material world to satisfy the Lord's own desire for combat.

## Approaching Krishna With Material Desire

- (Opening, mid-thought — a seeker's testimony:) Having exhausted the "materialistic science" of the West, "the only thing I can find in India is *bhakti* — real love for Krishna, love for God, that I can't find anywhere else. So I've come to join this mission."
- **One can approach the Supreme Lord even with material desire.**
- **Srimad Bhagavatam 2.3.10** — *"akāmaḥ sarva-kāmo vā mokṣa-kāma udāra-dhīḥ / tīvreṇa bhakti-yogena yajeta puruṣaṁ param"*: Whether one has **no material desire** (*akama*), whether one is **full of material desires** (*sarva-kama*), or whether one is **desiring liberation** (*moksha-kama*) — in all circumstances one should approach **Krishna, the Supreme Whole.**
  - Why? Because the result of approaching Krishna with those desires is that **we become purified.**

## The Fire Analogy — Benefit by Association

- "It's like when you approach the fire. Even a blind man who can't appreciate the good qualities of the fire — by the association of the fire, he gets warm." He can't see or appreciate it, but by association he still benefits.
- So one may approach Krishna *blindly*, with material desire: "Please satisfy this desire, my Lord." But whether that desire is fulfilled or not, **one becomes purified.**
- **Chanting even in the offensive stage:** Therefore we can chant Hare Krishna even in the offensive stage.
  - We should *not* commit offense on the strength of chanting Hare Krishna.
  - But if we are offensive due to our materialistic past, the Lord sees the sincere desire of the candidate, and **the holy name will purify.**
- By approaching the Lord with material desire — even with no desire for liberation — and recognizing the supreme authority of the Lord as the one fulfilling those desires, **one begins Krishna consciousness.**

## Contrast: Demigod Worship

- Those who worship the **demigods** for the same facility do not realize that the Supreme Lord is the ultimate one.
  - They do not become Krishna conscious — they become "Durga conscious, or Indra conscious, or Ganesh conscious": "Thank you, Ganesh, for satisfying my desire." "Thank you, Indra."
- But a **devotee** realizes: "Thank you, my Lord." He thinks of Krishna — **just like Dhruva Maharaj.**

## Dhruva Maharaj — The Pebble and the Diamond

- **Dhruva Maharaj** approached the Lord with very strong material desires.
- But after he got what he wanted, he realized how insignificant it was — because **he saw the Lord.**
- Comparing his fulfilled material desire with the vision of the Lord, he said: "This is like a **pebble**, and this is like a **diamond** — I can't compare the two." And then **he gave it up.**

## Higher Taste — Giving Up Sense Gratification

- So we may approach the Lord with material desire, but we should come to the stage of **giving up** those material desires by getting a **higher taste** of devotional service.
- **Bhagavad Gita 2.59** — *"paraṁ dṛṣṭvā nivartate"*: By experiencing a higher taste, the devotee gives up sense gratification.
- It is *not* that he gets Krishna and also tries to keep sense gratification.
- **The monkey and the banana:** That's like the monkey who put his hand into the bottle to grab the banana, but wouldn't unclench his fist to pull it out. He wanted **both** — the freedom *and* the banana — but he couldn't have both.

## The Central Teaching: Oil and Water

- **We cannot have Krishna consciousness and sense gratification.**
- "**Sense gratification and Krishna consciousness go ill together**" — as Prabhupada said in a **purport in the Bhagavad Gita.**
- **Just like oil and water:** No matter how hard you try, from now to eternity, you'll never mix oil and water. "You squish them, you mush them, whatever you do — it'll always break off into little beads."

## From Childish Taking to Mature Giving

- Coming with material desire is fine — **but that's only the beginning.** One should come to the stage of **unmotivated, uninterrupted service** (*ahaituki, apratihata*).
- **The parenting analogy:**
  - In the beginning, the father tolerates the childish activities of his children. A little child does something naughty, but because he is small, there is no punishment.
  - The child is always *taking* from the parents: "Give me this, give me that, give me, give me, give me." Anyone who's had children knows — unlimited material desires, even at a young age.
  - But the father tolerates and helps the son or daughter, understanding that in due course they will grow up and help the business.
- **So the spiritual master may be tolerant.** Prabhupada said the spiritual master is very compassionate upon the disciple:
  - The disciple may make mistakes, may even fall down, may be immature in many ways — but the spiritual master is very compassionate, and tolerates.
  - But not that after so many years we are still saying, "Give me, give me, give me."
- At some stage, the devotee should become mature and **want to give** to the spiritual master and to Krishna.
  - This is **nishtha**, the steady stage of devotional service.
  - We should become responsible, grown up, mature.

## Becoming Good Association Ourselves — The Devotees in Russia

- The other day someone said they are feeling a **lack of association**, and because of that they don't know to whom to reveal their mind or what to do.
- **The remedy: we have to be mature. We should ourselves become good association**, since we've been in this movement for so many months or years.
  - "Sometimes the bird has to get kicked out of the nest to learn to fly." We should all become **shelter for other devotees.**
- **The example of Russia:** Think of our godbrothers and godsisters in Russia, who have no facility for devotional service in a material sense — so persecuted:
  - No godbrothers or godsisters to talk to; no temple president; no vice-president; no temple — "only demons everywhere."
  - They are all young — 16, 17, 18 years of age. But they go out; they are responsible; **they understand the mission of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.**
  - On their own they go out and preach. No Bhagavatam class, no Bhagavad Gita class. They have to read "in a dark room at night so no one can see them."
  - So they become responsible and mature. They are **not lamenting**, "I don't have this, I don't have this."
- By contrast, "we have everything, especially in this temple — so much facility." This is the **stage of maturity.**

## Come With Material Desires — Then Rise to Ahaituki Service

- We may have come for many material reasons — even to take shelter because it's the best place to take shelter; you may have come in distress.
  - "You've come to the right place, because **Hari means one who relieves distress.**"
- So we encourage people: **Come with your material desires to Krishna consciousness**, because the Lord will fulfill them. And once those insignificant desires are fulfilled and you've experienced a higher taste of Krishna consciousness, then you can begin **ahaituki** — unmotivated, uninterrupted service.
- **The mood of a devotee** (a prayer/song quoted): "Happiness and distress, heat and cold — we want to keep fighting on, delivering Your message as You asked us to, while we're enthusiastic, youthful, and strong."
- **Prabhupada's charge:** "I'm an old man — what can I do? But you are all young, so you can take up this movement."
- **Service is what we are here for** — not to satisfy our material desires. Prabhupada said: **"The temple is not a place for eating and sleeping; it's a base to go out and preach Krishna consciousness."**

## Taking Only What Is Necessary — Toward Desirelessness

- Everyone is allowed some material facility — Prabhupada was always very concerned that the devotees have proper facility.
- **But we should learn what to take: only what is necessary.**
  - **Sri Ishopanishad, Mantra 1** — *"īśāvāsyam idaṁ sarvam"*: take what is necessary [as one's allotted quota].
- Then the balance of our time becomes enthusiastic to serve the Supreme Lord.
- Eventually, through this process, we come to the stage of **nishkama** — **desirelessness** — and we can follow in the footsteps of the **gopis.**

## The Gopis — Prabhupada's Tears

- "I was listening to a very nice class yesterday" in which Prabhupada was discussing different types of devotees — first materialists, then demigod worshippers, then different types of devotees.
- Then he came to the **gopis** and said: **"But the gopis — they never asked anything from Krishna."** And then **Prabhupada began to cry.**
  - "We can hardly understand what moved Prabhupada to cry. But this is the mood of the pure devotee — they never asked anything from Krishna. He simply wants to glorify Krishna."
- **This is pure devotional service.** **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 1.1.11 / Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 19.167** — *"anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam / ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānu-śīlanaṁ bhaktir uttamā"*: devotional service to Krishna must be rendered without desire for gain or profit through fruitive activity or philosophical speculation.
  - This is best exemplified by the cowherd girls of Vrindavan, who are simply constantly thinking, **"How can I please the Supreme Lord?"**
- By Prabhupada's grace, we too can come to this stage one day. This is **real devotion.**
  - Devotion does *not* mean worshiping the demigod for some material facility — that is being *alpa-medhasam*, **less intelligent** (cf. **Bhagavad Gita 7.23**).

## Q&A — Material Opulence Is Like Playing With Fire

- **Question** (about material opulence). **Answer:** Yes, but the danger is that material opulence will cause them to forget Krishna.
  - They may worship Durga in their youth to get some material facility, but "it's like **playing with fire**." As said before, fire can purify or destroy — it must be handled very carefully.
- **The safeguard:** If that material facility is **supervised by Krishna or the devotee of Krishna**, one can gradually become detached from it. But without the blessings of Krishna while dealing with it, it can cause forgetfulness.
- We all have material attachments, but we are working with them **under the guidance of the spiritual master and Krishna**, learning gradually how to take what is necessary and leave the rest aside.
  - Without that strict guidance, we can easily make a mistake and become more entangled in sense gratification.
  - So if one gets that facility from the Supreme Lord, His representative, or the organization, one can be protected from falling down under its influence.

## Q&A — "I Can't Remember My Past Mistakes" Is No Excuse

- **Question** (raised on behalf of a doubter, e.g. a university student): How can the Lord punish me for something I can't even remember doing?
- **Answer — the general principle:** Even if one can't remember exactly what he's done, the principle is that **we've done something wrong, because here we are again.** "Once burnt, twice shy."
  - Whatever caused us to take another birth, we may not know the detail, but the principle is that **we transgressed the laws of God.**
  - So now we should try not to transgress *any* law, because by transgressing one, two, three, or four — "here we are again."
  - We may not remember the exact mistake, but we made a mistake; there are many mistakes that can be made. Therefore we should be very serious to understand the **laws of God** and not transgress them.
- **The intoxication analogy:** A person who is intoxicated may commit a crime, then in court plead, "But I was intoxicated — it's not my fault, I didn't know what I was doing." But the point is: **he shouldn't have been intoxicated in the first place.**
  - Likewise, "we should have been a devotee last lifetime, and the lifetime before that." "I can't remember" is not an excuse.
- **Prabhupada's story — "Everyone's crazy":** In court, a defense lawyer argued, "Your Honor, he's a crazy man, so he can't be responsible for his acts." A psychiatrist was called in to study the case; his conclusion to the court was: **"Your Honor, everyone's crazy in this material world."** ("He was probably a sadhu.")
- **Independence and responsibility:** Everyone has their own independence. Everyone is **the architect of his own destiny** — his karma, his consciousness. No one can say, "How can the Lord be so unkind?" Everyone is in the particular position they are in **out of their own desire.** So even one's *ignorance* is a result of the misuse of one's independence.
- **Prabhupada's drowning-man analogy:** Whether we're full of ignorance or coming out of ignorance, we should **take shelter of Krishna.** The reason doesn't matter.
  - You're in the water, drowning; a boat comes and says, "Come on, get in." You don't say, "Could you first explain exactly how I got in this water, how long I've been here, and how long I have to live?" When you're drowning, there's an emergency — you get in the boat and go to shore. Those things will be revealed at a later stage.
- **The bottom line:** We're in this ocean of material existence. Why we're at this particular point now — Krishna says in the **Bhagavad Gita** that the **intricacies of karma are very difficult to understand** (*"gahana karmaṇo gatiḥ,"* **BG 4.17**). So how, why, when, where is not so important.
  - The importance is: **now you have the opportunity to surrender to Krishna. Are you going to accept, or again make a mistake?**
  - "Here's the opportunity, here's Krishna consciousness, my dear university student. You don't want to surrender because you feel God is unkind for not letting you remember your mistakes. But here's the solution: **don't make another mistake — accept Krishna consciousness.**"

## Q&A — Jaya and Vijaya, the Four Kumaras, and the Lord's Desire to Fight

- **Question** (about the four Kumaras / Jaya and Vijaya). **Answer:** Their desire, ultimately, was to satisfy the desires of the Lord. **They are liberated souls.**
- So the whole pastime — of "cursing," of not letting the four Kumaras into the spiritual world — **was the plan of the Lord.**
  - Just like **Arjuna** became bewildered so that the Bhagavad Gita could be spoken. He is an eternal associate of the Lord; there's no question of his being overwhelmed by Maya.
- **The Lord's fighting spirit:** The Lord actually has a fighting spirit. Where does the fighting spirit come from in the material world? It's a **perverted reflection of the Lord's desire for combat.**
  - But in the **Vaikuntha** planets there are no devotees to fight with, because everyone is in loving devotional service.
  - So sometimes the Lord feels this tendency — "I want to fight. Whom will I fight with?" — so He arranged for **Jaya and Vijaya** to fall from the spiritual world.
- So their desire to take birth as demons was not ultimately because they wanted to get *back* to the spiritual world, but because they wanted to **fulfill the Lord's desire to fight.** The Lord doesn't just fight with any ordinary demon — He likes to do these things with His devotees.
- **The "ghastly" rasa** (as the speaker names it): a relationship harder for us to understand, where the devotee likes to throw arrows at the Lord's body and see the blood come, and the Lord likes to throw arrows at His devotee's body and see the patches of blood.
  - **Example: Bhishmadeva.** Bhishmadeva's supreme vision of Krishna is when the Lord rushes at him — hair unfurled, angry, with a chariot wheel, ready to kill Bhishma. ("Come on, come on...")
  - Whereas we want to come and see Krishna, "You're so beautiful," Bhishma wants to see the Lord rushing at him to kill him. This is that *rasa* with Krishna.
- So the Lord doesn't want to have this *rasa* with just "some envious rascal" — He wants this pleasure of fighting with His devotee. So **Jaya and Vijaya came to this world to fight with Krishna.**
- "This is the higher understanding. There are different understandings on different levels, but this is one way of answering the question."

## Glossary

- **akama** — free from material desire
- **sarva-kama** — full of all material desires
- **moksha-kama** — desiring liberation
- **nishtha** — the steady, fixed stage of devotional service
- **nishkama** — desirelessness in devotional service
- **ahaituki apratihata** — unmotivated and uninterrupted (devotional service)
- **Hari** — the Lord as the one who removes and relieves distress
- **gopis** — the cowherd girls of Vrindavan, exemplars of pure devotion
- **raudra rasa** — the fighting or 'ghastly' relationship with Krishna, as in Bhishma's vision
