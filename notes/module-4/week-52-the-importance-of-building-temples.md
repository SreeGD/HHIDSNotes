# The importance of building temples

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 52 · Foundations in Bhakti · 53:28

## Summary

Speaking at Sridham Mayapur around the appearance day of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, as ISKCON leaders gathered for strategy meetings and the great chakra was about to be installed on the new temple (the Temple of the Vedic Planetarium), Indradyumna Swami explains why building grand temples is essential to Krishna consciousness. Although Gaudiya Vaishnavas personally need nothing more than a mala and a place to chant the holy name, magnificent temples are required to attract and train the general public, who after hearing the Hare Krishna mantra need a place to come, be inspired, and see the Lord in His deity form. He traces this mission from Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's instruction to the six Goswamis to write books, rediscover the Lord's lost pastime places, and build temples, through Bhaktivinoda and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, down to Srila Prabhupada, who established 108 temples. He illustrates the principle that 'along with the order of the guru comes the ability to fulfill it' with the story of Lala Babu, a wealthy Calcutta businessman turned Vrindavan renunciate who, ordered by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati to build a grand temple, begged even his enemies for donations; when his greatest enemy dumped a bucket of dirty ashes on his head, he simply said 'thank you very much' and walked away, and this humility so moved Kolkata's Vaishnava community that the temple (Lala Babu Mandir, near Gopishwara Mahadeva) was funded in three days. He also recounts Prabhupada's one-line letter that revived the discouraged early London devotees ('If you build a temple of Lord Krishna in this world, Krishna will build a palace for you in the spiritual world'), and the elaborate story of Tirumangai Alvar, who enlisted thieves and four mystic yogis to fund the seven-walled Sri Rangam temple, after which the greedy thieves drowned chanting 'money is the honey' yet still attained liberation because they had rendered some service to the Lord. Comparing Ambarish Prabhu (Alfred Ford) to those mystic financiers who are now supplying the balance of the funds, he urges everyone to help build the Mayapur temple through donations, work, or even good words. He closes by quoting Srimad Bhagavatam that building a temple makes one ruler of the three worlds, and by describing India (Bharata) as meaning 'light'—transcendental knowledge whose greatest gift is the teachings of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, destined to shine from Mayapur across the world so that people may come by the millions and go back home, back to Godhead.

## Key Points

- Gaudiya Vaishnavas need only the holy name, but grand, opulent temples are essential to attract, train, and inspire the general public, who after hearing Hare Krishna need a temple where they can see the Lord in His deity form.
- Temple-building is a core Vedic and Gaudiya tradition: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu ordered the six Goswamis to write books, rediscover holy places, and build temples, a mission continued by Bhaktivinoda and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura and by Srila Prabhupada, who established 108 temples.
- 'Along with the order of the guru comes the ability to fulfill it': Lala Babu, a rich businessman turned Vrindavan renunciate, humbly thanked the enemy who dumped ashes on his head, and that humility inspired Kolkata's devotees to fund his temple in three days.
- A pure devotee's selfless desires are certain to be fulfilled because they are ultimately Krishna's own desires (love is reciprocal); Prabhupada's single-sentence letter about building a temple revived the discouraged London devotees, who then acquired the Bury Place temple.
- Tirumangai Alvar, finding a neglected jungle temple with rats on the altar, funded the seven-walled Sri Rangam temple by engaging thieves and four mystic yogis (one walking on water, one opening any lock, two freezing people via shadow or mind); the greedy thieves drowned chanting 'money is the honey' but still went back to Godhead for the service they rendered.
- Modern parallel: Ambarish Prabhu (Alfred Ford) is providing the balance of the funds for the Mayapur temple; everyone can and should participate according to their ability—through donations, physical work, or even encouraging words—and thereby gain eternal benefit.
- India (Bharata) means 'light,' i.e., transcendental knowledge, and its greatest gift is the teachings of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu; the Mayapur temple is to be a lighthouse for the world, fulfilling the vision of Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Srila Prabhupada.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 11 (Deity-worship / kriya-yoga instruction, likely 11.27.52), paraphrased in the closing: 'By installing the deity of the Lord, one becomes king of the entire earth; by building a temple for the Lord, one becomes ruler of the three worlds; by worshiping and serving the deity, one goes to the planet of Lord Brahma; and by performing all three of these activities, one attains a transcendental form like the Lord's.'
- Nrisimha Purana (verse number not specified in the talk), quoted by Tirumangai Alvar: one who helps build a beautiful temple for the Lord is freed from all sinful reactions and very soon enters the Vaikuntha planets—used to explain why the drowned thieves went back to Godhead.
- Bible, Gospel of John (10:30), 'the Father and I are one'—cited to illustrate the pure devotee's oneness of desire with God (the speaker clarifies this does not mean Jesus is God, but that he knew every one of God's desires).
- Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta (Satsvarupa dasa Goswami), the 'Let there be a temple' episode—referenced regarding Srila Prabhupada envisioning a Radha-Krishna temple in New York with only about 40 rupees, and ultimately establishing 108 temples worldwide.
