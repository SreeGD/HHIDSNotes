# The importance of building temples

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 52 · Foundations in Bhakti · 53:28

**Themes:** temple building · the guru's order · humility · vedic culture · fundraising · mayapur

## Summary

Speaking at Sridham Mayapur around the appearance day of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, as ISKCON leaders gathered for strategy meetings and the great chakra was about to be installed on the new temple (the Temple of the Vedic Planetarium), Indradyumna Swami explains why building grand temples is essential to Krishna consciousness. Although Gaudiya Vaishnavas personally need nothing more than a mala and a place to chant the holy name, magnificent temples are required to attract and train the general public, who after hearing the Hare Krishna mantra need a place to come, be inspired, and see the Lord in His deity form. He traces this mission from Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's instruction to the six Goswamis to write books, rediscover the Lord's lost pastime places, and build temples, through Bhaktivinoda and Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, down to Srila Prabhupada, who established 108 temples. He illustrates the principle that 'along with the order of the guru comes the ability to fulfill it' with the story of Lala Babu, a wealthy Calcutta businessman turned Vrindavan renunciate who, ordered by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati to build a grand temple, begged even his enemies for donations; when his greatest enemy dumped a bucket of dirty ashes on his head, he simply said 'thank you very much' and walked away, and this humility so moved Kolkata's Vaishnava community that the temple (Lala Babu Mandir, near Gopishwara Mahadeva) was funded in three days. He also recounts Prabhupada's one-line letter that revived the discouraged early London devotees ('If you build a temple of Lord Krishna in this world, Krishna will build a palace for you in the spiritual world'), and the elaborate story of Tirumangai Alvar, who enlisted thieves and four mystic yogis to fund the seven-walled Sri Rangam temple, after which the greedy thieves drowned chanting 'money is the honey' yet still attained liberation because they had rendered some service to the Lord. Comparing Ambarish Prabhu (Alfred Ford) to those mystic financiers who are now supplying the balance of the funds, he urges everyone to help build the Mayapur temple through donations, work, or even good words. He closes by quoting Srimad Bhagavatam that building a temple makes one ruler of the three worlds, and by describing India (Bharata) as meaning 'light'—transcendental knowledge whose greatest gift is the teachings of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, destined to shine from Mayapur across the world so that people may come by the millions and go back home, back to Godhead.

## Opening Obeisances and Occasion

- Offers humble obeisances to His Holiness Bhaktividya Purna Maharaja and to godbrother Naraswara Prabhu for supporting him this afternoon.
- Greets the assembled godbrothers and godsisters gathered for the strategy meetings, and all the ISKCON leaders present: temple presidents, regional secretaries, GBCs, and everyone who has come as the movement approaches the auspicious appearance day of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- Notes it is especially nice to come at this moment because in a couple of days the big **chakra** will be installed atop the temple.

## The Temple Rising from the Chintamani Sands of Mayapur

- One day this great temple, manifesting from the Chintamani sands of Mayapur, will be famous all over the world — just as the **Hare Krishna Maha-mantra** (Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama...) is now, "for better or worse," famous all over the world.
- This temple will make the Mayapur Chandrodaya Mandir look like what it truly is: the world headquarters of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness.
- The crowds will grow — thousands, then tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands, and it is "not too far out" to say that in the future millions will come, just as people go to **Sri Rangam**, **Tirupati**, and so many holy places.
- This may very well become the most famous tirtha (holy place of pilgrimage) in India — probably the most popular place in India.

## Temple-Building Is Part of Vedic Culture

- Constructing such temples is very much part of Vedic culture. Having traveled India extensively over the last 48 years, the speaker observes there are thousands — hundreds of thousands — of temples; including home temples, there are millions in the country. They are very important in the fabric of the people's spiritual lives.
- In one sense, as Gaudiya Vaishnavas we do not need such big structures for ourselves. We are fully satisfied sitting under a banyan tree, a **kalpa-vriksha** (desire tree), or in a mud hut, chanting on our mala or chanting in congregation. We do not depend on a large temple.
- **But to attract the general public, such grandeur is absolutely required** — and that is part of our life.

## Our Whole Life Is Chanting and Spreading the Holy Name

- Our life is either chanting Hare Krishna or, according to our ability, spreading the chanting of Hare Krishna. That is all we do; everything in the movement is more or less centered around chanting the holy name. So we are either chanting or distributing the holy names.
- After people hear Hare Krishna, they need a temple to come to — to be trained, to be inspired, to see the Lord in His deity form.
- The temple is built for the people of Nadia, of India, of America, France, Germany, Mongolia, China — so they can come and learn about Krishna.

## Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Prepared for ISKCON

- For this reason the Lord, who introduced the Yuga-dharma of chanting Hare Krishna, instructed His immediate followers, the **six Goswamis**, to go to Vrindavan, write books on the science of Krishna consciousness, discover the lost places of the Lord's pastimes, and build big temples. He was preparing for ISKCON, coming 500 years later.
- This was also the mood of **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**, who really pushed the Sankirtan movement forward in this country and had the vision that it would go around the world — as inspired by his father, **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura**.

## The Story of Lala Babu and the Vrindavan Temple

- Throughout India there are many Gaudiya Matha temples established by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati; having some 50,000 disciples, he needed many temples.
- One disciple was especially inspired to build a temple for his Guru Maharaj: **Lala Babu**. He was a renunciate, but before joining the Gaudiya Matha he was one of Calcutta's wealthiest and most successful businessmen. He gave up all his riches after being introduced by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura to the chanting of Hare Krishna.
- Practically he became like a baba — a loincloth, his mala, and a water pot — and retired to Vrindavan, living in the holy name. Like **Akinchana Krishna Das Babaji**, who just chanted all day (which was fine with Bhaktisiddhanta, as he set a good example), Lala Babu would sit in the matha 22 hours a day chanting "three lakhs of rounds" — Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna...
- **The visit:** One day Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura visited that matha in Vrindavan, went into the brahmachari room, and saw Lala Babu chanting in the corner — very satisfied, very happy, no hankering, no lamenting. He said: "I want you to build a big, glorious, fabulous, wonderful temple here in Vrindavan, where people can come and see the **Vigraha of Radha and Krishna**. It should be very grand, very big, very beautiful, and very expensive."
- Skinny Lala Babu, with his mala and half-full water pot, protested: "Guru Maharaj, how am I going to build a temple? I'm a baba." One of Bhaktisiddhanta's servants spoke up: **"Along with the order of the Guru comes the ability to fulfill it. Do it!"**
- **The begging:** Being a sincere disciple, Lala Babu got up and took a train back to Calcutta. He visited even his enemies — for in business and politics there is much fighting to reach the top, and he had powerful enemies. He went to the houses of his friends, associates, and even his enemies, begging even a single rupee to help fulfill the order of Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura. A devotee has no friends or enemies; he sees everyone as a candidate for devotional service.
- **The dirty ashes:** His biggest enemy was another big businessman whom he had hated. He knocked on the door; the enemy was shocked — "Who are you?" "I am Lala Babu, formerly such-and-such. Could you please give me a donation to build a temple?" The enemy said, "Yes, I will give you something." Having just had a fire and cooked something, he collected all the dirty ashes into a bucket, hid it behind his back, opened the door, and dumped the ashes on Lala Babu's head. Covered in ashes, Lala Babu simply said, "Shri Krishna Chaitanya... thank you very much," and walked away.
- **The result:** When that story spread through the Vaishnava community of Kolkata — the land of Gauranga, full of pious people — they all came and supported him. He raised the money in about three days. "The ways of the Lord are mysterious."
- Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura was very pleased; the temple was eventually constructed, and Guru Maharaj came and opened it, mentioning the incident of the dirty ashes — showing the humility of his disciple and how it attracted the attention of Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava.
- Today, when you go to Vrindavan, you can visit the **Lala Babu Temple**, near Gopishwara Mahadeva (about 50 meters away). It is one of the biggest temples in Vrindavan; though not the most popular, people who know the story go there and pay respects not only to the deities but to Lala Babu himself.

## Srila Prabhupada's Vision of a Temple

- Practically from the day Srila Prabhupada stepped onto the pier at Boston and went on to New York, he walked around New York City alone, looking at big multi-million-dollar buildings and thinking, "Yes, this would be a nice temple for Radha and Krishna." He had only about 40 rupees, yet he was envisioning grand temples.

## The Reciprocal Nature of a Pure Devotee's Desire

- If you desire something as a jiva (jivatma), you may or may not get it, depending on your past activities in this life and previous lives, according to your karma. That is life.
- But if you are a pure devotee, selfless, desiring things only for Krishna's pleasure — which is one definition of devotional service — it is sure and certain that in due course of time that desire will be fulfilled, because ultimately it is Krishna's desire.
- **Jesus said, "The Father and I are one."** This did not mean Jesus was God, but that he was so close to God he knew every one of God's desires — and God knows every one of the pure devotee's desires to please Him. Love is reciprocal: just as the devotee wants to please Krishna, Krishna also wants to fulfill the spiritual desires of His devotee.
- Just like the famous chapter in the **Prabhupada-lilamrita**, "Let There Be a Temple" — it all came to pass. By the time Prabhupada left, there were about 108 established temples around the world.

## Prabhupada's Followers Are Obliged to Build Temples

- As did Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, as did Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, as did Srila Prabhupada — so Prabhupada's followers are also obliged to establish temples, and we should have the same faith that we can do it.
- **The London story:** When Shyamasundara, Malati, Gurudas, Yamuna, Mukunda, and Janaki went to London, they had no money and nothing. Prabhupada told them to establish a temple in London. They landed with no money, and for two years nothing happened. Getting frustrated and discouraged, they wrote to Prabhupada saying it was too hard and they didn't think they could do it.
  - Prabhupada wrote back just one sentence, and that one sentence fired them up so much that they got the first temple — at **Bury Place** ("Seven Bury Place") — which the speaker visited and found to be a fabulous temple.
  - (Aside on the children being the most fired up in kirtan, in glorification, and in building a temple — "Jesus said, from the mouth of a child... I sound like a Christian preacher.")
  - **The one sentence:** *"If you build a temple of Lord Krishna in this world, Krishna will build a palace for you in the spiritual world."* (The audience is invited to repeat it.)
- Of course, we are not aspiring for anything other than devotional service; this is just an indication of Krishna's love for His devotees.

## Part of the Great Temple in Sridham Mayapur

- Now we all have the good fortune, each in our own way, to be part of the great temple manifesting here in Sridham Mayapur.
- Against all odds, this temple is going up. To push a spiritual culture forth in the material world goes against the grain — it is really not possible except by the mercy of Guru and Krishna.

## The Story of Tirumangai Alvar and the Sri Rangam Temple

- **The dilapidated temple:** Tirumangai Alvar was one of the twelve Alvars of South India — a great scholar, a great devotee, with thousands of disciples, including four mystic yogis. Traveling through the jungle of South India with several hundred disciples, near a town they came across a very dilapidated temple: dirty, no visitors, rats on the altar, the deities looking lean and hungry.
- He became angry: "Such is the state of the world in Kali Yuga that people are no longer interested in taking care of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who Himself is their caretaker." He told his disciples: **"Let us build a beautiful, magnificent temple that no one can resist — so glorious, so wonderful, with so many wonderful activities constantly going on, that even a sinful person will want to visit and become purified."**
- **The problem of no money:** But all his followers were like Lala Babu — with no external opulence, only the real opulence within: **bhava** and devotion. Externally they were all babas (loincloth, mala, water pot) doing **madhukari** (begging for food). "Guru Maharaj, how are we going to build a big temple?"
- **The failed collection:** Tirumangai proposed that they go to a large, wealthy town nearby and beg dakshina to build a temple for Krishna/Narayan. Hundreds of disciples and mystics went into town, very excited, spent the whole day, and collected not a single anna. Thousands of wealthy people, and no one gave. When the sadhus tried to beg, people threw them off their property, insulted them, said bad words, and pushed them off: "Go away, you rascal beggar!" — the whole town.
- **Innovation of the acharya:** Heads of spiritual institutions must be innovative, because times change and they must present Krishna consciousness in novel ways without sacrificing the tradition. This can only be done by acharyas, like Srila Prabhupada. "Necessity is the mother of invention."
  - Tirumangai gathered his disciples: "Go out and get all the thieves, robbers, and dacoits, and bring them to me. I want to talk to them." The acharya is expert at engaging people according to their propensity.
  - (Aside: Prabhupada wrote to godbrother **Bhagavan Das**, "You are a good leader because you know how to engage all your followers according to their propensities." That is the definition of **Sankirtan** — the complete glorification, in which everyone can participate according to his ability; everyone is welcome.)
- **Recruiting the thieves:** So Tirumangai had an assembly of robbers and dacoits — dirty, with big moustaches, scars, and tattoos — addressing him as "my dear Prabhu." He instructed them: "Go into this town for the next few days and steal the money of all these wealthy people." The thief exclaimed, "Can you be my guru? I never met a guru like you! Jaya Guru Deva!" Tirumangai said, "Yes, but you must bring me all the money so I can build this temple, and we'll keep some in reserve for you as well."
  - Like Robin Hood in England, who stole from the rich to give to the poor — but these thieves took from the rich and gave to the Lord.
- **The guarded houses:** It was not easy: the wealthy have fences, security, and dogs.
  - (Aside — the speaker's own Sankirtan story in France: He knocked on a door, no one answered, so he opened it to see if anyone was home — and out came a big black Doberman Pinscher, huge teeth, red eyes, foaming at the mouth. Unable to outrun it, he climbed a porch onto a roof. The dog ran up the stairs to the attic, jumped out the attic window onto a ledge and onto the roof, and chased him. He jumped from one roof to the next, and the dog followed. The next roof was very far; running as fast as he could, he jumped and caught the gutter just in time, pulling himself onto the roof, leaving the dog behind. The homeowner came up: "What are you doing on my roof?" "Running away from dogs." "Are you crazy? Come on.")
  - So every house was guarded, and the thieves came back with not one rupee.
- **The four mystic yogis' powers:** His mystic-yogi disciples could do what others cannot:
  - **First yogi** could walk on water. Many wealthy people lived on houseboats in the middle of the river for safety; while they were at work, he walked on the water, boarded their houseboats, and stole their jewels.
  - **Second yogi** could unlock any lock without a key — no lock in the whole world could resist him. While people were at work, he unlocked their doors and stole their jewels.
  - **Third yogi** could freeze anyone by stepping on his shadow. (Aside: "Why would someone develop a mystic power like that over hundreds of thousands of years? Why not just chant Hare Krishna and go back to Godhead?") On the street he stepped on people's shadows: "Give me a donation and I'll let you go." They would give, and he'd step off.
  - **Fourth yogi** could freeze someone during conversation by the power of his mind. He left about twenty people frozen on the street: "Can you give me a donation? All right, now I'll release you." He made the biggest collection.
- This is one version of the famous story of how Tirumangai built the famous **Sri Rangam temple**, with its seven walls around it, together with his four disciples and their mystic yoga; they collected all the money.
- **Application to the present:** "Now we have the mystic yogi **Ambarish Prabhu**, who is providing the balance of the money for this temple." The dacoits collected a little bit.
- **The thieves demand their share:** At the end, the temple was built. The thieves came to Tirumangai: "You promised us some of the collection. Give us our money." ("Money is the honey, so sweet, it can't be beat" — but that is a materialistic mantra; it's not true.) Tirumangai replied: "I have already given you something more valuable than money — you got to serve the Lord and build His temple. That is much more valuable than money. Be satisfied with the seva you have done." They said, "We're not interested in your philosophy; we want our money."
- **The island and the sinking boat:** Tirumangai told them the balance of money was kept on an island in the river, under a big tree: "Take a boat and go get it." Ten got on the boat, then 20, 30, 40, 50 — the boat could only carry 15, but 110 thieves got on. As they rowed it was very hard and slow; the head thief said, "Chant the mantra — money is the honey, so sweet," and the boat began moving. But slowly, going across the river, the overloaded boat sank — "blub, blub, blub" — and they all drowned.
- **Tirumangai's equipoise:** When this news reached Tirumangai, he expressed neither happiness nor sadness; he was equipoised and transcendental, seeing the event differently. He quoted a famous verse from the **Nrisimha Purana**: *"O devoted one, one who helps build a beautiful temple for the Lord will be freed from all sinful reaction, and very soon he will enter the Vaikuntha planets, the spiritual world."* He saw that because the robbers had given some seva to the Lord, their hearts were purified and they passed away in a sacred river — going back home, back to Godhead.

## Closing Scriptural Support and Exhortation

- Concludes with a quote from **Srimad Bhagavatam, Eleventh Canto**: *"By installing the deity of the Lord, one becomes king of the entire earth. By building a temple for the Lord, one becomes ruler of the three worlds. By worshiping and serving the deity, one goes to the planet of Lord Brahma. And by performing all three of these activities, one attains a transcendental form just like Mine."*
- Let us all fulfill the vision of **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura** and the deep desire of our own spiritual master — to see a beautiful temple rise here from the sands of the Ganga — by trying our best to engage the people of this world: giving donations, doing work on the temple, or at least giving their good words. Engage as many people as possible; just like those thieves (though people need not be thieves), let them render some seva from which they will get eternal benefit. This is a great opportunity not only for us but for the entire world.

## The Temple as the Light of the World

- Let us not think of it merely as a great, beautiful, ornate construction — let us think of it as **the light of the world**.
- The word **Bharat** has many meanings in Sanskrit; one meaning is "light" — not merely sunshine, but **divya-jnana**, transcendental knowledge, by which the whole world can be saved and delivered. India is a spiritual light.
- What is the best that India has to offer? The teachings of **Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** — the most important thing India gives to the world.
- The author **A. L. Basham** wrote a book called *The Wonder That Was India*. Let us bring back "the wonder that was India" to "the wonder that is India" — incredible India — the teachings of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, meant to go from this holy place of Mayapur all over the world.
- Just as sailors are guided by the big lighthouse on the coast, safely passing any dangers, so let people come here to Mayapur by the millions, take shelter of the teachings of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, be saved and delivered, and go back home, back to Godhead.
- Hare Krishna.

## Glossary

- **baba / babaji** — a renunciate who lives simply with only a loincloth, water pot, and mala
- **madhukari** — begging alms door to door, taking a little from each household
- **Alvar** — one of the twelve great South Indian Vaishnava saint-poets
- **arca-vigraha / Vigraha** — the deity form of the Lord installed for worship
- **dakshina** — a donation or offering given to a holy person or sacred cause
- **acharya** — a spiritual teacher who teaches by example and can present Krishna consciousness in new ways without changing the tradition
- **Vaikuntha** — the eternal spiritual world, the abode of Vishnu
- **yuga-dharma** — the recommended spiritual process for the age; for Kali-yuga, chanting the holy name
- **Bharata** — a name of India, one meaning of which is 'light' (transcendental knowledge)
- **Gaudiya Vaishnava** — a follower of the devotional tradition of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu
