# Think Big

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 67 ·  · 1:13:01

## Summary

Delivered on the day of the Panihati (Chipped Rice) Festival, this lecture by Indradyumna Swami reads from the Chaitanya-caritamrita the pastime in which Nityananda Prabhu playfully calls the wealthy young Raghunath Das a 'thief' and 'punishes' him by ordering a feast of chipped rice and yogurt for all the devotees on the bank of the Ganges near Panihati. Indradyumna Swami uses Raghunath Das Goswami's life to illustrate genuine renunciation: because he possessed the highest thing (attachment to Radha-Krishna and Lord Chaitanya), he could easily abandon his wealth, wife, and comforts, following the niti-sastra logic that 'you can only give up something if you get something better.' He stresses that devotees need not imitate artificial austerities; renunciation comes naturally by chanting Hare Krishna, honoring prasadam, hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam, and serving devotees, with unwanted desires falling away like a snake's shed skin as one matures, though pratistha (the subtle desire for honor) is the hardest impurity to remove. Turning to the goal, he identifies Raghunath Das Goswami as the 'prayojana guru' who reveals the highest attainment: pure love of God (prema) in the mood of the gopis of Vraja, and especially love in separation (vipralambha), which is described as more relishable than meeting Krishna. Reading through the Shikshashtaka, Prabhupada's letters, and the Nectar of Devotion, he teaches that one should learn to cry for the Lord and hanker for that goal even while practicing the basics. He recounts the pastime of Advaita Acharya, whose tears of love Mahaprabhu 'locked in a knot' on his japa rope so that he could keep managing and preaching, to show that prema is sometimes withheld from preachers whose service the mission still needs. The central exhortation, echoing Prabhupada's plan for an 18-day Mahabharata film in Nairobi, is to 'think big': faithfully do one's daily service now, but aspire boldly to go back to Godhead, where the spiritual master takes the disciple's hand and places it in Krishna's.

## Key Points

- The Panihati (Chipped Rice) Festival pastime: Nityananda Prabhu 'punishes' Raghunath Das by ordering a feast of chipped rice and yogurt for all the devotees; the cooling foods (dahi, bananas) were chosen for the hot monsoon season, and this festival is now observed in ISKCON temples worldwide.
- Genuine renunciation flows from possessing something higher: Raghunath Das gave up immense wealth, a beautiful wife, and all opulence because of his deep attachment to Radha-Krishna and Lord Chaitanya, following the logic 'you can only give up something if you get something better.'
- Artificial austerity is unnecessary (illustrated by the sadhu at Chiragat sitting under hot coals); anarthas fall away naturally through chanting, prasadam, and hearing, like a snake shedding its skin, while pratistha, the desire to be honored, is the subtlest desire to conquer.
- Prasadam is 'Lord Chaitanya's secret weapon' and is absolute (non-different from Krishna), so it is to be respected, not merely eaten; Prabhupada took prasadam with great awe and reverence as Krishna's mercy.
- The ultimate goal (prayojana) is prema, love of Krishna in the mood of the Vraja gopis, revealed by Raghunath Das Goswami in works like Vilapa-kusumanjali; love in separation (vipralambha) is more relishable than union.
- Prema may be deliberately withheld from active preachers: Mahaprabhu 'locked' Advaita Acharya's tears in a knot on His rope so he could keep managing devotees, and Indradyumna Swami's godbrother tells him prema now would end his preaching mission.
- 'Think big': as Prabhupada thought big (the proposed 18-day Mahabharata movie), aspire boldly for the highest spiritual goal while doing simple daily service (chant, dance, hear, take prasadam, serve), because the guru ultimately walks the disciple into the kunja and places his hand in Krishna's.

- Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya 19.151 ('brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva') - wandering through the universe since time immemorial, quoted on the soul's long entanglement.

- Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.16 ('susrusoh sraddadhanasya...') - by serving great devotees free from vice one gains a taste for hearing the messages of Vasudeva ('O twice-born sages...').

- Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 7 (Prahlada Maharaja) - Lord Nrsimhadeva placing His hand on Prahlada's head removes the last vestiges of material desire; used to discuss how subtle desires linger like the lingering smell of camphor.
- Sri Shikshashtaka (Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu), verses 2, 5, 7, and 8 - verse 5 ('place me as an atom of dust at Your lotus feet, lift me from the ocean of birth and death'); verse 7 ('yugayitam nimesena... govinda-virahena me' - a moment feels like twelve years, tears flow like torrents); verse 8 ('aslisya va pada-ratam...' - I know no one but Krishna even if He handles me roughly). - Sri Shikshashtaka, verse 1 ('ceto-darpana-marjanam') and verse 3 ('kirtaniyah sada harih') - cleansing the mirror of the heart and always chanting the holy name.
- Vilapa-kusumanjali by Raghunath Das Goswami - 'I am yours, I am yours, I cannot live without you... O queen, please bring me to your lotus feet,' cited as the essence of the devotee's ultimate prayer; his Manah-siksa, Dana-keli-cintamani, and Mukta-carita are also recommended.
- Padyavali (Rupa Goswami) - 'If I had to choose between union with Krishna and separation, I would choose separation, for with Krishna I see only one Krishna, but in separation I see Him coming toward me from every corner of the universe.'
- Nectar of Devotion, p. 83 - 'One should learn how to cry for the Lord... this is called laulyam (lolya), and such tears are the price for the highest perfection.'
- Nectar of Devotion, p. 107 - 'For persons who are not inclined to clear the dust from their hearts, who want to keep the dirty things as they are, it is not possible to derive the transcendental result of chanting Hare Krishna.'
- Srila Prabhupada, letter (Seattle, 1968) - 'as we go on chanting in the pure status we shall be on the loving platform... that feeling of separation from the Lord is more relishable than meeting the Lord.'
- Srila Prabhupada, lecture at 26 Second Avenue (1966) - 'The more you feel separation from Krishna, you should understand that you are advancing... but don't try to see Krishna artificially; be advanced in separation feelings.'
