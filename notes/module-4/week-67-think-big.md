# Think Big

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 67 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:13:01

**Themes:** renunciation through higher taste · panihati festival · prasadam · love in separation (vipralambha) · the ultimate goal (prema) · think big

## Summary

Delivered on the day of the Panihati (Chipped Rice) Festival, this lecture by Indradyumna Swami reads from the Chaitanya-caritamrita the pastime in which Nityananda Prabhu playfully calls the wealthy young Raghunath Das a 'thief' and 'punishes' him by ordering a feast of chipped rice and yogurt for all the devotees on the bank of the Ganges near Panihati. Indradyumna Swami uses Raghunath Das Goswami's life to illustrate genuine renunciation: because he possessed the highest thing (attachment to Radha-Krishna and Lord Chaitanya), he could easily abandon his wealth, wife, and comforts, following the niti-sastra logic that 'you can only give up something if you get something better.' He stresses that devotees need not imitate artificial austerities; renunciation comes naturally by chanting Hare Krishna, honoring prasadam, hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam, and serving devotees, with unwanted desires falling away like a snake's shed skin as one matures, though pratistha (the subtle desire for honor) is the hardest impurity to remove. Turning to the goal, he identifies Raghunath Das Goswami as the 'prayojana guru' who reveals the highest attainment: pure love of God (prema) in the mood of the gopis of Vraja, and especially love in separation (vipralambha), which is described as more relishable than meeting Krishna. Reading through the Shikshashtaka, Prabhupada's letters, and the Nectar of Devotion, he teaches that one should learn to cry for the Lord and hanker for that goal even while practicing the basics. He recounts the pastime of Advaita Acharya, whose tears of love Mahaprabhu 'locked in a knot' on his japa rope so that he could keep managing and preaching, to show that prema is sometimes withheld from preachers whose service the mission still needs. The central exhortation, echoing Prabhupada's plan for an 18-day Mahabharata film in Nairobi, is to 'think big': faithfully do one's daily service now, but aspire boldly to go back to Godhead, where the spiritual master takes the disciple's hand and places it in Krishna's.

## Appreciation of the Devotees' Offerings

- Thanks for the very beautiful offerings — scholarly, erudite, based on the authority of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and above all full of **bhakti** (devotion) to the Lord, to the spiritual master, and to the Vaiṣṇavas. Everyone was pleased.
- Kṛṣṇa is pleased seeing His devotees advance; the spiritual master is pleased seeing his disciples remain steady in Kṛṣṇa consciousness year after year. It is not so easy — "you always say it's easy: chant, dance, feast."
- **Kṛṣṇa's warning** is like a warning sign while driving (slow down, think how you take the turn): **BG 7.14** (*daivī hy eṣā guṇamayī mama māyā duratyayā...*): this divine energy of Mine, consisting of the three modes of material nature, Kṛṣṇa admits is difficult to overcome — but those who surrender unto Me can easily overcome it.

### Analogy — The Spider's Web
- Prabhupāda's analogy: the spider spins the web, and because it comes from her and is her nature, she can walk all over it without getting entangled. But as soon as a little fly unknowingly gets caught in the web, he's done for.
- For materialistic persons who have no knowledge of how to extricate themselves from the entanglement of material life by becoming devotees, it's a terrible place. But for devotees who have the guidance of a spiritual master — and our founder-ācārya Śrīla Prabhupāda, and our previous ācāryas — it's not that difficult. We're like the spider in the web: we do like everyone else, but we're doing it for Kṛṣṇa, so there's no entanglement.

### "Crying, Crying, Crying" — the Essence of the Movement
- Prabhupāda said one can understand the quality of a Vaiṣṇava or Vaiṣṇavī when they speak. There were many tears today.
- **Gour Govinda Mahārāja** was once asked by a newspaper reporter, "What is the essence of your movement? How would you describe this Hare Kṛṣṇa movement?" The speaker himself would have said: our philosophy is based on an ancient spiritual tradition at least 5,000 years old, the Bhagavad-gītā; human life is meant for spiritual advancement; the first step is to understand "I am not the body"; and the prescribed method of becoming self-realized and God conscious is to chant the holy names.
- But Gour Govinda Mahārāja replied, crying: **"Crying, crying, crying, crying"** — not sad tears, but tears of happiness. We begin to experience that love in Kṛṣṇa consciousness in each other's association, under the direction of our spiritual preceptor, and it all culminates in love for the divine couple.
- "This is one of my favorite days of the year — to sit here and get glorified. No, it's all Prabhupāda's mercy, so we can have loving exchanges."

## The Panīhāṭī (Chipped-Rice) Festival

- Today is also a most special day: on the Vaiṣṇava calendar, the celebration of the **Panīhāṭī Festival, or the Chipped Rice Festival.** For the speaker it is even more important, because it is a pastime of **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu**, the Yuga-avatāra, by whose mercy we are all here. This pastime also contains many instructions about the **guru-disciple relationship.**
- Reading from the **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** (Antya-līlā) by Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī, from text 43 (the Bengali skipped — "you need a magnifying glass to read this print").

### The Pastime (CC Antya-līlā 6.43 onward)
- In the village of **Panīhāṭī**, **Raghunātha Dāsa** obtained an interview with **Nityānanda Prabhu**, who was accompanied by many kīrtana performers, servants, and others. Sitting on a rock under a tree on the bank of the Ganges, Lord Nityānanda seemed as effulgent as hundreds of rising suns, with many devotees seated around Him on the ground.
- Seeing the influence of Nityānanda Prabhu, Raghunātha Dāsa was astonished. He offered his obeisances by falling prostrate at a distant place. The servant of Nityānanda pointed him out: "There is Raghunātha Dāsa offering You obeisances."
- Lord Nityānanda said: **"You are a thief. Now you have to come see Me. Come here, come here — today I shall punish you."** The Lord called him, but Raghunātha did not go near. Then the Lord forcibly caught him and placed His lotus feet upon Raghunātha's head.
- Lord Nityānanda was by nature very merciful and funny. Being merciful, He spoke: "You are just like a thief, for instead of coming near, you stay away at a distant place. But now I have captured you; I shall punish you." And the punishment: **"Make a festival and feed all of My associates yogurt and chipped rice."**
- Hearing this, Raghunātha Dāsa was greatly pleased. He immediately sent his men to the village to purchase all kinds of edibles, bringing chipped rice, yogurt, milk, sweetmeats, sugar, bananas, and other edibles, placing them all around. As soon as they heard a festival was to be held, all kinds of brāhmaṇas and other gentlemen began to arrive, until in due course there were innumerable people.

### The Feast of Chipped Rice and Yogurt
- This festival is now observed all over the world in all the temples of ISKCON. In honor of it, this morning some chipped rice, bananas, and yogurt were served — not exactly a Sunday feast with rasgullās, jalebīs, burfī, all kinds of sabjīs, samosās, and kacaurīs ("I'm getting hungry").
- The pastime took place on the bank of the Ganges at Panīhāṭī, just north of Calcutta, during the **monsoon season** — very hot and muggy. So Nitāi very intelligently ordered foodstuffs the devotees considered cooling, like **dahi** (yogurt). In the old days in India, without much medicine, when someone got sick with diarrhea they wouldn't drink milk (which heats the body); yogurt cools the body, and to some degree so do bananas. Instead of the allopathic doctor or even Āyurveda, they'd just buy some yogurt and bananas — and sometimes it worked.
- Out of the process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness we enjoy chanting, dancing, and harikathā, and we look forward to the feast. ("It's not out of your mind today that there's going to be prasādam.") As said humorously yesterday, at ISKCON temples — Sydney, Auckland, Rio de Janeiro — the Sunday lecture may have 20 people, the kīrtan/ārati 30 people, but when it's time for prasādam around 7 o'clock, there are 500. So Mahāprabhu described that **prasādam is Lord Caitanya's secret weapon.**

### Prasādam Is Absolute
- We must always remember it is **absolute.** The name, fame, form, paraphernalia, and pastimes of the Lord — by **tadīya**, by association with the Lord — are absolute, non-different from the Lord. So when we take prasādam, we don't *eat* prasādam, we *respect* prasādam.
- The speaker remembers that whenever Prabhupāda would take prasādam, he would always take a long time. The young, passionate men and girls would say the prayer very fast, but Prabhupāda, when the prasāda was placed before him, could be seen praying, looking at it with great awe and reverence, then taking it and putting it in his mouth — meditating on it, as the speaker now understands, as **Kṛṣṇa's mercy — and mercy we need.**

## The Renunciation of Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī

- Raghunātha Dāsa was engaged in this because feasts are expensive. A temple manager knows that a great part of the budget is prasāda, which we sometimes take for granted; managers get the headache of where to find the lakṣmī to serve the Lord and His devotees nicely. So this was an expensive endeavor, and when the word got out, many people came.
- Raghunātha Dāsa was born in a very wealthy family (no time for the whole līlā), married to a very beautiful girl, with all the opulences one could dream of — in those days, "the 1%." (In modern society we say 90% of the world's wealth is owned by 1% of the population — the Rockefellers, etc.) Generally that kind of money goes to the head — "money makes money, why do I need God? I have security, I have God" — the fallacy of arrogance. But not in the case of Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī. His renunciation is particularly remarkable: if you have nothing and renounce, okay; but if you have everything and renounce it for the service of the Lord, that is something to be noticed. He took this service very seriously — you could say it was the beginning of his renunciation — and used that money in the service of the devotees.

### Serving Devotees Brings Auspiciousness
- **SB 1.2.16** (paraphrased): "O twice-born sages, by serving those devotees who are completely freed from vice, great service is done. By such service one gains an affinity for hearing the messages of Vāsudeva."
- And the benefit of hearing the messages of Vāsudeva? Harikathā, Kṛṣṇa-smaraṇam — you can remember Kṛṣṇa. Hear enough throughout your lifetime and your heart becomes purified, and it awakens your love for Kṛṣṇa.
- How do you always remember someone and never forget them (the meaning of Kṛṣṇa-smaraṇam)? It's impossible if you hate them. **Kaṁsa** hated Kṛṣṇa (having heard the omen that the eighth son of his sister Devakī would be his death), and was always thinking "where is this rascal?", every day sending a demon to Vṛndāvana — so he was always thinking of Kṛṣṇa. Is that Kṛṣṇa consciousness? No. **Rūpa Gosvāmī** says it has to be done **favorably** (*ānukūlyena*) — that's bhakti.
- The whole purpose of this movement — we're busy as bees, but let us not forget the goal: to love Kṛṣṇa, to love the divine couple, Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. **SB 1.2.18** (paraphrased): by regular attendance in the Bhāgavatam class and by rendering service to pure devotees, all that is inauspicious within the heart is destroyed almost to nil, and pure devotional service awakens. So we're in the right place, at the right time, doing the right thing; as much as we can engage in this **śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ** — serving the devotees and hearing the Bhāgavatam — it's to our benefit.

### A Nitya-siddha Who Acted Like an Ordinary Person
- Raghunātha Dāsa was an eternally liberated soul, a **nitya-siddha**, but for our benefit he acted like an ordinary person. Everyone needs a hero — unfortunately today's heroes are Spider-Man and the Avengers; but if we have heroes who are liberated souls, then to some degree our liberation is guaranteed.
- Sometimes these nitya-siddhas come to this world. It's like meeting a dignified, high-class person of great reputation in a poor part of town — "what's a nice person like you doing in a place like this?" — or meeting the president of the country in the red-light district. So one may ask, why do these great souls come to **Martya-loka** (the mortal world)? (London is also included in that verse.) **BG 8.16** (paraphrased): from the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death take place.
- **Gour Govinda Mahārāja** stated there are **two types of living entities** who come from the spiritual world: one who comes of his own compassion, and one who is ordered by the Lord to come. Such a person is our Śrīla Prabhupāda. In a confidential talk with the speaker's godbrother **Bhavānanda** (now writing his memoirs, which the speaker is helping to edit), Prabhupāda said: **"I did not want to come to this horrible place. But Kṛṣṇa said, 'No, you go down.' I said, 'So many austerities.' And Kṛṣṇa said, 'That's all right. You go down and write books on the science of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. I'll provide what you need, and protect what you have, and provide what you lack.'"** — the perfect personification of that verse from the Gītā (**BG 9.22**, *yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham*).
- Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī is one such personality — one of the mañjarīs in the spiritual world. Since **you can only give up something if you get something better,** and if you have the best you'll never be attracted to anything inferior, we understand his renunciation by his devotion. As Prabhupāda said of himself: "In my lifetime I did not know it was sinful activity, and I never forgot the lotus feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa." We never read about Prabhupāda slipping or being tempted — these are personalities from the spiritual world. So when looking for a saintly person to take shelter of, we make sure that person is, first, renounced from this world, and even more than renunciation, that he is deeply attached to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. Because Raghunātha Dāsa walked away from that kingdom, that beautiful young girl, and all opportunities for sense gratification to serve Lord Caitanya, we can understand we can take shelter of him.

### The Verses on His Renunciation (CC)
- *Vairāgyera kathā tāṅra adbhuta kathana / ajanma nā dila jihvāya rasera sparśana* — "The topics concerning his renunciation are wonderful. Throughout his life he never allowed his tongue sense gratification."
- *Chiṇḍā-kāni-kanthā vinā nā pare vasana / sāvadhāne prabhura kailā ājñāra pālana* — "Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī never touched anything to wear except a small torn cloth and a patchwork wrapper. Thus he very rigidly executed the order of Caitanya Mahāprabhu."
- As he grew older, he renounced things to a degree unimaginable to us, because he had no interest.

### The Comparison with Prahlāda Mahārāja — Subtle Desires
- Like **Prahlāda Mahārāja:** when Prahlāda came before Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva after Nṛsiṁhadeva had killed his father Hiraṇyakaśipu, the Bhāgavatam describes that Prahlāda came forward fearless and sat on the lap of the Lord, who put His lotus paw on Prahlāda's head — and the very last vestiges of material desire immediately disappeared from the heart of Prahlāda Mahārāja. This is quite amazing: Prahlāda was so advanced that the Lord invested one incarnation just to come and deliver him — **one incarnation for one devotee** — that's how much Nṛsiṁhadeva loved Prahlāda.
- But there was still something subtle — like camphor on the table: take it away and put it elsewhere, and you still smell the camphor; it's there in a very subtle way. To free the heart of material desires — we think the lust is gone, the anger is gone, the greed is gone — but there is so much in there, because we've been in this material world since time immemorial. As you could drag someone through a coal field full of coal and dust: **"Brahmāṇḍa bhramite kona bhāgyavān jīva"** (*CC Madhya 19.151*) — we've been here since time immemorial, so there are also subtle desires, like **pratiṣṭhā** (desire for prestige).
- You can be free from the desire to enjoy material objects, even free from sex desire, but more difficult than that is the desire to be honored. At the **Mahā Kumbha-melā** many years ago, the speaker saw renunciates sitting on top of elephants — saintly persons of different traditions, naked, with no possessions, not looking at the women, clearly detached in that way — but their chests were very broad and they were position-conscious. This is the cardinal sin of the living entity: that he falls from the spiritual sky out of **envy** — and envy means he wants to be number one. These things are very subtle.

### The Long Road Back to Braj — Cleansing the Heart
- It's a long road back to Braj. **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** said the road to Vṛndāvana is strewn with thorns and brambles — figuratively meaning that to reach the point of **anartha-nivṛtti** (necessary before one can even begin to speak about **prema**, love of God) requires a lot of cleansing.
- "Scrub-a-dub-dub, three men in a tub" — a good title for the lecture. Do you swallow a bar of soap? No — Hare Kṛṣṇa. Just as you take a bath daily (Vedic-culture sannyāsīs would bathe three times a day to stay clean), the heart, by looking at this world and getting its impressions — even just looking at a materialistic person sometimes — gets contaminated. So: **ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam** (Śikṣāṣṭaka 1) — cleansing. Mahāprabhu said **kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ** — always chant, never done "for the day." Hare Kṛṣṇa.
- One naturally becomes detached as one grows older, by the process of purification; it becomes easier. ("I told them today, I'm not lamenting — I'm 70, it's the best part of my life, it's easier.") **Lokanātha Gosvāmī** once said, "I love growing old, because the senses become dead and you lose your attraction for the material world — this is the best part of it." You should all look forward to it and pray to Kṛṣṇa: "I want to be 70." Just as by nature's way the snake sheds his skin (walking in the jungles of India one sees snake skins ten meters long), so as one advances one doesn't need artificial renunciation or unnecessary austerities — like going to the Himalayas and sitting naked in the snow — to come to the understanding "I am not the body."

### Story — The Jogī at Cīra-ghāṭa
- Recently in Vṛndāvana (seen in the photo albums sent some months ago), the speaker met one jogī out at Cīra-ghāṭa — the original Cīra-ghāṭa, not the one in Vṛndāvana proper, but some distance away, where Kṛṣṇa stole the clothes of the gopīs — a very beautiful place in the forest on the banks of the river.
- The sādhu said a few words. Then, around 7 o'clock in the morning, as the sun rose and it got hot (India gets hot fast), he put cow dung and wood around himself and lit it on fire, so it got really hot; and by around 9 o'clock he took a bucket, put coal in it, lit the coal, and put it on top of his hair (he had a topknot) — telling the speaker, "That is why I have the topknot, to put hot coals." He sat there sweating until 12 noon. The speaker was impressed — while sitting in the shade himself — but **that is not necessary to become renounced.**
- As the wisdom says (*niti-śāstra*), **you can only give up something if you get something better.** Bhakti-yoga is so wonderful that, from the very beginning, Prabhupāda said, "You take one step toward Kṛṣṇa, and He takes a thousand steps" — He is even very interested in the aspiring devotee.

### BG 18.66 — Spoken to Those "Looking in the Window"
- **BG 18.66** (*sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja, ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi*): This verse is not directed to madhyama-adhikārīs or uttama-adhikārīs — not even to kaniṣṭha-adhikārīs. Kṛṣṇa is saying: just abandon all your speculative dharma, surrender unto Me, and I will protect you from sinful reaction. But uttama-adhikārīs don't have sinful reactions in their hearts, and madhyamas are well on the path of purification. He's speaking to those who are just **looking in the window** — "What is this Hare Kṛṣṇa temple?" Kṛṣṇa wants us to come back to Godhead more than we do. Knowing how attached and fallen people are in this age, He makes a compromise: just chant. But even that we can't do — we can't maintain our vow of chanting from the cradle to the grave, even though that compensation is there.
- So talks like this are like the **"State of the Union address"** (as when the American president gives the nation a picture of where we are and where we're going) — you get a little push: chant, chant, chant. **All problems are solved by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa.**

## Mahāprabhu Came as a Devotee — and We Must Not Be Artificial

- Even Caitanya Mahāprabhu, God Himself, chose not to come as an avatāra, as God — because in this age everybody wants to be God. (Prabhupāda said in the 1970s there was an incarnation of God at every street corner of Bombay.) If He had come as God, people would imitate Him to become God. So He very intelligently came as a **devotee** — and He's so attractive that a devotee says, "I want to be like that." (You meet people and want to be like that football star or that singer — you try in the shower, and it doesn't work.) In the beginning Mahāprabhu didn't reveal His divinity; He revealed it well into His life, when He always had a group of people who trusted Him — as when Sārvabhauma [Bhaṭṭācārya] asked, "You're Nimāi — You're God?" "Yes." The example is there for all of us.
- Prabhupāda said **bhakti begins — real love begins — when one is freed from sex desire.** That's a challenge. So some renunciation has to be there, but we can't be artificial. Many times people are artificial — they pretend to be who they're not: **sahajiyā.** You begin chanting, and those things which are not favorable to Kṛṣṇa consciousness fall away like the snakeskin. You don't have to worry, or practice unnecessary austerities — just chant, dance, harikathā, and feast; and as you grow older you'll naturally renounce.
- Everyone has their own pace in Kṛṣṇa consciousness; we must be very patient with others. We shouldn't be hard on others and easy on ourselves — we should be **easy on others and hard on ourselves.** We come into this movement with some dirt in our hearts, and it takes time to scrub it. We shouldn't highlight people's faults, but their good qualities — **like Gaura-Nitāi.**
- **Gaura-Nitāi don't consider our offenses.** They would not consider anyone's offenses; they accepted only **sevā.** That's why, when **Guru Kṛpā Swami** early in the movement showed Prabhupāda a Deity of Nṛsiṁhadeva and asked, "Can I worship this Deity?", Prabhupāda said, "Oh no — He's very angry." (Sometimes we find a Deity in the market — "Oh, Nṛsiṁhadeva, I love you" — but Prabhupāda saw this Deity and said, "He's very angry, no. If you want, you can worship Nṛsiṁha, but with Gaura-Nitāi — because Lord Caitanya doesn't consider our offenses; otherwise no one would make it back. He accepts only our sevā.") To this day Guru Kṛpā has the Nṛsiṁha Deity, and a very beautiful Gaura-Nitāi.
- Technically we are **Gaura-bhaktas**; we consider ourselves Kṛṣṇa-bhaktas. But Prabhupāda said, when many instructions are given, the **last instruction is the most relevant.** Many incarnations come and, according to time, place, and circumstance, give instructions — but the most recent incarnation is glorified: **"namo mahā-vadānyāya kṛṣṇa-prema-pradāya te / kṛṣṇāya kṛṣṇa-caitanya-nāmne gaura-tviṣe namaḥ."** By His preaching and by His example, harināma-saṅkīrtana — just like **our Govinda Ji.** (Pointing him out again as one of our heroes: on Thursdays he chants all day; instead of going to bhajana and chanting 192 rounds, he goes out on the street and chants all day — he'll get more blessings than the bābās at Rādhā-kuṇḍa. The idea is: go out and serve the holy name by chanting it to all the Londoners and all the people from around the world. This is the best example.)

## Raghunātha Dāsa's Ever-Increasing Renunciation

- As we advance, we should experience ourselves losing material desires; otherwise something's wrong, and we have to go to our mentor.
- Later, as Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī made more advancement — after the departure of **Sanātana Gosvāmī** — he gave up eating all foodstuffs except as much buttermilk as could be placed into a leaf cup. There were some big leaf cups from the forest of **Candrāvalī**; people thought, "Oh, you're just eating a leaf cup of buttermilk, so we brought you this big leaf from Candrāvalī." But — since there are left-wing and right-wing gopīs, and Candrāvalī is the competition for Rādhārāṇī — being a Rādhārāṇī bhakta, he refused the bigger Candrāvalī leaf and kept to his small one.
- After the departure of **Rūpa Gosvāmī**, his renunciation increased further — he took only as much buttermilk as could fit in his hand (like the Caraṇāmṛta you take at the temple). Not that we can imitate that, but we can follow in the footsteps: gradually, gradually, knowing our own hearts, giving up those things that are not favorable to Kṛṣṇa. **Kṛṣṇa consciousness means to accept those things favorable for your progress and reject those unfavorable** — and how do you know which is which? Because Prabhupāda tells us so.
- Prabhupāda said that because the Gosvāmīs were so renounced and so attached to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa (the secret), they were very skinny and a little sickly ("I heard"), because they didn't care about their bodies. **We cannot imitate that, because we have to be strong.** Prabhupāda always signed his letters, "Hope this meets you in the best of health, your ever well-wisher, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda." Because we have sevā to do for society, we have to be in good shape. But the Gosvāmīs had no interest in this world; they were so immersed in Kṛṣṇa-kathā they were like that.
- His renunciation was so famous it was "set in stone" — like an inscription on Nelson's Column at Trafalgar Square, marking that he conquered somewhere; but Raghunātha's conquest was renunciation. (*Nāviṣeśa guṇamayī* — Kṛṣṇa says the material energy is very difficult to overcome; **BG 7.14** again.) Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī overcame it because he was so attached to Lord Caitanya.

### The Example of Bilvamaṅgala Ṭhākura
- He was like **Bilvamaṅgala Ṭhākura**, who lived in Vṛndāvana 800 years. Previously he was visiting a prostitute, but he became purely devoted, and Kṛṣṇa appeared before him (he was blind, but Kṛṣṇa took him by the hand). Bilvamaṅgala said that ever since he became Kṛṣṇa conscious and began enjoying the deeper rasas of devotional service (meaning he had a relationship with Kṛṣṇa), whenever he thought of the things he used to do in material life — Prabhupāda would quote him as saying, **"I spited the thought."** (Bilvamaṅgala was saying, "I spit at the thought," but Prabhupāda's charming English rendered it "I spited the thought.")
- We want to get to that point — like the anti-drug campaign in America during the Reagan administration, whose slogan (not a śloka, since it's material life, but a slogan) was **"Just say no."** When the drug pusher meets you coming out of high school — "No." (Maybe it worked; it didn't work.)
- The same with us: **Māyā Devī** is tempting us to test our sincerity; they don't want to let anyone go back to the spiritual world. If you show any envy toward Kṛṣṇa, any material desires — "you don't want to spoil the preparation, put some sand in the sweet rice, spoil it somehow" — you say, "No way, José."
- The verse: "I do not know how much nectar the two syllables 'Kṛṣ-ṇa' contain. When the holy name of Kṛṣṇa enters my ear and goes into my heart, they create a jubilant festival of ecstasy." No to Māyā, yes to Kṛṣṇa — Hare Kṛṣṇa.

### Better Off Starting Young
- You're better off if you start young — you've got better chances. Like this young boy and girl in front: at one time they were not part of the movement, but they saw the Ratha-yātrā coming down the street; the speaker waved, came over, talked to their mommy, and said "Hare Kṛṣṇa" — and here they are today, sitting in the front, not fidgeting, totally absorbed and listening. Maybe they understand, maybe they don't.
- As Prabhupāda challenged the speaker when he was sleeping in class: "Why are you sleeping in my class?" "Oh — you're speaking Hindi, probably." "It does not matter. Stay awake, Indradyumna. This is **śabda-brahma**, transcendental sound — it will act." So for whatever reason, staying awake, it acts.

## The Goal — Prema, and the Prayers of Vilāpa-kusumāñjali

- Raghunātha was famous for his renunciation — but why? Because of his devotion. Again, niti-śāstra logic: you can only give up something if you get something better; he had the best, so he could give up the worst. (We think it's the best, but it's the worst; he could give up the worst because he had the best.)
- He is perhaps more famous for his deep realizations — not only realizations, but his deep **rasa** with Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. He is our **Prayojana guru.** (Sambandha, Abhidheya, and Prayojana.) There is a higher goal, which is **prema, love of God** — "I'm not qualified to speak on it, but we know there is a higher goal." That's where Raghunātha Dāsa comes from, on the highest level. You can know someone by the way they speak. He composed a beautiful prayer, **Vilāpa-kusumāñjali** — there's nothing higher than that understanding.
- Young devotees should at least know what the goal is. Bob Marley said, "We know who we are, we know where we're going." We know we're spirit souls, but where are we going? Generally, the followers of Caitanya Mahāprabhu want to become **mañjarīs** in **Goloka Vṛndāvana.**
- "I'll just say it publicly, because when I read the 45th chapter of the *Kṛṣṇa* book last night it was all about rasa, especially **Vipralambha** — separation from Kṛṣṇa." Prabhupāda said the *Kṛṣṇa* book will give solace to the members of ISKCON when they come to the stage of **Vipralambha-bhāva.** He expects us to distribute a lot of books, but he also wants us to become pure devotees — becoming more valuable instruments in the hands of Guru-Gaurāṅga for impressing people about Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Look what one pure devotee did: Śrīla Prabhupāda single-handedly brought the whole Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition to the shores of the West. So he wants us to become pure devotees too.
- We should at least theoretically know where we want to go — not Vāmana-loka, not Nṛsiṁha-loka, but **the playground of God.** There's no **aiśvarya** (opulence/reverence) there — there's just **prema.** That's our goal, and we should think about it. Like the **turtle:** she lays her eggs in the sand and goes into the water, and from some distance away — a hundred meters — she meditates on the eggs hatching; she's far away, but by her meditation the little turtles come out. So we're far away from the goal, but we have to meditate on it, think about it — not be sahajiyā, but know the goal and have a strong desire to go there.

### Raghunātha's Prayer, and the Sailor Watching for Land
- We know the heart of Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī by what he said (to Rādhā): **"I am Yours, I am Yours — I cannot live without You. O queen, please understand this and bring me to Your lotus feet."** That's our goal.
- We're crossing over the ocean of material existence, but we want to reach the land — like a **sailor.** A good sailor on a wooden boat crossing the sea from Britain to New York (which is where the name came from) fixes the sails, varnishes the deck, polishes the brass, and takes out his sextant (the old GPS) to see where he is and make adjustments — but he never thinks for a moment that he's a creature of the water; he's actually a little nervous of it, and always thinking, "Where's the land?" So when the land mass appears after six months at sea — **"Land ho! Land ho!"**
- We're like that: in this world, but we don't want to become worldly or too intimate with it.
- **Story — Fakir Mohan:** A year or two ago the speaker was with the sādhu Fakir Mohan (a famous devotee, a fable to our movement, who gave many wonderful lectures at the Krishna-Balaram Mandir; a Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava, now passed away). One day they were chanting, and the speaker, thinking he was doing really well, was chanting "Hare Kṛṣṇa"; Fakir Mohan looked at him and said, **"Maharaj, I'm not of this world. Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa."** It made such an impression, the speaker never forgot it. "I want to be like you." "Then keep chanting."
- So we don't think for a moment that we're creatures of this world. The sailor's mind is really on the land. We go through life — education is important, career is important, marriage is important, society and the environment are important (on global warming, "we know the solution: take what you need, not according to your greed" — we're concerned because there will be future generations of Vaiṣṇavas, and we don't want them to live in some hell-hole).
- **The married woman analogy** (which came from Mahāprabhu — Rūpa and Sanātana asked how to remain Kṛṣṇa conscious in separation from Mahāprabhu, and He wrote them a letter): just as a married woman who has a paramour is very dutiful at home so no one is suspicious ("Yes, honey, your dinner is coming quickly; the kids are going to school"), but in her heart of hearts is always thinking of her paramour — similarly, we should always be thinking of our divine lover. (Don't misuse the analogy — no analogy is perfect in this imperfect world, but we can draw from analogies to understand deep points.) All of us have a connection with Kṛṣṇa; He's the one person who can love everyone simultaneously to their full satisfaction (a king may have 36 wives, but they're complaining in the background). So the goal is to keep Kṛṣṇa in mind.
- How to keep Kṛṣṇa in mind? You have to hear about Kṛṣṇa, talk about Kṛṣṇa, remember Kṛṣṇa, and serve the lotus feet of the Deity of Kṛṣṇa — **SB 7.5.23:** *śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam / arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ sakhyam ātma-nivedanam.*

## The Highest Goal — Love in Separation

- Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī is our hero. There are the four goals — **dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa** — which everybody is into. But Mahāprabhu revealed a higher goal: love for Kṛṣṇa. What type of love did He introduce? **Braja bhakti** — the love of the residents of Vṛndāvana, and in particular the maidservants, the gopīs, the cowherd girls.
- And to whom did He give it? To the people of London, on Oxford Street, going into Starbucks for a coffee and a meat sandwich and going to make money. He gave it to them. How does He give it? Because **Govinda** is on the street giving the **Yuga-dharma** — Hare Kṛṣṇa.
- That love, that Braja bhakti, Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī wrote down for us. Now we can get bits and pieces of it, hear about it — but especially as one becomes advanced, books like his **Manaḥ-śikṣā** are very valuable, as are **Vilāpa-kusumāñjali, Dāna-keli-cintāmaṇi,** and **Muktā-carita.** These books are about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; they're better than any fairy tale, better than any Avengers movie.
- This highest goal is really **Vipralambha, separation.** Kṛṣṇa leaves Vṛndāvana, and what happens? Everyone loves Him more than anyone's ever been loved before — because it's the nature of separation. Separation makes the heart grow fonder. So Kṛṣṇa is tricky: He leaves ("Oh Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa"), then He comes back.

### The Śikṣāṣṭaka — the Mission Statement of ISKCON
- It's no secret, because if you read the **Śikṣāṣṭaka** prayers — Mahāprabhu never really wrote anything down, but He was surrounded by devotees like **Rāmānanda Rāya** and **Svarūpa Dāmodara Gosvāmī** who wrote down His most poignant sayings — you find it there. The Śikṣāṣṭaka prayers are the **mission statement of ISKCON:** how to go from A to Z, from **śraddhā to prema**, how to become a pure devotee and fulfill Prabhupāda's desire.
- It begins **"ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam... paraṁ vijayate śrī-kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtanam"** (Śikṣāṣṭaka 1). Do some sevā, get out there, distribute books, preach, have children and raise them in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, become a teacher and throw in a few ślokas during your history class — there's something for everyone. We recite these eight verses every morning.
- In the beginning the verses are about **meeting Kṛṣṇa** (as they say in Christianity, "meet thy maker"): the idea "My dear Lord, please pick me up from this ocean of death and place me as an atom at Your feet" — poetic, meaning "I want to be right next to Kṛṣṇa; I haven't seen You in a million years — please pick me up and let me wash the pots, do anything, I just want to be with You." **Śikṣāṣṭaka 2:** "O my Lord, out of kindness You enable us to easily approach You by Your holy names." **Śikṣāṣṭaka 5:** "Pick me up from this ocean of birth and death and fix me as an atom [at Your lotus feet]." All about meeting Kṛṣṇa.
- But as we go on, in the seventh and eighth verses, Mahāprabhu introduces the idea of loving God **in separation:**
  - **Śikṣāṣṭaka 7:** "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I am feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence."
  - **Śikṣāṣṭaka 8:** "I know no one but Kṛṣṇa as my Lord, and He shall remain so even if He handles me roughly by His embrace, or makes me brokenhearted by not being present before me."
- So that idea of separation is there; at least theoretically and philosophically, we know that's where we're going.

## How to Begin — and Learning to Cry

- This is the subject matter given by Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī, and Prabhupāda talked about it at **26 Second Avenue** in a lecture (heard recently). Prabhupāda told the hippies who were becoming "happies," and the drug addicts coming down on LSD (word was the best place to come down on LSD was 26 Second Avenue, to hear from the Swami and take an apple): **"The more you feel separation from Kṛṣṇa, you should understand that you are advancing" (1966). But don't try to see Kṛṣṇa artificially. Be advanced in separation feelings, and then it will become perfect. That is the teaching I am introducing, as given by Caitanya Mahāprabhu."**
- And in the **Nectar of Devotion** (the law book of ISKCON), page 83: **"One should learn how to cry for the Lord. One should learn this small technique, and he should be very eager and actually cry to become engaged in some particular type of service. This is called laulyam. And such tears are the price for the highest perfection."**
- But how do you do it? You don't jump over. How do you begin? **"Guru Mahārāja, I promise you on this day that I will refrain from four sinful activities — no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, and no gambling — and I will chant 16 rounds every day. I will not hear criticism of devotees, I'll serve my authorities, and I'll go out on saṅkīrtana."** Confucius said the longest journey begins with the first step — that's what it is, and in time it will come.
- If it's not coming, we should lament. Generally we lament when we don't get something material ("I didn't get that present at Christmas, I didn't get that raise"). No — we should lament on the spiritual platform: that we've been practicing Kṛṣṇa consciousness for 50 years and we're not crying when we chant.

### Personal Sharing — "When Will I Get Kṛṣṇa Prema?"
- "The other day was my actual birthday, May 20th, 1949 (sounds like a dinosaur)." With a friend (and it was recorded), the speaker said: **"Since I took shelter of my spiritual master when I was 19 years old, I have spent much of my life preaching and doing spiritual activities for his pleasure — but after all these years I haven't achieved Kṛṣṇa prema. When will I get Kṛṣṇa prema?"**
  - One devotee nearby said, "Maharaj, don't worry about those things — just preach and distribute books." "Yeah, but I know my spiritual master wants me to become pure; he doesn't want to have to come back and save me — because if the disciple doesn't make it back, the guru has to come back and take birth again to deliver the disciple." (Prabhupāda said, "Be kind on your old spiritual master." Now I can say it.)
  - But his friend, a very advanced devotee, gave a different answer: **"Maharaj, you won't get Kṛṣṇa prema now, because if you get Kṛṣṇa prema you won't be able to preach. You will leave all your preaching and come under a tree in Vṛndāvana and shed tears all day. Then how will the preaching mission of Mahāprabhu go on? Right now you have taken the whole world on your shoulder. If you come to Vṛndāvana and live like a Bābājī, you will need two boys just to carry you around and serve you. The Lord is not going to let that happen."**

### Story — Advaita Ācārya's Tears Locked in a Knot
- The friend then shared a story from śāstra (one of the speaker's disciples referred to it today from Satsvarūpa? / Sacīnandana Swami). It's not in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta or Caitanya-bhāgavata, but there are many līlās in more minor śāstras — as Vṛndāvana Dāsa Ṭhākura says at the beginning of Caitanya-bhāgavata, if he mentioned all of Mahāprabhu's pastimes, the book would become too bulky. This one shows that sometimes prema is restricted (not the speaker's case — "I'm not at anartha-nivṛtti yet" — but an interesting discussion): prema may not be granted to a devotee because **preaching is the ultimate duty in this world.**
- After Caitanya Mahāprabhu took sannyāsa, He came to Śāntipur to visit **Advaita Ācārya**, and many of His followers assembled there. Mahāprabhu stayed for a day, and when He was leaving everyone was crying, "When will that day be ours?" — but not Advaita: no tears came from his eyes; as a pastime, he apparently wasn't feeling any emotion. So he went to Mahāprabhu, fell at His feet, and said, "Mahāprabhu, wait. My heart is like stone; I'm not feeling any emotion for You. Please give me some love for You."
- Mahāprabhu said: "If you cry tears all day and night, who will manage these men, these devotees? I've come here for a purpose — to spread the Saṅkīrtana movement. You need to help them and guide them now." Then He showed Advaita a knot — Mahāprabhu wore a rope around His waist, tied in knots, on which He did japa — and holding up one of the knots He said, **"Your tears are locked up in this knot. The day I untie this knot, you will get love of God and cry all day and night. But for now, get out and preach."**
- But we should **hanker** for that — be patient, but as the song goes, "When, oh when, will that day be mine?" Like the **caterpillar** — an ugly little beast, hairy, with 32 legs and bulging eyes, but she knows she has a higher being, so she goes into the cocoon, undergoes metamorphosis, and comes out a butterfly. We have a very high calling, and we have to do what's necessary to get there: follow the rules and chant Hare Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda said many times (and it's in writing): **"Just by following the rules and regulations and chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, you'll go back to Godhead."** He wanted us to come to the point of crying, crying, crying.

### Separation Is More Relishable Than Meeting
- In a letter Prabhupāda wrote in Seattle in 1968 (to a brand-new devotee): **"As we go on chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa in the pure status, we shall be on the loving platform, and at that time we shall feel the greatest separation from the Lord. But that feeling of separation from the Lord is more relishable than meeting the Lord."** These are transcendental understandings, which we grasp gradually by the grace of the Supreme Lord, who is in everyone's heart and reveals Himself to the devotee as he progresses — if he wants it more than material life.
- **Nectar of Devotion, page 107:** "For persons who are not inclined to clear the dust from their hearts, who want to keep the dirty things as they are, it is not possible to derive the transcendental result of chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa."
- It's inconceivable — right now we'd rather be *with* our husband/lover than separated. So how is separation from Kṛṣṇa better than being with Kṛṣṇa? (Apology to the gṛhasthas — that's a joke.) As quoted at Soho Street, **Rūpa Gosvāmī** says in the **Padyāvalī:** "If I had to choose between union with Kṛṣṇa and separation from Him, I would choose separation — because when I'm with Kṛṣṇa I see only one Kṛṣṇa, but when I'm separated from Him I see Kṛṣṇa coming toward me from every corner of the universe." **Vipralambha-bhāva ki jaya!**
- Of course, before you can feel separation you must become attached. So don't worry about the separation part now — you won't feel separation until you're attached. And how do you get attached? Hare Kṛṣṇa. As your spiritual teacher, I have to give you the whole gamut, A to Z — but it's actually like "Polish in 10 easy lessons": you don't have to do the austerities of the śāstras. You chant, you dance, you study the śāstras, take prasādam, do some service — **but think big.**

## Think Big

- Prabhupāda thought big in preaching Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Every marathon he would tell us, "**Tax your brains** how to spread this movement." So also think big about what you want to achieve in Kṛṣṇa consciousness by the mercy of Guru and Kṛṣṇa: we want to go back to Godhead. It begins by becoming attached and then bearing that separation — but it will be blissful. Think big.

### Story — Prabhupāda's 18-Day Movie
- One time in **Nairobi**, Prabhupāda was on a morning walk in a little jungle area with some senior devotees (Śruti-kīrti, Bhavānanda, Brahmānanda, Gargamuni). Prabhupāda stopped and said, "**I want to make a movie.**" (No one had ever heard Prabhupāda want to make a movie.) Śruti-kīrti asked, "Really, Prabhupāda? What movie?" Prabhupāda said: **"Yes — Mahābhārata. And my movie will be 18 days long. You'll come one day for the first session, then the second session — it will be 18 days long, one day for every day of the Battle of Kurukṣetra. And Śyāmasundara, you'll be Arjuna; Brahmānanda, you'll be Bhīma; Gargamuni, you can be the evil-minded Śakuni; and Bhavānanda will be my assistant. I will direct every scene, and your disciples will assist me. It would be a famous movie around the world — 18 days."** That is how Prabhupāda thinks.
- So in your sevā you should think big, and in your ultimate aspirations you should also think big.

### The Ultimate Reunion
- How to attain that goal of union with Kṛṣṇa? As Prabhupāda said: when you get back to the spiritual world, your spiritual master is waiting for you; he takes your hand and walks you into the **kuñja** (the grove), and he puts your hand in the hand of Kṛṣṇa and leaves the two of you alone.
- What compares to that in this world? Politics? Rock and roll? Hip-hop? ("Excuse me, I don't know the music — it all sounds like the clashing of pots and pans, and some mumbling.") And the "finer things of life," *les connoisseurs, voilà* — whatever: dry stool, wet stool. Nothing compares to that.
- How do you get back there? Prabhupāda made it very clear: **by assisting him in his service here, we qualify ourselves to assist him in his service there.** He is our ever well-wisher, our deliverer from the world of birth and death to the lotus feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in the spiritual world. So fix your mind on your service — but at night, read *Kṛṣṇa* book chapter 45 and drink hot milk.
- **"To the degree that we surrender to Lord Caitanya's service, to that degree we gain qualification for the service of Rādhārāṇī's lotus feet in Vṛndāvana."**

## Closing Blessing
- We wish you all the best, because Prabhupāda said, "I am your ever well-wisher" — so, as his representative, I am also wishing you. It's more than a wish: I'm praying, hoping and praying, that you'll make it to the spiritual goal. And my promise to all of you is that I will give you my very best to help you achieve it.
- Thank you very much. Hare Kṛṣṇa.
