# Lecture: Lord Chaitanya the yoga avatars

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 60 · Festivals and Kirtans · 26:57

## Summary

Speaking on Gaura Purnima—the appearance day of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and the start of the Gaudiya Vaishnava new year—Indradyumna Swami explains that Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is the yuga-avatar who descends in each age to give the process of self-realization suited to that time. He likens these processes to medicines prescribed for the universal "disease" of forgetfulness of God: meditation in Satya-yuga, fire sacrifice (yajna) in Treta-yuga, and temple worship in Dvapara-yuga, whereas for the deeply fallen souls of Kali-yuga only the potent medicine of chanting the holy names is strong enough (harer nama... nasty eva gatir anyatha). Citing the golden-avatar verse of the Eleventh Canto (krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam), he notes that Chaitanya appears not in Krishna's usual blackish hue but in gold, because internally He assumed the mood and complexion of Srimati Radharani—compared to the golden champak flower—in order to taste the depth of Her love, mahabhava. Thus the Lord descended with two purposes: the external one of inaugurating the sankirtana movement, and the confidential internal one of relishing and distributing Radharani's love, both of which were revealed through the mercy of Srila Rupa Goswami, the rasa-acharya. He illustrates this with the Jagannath Puri Ratha-yatra pastime, in which Chaitanya danced before the stopped cart and sang of "the same lover and the same moonlit night," and with the story of a verse Rupa Goswami wrote and tucked into the thatch of Haridasa Thakura's bhajan-kutir—a verse ("I am the same Radha, you are the same Krishna") that perfectly captured the Lord's inner heart and earned His praise that Rupa's lines were like strings of pearls. He recalls Haridasa Thakura, the Namacharya, who out of humility would not enter the Jagannath temple, so the Lord visited him daily. The Swami closes by stressing that this is a missionary movement—the holy name cleanses lifetimes of sinful reactions and awakens love of God even in the most fallen (hence Patita-pavana)—and urges the Seattle devotees to keep the preaching spirit alive, praising the local leadership of Harivilas Prabhu and the new temple under construction.

## Key Points

- Gaura Purnima is the Gaudiya Vaishnava new year; a devotee's true resolution is spiritual, not material—quoting Chaitanya's Sikshastaka (na dhanam na janam), desiring only causeless devotional service birth after birth, not wealth, followers, fame, or even liberation.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is the yuga-avatar; each age has its own prescribed dharma, likened to medicine dispensed by a pharmacist: meditation (Satya), fire sacrifice (Treta), Deity/temple worship (Dvapara), and chanting the holy name (Kali).
- In Kali-yuga only harinama is potent enough for our diseased, God-forgetful condition—harer nama... kalau nasty eva gatir anyatha, 'no other way, no other way, no other way.'
- Chaitanya is golden rather than blackish because He assumed Radharani's mood and complexion (golden like the champak flower) to understand Her mahabhava; only Radha, Madhavendra Puri, and Chaitanya Himself grasp the depth of that love.
- The Lord appeared for two reasons: externally to inaugurate the sankirtana movement, internally to relish and distribute Radha's love—both revealed by Srila Rupa Goswami, who understood the Lord's heart (illustrated by the Ratha-yatra song and the verse hidden in Haridasa Thakura's thatched roof).
- Gaudiya Vaishnavism, unlike the awe-and-reverence (aishwarya) mood of earlier Vaishnavas like Ramanuja and Madhva, gives the sweet, intimate love of the residents of Vrindavan.
- This is a missionary movement: the holy name delivers even the most fallen (Patita-pavana), preaching is what gives life to a community, and none is more dear to Krishna than one who preaches—so the Seattle devotees should keep the preaching spirit, with praise for Harivilas Prabhu and the temple being built.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 11.5.32 — 'krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam sangopangastra-parshadam / yajnaih sankirtana-prayair yajanti hi su-medhasah' — the central Gaudiya verse establishing Chaitanya as the golden yuga-avatar of Kali, surrounded by His associates and worshipped by the intelligent through sankirtana.
- Sikshastaka, verse 4 (Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu) — 'na dhanam na janam na sundarim kavitam va jagadisha kamaye / mama janmani janmanishvare bhavatad bhaktir ahaituki tvayi' — cited for the devotee's spiritual 'new year resolution' of unmotivated devotion.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.10 — 'teshaam satata-yuktaanaam bhajataam priti-purvakam / dadaami buddhi-yogam tam yena mam upayaanti te' — Krishna gives the understanding by which His loving servants gradually return to Him; quoted on the meaning of auspiciousness.
- Brihad-naradiya Purana, quoted in Chaitanya-caritamrita (Adi-lila 17.21) — 'harer nama harer nama harer namaiva kevalam / kalau nasty eva nasty eva nasty eva gatir anyatha' — chanting the holy name is the only means of deliverance in Kali-yuga.
- Rupa Goswami pranama verse — 'sri-chaitanya-mano-'bhishtam sthapitam yena bhu-tale / svayam rupah kada mahyam dadati sva-padantikam' — honoring Rupa Goswami, who understood and established Chaitanya's inner desire (the rasa-acharya).
- Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya-lila (Ratha-yatra section, verse near 1.76 / 13.121) — Rupa Goswami's verse expressing the Lord's mood, paraphrased in the lecture as 'I am the same Radha, you are the same Krishna, these are the same moonlit nights, let us again enjoy our pastimes'; written and tucked in the thatch of Haridasa Thakura's kutir (exact verse number uncertain).
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Arunodaya-kirtana (Gitavali) — 'jiv jago, jiv jago, gauracandra bole' — Chaitanya as the physician calling sleeping souls to take the medicine of the holy name and become healthy in Krishna consciousness.
- Bhagavad-gita 18.68–69 — 'na cha tasman manushyeshu kashchin me priya-krittamah' — none is, or ever will be, more dear to Krishna than one who preaches His message; cited for the missionary spirit.
- Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (published as The Nectar of Devotion) by Srila Rupa Goswami — the treatise on the deeper mellows of devotional service that Srila Prabhupada gave to ISKCON very early in the movement.
- Vedanta-sutra 1.1.1 — 'athato brahma-jijnasa' — cited that the sole purpose of human life in every age is self-realization / inquiry into the Absolute.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 6.3.19 — 'dharmam tu sakshad bhagavat-pranitam' — real religion is enunciated directly by the Lord; used to explain why He personally gives the age-appropriate process.
- Rupa Goswami's verse on the holy name (Vidagdha-madhava 1.15, also quoted in Chaitanya-caritamrita Antya-lila) — paraphrased as wishing for thousands of tongues and millions of ears to chant and hear the holy name; Chaitanya praised his poetic lines as 'strings of pearls' (exact citation approximate).
