# Lecture: Lord Chaitanya the yoga avatars

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 60 · Festivals and Kirtans · 26:57

**Themes:** yuga avatar · harinama · mahabhava · rupa goswami · gaura purnima · preaching

## Summary

Speaking on Gaura Purnima—the appearance day of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and the start of the Gaudiya Vaishnava new year—Indradyumna Swami explains that Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is the yuga-avatar who descends in each age to give the process of self-realization suited to that time. He likens these processes to medicines prescribed for the universal "disease" of forgetfulness of God: meditation in Satya-yuga, fire sacrifice (yajna) in Treta-yuga, and temple worship in Dvapara-yuga, whereas for the deeply fallen souls of Kali-yuga only the potent medicine of chanting the holy names is strong enough (harer nama... nasty eva gatir anyatha). Citing the golden-avatar verse of the Eleventh Canto (krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam), he notes that Chaitanya appears not in Krishna's usual blackish hue but in gold, because internally He assumed the mood and complexion of Srimati Radharani—compared to the golden champak flower—in order to taste the depth of Her love, mahabhava. Thus the Lord descended with two purposes: the external one of inaugurating the sankirtana movement, and the confidential internal one of relishing and distributing Radharani's love, both of which were revealed through the mercy of Srila Rupa Goswami, the rasa-acharya. He illustrates this with the Jagannath Puri Ratha-yatra pastime, in which Chaitanya danced before the stopped cart and sang of "the same lover and the same moonlit night," and with the story of a verse Rupa Goswami wrote and tucked into the thatch of Haridasa Thakura's bhajan-kutir—a verse ("I am the same Radha, you are the same Krishna") that perfectly captured the Lord's inner heart and earned His praise that Rupa's lines were like strings of pearls. He recalls Haridasa Thakura, the Namacharya, who out of humility would not enter the Jagannath temple, so the Lord visited him daily. The Swami closes by stressing that this is a missionary movement—the holy name cleanses lifetimes of sinful reactions and awakens love of God even in the most fallen (hence Patita-pavana)—and urges the Seattle devotees to keep the preaching spirit alive, praising the local leadership of Harivilas Prabhu and the new temple under construction.

## Every Day Is Auspicious for a Devotee — and Some Days Especially So

- The speaker is very happy to be back in **Seattle** with everyone on this auspicious day of **Gaura Purnima** (the appearance day of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu).
- Actually, every day in the life of a devotee is auspicious, because a devotee is gradually making progress back home, back to Godhead.
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 10.10 ("teṣāṁ satata-yuktānāṁ bhajatāṁ…"):** Krishna says that to those who serve Him with love and devotion, He gives the understanding by which they can gradually come back to Him.
  - **Our definition of auspiciousness:** that which brings us closer to the Lord; inauspiciousness is activity that takes us further away. Because devotees are always engaged in the nine processes of devotional service, they make gradual progress — so every day is auspicious.
- But some special days give the opportunity to make "quantum leaps" in our progress in Krishna consciousness — holy days like this.
  - **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** said that holy days like Ekadashi and the appearance days (Janmas) "are the mothers of devotion for devotees who properly respect them."
  - In the **Nectar of Devotion (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu), Sri Rupa Goswami says** we should not neglect to observe all the festive days in our Vaishnava calendar — days of the appearance of the Lord, His devotees, or special pastimes. These are all to be acknowledged, remembered, and worshipped; this helps us advance, and **to neglect them is actually offensive.**

## Gaura Purnima — The New Year and Our Lifetime Resolution

- It is very nice to see such a large turnout for Gaura Purnima, "one of the most auspicious days in our Vaishnava calendar." Actually, this is the day our calendar starts — **the new year for us.**
- We don't take material New Year's resolutions; our resolutions are of a spiritual nature.
  - **Shikshashtaka, verse 4 ("na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ…"),** as Mahaprabhu prayed: We have no desire for material wealth; we're not fascinated by the opposite sex; we're not after name, fame, or prestige to be known as a great devotee. **"mama janmani janmani…"** — we're not even after liberation. If we need to come back by the will of the Lord to continue serving the Sankirtana mission, we're willing to do that as long as we have some **seva** — "a little seva, to be honest." We have Radha-Krishna. **This is our lifetime resolution.**

## Lord Chaitanya as the Yuga Avatar — and the Cycle of Ages

- The whole Chaitanya Charitamrita is full of the lilas of the Lord, the philosophy of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas — Adi Lila, Madhya Lila, and Antya Lila. "So what can be said in such a short time?"
- We can begin by saying that **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is the Yuga Avatar.** Yuga avatars are the incarnations of the Lord who appear in each age to deliver the particular process of self-realization appropriate for that age.
- There are four yugas, just as our year goes through four seasons (we're just coming out of winter into spring, then summer, then fall, and again winter). Similarly, on the cosmic level the creation goes through four ages: **Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali.**
  - Contrary to the scientific view that things go from worse to better, **the Vedic view is that it goes from best to worst.** Unfortunately, we're "at the end of the stick" — this is the worst age, Kali Yuga.

## The Same Central Purpose in Every Age — and Why the Lord Alone Gives Dharma

- The Lord gives a process in each age because, regardless of the age, there's a central theme: **Vedanta-sutra, "athāto brahma-jijñāsā"** — now that we've come to the human form of life, the sole purpose is self-realization. Whether Satya, Treta, Dvapara, or even Kali Yuga, the same purpose of life is there.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (6.3.19): "dharmaṁ tu sākṣād bhagavat-praṇītam"** — the Lord enunciates the process of dharma (religion) for each age. But no one *else* can enunciate the process of religion.
  - With our limited minds and imperfect senses, we can barely understand the world around us. The scientists understand only "the tip of the iceberg," and even most of that is nonsense. What to speak of the transcendental world?
  - There is a world beyond — the spiritual world — and if someone is sincere about understanding that which is beyond, there is an **authorized process.** Prabhupada said this Krishna conscious movement is authorized, and **the supreme authority is the Adi Guru, Krishna.**
- He very kindly gives a process of religion for each age, because not every religious process is appropriate for any other age.

## The Process as Medicine for a Diseased Condition

- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu refers to this process like a medicine. **Bhaktivinoda Thakura's "Jiv Jago" ("jīv jāgo, jīv jāgo, gorācānda bole"):** "Wake up, wake up, sleeping souls! How long will you sleep in the lap of the witch called Maya? Take this medicine of the Hari-nama, revive yourself, become healthy in Krishna consciousness."
  - It's like a medicine because we are all in a diseased condition. Someone may say, "No, I'm very healthy" — but **the real disease is forgetfulness of God.** Because of that forgetfulness we suffer so many diseases and miseries. When you're diseased and suffering, you take medicine. **Krishna says in the Gita He is the healing herb** — there's a remedy to come back to the natural state.
- But not every medicine is appropriate for every disease — like going to the pharmacy where there are hundreds of thousands of medicines; the learned pharmacists decide what is appropriate for your particular disease.
- **The medicine of each age:**
  - **Satya Yuga** — meditation.
  - **Treta Yuga** — fire sacrifice (yajna).
  - **Dvapara Yuga** — temple worship.
  - **Kali Yuga** — a very potent medicine is required. Those earlier medicines are valid and powerful, but not powerful enough for our diseased condition: "We're very diseased, very forgetful of God, very attracted by the material energy, and our desire to enjoy is very strong — even to the point where we engage in adharmic, sinful activities."

## The Predicted Medicine — The Chanting of the Holy Names

- The Lord brings a special medicine in the age of Kali, and it was predicted.
- **Chaitanya Charitamrita (Ādi 17.21), quoting the Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa: "harer nāma harer nāma harer nāma eva kevalam / kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā"** — in the age of Kali the most effective and sublime means is the chanting of the Holy Names; there's no other way, no other way, no other way.
- Specifically, that means **not** by any material activity, not by yoga, not by jnana (although they may have been appropriate in previous times) — only by the chanting of **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.**

## Why the Kali-Yuga Avatar Is Golden, Not Blackish

- Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is that avatar who comes. Usually in Kali Yuga the avatar is also blackish in color, but this is a *special* Kali Yuga we live in: the Lord comes in a **golden color** to inaugurate the Sankirtana, and there is a reason.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (11.5.32) — a very fundamental verse for the Gaudiya Vaishnavas:** In the age of Kali the Lord appears, but not as He did previously in Dvapara Yuga (blackish, more like monsoon color); in this age He appears in a **golden color,** and He is to be recognized by the fact that He is surrounded by His loving associates who are all inaugurating the dharma of chanting the holy names. **Su-medhasaḥ** — those who have intelligence will worship that form of the Lord.
- **Why golden?** **Kaviraj Goswami describes in Chaitanya Charitamrita** that Krishna wanted to understand the loving sentiments of Radharani, His beloved consort. He is the object of her love and devotion, and that love is very deep — called **Mahabhava.**
  - Only three personalities really understand the depth of that divine love that Radha has for Krishna: **Radha herself, Madhavendra Puri** (who appeared just before Chaitanya Mahaprabhu), and **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself.**
  - Even for Krishna it was difficult to understand, so He wanted to take the position of the servant, of the lover, and worship Himself as Krishna. "It's quite esoteric." So He took the mood of Radharani during His incarnation in Kali Yuga.
  - Because His **bhava,** His mood, that love was His disposition, His complexion was thus — "the face is an index of the mind"; your inner thoughts are reflected in your outer complexion. The color of Radharani is golden, like the **Champak flower.** One time Prabhupada was given a Champak flower (golden-white); he looked and said, **"This is the color of Radharani."** So the Lord's external appearance was in the mood of Radharani.

## The Two Reasons for the Appearance — Revealed by Rupa Goswami

- The Lord had several reasons for appearing:
  - **External reason:** to inaugurate the Sankirtana movement as the Yuga Avatar.
  - **Internal reason:** to understand the special love that Radharani has for Him.
- All of this was revealed to us by the mercy of **Sri Rupa Goswami.**
  - **Rupa Goswami pranama-mantra ("śrī-caitanya-mano-'bhīṣṭaṁ sthāpitaṁ yena bhū-tale / svayaṁ rūpaḥ kadā mahyaṁ dadāti sva-padāntikam"):** We pray for the mercy of Rupa Goswami, who understood the inner mood of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Without the mercy of this **Rasa-acharya** — we are all **Rupanugas,** followers of Rupa Goswami — we would not have understood the heart of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (the internal and external reasons for His appearance).
  - The internal reason — to understand this divine love of Radharani and share it with others — gives us the opportunity to also develop that special love. Before the appearance of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, most Vaishnavas (**Ramanuja, Madhva**) worshipped in **Aishwarya** — great awe and reverence. But our Acharyas have given us a very sweet love — the love of the residents of Vrindavan. Rupa Goswami understood this.

## The Rathayatra Festival and Mahaprabhu's Song of Love

- During the **Rathayatra festival at Jagannath Puri,** which Chaitanya Mahaprabhu participated in each year, He would come before the cart and the deities of **Jagannath, Subhadra, and Baladeva** and dance in great ecstasy. There was a wonderful exchange of love between Jagannath and Lord Chaitanya, because **Jagannath is Krishna** and **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was experiencing that divine love of Radha.**
  - Sometimes Mahaprabhu would disappear from the front of the cart, and the cart would stop; "all the king's horses and all the king's men" — and even the elephants — couldn't push it. Only when that divine personality, imbued with Mahabhava, appeared before Jagannath did the cart start again.
- During one of those ceremonies, from Mahaprabhu's lotus mouth came one very beautiful song. **Paraphrased, He sang:** "This is the same beautiful spring night, and you are my same lover; we are here in the forest, and I am enjoying these wonderful pastimes again."
  - Some devotees were confused — "What is this? Mahaprabhu is a sannyasi, and he's singing this song about love?" They couldn't understand, but later it was revealed by the mercy of Rupa Goswami: this was the divine motive of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.

## Haridasa Thakura and the Discovery of Rupa Goswami's Verse

- When Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was in Jagannath Puri, He used to visit the great saint, the **Namacharya Haridasa Thakura,** who lived some distance from the temple.
  - Haridasa was actually the greatest Vaishnava, the most qualified — but out of humility he would not enter Jagannath's temple. By the Lord's arrangement he had taken birth in a lowly family, so that — to demonstrate the power of the holy names and give us faith in them — he could be elevated from that position to Namacharya. In his humility he would not enter the Jagannath temple.
  - But out of love for Haridasa Thakura, **Lord Jagannath would visit him daily in the form of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu,** because there is no difference between Jagannath and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- One day Chaitanya Mahaprabhu went to the **Bhajan Kutir of Haridasa Thakura,** where **Rupa Goswami** was also present. These saintly persons associated all day long, having Harikatha, doing kirtans together, writing their books and exchanging their realizations — "a very historic time; we missed it, 500 years ago."
- While going there, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu found a piece of paper in the thatched part of the roof. He took it, read it, and was amazed. He came inside and asked, **"Who has written this verse?"** The devotees pointed to Rupa Goswami, who had written it and tucked it into the thatched roof.
  - The verse put a divine aspect on the same mood He had been relishing: **"I am the same Radha, and you are the same Krishna; these are the same moonlit nights; let us enjoy our transcendental pastimes again, as we did before"** — before Krishna left Vrindavan, when his associates were engaging in so many wonderful pastimes.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu asked, **"Who has understood my mind?"** The devotees pointed to Rupa Goswami, and Mahaprabhu glorified him for understanding this divine loving mood between Radha and Krishna and their transcendental associates. "We are simplifying it," but in this way Rupa Goswami was glorified by the mouth of the personality of Radha.

## Rupa Goswami's Verse on the Glories of the Holy Name

- Mahaprabhu then asked Rupa Goswami to give some nice verses he had written, and Rupa spoke one verse about the glories of the holy name: **he wished he had thousands of tongues and millions of ears** by which he could hear and embellish the chanting of **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare…** — how these holy names enter into our ears and into our heart and create "a jubilant festival of Krishna consciousness."
  - "My presentation is quite [simple], not the erudite, poetic, bhakti-imbued presentation that Rupa Goswami" gave of the pastimes and holy names of Krishna and His devotees.
- Rupa Goswami was glorified: **"The lines of your poems are like strings of pearls."** In this way Chaitanya Mahaprabhu glorified Rupa Goswami as the person who could understand His mind, His mood, and why He had actually appeared in the world for these two reasons.

## Summary of the Two Reasons — External and Internal

- **External reason:** to inaugurate the chanting of Hare Krishna, by which we can cleanse our hearts of all the sinful reactions accumulated for millions and millions of lifetimes — the source of all our complex problems, sufferings, and great obstacles to going back to Godhead — and the material desires that have been brewing in our hearts for millions of lives.
- **The real glories of the holy name:** ultimately, to awaken our love for Krishna, to give us that wonderful treasure house of love of Godhead — and to give it to **everyone, anyone, even the most fallen.** Therefore Mahaprabhu is sometimes called **Patita Pavana,** the deliverer of the most fallen.
  - This is a very historic time in the spiritual history of Bharata-varsha; therefore, as we hear, the demigods are lining up on the heavenly planets to take birth in Kali Yuga on the earth planet, so they can participate in these pastimes.
- **The internal reason,** as revealed by Sri Rupa Goswami, was to understand these deeper mellows of devotional service — and because He understood it, He wrote about it. Therefore we have **Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu.**
  - Prabhupada gave us the **Nectar of Devotion** very early in the movement — even before we were qualified ("even if we're qualified now, it's questionable") to understand these deeper things — in order to establish our movement for the history of the world. It was written by Srila Rupa Goswami.

## Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's Mission Is a Missionary Movement

- These days are meant to revive in us a sense of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's mission, because we have been blessed by His mercy, and we must always remember **this is a missionary movement.**
- Mahaprabhu appeared in this world and He Himself went out into the villages, among the common people, chanting and dancing and speaking a little about Krishna consciousness. Those who follow in this line are great acharyas — all liberated souls — who come to this world only for the same purposes. If we follow in their footsteps, we really get the blessings of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- **In Bhagavad-gita (18.68–69), the Lord says** that to those who preach this message of Bhagavad-gita, there is none more dear to Him, nor in the future shall any be more dear, than those devotees who are preaching.
  - So if we want the blessings of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, we have to actively serve Him. It's a loving relationship we have with the Lord, and **love means service.**

## What Service? — Keep the Preaching Spirit Alive

- What type of service should we do in this world? We may have any kind of service in the spiritual world (we're not meant to speculate on that now; we'll have service by God's grace there). But in relationship to Prabhupada, our previous Acharyas, and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, our service is to **follow in their footsteps and preach Krishna consciousness.**
- **Preaching gives life.** If the preaching stops, things dry up, it gets boring, and people leave. So we have to keep the preaching spirit going — and this way we'll get the full mercy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, a very merciful avatar. "If you want the mercy, this is the best way to get it: to be active and to spread [it]."
- When Prabhupada came around, he taught us — he was asking for the *results* of our preaching: **how many books distributed, how many devotees made, how many temples opened.**

## Glorifying Seattle, the Preaching Spirit, and Its Leadership

- Here in Seattle "there's lots of life" — a very vibrant yatra. The senior men like to come and visit here "because there's life here; you have the preaching spirit," because you have such an exalted leader in the personality of **Harivilas Prabhu,** who "captured that spirit very early in the movement."
- **Personal recollection:** When the speaker joined the movement, Prabhupada asked a couple of them to go to Europe and help with the preaching. When they got to France, he wondered why they had been sent, "because the preaching is going on so nicely" — because Harivilas, in the very beginning of the movement, was very active in preaching and set a nice example in so many ways.
- "Here we come in the autumn of our life — we're all in our 60s now." The fruit of all our hard labor to serve our spiritual masters is bearing fruit now. We're very thankful to see these results in our lifetime: so many devotees coming, and on the eve of building a beautiful temple for the most beautiful deities.
  - Construction is going to begin in a few months — "an exciting, very historic time here in Seattle." We can see the beautiful temple in the picture now, but it will manifest before our eyes very soon, and then you can worship your deities in their true glory. **"We should give the deities the very best."** They'll be very happily offered to Prabhupada and Raja-Tanya[?] as a very nice preaching result.

## You Are Fortunate — Any Program Is Only as Good as Its Leader

- "You are all very fortunate." We travel all over the world and visit many centers, temples, and regions, but this region is very nicely managed by Harivilas Prabhu. "I just want to remind you of your very good fortune."
- **Prabhupada said any program is only as good as the leader.** You have your leadership structure here; Harivilas is proud of the team helping him, "because we couldn't do without teamwork," and all of you are part of the greater team as well.
  - **Vedic culture analogy:** we have the shudras, the vaishyas, the kshatriyas, and the brahmins — "but the brahmins are the most important, because if you lose the head, Prabhupada said, everything goes awry." So thank the Lord that you have a wonderful head of this project — a very sincere disciple of Srila Prabhupada, "very much imbued with that Sankirtana spirit of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and certainly blessed by my Prabhupada." "Thank you for working so nicely along with him."
- "I think when I come back next year, we're going to see that temple coming up."

## Glossary

- **Yuga-avatar** — the incarnation who appears in each age to give its appropriate process of self-realization
- **Harinama** — the holy name of the Lord
- **Mahabhava** — the highest, deepest ecstasy of love of God, epitomized by Srimati Radharani
- **Sankirtana** — congregational chanting of the holy names
- **Rupanuga** — a follower in the line of Srila Rupa Goswami
- **Rasa-acharya** — the teacher of devotional mellows; Rupa Goswami, who understood Chaitanya's inner heart
- **Aishwarya** — the mood of awe and reverence toward God, as in Vaikuntha worship
- **Patita-pavana** — the deliverer of the most fallen; a name of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu
- **Namacharya** — the authority and teacher of the holy name; Haridasa Thakura
- **Champaka** — a fragrant golden flower, likened to Radharani's golden complexion
