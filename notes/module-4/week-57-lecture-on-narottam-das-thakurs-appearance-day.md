# Lecture on Narottam Das Thakur’s Appearance Day

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 57 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:12:26

**Themes:** narottama dasa thakura · guru-disciple relationship · humility · menial service · surrender · unity in diversity

## Summary

Delivered on the appearance day of Narottama Dasa Thakura, this lecture presents him as one of the three great acharyas (with Shyamananda and Srinivasa Acharya) who carried on Mahaprabhu's sankirtana movement after the Panchatattva departed. Though an eternally liberated soul (nitya-siddha) who wrote only two books, Narottama exemplified the perfect disciple and sadhaka, and Indradyumna Swami stresses that acharyas, and even the Lord in His incarnations, descend to set an example, including how to accept and serve a spiritual master. He recounts how Narottama, a Bengali prince of Kheturi, was directed in a dream by Lord Nityananda to bathe in the river where Chaitanya Mahaprabhu had deposited His prema, and then instructed by Mahaprabhu to take shelter of Lokanatha Goswami in Vrindavan, renouncing his throne and betrothed bride with the words that, having given his heart to Radha and Krishna, he could give it to no one else. Central stories illustrate humility and menial service: Lokanatha Goswami at first refused to initiate him ('I am not even a devotee'), and Narottama secretly cleaned his guru's latrine until discovered, thereby capturing his guru's heart. The Swami weaves in the Bhagavad-gita method of approaching a guru (pranipata, pariprasna, seva) and Rupa Goswami's progression from shraddha through sadhu-sanga, bhajana-kriya, and anartha-nivritti, arguing that real advancement requires sacrifice suited to one's station: travel and preaching for the renounced, and charity for householders (his refrain 'cut your darlings'). He also emphasizes that diksha and shiksha gurus are equal, citing how Narottama, Shyamananda, and Srinivasa studied together under Jiva Goswami despite different diksha gurus as a model of unity in diversity for ISKCON. He closes with the gopis as the supreme model of surrender, who abandoned reputation, family, and social standing at the sound of Krishna's flute, showing that service to guru and Krishna must be one's very life and soul rather than a token offering.

## The Duty of the Renounced Order to Write

- This morning we discussed some of the glories of **Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura**, particularly in reference to his writings.
- Prabhupada himself writes that it is the duty of those in the renounced order of life to write, to produce some literature — because a talk comes and goes, but the books stay.
- This is one of the main reasons Prabhupada dedicated so much time to writing his books, or, more precisely, purports to the books (as **Krishna-dvaipayana Vyasadeva** compiled the epic **Srimad Bhagavatam**). Prabhupada would translate very early in the morning — around 1 o'clock — fulfilling the duty of those in the renounced order to produce literature that acts as a permanent contribution.
- Prabhupada said his books would be the **law books, the spiritual law books, for mankind for the next 10,000 years.**

## Narottama, the Illustrious Acharya

- Narottama Dasa Thakura is the great, illustrious acharya who, as mentioned this morning, carried on the Sankirtan movement of Mahaprabhu.
- The **Panchatattva** — Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Lord Nityananda, Advaita Acharya, Gadadhara, and Srivasa — are the Lord appearing in Kali Yuga: five truths, five absolute truths, very prominent and pervading in this room. After they finished their lilas and returned to the spiritual sky, the baton was passed to three devotees in particular.
- Narottama was a **prolific** writer. Prolific means one who produces a lot — yet, quoting **Chaitanya-charitamrita** (Kaviraja Goswami), "elegance is truth spoken concisely." Like the **Vedanta-sutra**, which presents spiritual knowledge in code-like form — short little stanzas ("Now that you have arrived at the human form of life, what is your business?" — codes, short sutras; the Vedas codified).
- So we say Narottama was a prolific writer, yet he produced only **two books**. But within those books is pregnant transcendental love — what we need in order to develop love for Krishna.

## Narottama as the Perfect Example (Acharya and Sadhaka)

- We glorified Narottama today as a perfect example, an **acharya**. Acharya means one who sets the standard, sets the example for his followers. He was a perfect **sadhaka** — a very good follower, a good practitioner, a deeply realized devotee.
- How did he become like that? Of course, he is a **Nitya-siddha**, an eternally liberated soul. He did not come to this world for any selfish purpose, as we did.

## Two Types of Devotees Who Come from the Spiritual World

- **Gour Govinda Maharaja**, once giving a lecture in Budapest, Hungary, said there are two types of devotees who come from the spiritual world:
  1. The devotee who, out of compassion for the suffering souls in the material creation, comes of his own accord.
  2. The eternally liberated soul who comes on the order of Krishna, personally requested: "Please go."
- **Srila Prabhupada** falls into the second category. Prabhupada did not want to come to this nasty place; Krishna ordered him. Prabhupada said, "I have never been inclined towards austerity in the sense of the tyagis who torture the elements of the body. Austerity is important, but I am not inclined to those very extreme austerities." Krishna said, "No, you go to the material world, you write books — the science of God consciousness — and I will arrange everything."
- Prabhupada mentioned in Detroit, Michigan, that the bhaktas come from the spiritual world for the enlightenment of the world, out of their compassion — giving the example of **Lord Jesus Christ** as one who comes of his own accord.
- So Narottama Dasa is that type of devotee: he did not take his birth for any selfish purpose as we did. But being acharya, while here he sets the example.

## Even the Lord Sets the Example

- Even the Lord, the topmost of all living entities — **Purushottama**, the best amongst all males — sets an example when He comes. He has appeared in many incarnations:
  - As **Nara-Narayana Rishi**, a brahmachari.
  - As **Krishna**, the perfect householder (even "the hen-pecked husband," controlled by love).
  - As **shaktyavesha-avatars**, living entities particularly empowered — like **Prithu Maharaja** and his wife Archi.
  - In Kali Yuga, as a **sannyasi** — **Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (cites **Srimad Bhagavatam 11.5.32**, *"krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam sangopangastra-parshadam..."* — the incarnation who chants Krishna's names, of golden complexion, accompanied by His associates, worshiped by intelligent people through sankirtana-yajna).
- So even when the Lord comes, He sets an example of how to be an ideal person, so we can achieve the goal of life — how to be an ideal brahmachari, grihastha, vanaprastha, and sannyasi. Without such examples, if we had only philosophy, it would be hard. Someone who demonstrates, exemplifies, and personifies the scripture is very valuable — you can follow in his lotus footsteps.

## Krishna Himself Accepted a Spiritual Master

- Krishna Himself accepted a spiritual master. Although He is God, the **Adi Guru**, the knower of everything, He accepted a guru: **Sandipani Muni**, whose ashram was in **Avantipur**.
- One great devotee, **Paurnamasi**, lived there (mentioned in a prayer by Rupa Goswami). Having heard the glories of Shyamasundara Krishna — Radha-Krishna in Vraja, in Vrindavan — she gave up her place in Avantipur and moved to Vrindavan, where she arranges all the nectarean background for the beautiful pastimes.
- Nowadays that Avantipur is called **Ujjain**. His Holiness **Bhakti Charu Maharaja** recently opened a temple there. When someone asked, "Why Ujjain? It's in the middle of nowhere," Maharaja replied: "Well, I think everywhere else is. Ujjain is Avantipur — a holy place. Sandipani Muni had his ashram there and instructed Krishna in all the absolute truth." Krishna thus set an example of how to have a spiritual master.
- When He appeared thousands of years later as **Sri Krishna Chaitanya**, He also accepted a spiritual master — **Ishvara Puri** — and as sannyasa-guru, **Keshava Bharati**. Similarly, great devotees who come to this world have no need to accept a teacher, but for our benefit they show how to accept and follow a teacher.

## How Narottama Chose His Guru

- Narottama Dasa Thakura also set this example: although he is Nitya-siddha, he came and demonstrated the perfect sadhaka, accepting a spiritual master — **Lokanatha Goswami**.
- It is always the life of a disciple to choose among sweet devotees; he has to accept one. How did Narottama make his choice?
- The normal way we hear of: "Going to India to find a guru, going to Mayapur — all the Maharajas will be there. I'm going to go to all the classes, listen, and watch them carefully. I'm going to find one." And the one who fires us in our hearts, who inspires us to let go of our material desires — the "live wire" we're attached to, which makes us suffer ("Are you happy?" "Oh yeah, I'm back [in pain]") — the one who helps us let go (which is not easy). **Sadhu** means one who cuts our attachments; by his speaking alone he convinces us, and he encourages us to catch hold of the lotus feet.
- So the Lord is actually making the arrangements. Anyone who helps us on the path we accept as a guru; we can have many **shiksha gurus**. (Aside: a servant, Prahlada, keeps six pictures of his gurus on his altar — Sridhara Maharaja, Bhaktividya Purna Maharaja, [Bhakti] Govinda Maharaja, "and me," among others.) To the person from whose lotus mouth we hear words that make us say "Yes" — that is Krishna's arrangement: "I will surrender to that person and be trained by that person."

## Lord Chaitanya Chose Narottama's Guru Through Dreams

- In Narottama's case, Lord Chaitanya picked his guru for him.
- Imagine how young he was — still living at home, still in **Kheturi**, a young boy. He had several dreams.
  - In one dream, **Lord Nityananda** appeared and told him to leave the house and go down to the river to take a bath in the middle of the night. He did so.
  - Previously, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — knowing Narottama would appear — had **deposited His prema in that river** (the Padmavati). He told Lord Nityananda that in the future this boy, Narottama, would come, bathe in that river, and become educated with the love of God. So Lord Nityananda facilitated the pastime by appearing in the dream: the boy woke, bathed in the river, and became mad with the love of God.
  - An astrologer had told his parents, "Never let this boy bathe in a river" (guessing he would go crazy) — and indeed he became mad with the love of God.
  - A few nights later they locked him in his room, considering him like a schizophrenic, because all he was singing was "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna." But then Chaitanya Mahaprabhu appeared in the room and said: "Now you must leave home. Go to Vrindavan and take shelter of **Lokanatha Goswami**, who will be your guiding light." (Aside: "Our picture guru makes it very easy — I know you've made the right choice. Who told you?")

## The Struggle to Reach Vrindavan

- It was a struggle to get to Vrindavan. The parents didn't want him to leave — a handsome, well-educated boy with all good qualities, the prince (his father was a king, and he was next in line to the throne). They had selected a beautiful girl for him, but he wouldn't even talk to her or look at her. He escaped.
- On the way, his father sent 15 or 20 soldiers to bring him back. In the forest they found the boy — having very tender feet (as a prince, it was hard for him to walk), he had gone into the forest so no one would see him, and, exhausted a couple of days into his journey, was lying there. The soldiers surrounded him: "Your father wants you back."
- He stood up and said: **"When a girl gives her heart to one boy, you cannot expect her to take it back or marry another man. Once a girl has given her heart, being a chaste girl she cannot give her heart to another."** (Aside on Vedic culture: marriages are arranged very young; a girl gives her heart to her husband, grows up, reaches puberty, and there is no question of anybody else.) "So I have given my heart to my beloved." "Yeah — she's back there waiting for you." "No — I have given my heart to **Radha and Krishna**. I can't give my heart to anyone else; I can't go back and give my heart even to my family."
- **Teaching:** This is what we all want to achieve — not that we should neglect our loved ones, but that the most beloved is Krishna. At some stage we have to be **atma-nivedanam** — give everything to Krishna (full dependence, **prapatti**). We are doing that gradually as brahmacharis, grihasthas, vanaprasthas, and sannyasis — helping each other give our hearts to Krishna.
- Narottama's heart was always with Him. The soldiers just laughed, and he went on to Vraja, where he was introduced to Lokanatha.

## Lokanatha Goswami's Extreme Humility

- Lokanatha Goswami said to him: **"I am not going to initiate you, because I am not even a devotee myself."** How can he say such a thing? This was a demonstration of extreme humility.
- The devotee never really feels himself qualified. If anything, he says to his Guru Maharaja: *"Your mercy is all that I am made of. But for you, what would I be? Just my normal self, a lost soul in this world. Your mercy is all that I am."*
- A devotee never feels very qualified; he always feels others are serving Krishna better than him. This is his **magnanimous vision** — he sees others serving the Lord, and rather than being envious, he is inspired, wanting to be like that.

## The Devotee's Vision Versus the Material Mentality

- In the material world: if someone is better than you, you envy him; if someone is equal, you feel competition; if someone is lower, you want to exploit him. That is the nasty mentality — better → envy, equal → competition, less → exploit.
- But the devotee, being spiritually advanced, sees the world differently — he sees through the eyes of shastra and Vaishnava (cites **"premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilochanena..."** — Brahma-samhita 5.38 — the saint sees with eyes anointed with the salve of love). Someone more advanced: "Let me serve that devotee." Someone equal: he develops a strong bond of friendship — "Let's serve Guru Maharaja together; many hands make small work; Gurudev will be pleased if we work together." Someone less advanced: "Let me help them."
- **Prabhupada's instruction:** A sannyasi asked if he could join another sannyasi to preach. Prabhupada said: "If two sannyasis can do more together than individually, let them work together."
- **Personal aside (Gour Govinda Maharaja):** The speaker proposed working together with Bhakti Gour Govinda Maharaja — "the only problem is you're in Kazakhstan, in the middle of nowhere." He was preaching there; "Come to Poland." They had this friendly argument. They met in Mauritius, where he is the GBC, and the speaker decided to do a festival program there — so Krishna fulfilled his desire: for the first time in his life he had a GBC man. ("I'm always traveling — a free spirit — I didn't have a GBC. Now my godbrother, my best friend, is my GBC — and he's a good GBC.") They made a nice plan to build a town/temple. This is real friendship.
- **Mundane vs. real friendship:** Mundane friendship means liking the same music (hip-hop, rock and roll), the same clothes, food, sex — that's not real friendship. Real friendship is to encourage your friend on the path; and to a less-advanced person, we want to give help.
- **Personal aside (feeling welcomed when he joined):** When the speaker first joined the movement, he shaved his head — very tanned, with a white scalp and a big shikha, having shaved his long beard, so his face was tan below and white above ("I looked like a freak"). He put on a brand-new dhoti that still had the starch in it, so "it looked like a big diaper," very awkward and out of place. Coming downstairs, his godbrother (a famous astrologer, a devotee of several years and quite prominent) said: "Look at this boy — he has feet like a brahmana. The toe next to his big toe is longer than his big toe." (It wasn't even true, "but he said it like that.") The speaker felt so encouraged — not uncomfortable, but "like one of the boys, one of the family." Here was a prominent senior not exploiting the newcomer ("Hey, you take my job and clean the temple"), but giving inspiration.

## Narottama's Menial Service

- So Narottama received the instruction from Chaitanya Mahaprabhu to take shelter of Lokanatha. Lokanatha felt himself very unqualified (demonstrating humility), and Narottama then demonstrated the proper mood of a disciple — **menial service**.
- No matter how great we are in this world, either we really are great (because of some accomplishment due to our karma) or we merely think we're great (a "wannabe"). Everybody is either great or thinks they're great; everyone wants to be master, and there is no servant. So Narottama demonstrated menial service. The disciple should be willing to do menial service to show that he feels himself very fallen and eager for spiritual life — and he does not even consider it menial.
- **Story (Prabhupada mopping the floor):** Prabhupada was trying to teach one of the girls how to clean the floor, and she didn't catch it, so he got down on his hands and knees, took the rag, and showed her how to wash the floor. A Paramahamsa, Maha-bhagavata, Nitya-siddha, a truly liberated soul — down on his hands and knees mopping the dirt off the floor.
- **The devotee's attitude:** "For so many millions and millions of years I have been bereft of service to Krishna. So if I get just one drop of nectar, one drop of seva, that is more valuable than all the gold in the world." He is not thinking, "I'm cleaning the toilet, the bathroom, the pots" — he is thinking, "Oh my gosh, I've got some service for Radha and Krishna. And if I do it in the proper consciousness, they'll notice me and bless me."
- **Quality over quantity:** The quality of the seva is more important than the quantity. Cites **Bhagavad-gita 9.26** — *"patram pushpam phalam toyam..."* — if you offer Me a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water, offer it with some bhakti, with feeling, with something from your heart. The devotee thinks, "I'd rather clean the bathroom than be president of America. Let Bush make all the mess."
- **Narottama's specific service:** He would clean his guru's latrine — going afterward to clean the place up, put fresh sand there, and bring lots of water — so that Guru Maharaja would return the next day and find it all very nicely arranged. One day **Lokanatha Swami** hid in the bushes and saw the brahmachari coming to clean. He came out: "What are you doing?" Narottama replied that he was offering this seva at his Guru Maharaja's lotus feet. That attitude of menial service endeared Narottama.
- We want to capture our Guru's heart, and we do it through service — giving up our own consideration and taking up the mission of Guru Maharaja. We are no longer **Godasa** but **Goswami**: Godasa means one who serves his own senses; Goswami means one who has controlled the senses and engages them in the service of the Lord. Not an easy thing — which is why those in the renounced order are given some recognition and respect: they have renounced the world and engage body, mind, and soul in service.

## Pleasing the Guru Through Sacrifice: Brahmananda in Africa

- Just as Narottama endeared Lokanatha by his service, we can endear ourselves to Srila Prabhupada. What pleases Prabhupada is that we preach.
- **Story:** A godbrother of the speaker, who served as Prabhupada's secretary for a while, was on a plane opening and reading Prabhupada's mail to him. ("It was so easy in those days — 5 or 10 letters. Now you can get 25 or 30 letters, email...") He read a letter from **Brahmananda**, who was preaching in Africa, describing all the austerities they were undergoing to establish the movement there — the humidity, the tsetse flies, engaging the Africans, so much red tape, government, and sickness — yet reporting that they were being successful. Prabhupada began to cry; tears streamed down his cheeks. What caused the tears was **the service rendered by his disciples** — the sacrifice of the disciple for the pleasure of the Guru.
- It is no secret how to make advancement. Why should we sacrifice for our guru? Because he has given us everything — taken away our karma, trained us, given us the opportunity to become pure devotees of the Lord. What more precious gift? So we are indebted to him and must render some service. Thus Brahmananda Prabhu endeared himself to Prabhupada eternally.

## Lokanatha Sends Narottama for Shiksha to Jiva Goswami

- Interestingly, Lokanatha Maharaja was very old at that time — the senior-most Goswami in Vrindavan, having gone there before even Rupa and Sanatana, when Mahaprabhu was still in the grihastha ashram. So he said, "I am an old man now, so you take **shiksha** from **Jiva Goswami**."
- Jiva Goswami was relatively young, being the son of **Anupama**. There were three brothers — **Rupa, Sanatana, and Anupama**. We don't hear much about Anupama because he passed away early. It is not mentioned how he died, but these devotees leave when the Lord wants them home — "Come home, son."
  - (Aside on the passing of great souls: Just as Sridhara Swami and Bhakti-tirtha Maharaja may die by cancer or a car accident — death is never pleasant, tearing the soul from the body — the real point is that the Lord wants them home. "You've endeared Me with your service; come home." We are the unfortunate ones left behind.)
- So Lokanatha said, "Take shelter of Jiva Goswami; he's young." Being the son of Anupama meant his uncles were Rupa and Sanatana Goswami. "Can you imagine? Who's your uncle? Rupa Goswami. Who's your father? Anupama."
- Jiva Goswami, because he kept studying, became a very great scholar — in fact the most prolific of all the Goswamis. Even today in **Benares** there is a whole institute where his books are studied. He wrote later because he was younger; when the elders passed away, he became the acharya. "The present student becomes the future master; when he's a good student, he becomes a good teacher."

## Personal Account: Receiving the Nrisimha Mantra

- With the permission of Bhakti Charu Maharaja, the speaker received the **Nrisimha mantra** from one of Prabhupada's godbrothers, way back — who later passed away at 103. He loved Prabhupada. Getting old (in his 90s), he wanted to pass on the Nrisimha mantra he had received from Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati.
- **The lineage of the mantra:** Nrisimha appeared in a dream and gave **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** the Nrisimha mantra. Bhaktivinoda Thakura gave that mantra to his son (Bhaktisiddhanta) along with the **Kurma deity**. Then Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati gave the mantra to seven sannyasis whom he hoped would go to the West and preach. All of them passed away except **Puri Maharaja** (Bhakti Pramoda Puri).
- Thinking he would die, Puri Maharaja wanted to leave it in his will with somebody. The word came to the speaker, who heard first. Not wanting to act without GBC sanction, he went to Bhakti Charu Maharaja (whom he considers his elder), who said, "Yes, go immediately." So he took a rickshaw up there and Puri Maharaja gave him the mantra: "This mantra will protect you from death — if death is in your face, you chant this mantra." The speaker has used it many times: people attacking with knives and guns, a gun put to him that didn't fire.
- When Puri Maharaja gave the mantra, the speaker asked, "Can I give it to someone else?" He said: "Yes, but don't think you're a guru. Be just the humble servant of our parampara. He asks us to teach, so on his order we teach; we don't think of ourselves as guru."

## Diksha Guru and Shiksha Guru Are Equal — Unity in Diversity

- So Narottama took instruction from Jiva Goswami as shiksha. He held that in the eyes of the disciple, the **Diksha Guru and the Shiksha Guru are equal**; they perform different services and give different mercy, and we need all the mercy we can get.
- So Narottama was fortunate to have both. Jiva Goswami opened up a **tola** (a school), and there came Narottama, **Shyamananda** Pandit, and **Srinivasa Acharya**. They all had different gurus but cooperated very nicely together — a good example for ISKCON.
- Sometimes in ISKCON there are cliques — "I had my guru and you had your guru" — and devotees sit in different parts of the temple. (Aside: "I don't think it goes on here, because Hari Vilas is expert in Krishna consciousness — he's your shiksha guru. These things don't last very long.") A very nice example: **unity in diversity.**
- The lineage of these three: the spiritual master of **Narottama Dasa Thakura** was Lokanatha Maharaja; the spiritual master of **Shyamananda** was Sri Hridaya Chaitanya; and the spiritual master of **Srinivasa Acharya** was Gopala Bhatta — three gurus. And their shiksha guru was Jiva Goswami. In this family there was no envy, no competition, no jealousy — a nice spiritual family, humble Vaishnavas living together and serving their spiritual masters. The mood of cooperation is what pleases the Lord.

## Pariprashna: The Debt of the Student

- So Narottama was a good student and learned — an ideal example. At the graduation ceremony, Jiva Goswami gave Shyamananda his title/service, and Srinivasa became "Srinivasa Acharya" (he was a little older, a leader, and a good organizer).
- Jiva said: "Now you have had the **pariprashna** (inquiry), I have answered your questions. How to advance now? You must pay the debt."
- Cites **Bhagavad-gita 4.34** — *"pariprashnena sevaya... upadekshyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darshinah"* — approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively, and render service; the self-realized souls who have seen the truth can impart knowledge. These spiritual authorities help us to see the truth — both the **relative truth** and the **absolute truth**.
- They help us understand: what is this material world, and what is the spiritual world? "Why do I know this?" — You don't know this. This is **maya**. You think you know this world, but you don't; you see it through your imperfect senses and come to a wrong conclusion. Krishna says **"duhkhalayam ashashvatam"** (Bhagavad-gita 8.15) — this is a temporary place full of suffering. So that is what you should see: the beautiful girl — a temporary place full of suffering; the handsome boy or your wife — see them as devotees, through the eyes of shastra. The spiritual master helps us see the relative and the absolute, so we see this world for what it is.
- As the prophet said, "I have been everywhere, seen everything; my advice is to detach." Not necessarily formally, but in the heart — we go on with our duties in this world while always cultivating that deeper sense of consciousness.
- **Analogy (given by Lord Chaitanya):** The woman who has a paramour is at home doing her duties, and her husband thinks everything is fine, but she is always thinking of her lover. (Lord Chaitanya gave this example — to Rupa and Sanatana.)

## Rupa and Sanatana Write to God — the Nine-Year-Old's Letters

- Rupa and Sanatana were hearing about Gauranga while living at **Ramakeli**, north of Mayapur, forced into the service of **Nawab Hussain Shah** (who threatened, "If you don't join my government, I'll kill all the Vaishnavas in my land"). They heard about Lord Chaitanya and wanted to go but couldn't, so they wanted to contact Him. Their mother said, "Why don't you write Him a letter?" — write God a letter, "Dear God."
- **Personal aside (Mauritius):** The speaker was at the house of a great grihastha couple and went to the temple room to pay obeisances, where he found all these letters addressed "Dear God." He read them:
  - "Dear God, I have no interest in this world. I think the only reason I've taken birth is just to understand once and for all the temporary nature of this world. Please bring me to Your lotus feet."
  - "Dear Lord, my chanting is full of offenses, so imperfect. I have so many desires in my heart. Please, in Your incarnation as Nrisimhadev, appear in my heart and rip out my desires, and in their place plant the seed of bhakti."

## Becoming Convinced, Then Patient

- So we have to hear from the guru about this world — the relative world — and become convinced as quickly as possible. "Sir?" "Yes, sir." The guru says something; "Sir, yes, sir." Then he can reveal the truth to us.
- **Tattva-darshinah** means those who have seen the truth — either he has literally seen the truth (Krishna), or he sees through the eyes of guru (different levels of guru). But the disciple surrenders completely, because he knows "my spiritual master has seen Krishna" — literally, or he has absolute faith in the words of his guru, the previous saints, and the scripture.
- **First step is Pariprashna (inquiry):** "Guru Maharaja, who am I?" "You're not the body." "Okay, got that. What next?" "You're a spirit soul." "Got that." "And your position as soul is that you are subservient to the Supreme." "Who is the Supreme Soul?" **"ishvarah paramah krishnah sat-chid-ananda-vigrahah..."** (Brahma-samhita 5.1). These are the discussions that go on between guru and disciple, along with discussing some seva. After some years of accumulated benefit — just as a snake sheds its skin — we shed this false conception; it comes naturally.
- **But be patient.** It is a dilemma: you have to hanker intensely, yet you have to be patient — you must have the greed to advance now, but be patient. (Aside: "The contradiction in our movement is that all the boys should stay brahmachari but all the girls should be married. You should hanker, but you should be patient." Like a girl who marries and wants children — she begs to be pregnant, and then must wait nine months.)
- How do I know it will come? Because there is always somebody more advanced than you — and more advanced than him, and more advanced than him — "thank the Lord." Not "oh my gosh, how far to the top," but: thank the Lord there is always someone more advanced, so one can take shelter of the Lord. We have faith that the process is there, and we need to seek out senior association.

## The Progression: Sadhu-sanga, Bhajana-kriya, Anartha-nivritti

- Cites **"sadhu-sanga, sadhu-sanga, sarva-shastre kaya, lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya"** (association of devotees is glorified in all scriptures) and again **"adau shraddha tatah sadhu-sango 'tha bhajana-kriya tato 'nartha-nivrittih syat"** — this is part of the process of inquiring from the spiritual master and rendering service.
  - **Sadhu-sanga:** some faith is created by associating with saintly people; maintain that association, and you become convinced.
  - **Bhajana-kriya:** take up the practices of Krishna consciousness — chanting Hare Krishna, following the four regulative principles, studying the scriptures, watering the Tulasi plant, going to the temple.
  - **Anartha-nivrittih syat:** *artha* means what is good; *anartha* means what is not — literally, what is not wanted. What is not wanted? Lust, anger, greed — "the three gates leading to hell" (Bhagavad-gita 16.21). The result is that you see the unwanted things go from the heart. It works.
- **Aside — aging aids anartha-nivritti:** As you get older, the combination of the body falling apart and no longer being such a valuable tool for sense gratification means "it doesn't work anymore" — you can't run, nobody looks at you, "the only person who looks at you is your mirror." A friend said, "I brush my teeth nowadays with my eyes closed." Chanakya Pandita has a verse: "My dear lady, why are you looking now?" She replies, "You fool, don't you know that the pearl of my youth has slipped away?" And someone said in the hospital, "I'm glad I'm getting old, because the serpents of my senses are no longer as strong as they were."
- So the combination of sadhana, Vaishnava association (sanga), guru-seva, the mercy of guru — plus getting older — makes one become disinterested in material things. What is then left? One is bereft of false expectations and needs somewhere to take shelter. Then one's chanting means more, philosophy means more, the deities mean more — these things that were previously "just kind of there" become precious as you lose all that is material. The deities become your **"prana-dhana he"** (life and soul). The chanting is no longer just about finishing rounds — "I wish I had time to chant more" — and the quality changes to the mood of a young child going in desperation: "Before I die I have to be saved; I don't want to take birth again and go through the whole business of the womb." Krishna, Krishna — the quality changes. This is Pariprashna.

## The Loving Relationship and the Burden of Love

- Then you are indebted — to your guru — and you do something (seva). It is actually a loving relationship. It is a little formal in the beginning (master/teacher and student), because there is so much gratitude on both sides: the disciple is grateful the guru has given all this nectar, and the master is grateful that the disciple is helping him serve his spiritual master — "You're helping me serve my Guru Maharaja while accomplishing all these things. I get all the credit, but actually I couldn't do it without you."
- Then it turns more into a fatherly relationship — father-daughter, father-son — though there is always some formality. In the Bhagavad-gita, when Arjuna accepted Krishna as his guru, he was no longer treating Him as his friend but as his guru; talks between spiritual master and disciple are always formal. Yet with Prabhupada we saw he was very loving with us — we always knew our place, and when he became angry with us for our foolishness, there was still that sweetness there.
- We are also assisting our guru in his service to Radha and Krishna: "I am grateful you are helping me, Guru Maharaja, and I am so anxious to see Krishna — I have faith it is just around the corner." So we don't feel our indebtedness as "a debt on our head" ("Oh, where am I going to get the money?"), but as a **burden of love** — that we have to help our Guru Maharaja. That turns into **Seva**. We do something; guru is always in our mind, at least three times a day. If we're not remembering our spiritual master at least three times a day... rather, we should be constantly thinking, "This is for Guru, that is for Guru — I'm doing all this work and even recreation, but it's all for Guru-seva" — not just once a week or on the Vyasa-puja, but always thinking Guru-seva.

## Narottama's Preaching After the Ashram

- That combination gives birth to advancement. We see that Narottama was such a good sadhaka and such a good sevaka. What did he do when he left the ashram? History records it:

  - Then he went to **Kheturi** and made the whole land Krishna-conscious.
  - He sent **Ganga Narayana Chakravarti** to Manipur, and disciples here and there. He did so much preaching — "out on the street preaching." A great devotee, but out on the street.
- **Prabhupada's example:** "Who knows? We asked Prabhupada, 'Who are you?' He said, 'If I told you, you would faint.'" Yet he was on the streets of New York in his thin dhoti in the icy wind — no association, no place to stay, going from here to there. Finally getting a place in the **Bowery**, living with a drug addict — a maha-bhagavata on the streets of New York, standing in line for the shower with the hippies and students. If they could do that — out of their intense love for the Lord — we can also do it. We can accept some austerity on their behalf.

## The Supreme Sacrifice: The Gopis Give Up Their Reputation

- The **gopis** give up the dearest thing. A person can give up much — even sex, even money — but it is really hard to renounce your **reputation**. The gopis say (in the mood Krishna praises): "I have no interest in the opposite sex, no interest in money, no interest in fame."
- "I've seen big yogis in India — they have renounced everything, nothing, nothing, nothing. But you can still be a little attached to being famous — at the Kumbha Mela, fighting on the big elephant, the **Naga sadhus**, naked, clothed only by the ten directions ('their clothes are the ten directions'). But they weren't Vaishnavas — with all due respect, a Vaishnava can give up even this fame."
- **The gopis' surrender:** Krishna plays the **venu** (flute) at Vamshivata; the flute's sound goes through the forest, into the gopis' ears and hearts, melting their hearts — "Krishna, Kanhaiya, come to me." These were married women, with husbands and babies, and they didn't care. In Vedic culture, for a girl to leave her home and go see a boy in the middle of the night when she is married means she is finished socially.
- **Contrast (Banasura's captured princesses):** Even the princesses captured by Banasura (Bhaumasura) did not go of their own accord — they were kidnapped, and he did not even enjoy them — yet no one would marry these 16,100 princesses, because Vedic culture was so strict about a girl having left the sanctity of home. So Krishna married them; they became His queens.
- The gopis knew all this, but if it would please Krishna, they came — their babies crying, the milk left boiling on the stove, their husbands left behind ("Where are you going?"). And when they arrived, Krishna started preaching to them: "What are you girls doing here? You should go home." "We left home!" It is a beautiful part of the lila. They surrendered — and because they surrendered everything, Krishna gave His very self.

## Why We Love Our Guru

- We too have to sacrifice a little. We don't just take initiation and render some token service; our service to our guru is our life and soul, because our guru is our connection to Krishna — he is teaching us how to serve. He is not taking that facility for himself; he is renounced. It is a burden — a burden of love. He accepts our service and gives it to Krishna.
- Why do we love our guru? Because our guru is teaching us to love Krishna. **It is not a personality cult.** We love our guru because he is teaching us to let go of the "live wire" of material attachment.
- He is happy — he is not holding onto the wire. He doesn't have anything, yet he is happy. How is it possible? That is how we are dear to our spiritual master — because he is taking us to Krishna's mercy.

## Glossary
