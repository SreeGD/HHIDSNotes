# Who is Krishna?

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 1 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:06:30

## Summary

In this Srimad Bhagavatam class (Canto 2), Indradyumna Swami takes up the question that troubles newcomers: if God is all-good and all-merciful, why does He create a material world full of suffering? The material energy, he explains, emanates from and works entirely under Krishna, and the world is a kind of prison for souls who become envious of the Lord and wish to imitate Him as the enjoyer. What we call happiness here is merely temporary relief from the three miseries (adhyatmika, adhibhautika, adhidaivika) — like a prisoner repeatedly ducked under water, or a dry, near-empty cheese sandwich with thick layers of hankering and lamenting on either side. Yet the prison is meant to rectify, not merely punish: the material world is a perverted reflection where the soul can only imitate the Lord (a child making mud-pies), grow frustrated, and turn back to Him. Krishna is the greatest friend, accompanying even the most fallen soul as the Supersoul. Contrasting the Bhagavatam's account of creation with the materialists' Big Bang and evolution, he argues that order cannot arise from an explosion (illustrated by a pressure-cooker of khichari that exploded across a Sankirtan van) and urges placing faith in scripture and Srila Prabhupada rather than in imperfect scientists — recalling Prabhupada tossing aside a newspaper that claimed the moon is only rocks. Since devotees have already traversed the first half of the path (sraddha, sadhu-sanga, bhajana-kriya, anartha-nivritti) and never been cheated, there is every reason to trust the rest through to prema.

## Key Points

- The material energy emanates from Krishna and works under His direction; the world is a 'prison' for souls who envy the Lord and want to enjoy in His place.
- Material 'happiness' is only temporary relief from the three miseries (adhyatmika / adhibhautika / adhidaivika) — the ducked-prisoner and dry-sandwich analogies.
- The prison is meant to rectify, not merely punish: the world is a perverted reflection where the soul can only imitate the Lord (a child's mud-pies) before turning back.
- Krishna is the best friend of every living entity, accompanying even the lowest-born soul (indragopa) as the Supersoul.
- Order cannot come from an explosion — the Bhagavatam's creation (Maha-Vishnu, Brahma on the lotus) versus the materialists' Big Bang / evolution (the exploding-khichari story).
- Place faith in scripture and Srila Prabhupada, not imperfect scientists (Prabhupada discarding the moon-rocks newspaper); having traversed half the path with no disappointment, trust the rest through nistha, ruci, bhava, and prema.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 2.5.31 — the verse under discussion: all material manifestations are situated in the Lord's material energies.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.10 — 'mayadhyaksena prakrtih suyate sa-caracaram'; material nature works under Krishna's direction.
- Bhagavad-gita 10.8 — 'aham sarvasya prabhavo mattah sarvam pravartate'; all that is material and spiritual emanates from Krishna.
- Bhagavad-gita 15.15 — 'sarvasya caham hrdi sannivistah...'; the Lord is seated in every heart as the source of knowledge, remembrance, and forgetfulness.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.3.28 — 'ete camsa-kalah pumsah krsnas tu bhagavan svayam'; Krishna is the original Personality of Godhead, source of all incarnations.
- Prema-vivarta (Jagadananda Pandita) — 'krsna-bahirmukha hana bhoga-vancha kare...'; turning from Krishna, the soul is captured by maya and sent to the material world.
- Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.4.15–16 — the progressive stages 'adau sraddha tatah sadhu-sanga... anartha-nivrttih syat', then nistha, ruci, bhava, prema.
- Brahma-samhita — the range of embodied beings 'from Brahma down to the indragopa', cited to show the Supersoul accompanies even the lowest soul.
