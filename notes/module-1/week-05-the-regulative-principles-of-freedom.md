# The Regulative Principles of Freedom

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 5 · Foundations in Bhakti · 38:16

**Themes:** yukta-vairagya · karma · regulative principles · book distribution · liberation · chaitanya's mercy

## Summary

Speaking on Bhagavad-gita 9.27-28, Indradyumna Swami explains Krishna consciousness as 'the art of all work': any action performed separately from Krishna's service plants a seed of karma, but action whose fruits are offered to Krishna (yukta-vairagya, as described by Rupa Goswami) burns up these seeds and clears the mirror of the mind. He stresses that even so-called good material activity causes bondage, quoting Krishnadas Kaviraj Goswami of the Chaitanya-caritamrita that there is nothing truly good in this world because everything is temporary and ends in suffering, even on the heavenly planets. He reframes the disciplines of sadhana-bhakti as 'the regulative principles of freedom': the non-devotee is a helpless slave of mind and senses (material nature as Rudrani, 'she who makes you cry forever'), while the devotee freely chooses to serve Krishna and is therefore liberated even now, since he follows the instructions of a liberated spiritual master. Prabhupada's mood of 'work now, samadhi later' and the supreme value of preaching and book distribution (sankirtan) are emphasized as the highest taste, following the example of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and Srila Prabhupada. He illustrates the liberated devotee's detachment with the lotus flower that grows in water yet never touches it. In the concluding Q&A he tells the story of Surya Narayan, a materialistic Spanish banker who blasphemed and had a book-distributing devotee arrested in Paris, then read the discarded Krishna books, gradually surrendered, and became a first-class devotee and temple treasurer in Barcelona, showing the unlimited delivering potency of Lord Chaitanya's mercy.

## Reading the Verse: Bhagavad-gita 9.28 (with 9.27 as Context)

- The class is on **Bhagavad-gita 9.28**. Because this verse stands in relationship to the previous verse, the previous verse is read first.
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.27** (the preceding verse) — *"Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform — do that, O son of Kunti, as an offering to Me."*
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.28** (this evening's verse) — *"In this way you will be freed from bondage to work and its auspicious and inauspicious results. With your mind fixed on Me in this principle of renunciation, you will be liberated and come to Me."*

### Purport by Srila Prabhupada (read aloud)

- One who acts in Krishna consciousness under superior direction is called *yukta*; the technical term is **yukta-vairagya**, explained by **Rupa Goswami**:
  - As long as we are in this material world, we have to act — we cannot cease acting. Therefore, if actions are performed and the fruits are given to Krishna, that is *yukta-vairagya*.
  - Situated in renunciation, such activities clear the mirror of the mind; as the actor progresses in spiritual realization, he becomes completely surrendered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and at the end he becomes liberated.
- This **liberation** is specified: he does not become one with the *brahmajyoti*, but enters into the planet of the Supreme Lord.
  - There are **five different stages of liberation**; here it is specified that the devotee who has lived his life under the direction of the Supreme Lord evolves to the point where, after quitting his body, he goes back to Godhead and engages directly in the association of the Supreme Lord.
- Anyone who has no interest but to dedicate his life to the service of the Lord is actually a **sannyasi**. Such a person always thinks of himself as an eternal servant, dependent on the supreme will of the Lord; whatever he does, he does for the benefit of the Lord, as a service to the Lord.
- He does not give serious attention to fruitive activities or the prescribed Vedic duties. For ordinary persons those prescribed duties are obligatory; but though a pure devotee may sometimes appear to go against the prescribed Vedic duties, actually it is not so.
- Vaishnava authorities say that **even the most intelligent person cannot understand the plans and activities of a pure devotee**. A person always engaged in the service of the Lord, or always thinking and planning how to serve the Lord, is to be considered completely liberated at present, and his going back home, back to Godhead in the future, is guaranteed. He is above all materialistic criticism, just as Krishna is above criticism.

## Krishna Consciousness: The Art of All Work

- This is a very important verse in which Krishna enlightens us how to advance in devotional service by **action in Krishna consciousness**.
- Prabhupada would describe Krishna consciousness as **"the art of all work."**
- Generally, those aspiring to free themselves from the cycle of birth and death understand that **action or work is the cause of bondage** — and in the material sense, that is a fact.
  - Actions performed separate from the interests of the Supreme Personality of Godhead **create reaction, create karma**. Whatever a person does apart from devotional service plants a seed that will fructify into a karmic reaction.
  - This applies to *every* action — not just big things, but even the smallest action separate from the Lord's interests produces a seed of karma.

### The Three Kinds of Karmic Seeds

- **Rupa Goswami** describes in the **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu**, and Srila Prabhupada elaborates in the **Nectar of Devotion**, that the seeds of karmic reaction fall into categories:
  - Some are **manifesting at the present moment**.
  - Some have **already fructified**.
  - Some are **lying dormant** within us, to manifest in the future as some particular reaction.
- Through the **process of devotional service**, these seeds can be **burned up** so that they will not fructify or develop.

### Story: The University Boy Who Kept a Notebook

- The speaker once met a boy at a university and discussed the principle of karmic reaction. The boy said: "All right, I understand that by engaging in sinful activity I'll get a bad reaction. But I have the answer — I don't need a spiritual master, I don't need to read *Bhagavad-gita*. It's very simple: I'll just counteract all the bad things with good things."
- He actually pulled out a **notebook** and marked down all the bad things he did that day — he argued with his brother, short-changed somebody, told a lie, etc. — and at the end of the day he would do something "good" to counteract them, thinking that by doing good he would counteract the bad and accumulate no karma.
- **The explanation given to him:** so-called "good" activities in the material sense **also create bondage**.

## Even the "Good" Is Bad: The Counterfeit Coin

- In the **Chaitanya-charitamrita**, **Srila Krishnadas Kaviraj Goswami** explains there is actually nothing good in this world — **even the good is bad**, because it is not in relationship to Krishna.
- How is the good "bad"? Because it is activity performed **in ignorance** — ignorance of our relationship to God, our constitutional position as the servant of God, i.e., ignorance of devotional service. It appears good from a mundane point of view.
- What is the result of good karma? One gets born in a so-called good condition — but the **whole material world is hellish**.
  - Krishna says that even if, by accumulated pious activity, one elevates himself to the heavenly planetary systems, still one has to undergo so much misery.
  - For everything material we enjoy, there is a painful side; **you cannot have one without the other**. That is the **counterfeit coin** of material existence: a counterfeit coin has no value on either side — flip it and get heads, flip it and get tails, both sides are bad.
- Any activity performed in this material world ultimately ends in suffering — because it is **temporary**. Whatever pleasure we enjoy will be taken away in due course of time (within a minute, a day, a month, a year, or a lifetime), and that loss means suffering.

### Anxiety Even on the Heavenly Planets

- Even on the heavenly planetary systems — where birth, death, old age, and disease are "conspicuous by their absence" — material nature is arranged by the Lord so that there is **great anxiety**.
- What is the anxiety of the heavenly planets? Prabhupada said: **"When will the demons come?"** — at any moment the demons can crash the party, so one cannot truly enjoy.
- **The four principles of material existence:** eating, sleeping, mating, and defending (sometimes termed **fearing**). Prabhupada said the **fearing** aspect is the most prominent.
  - His example: a **little bird** picking up seeds off the ground, apparently enjoying — but if you watch him, he is always looking around: "Where's the danger?" Even while engaged in his activity, he is in complete anxiety.
- From Brahma's planet down to the lowest, all are places of misery, subject to repeated birth and death. **There is no shelter within material existence**; it is arranged to cause us anxiety.

### The Car Built to Break Down

- Analogy: when a motor company creates a car, they build it so that in a few years it will break down — so you have to buy new parts, eventually discard it, and buy a new one. If they wanted, they could make a car to last 20, 30, 40, or 50 years.
- Some original cars (Model T Fords) are still around. Driving from Zurich, the speaker noticed old-model cars 30–40 years old still on the road, but no recent models (10–15 years old) — because the old ones were made to last.
- **The point:** Krishna creates this material world in such a way as to cause anxiety, so that ultimately we come to our senses and surrender to Him. That is the purpose of material existence.

### Why Does a Merciful God Create Such a World?

- We want to enjoy separately from the Lord; therefore the material world is there.
- Question: if God is all-merciful, why does He create a material world like this? **Response:** why does the government create a **prison house**? Because there is a certain class of men who commit criminal activity, and this is where they must go.
- Though Krishna gives us the facility to try to enjoy separately from Him, He teaches us an ultimate lesson by making us become **completely frustrated** in that separate existence. After many, many lifetimes, we have to take shelter of His lotus feet, or His representative, to free ourselves from this misery.

### All Work Ends Badly

- Transcendentalists understand that **all work ultimately has a bad result**. Even one who is very careful to perform as much pious activity as possible still has to take birth again; and because he takes birth, he must grow old, and with old age come disease and again death. "So what type of bargain is this?"
- Therefore Krishnadas Kaviraj Goswami says there is no good — it is all bad, because the result is simply anxiety.

### "Sex Life, Licit or Illicit, It Is All Illicit"

- Prabhupada once said: **"Sex life, licit or illicit, it is all illicit."**
- Although in the *Bhagavad-gita* Lord Krishna says **"I am sex life which is not contrary to religious principles"** (that is a fact), still, along with the act of procreation comes the responsibility and anxiety of raising children properly, both materially and spiritually.
- Prabhupada was capturing a particular mood in that statement. Unless one is very careful and responsible as a devotee, then of course it becomes devotional service.

## Bondage vs. Freedom: The Prisoners and the Regulative Principles

- Actions performed separate from the interests of the Supreme Lord bring bondage.
- **Prabhupada's classic example of the prisoners:**
  - One prisoner, due to his criminal activity, lives in **solitary confinement** — no windows, a board to sleep on, bread and water.
  - Another, due to good behavior, gets a **special room** with television, weekend family visits, and facilities to buy things at the snack bar.
  - The speaker adds a newspaper report: in some affluent countries the prisons are so nice — governments spending so much money — that **life inside is better than life outside**, so some prisoners get out and deliberately commit crime to go back in. This is a type of insanity: although one has so much facility, still one's freedom is limited.
- **The key point:** until we become Krishna conscious, it does not matter whether we are on **Brahma-loka, Siddha-loka, or Gandharva-loka**. Our original heritage and nature — **"jivera svarupa haya krishnera nitya-dasa"** (the constitutional position of the living entity is that of eternal servant of Krishna) — the freedom to serve Krishna without any restrictions in the abode of **Goloka Vrindavan**, is inhibited there.

### "The Regulative Principles of Freedom"

- In the *Bhagavad-gita* Krishna describes the principles, rules and regulations, and discipline of devotional service, calling it **"the regulative principles of freedom."**
- An ordinary person may see a devotee following the **four regulative principles** and all the rules and regulations of **sadhana-bhakti** and think, "These poor people have to follow so many rules — what a difficult option for your life."
- But the reply is: "Yes, sir, so do you." A materialist has to follow his **mind and senses, which are merciless masters**.
  - The material energy is under the control of **Durga**; another name for Durga is **Rudrani**, the wife of Lord Rudra (Lord Shiva). *Rudrani* means **"she who makes you cry forever"** — that is material nature.
- Following our mind and senses: because of uncontrolled senses, those too addicted to materialistic life gradually glide toward hellish conditions, **"repeatedly chewing that which has already been chewed"** (*punah punash charvita-charvananam*), and neither their own efforts nor the efforts of others can wake them up to Krishna consciousness. Such foolish **mudhas** are struggling hard in material existence under the dictation of the mind and senses — an impossible struggle.
- The devotee, by contrast, struggles to control his mind and senses and to engage his body, mind, and words in the service of the Lord. It may be difficult, but **material life is impossible**. We have to struggle one way or the other; there is struggle in spiritual life too, due to our past conditioning — but the **future is very bright**, whereas the struggle for material existence always ends in death and hopelessness.

## Same Activity, Different Consciousness

- Though a devotee and a non-devotee may appear to engage in the same activity, the devotee's mind is fixed on Krishna, following the formula of the previous verse:
  - **Bhagavad-gita 9.27** — *"yat karoshi yad ashnasi yaj juhoshi dadasi yat..."* — "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, whatever austerity you perform — do that, O son of Kunti, as an offering to Me."
- Because of that formula alone, under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master, one is to be considered **liberated even while in this world**.

### Even a Third-Class Devotee Is Liberated

- The speaker had been reading in the **Third Canto** the night before — Prabhupada explaining **Lord Kapila's teachings to Devahuti** — that even a **third-class devotee** is considered liberated.
- Generally we think of a first-class devotee as liberated, but even a third-class is liberated, because the devotee is **following the instructions of his spiritual master**, who is a representative of Krishna delivering liberated life (devotional service). Because he is meditating on fulfilling those instructions, he is to be considered liberated. "He's liberated because he's following the instructions of a liberated person."
- Whether attending arati and looking at the forms of **Jagannatha, Subhadra, and Baladeva**; attending guru-puja and worshipping the lotus feet of Srila Prabhupada; or going out distributing books, working hard, sweating, planning, thinking, and taxing our brains — **we're doing it all for Krishna**.
- Therefore it is **forbidden** to think that a devotee is engaged in material activity, because his mind is fixed on Krishna. Even if his mind is not fixed on the transcendental form of the Lord, it is fixed on the **instructions of his spiritual master, which are non-different from the Lord**. A devotee is always working in his consciousness — in fact, working very hard.

### "Work Now, Samadhi Later" — Preaching as the Highest Taste

- Prabhupada said: **"Work now, samadhi later."** We don't even care if we have to take birth again, as long as we are assured of the opportunity to again perform devotional service.
- We especially get this understanding in **sankirtan**, by preaching. **Preaching is actually the highest taste.**
  - **Mahaprabhu Himself preached.** The Lord comes to this material world to perform His transcendental pastimes — and what are His pastimes? Chanting, dancing, preaching, distributing prasada.
  - What did Srila Prabhupada do? He did not stay in Vrindavan in samadhi; he **came to the West**, and said that **"preaching in the snows of Russia is sweeter than the sweetest mango."** If it is good enough for God, it is good enough for God's devotees.
  - Chaitanya Mahaprabhu said this **sankirtan movement / preaching gives us a taste of the nectar we are always anxious for.**
- This is Prabhupada's mercy: he gave us our spiritual program in the morning and various devotional activities during the day so we can completely absorb ourselves in Krishna consciousness.
- Anyone who thinks that such activity is material is **"fool number one."**
  - **The warning proverb in Bengal:** "If you see Nityananda Prabhu go into the liquor shop or into the brothel, don't think anything auspicious — He's going there to deliver them from the repetition of birth and death." (Some things devotees do are liberated activity that materialistic persons cannot understand.)

## Freedom of Choice

- If we strictly follow the rules and regulations — the **regulative principles of freedom** — we are free. **We have the choice; they have no choice.**
  - The non-devotees are just doing what the mind says, what the tongue says, what the belly says, what the genital says — at every minute simply falling.
  - The devotee has the choice: "No, I won't follow my mind and senses — they've been bad masters for so many lifetimes. I'll follow the instructions of my spiritual master and Krishna, and thereby be happy and blissful in Krishna consciousness."

### Do Not Worry About Auspicious and Inauspicious

- Krishna says to Arjuna in the *Bhagavad-gita*: don't be worried about inauspicious and auspicious times. He enlightens Arjuna to a very important point: you are going to die, and **whatever you are thinking at the moment of death, that state you will attain** (cf. **Bhagavad-gita 8.6**).
- Krishna goes on to explain the auspicious and inauspicious times of passing — when the sun is on one course or another, the day, the night, the season (**Bhagavad-gita, Chapter 8**) — but in the end He says: **don't worry about this; always be engaged in thinking of Me, and then surely you will attain to My nature, to the kingdom of God** (cf. **Bhagavad-gita 8.7**, *"mam anusmara yudhya cha"*).
- This is the beauty of devotional service: we don't worry about inauspicious or auspicious; even death, considered inauspicious, we don't worry about. A devotee simply engages in the process of devotional service, and death may come while he is distributing books, worshipping the Deity, or cooking; his consciousness is completely fixed, and naturally he just continues on in the spiritual world, perhaps in another body. Such things are of not much significance.
  - **Jesus said** one can be **"in this world but not of this world."**

### The Lotus Flower Analogy

- Example of the **lotus flower**: it grows in the lake, its stem going down into the mud, and a flower grows — but the flower never really touches the water; it sits on the leaf a little above.
- In **South Africa**, the speaker read this example and, knowing there is a **botanical garden in Durban**, purposely drove down with the book, went to the pond, and confirmed that the lotus flower was in the water but not touching it — a perfect example.
- This is the situation of a devotee always engaged in Krishna consciousness: he is **not affected**, he is **liberated**.
  - What does "liberated" mean? He is not affected by the modes of material nature — goodness, passion, and ignorance — but is always fixed in Krishna consciousness by following the instructions of the spiritual master.

### The Taste in Intense Devotional Activity

- That taste is there when we engage intensely in devotional activities, especially **preaching** and **book distribution** — as here in Switzerland and other countries under the direction of **His Holiness Harikesha Maharaja**, where the devotees are so blissful and liberated.
- **Bhagavad-gita 14.26** — *"mam cha yo 'vyabhicharena bhakti-yogena sevate..."* — "One who engages in full devotional service, who does not fall down under any circumstances, at once transcends the modes of material nature and comes to the level of Brahman."
- We may be third-class or fourth-class devotees, but if we are engaging in and following these instructions, we are **liberated**.
- Symptom of a liberated soul: he is **not hankering, not lamenting** — **"brahma-bhutah prasannatma"** (cf. **Bhagavad-gita 18.54**) — and he is always joyful.

### The Letter to Harikesha Maharaja

- The speaker wrote a letter recently to Harikesha Maharaja while traveling through **Yugoslavia, Bulgaria, and Czechoslovakia**, where so many disciples are.
- He appreciated how the devotees are so determined to follow Srila Prabhupada's instruction to **distribute books, distribute books**, and therefore everything was so auspicious.
- Though they had come to *give* association, by the time they left they understood they had come to *get* association.

### Liberation Stands at the Devotee's Door

- Liberation is not a very difficult thing. **Srila Bilvamangala Thakura** said that **liberation simply stands with folded hands at the doorstep of the devotee who is engaged in devotion.**
- We are not even concerned about liberation, but it is there just by engaging in these activities.
- So we should always remember this and follow Srila Prabhupada's plan — **24 hours a day** engaged in Krishna conscious activity: sadhana in the morning, service during the day, and again some sadhana at night. And then liberation — more than liberation, Krishna conscious devotional service.

### Re-reading the Verses

- **Bhagavad-gita 9.27–9.28** are read once again — "Whatever you do... do that, O son of Kunti, as an offering to Me. In this way you will be freed from bondage to work and its auspicious and inauspicious results; with your mind fixed on Me in this principle of renunciation, you will be liberated and come to Me."

## Question & Answer: Delivered Even While Doing Inauspicious Things

- The speaker offers an example of how someone becomes Krishna conscious by the mercy of Lord Chaitanya **even while doing inauspicious things** — such is the potency of Krishna consciousness.
- Generally, it is stated that by **many lifetimes of pious activities** one gets the opportunity to associate with the devotees of the Lord. But even if one is "rascal number one," Mahaprabhu can still deliver that person.

### The Story of Surya Narayan (Barcelona)

- In Spain there is a devotee named **Surya Narayan**, who told the speaker how he came to Krishna consciousness.
- He was a **banker**, a very materialistic person, addicted to wine — "fried."
  - **Aside on the word "fried":** this word has "transcended all other languages" in ISKCON. In France they say *"Voila Prabhu, je suis vraiment fried"* ("I'm really fried"); in Italian, *"frito"* ("cooked"). In some ways it is not bad to be fried — but you want to fry the **right seed, not the wrong seeds**; we don't want to fry the **bhakti-lata-bija** (the creeper of devotion).
- Surya Narayan had a big beer belly and a fancy suit. His company sent him to **Paris** on business; he did his business, visited some bars, and made money. Then he boarded the **train** back to Madrid with a hangover, sitting in first class, with some francs left in his pocket.
- A young lady in Western dress approached him for a **donation** for their farm project — "simple living, high thinking, and helping people." He reached in his pocket and gave about **80 francs**.
- She thanked him and offered him two books — **Volume 1 and Volume 2 of the Krishna Book** — as a present for helping their community.
- He lit a cigarette, asked "What is this?" and decided "This is some kind of sect... some kind of cult." He became disgusted, **threw the books on the ground**, and grew very angry that this young lady had come requesting a donation and giving books about God.

### The Offense and the Arrest

- He went to the **station master** (the train was leaving in 10–15 minutes) and complained that a young girl representing "some cult" had sold him books about "this Krishna" — "Have her arrested immediately."
- The station master called the **gendarme**; together they found her, dragged her out of the compartment, and the police arrested her and hauled her off. The man felt he had "done his job," got back on the train, and was satisfied to have "arrested this nonsense."
- **This is the greatest offense** — blasphemy against the holy name and against devotees who have dedicated their lives to preaching the glories of the Lord; he disrespected revealed scripture by throwing it on the ground; he committed so many offenses directly against Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's sankirtan movement. That mataji, who could have kept going distributing so many books, went to jail for the night; in so many ways he impeded the sankirtan and was offensive.

### The Gradual Deliverance

- On the way down to Madrid, he got bored, found the books under the seat, pulled them out, and started **looking at the pictures**. "Who? Krishna?" He kept looking and reading — that the human form of life is meant for this, that only fools and rascals will avoid the mercy.
- By the time he got to Madrid, he **felt horrible**, realizing what he had done, and there was nothing he could do. He took the books and placed them very carefully in his bookshelf — but then he became covered again by the material energy and completely forgot.
- **Three years later**, in a time of great distress — he had lost a close family member, his business was going bad, and his kids were doing badly in school — in desperation he went to those books, pulled them out, and **read them from cover to cover**. Then he decided, "I don't want any more of this materialistic life; I want to be a devotee of Krishna." He **sold his house, gave up his business, went to the temple, and became a first-class devotee** of the Lord. Now he is the **temple treasurer in Barcelona**.

### The Power of Sankirtan

- So, generally we think in terms of auspicious and inauspicious, but the **mercy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is so powerful it will deliver everyone**. This is the beauty of sankirtan.
- Prabhupada said: **"You make your life fortunate by distributing these books, and you make other persons' lives fortunate"** — and then all the bad and the so-called good in this material world will just vanish, by the mercy of sankirtan.

## Closing

- The class ends with cheers glorifying Srila Prabhupada and **Sri Sri Jagannatha, Subhadra, and Baladeva**, and an announcement introducing the next day's **Ratha-yatra festival** of Lord Jagannatha.

## Scriptural References

- Bhagavad-gita 9.27 (yat karoṣi yad aśnāsi yaj juhoṣi dadāsi yat) — the previous verse read aloud: whatever you do, eat, offer, give away, or perform as austerity, do it as an offering to Krishna.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.28 — the main verse of the class: by this offering one is freed from bondage to work and its auspicious/inauspicious results and, with mind fixed on Krishna in renunciation, becomes liberated and attains Him.
- Bhagavad-gita 7.11 — cited as 'I am sex life which is not contrary to religious principles' (dharma-aviruddha kama), while noting the attendant responsibility and anxiety.
- Bhagavad-gita 8.6 — 'whatever you are thinking at the moment of death, that state you will attain,' introducing Krishna's instruction to Arjuna.
- Bhagavad-gita 8.16 — cited as 'from Brahma's planet down to the lowest, all are places of misery' subject to repeated birth and death, so even elevation to heaven brings suffering.
- Bhagavad-gita (Chapter 8, esp. 8.23-28) — Krishna describes auspicious and inauspicious times of departure (sun's course, day/night, seasons) yet concludes: do not worry about them, always be engaged in thinking of Me and you will attain My nature.
- Bhagavad-gita 14.26 — 'one who engages in full devotional service, who does not fall down under any circumstances, at once transcends the modes of nature and comes to the level of Brahman.'
- Bhagavad-gita 18.54 — cited as the symptom of a liberated soul: brahma-bhutah prasannatma, one who neither hankers nor laments and is always joyful.
- Srila Rupa Goswami's Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, elaborated in Srila Prabhupada's Nectar of Devotion — the concept of yukta-vairagya and that seeds of karmic reaction (some fructified, some manifesting, some dormant) can be burned up by devotional service.
