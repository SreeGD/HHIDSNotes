# Introduction to Śrīla Prabhupāda

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 4 · Foundations in Bhakti · 54:15

**Themes:** spiritual master · srila prabhupada · initiation · separation · service · mercy

## Summary

On the appearance and disappearance days of his spiritual master, Indradyumna Swami offers a heartfelt personal glorification of Srila Prabhupada, calling him the be-all and end-all of his existence and the one through whom he understands Krishna consciousness. He recalls how Prabhupada himself always deflected praise, saying 'I do not know who is Krishna, I only know my Guru Maharaja,' attributing everything to his own guru, Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura. The Swami then narrates his own journey: as a teenager he sold all his possessions in a yard sale for about $1,850, joined the Detroit temple, was shaved up within fifteen minutes, and gave his first Srimad Bhagavatam class within seventy-two hours. He shares intimate memories, including missing initiation by a few days and offering Prabhupada his last $10 with a note, then receiving a personal letter promising to accept him as an initiated disciple, with a postscript that he too misses his own Guru Maharaja but always feels him watching over and protecting him. The bulk of the lecture recounts accompanying Prabhupada on a flight from New York to London, where he nervously prepared oranges as prasadam, ate the burning orange peels as maha-prasada (keeping the last one for six months like Madhavendra Puri's sweet rice), and witnessed Prabhupada laughing at a Charlie Chaplin film and teaching that Krishna is the source of all original things and that we must learn to see Krishna everywhere. He describes being made to wait for a lost suitcase full of Prabhupada's Sanskrit and Bengali reference books, missing the kirtan and feast, then improperly dragging it into Prabhupada's room and receiving a hard slap on the back along with the benediction: 'so much endeavor in this material world; when I take you back home to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime.' He closes with the Paris story of a three-month Metro book-distribution marathon undertaken to win the honor of washing Prabhupada's feet, after which he and his godbrother secretly drank the entire bowl of charanamrita (foot-wash water) while brahmacharis pounded on the locked office door, using these memories to encourage devotees to keep serving despite opposition and difficulty in preaching.

## The Occasion: Glorifying the Spiritual Master

- This evening's class was requested as a sharing of memories of the speaker's spiritual master, the Founder-Acharya of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, His Divine Grace Om Vishnupada Paramahamsa Parivrajakacharya Ashtottara-shata Sri Srimad A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Srila Prabhupada.
- These are special days: the **appearance day** of the divine master, and tomorrow the **disappearance day** of the spiritual master. On such days devotees:
  - Extend deep gratitude to him.
  - Reconfirm their vows of service to him.
  - Pledge themselves to him forever after, with great expectation, looking forward to *nitya-seva* (eternal service) to the master in the spiritual sky.
- On such days there is great pleasure in glorifying the spiritual master.

### Unlimited Subject Matters in Krishna Consciousness

- For those who speak every day, there are almost unlimited subject matters in Krishna consciousness. Yet in essence, the whole of it — the essence understood in *Srimad-Bhagavatam* — is about **the Lord** and **the process of devotional service**.
- It is almost mystical how a devotee can take one aspect of the philosophy and develop, embellish, and speak on it for hours on end.
- Example: **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura** once lectured on the **first verse of the Srimad-Bhagavatam** in Dhaka (now Bangladesh) — described as "the second Vrindavan" for its many temples flourishing under Krishna consciousness — and he spoke on that single first verse **for three months**.

### The Spiritual Master as One's Very Life

- Of all subjects, this one — one's spiritual master, be he *diksha-guru* or *shiksha-guru* — is as dear to a Vaishnava as his very life. It is the speaker's favorite subject.
- It is by the spiritual master, and him alone, by his mercy, that a devotee can understand: the process of Krishna consciousness, the practice of Krishna consciousness, the goal, the *siddhanta*, and can one day enter into the *nitya-seva* of the divine couple, **Sri Sri Radha-Madhava**, in the spiritual sky.
- The spiritual master is therefore the "be-all and end-all" of one's existence: he delivered the disciple from the depths of illusion, the ocean of material existence; he maintains him in the struggle to advance to the highest perfection; and by his grace the disciple will one day be delivered.
- **Prabhupada's own example:** Prabhupada once said, *"I do not know who is Krishna. I only know my Guru Maharaja."* His life itself revolved around his own spiritual master.
  - Whenever the young disciples, in their infancy in spiritual life, would give Prabhupada credit and glorify him, he would bow his head in true humility and say, "No, it is all the mercy of my spiritual master, Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura."
  - This reflects the deeper feelings of a Vaishnava: he owes everything to his spiritual master.

### A Class Spoken from the Heart

- This is not a class one prepares for with notes or a mental outline; it is a subject matter of the heart, spoken from deep realization — because the devotees themselves have experienced what it is to be saved from the hellish conditions of this world.
- They know to whom the credit is due for the deep satisfaction of rendering devotional service and being reinstated in their **original constitutional position**.
  - He notes "constitutional position" is an almost formal phrase of the previous century's British English, still around in Prabhupada's early days; one won't find such a phrase in modern English. It means to give up all the false designations of the body and simply to be Krishna's servant, with the happiness and joy that grows every day as one makes progress on the path of pure devotion.
- Though given only one hour (and a half-hour already spent singing), one could glorify Srila Prabhupada for hours and hours.

## First Contact: Meeting Prabhupada Through His Devotees

- It is rare that the first person one meets on entering the divine life of Krishna consciousness is one's spiritual master himself; usually one meets his representative, whose mercy — being the spiritual master's mercy — is potent enough to convince one to take up the process.
- The speaker first came in contact with **devotees who truly loved Srila Prabhupada**, and this love was *contagious*.
- After only **three days** of association, living with some sannyasis (maharajas), he became determined to practice Krishna consciousness.

### Selling Everything and Joining

- He gave up everything near and dear and sold all his possessions — as a young teenage boy, all he had amounted to only about **$1,800**.
- He held a yard sale: put everything out in the front yard with a "for sale" sign; people stopped in their cars; at the end of the day it all totaled **$1,850**.
- He presented himself at the **Detroit Temple**, where he had been visiting for about six months (since the sannyasis' visit), with long hair and a young boy's "baby beard."
  - He knocked; the temple president answered: "Oh, Brian, you're here." He replied, "Yes, I want to join ISKCON." The president: "I never thought you would join — you have so many things going on out there in life." He answered: "No, I've understood, after chanting Hare Krishna and following the principles for some time, it's all nonsense."
  - Within **15 minutes** he was shaved up — "That's how fast I was in those days." While they shaved him, his former wife was being put in a sari; within **30 minutes** they were sitting in the evening *Bhagavad-gita* class (taught by Kunti-mala).
- This process acts very quickly in the heart. There was no time to procrastinate.

### Rapid Advancement

- Within **three days** he was asked to give his first *Bhagavatam* class. (Seventy-two hours after joining.) His whole class: "*Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya.* Krishna is blue, He has a peacock feather, He wears a yellow *dhoti*, and He plays the flute. Are there any questions?"
- A little more than ten days later (Mother Sitala, who used to preach to him, could set the date straight), within a very short time he was the **sankirtan leader**, taking the *Harinama* party out.
- Things moved very quickly — "When you're doing something you don't like, time moves very slow; when you're doing things you enjoy, time moves very quickly." Speaking of events over **35 years ago**, it seems like just yesterday — because service to Guru and Gauranga, one's constitutional position, is the most relishable thing.

## Prabhupada's First Visit to Detroit

- Within a month or so, **Srila Prabhupada visited the Detroit temple** — an event everyone was waiting for. One of his godsisters wrote a book, *Prabhupada Is Coming.* When Prabhupada would come, the whole temple would be renovated, everything new and fresh.
- Prabhupada captured the speaker's heart with his scholarly, devotional lectures on the *Bhagavatam* and *Bhagavad-gita*; he endeared him by his perfect example in Krishna consciousness; he charmed him by his devotion to the Lord. Whatever surrender was still lacking was there by the end of Prabhupada's visit.

### The Gift of $10 and the Note

- When Prabhupada was walking out of the temple to leave in a car, the speaker was already feeling deep separation. He had not met Prabhupada personally, and had not been initiated — he had been a devotee only six or seven months, and one had to be a devotee about eight months to get initiated. He was a few days shy, and was devastated.
- He ran upstairs and took out the **$10** he had kept back (out of the $1,850) for emergencies — "Please don't tell my temple president." He decided to give it to Srila Prabhupada as all he had left.
- He quickly wrote a note (paraphrase): *"Dear Srila Prabhupada, please accept my humble obeisances, all glories to you. Thank you so much for coming to the Detroit temple. I'm a new member, probably the newest aspiring disciple. I just missed initiation. I hope you'll initiate me one day. I love you very much, I miss you. Hare Krishna. Bhakta Brian."* He put it in an envelope with the $10 carefully attached.

### Calling Out to Prabhupada

- He ran down as Prabhupada was getting into his car, surrounded by a few hundred devotees. In his novice state he yelled out, "**Srila Prabhupada!**" — and everyone froze, looking around in shock.
- Prabhupada smiled, looked around, and (in his characteristic way) said, *"I don't know what is yes or what is no. I don't know."* — indicating the boy should come forward.
- The line of devotees opened up; shaking, he walked forward and said, "Srila Prabhupada, this is for you." Prabhupada took it, folded it, put it in his kurta pocket, smiled; then the door closed and the car left.
- He felt satisfied at having given a personal gift to Prabhupada, and thought that was the end of it.

## The Letter from Prabhupada

- A couple of weeks later, they heard the **conch**. In the Detroit temple, whenever a letter from Prabhupada arrived for a devotee, the conch would blow and all the devotees would assemble in a small living-room-like room off to the left of the entrance; the devotee could then read the letter aloud if he wished.
- One day, in the middle of work, cooking, and cleaning, a letter from Prabhupada arrived. Everyone stopped whatever they were doing — "like the gopis: they were doing so many things; Krishna played His flute; they dropped everything" — and ran.
- **Urukrama Prabhu**, taking care of the mail, announced: "Today Prabhupada has sent a letter to Bhakta Brian." Someone next to him said, "That's you." He stood, took the letter, and read it aloud.
- **The letter (paraphrase):** *"My dear Bhakta Brian, please accept my blessings. I want to thank you so much for your letter and for your very kind donation towards my newly founded Bhaktivedanta Book Trust. I have information from the senior devotees in the Detroit temple that you are doing very nicely in Krishna consciousness. So my request to you is that you always continue to follow in the footsteps of advanced devotees, become fixed up in Krishna consciousness, and in a very short time I will accept you as my duly initiated disciple."*

### Story: Mukunda Datta and Lord Chaitanya's "One Million Births"

- Reading the promise "in a very short time I will accept you" reminded him of a pastime that is "etched in stone" in the hearts of Vaishnavas.
- **Mukunda Datta** had made an offense by going to hear some non-devotional lectures he was not supposed to attend. When **Lord Chaitanya** heard of it, He ostracized him: "I will not allow this Mukunda to come into My association."
- The devotees insisted it was only a small deviation and that Mukunda was a wonderful devotee. Mahaprabhu then declared: *"I will only accept the service of this Mukunda Datta in about one million births."*
- When that news reached Mukunda, he started **dancing in ecstasy**: "Oh, He has said He will definitely grant me audience — in one million births!"
  - Why the joy? Because we have been in this material world since time immemorial — so long you cannot even count it. "One million births" is nothing by comparison; it is even a way of saying it is guaranteed.
- Likewise, when the speaker heard Prabhupada would grant initiation, he knew Prabhupada had **already accepted him in his heart, which is the real initiation**. He was overjoyed.

### The Signature and the Postscript

- The letter was signed: *"Your ever well-wisher, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami,"* with just the initials "A.C."
- **The postscript** was what gave the deepest solace and comfort: *"I very much appreciate your feelings of separation from me. I am also missing my Guru Maharaja, I am also feeling his separation; but I always feel that he is watching over and protecting me."*
- **Separation deepens over time in Krishna consciousness.** Years later, when Srila Prabhupada actually departed the planet, it was devastating — the devotees never thought Prabhupada would leave; it seemed that joyous life of Krishna consciousness would go on forever (as it should be, and will be one day in the spiritual world).
  - In material life, when someone passes away, time heals the wound as one develops other relationships. But in Krishna consciousness the speaker found the **opposite** to be true: his attachment to his spiritual master **grows** with every day, month, and year in separation. ("Separation makes the heart grow fonder.")
- That letter, though unfortunately lost in time, remains etched within his heart. When he misses Srila Prabhupada — his savior, deliverer, eternal guide, the dearmost person in his life — he closes his eyes and remembers that postscript: *"I'm also missing my Guru Maharaja, but I always feel that he's watching over and protecting me."*
- It seemed Prabhupada knew exactly what to say to each disciple to bring him to Krishna consciousness, to maintain him, and to deliver him.

## The Journey to Europe

- A year later, Prabhupada expressed the desire that some of his senior devotees from the United States go to **Europe** to further pioneer the Krishna consciousness movement.
- Great stalwarts and pioneers — to be eulogized for generations — such as **Shyamasundar Prabhu and Mother Malati**, **Guru Das**, and many wonderful devotees, went to **London** and established the movement; others went to different places.
- Prabhupada had heard about Europe, had visited London once or twice, and wanted some senior men there. The speaker's **GBC / temple president volunteered to go**.

### The Adventurous Spirit and a White Lie

- Hearing this, an adventurous spirit awoke in the speaker, recalling that in the *Bhagavad-gita* Krishna says, "I am adventure."
  - Real adventure is **not** going out to find the secrets of the material world, pioneering materialistic civilization in some new part of the world, or pioneering space.
  - Real adventure is depending on the Lord, opening new temples, making new devotees, and translating into foreign languages — spreading Krishna consciousness.
- He went to his temple president and asked to come along. The president thought it would be good to have an assistant and asked, "Do you know Europe?"
  - By Krishna's arrangement, the speaker *had* been to Europe a couple of years before as a tourist for a few weeks, so he told "a little bit of a white lie": "I know Europe like the back of my hand — I know London, I know Barcelona, I know Zurich, I know Sydney..."
  - He got caught when the president asked, "Do you know Paris, France?" "No, but I can learn."
- The president agreed on one **condition**: the speaker must get them onto the **same plane as Srila Prabhupada**, who was going from New York City to London in two weeks. This was a challenge to test whether he could be an efficient secretary and not "just a talker."

### The Phone Call to TWA

- Within minutes he was in the office making phone calls. He called **TWA (Trans World Airlines)** and asked for two seats on **flight 103, New York to London**, on (about) July 2nd.
- The representative replied that the flight was already **overbooked**, with **164 people on the waiting list** — impossible.
- He would not accept "no." He began **preaching** to her that he needed to serve his spiritual master, quoting:
  - **Chaitanya-charitamrita** — *"brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krishna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija"* — the living entity transmigrates from time immemorial, from one universe to the next, from one species and body to the next; it is only by great mercy (of guru and Krishna) that he receives the seed of devotion.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 4.34** — *"tad viddhi pranipatena..."* — every intelligent man and woman should approach a guru. "Do you have a guru?"
- The representative was baffled ("Sir, I don't have a clue what you are talking about"), so he changed approach:
  - "Do you love your father?" She said yes — her father raised her, was loving and caring, educated her, put her through school. He replied, "It's something like that. This is my spiritual father; just as your father helped you advance in material life, he's helping me in spiritual life."
  - "Do you believe in God?" "Yes, I'm a good Christian." "Well, just like Jesus, he's helping me."
- After about 20 minutes she excused herself; her supervisor was calling. She came back and said: *"The good Lord must be looking down on you, boy."* While she was with the supervisor, they got **two cancellations**; she told the supervisor there was a nice young boy on the phone who wanted to be with his spiritual father, and they moved them to the front of the line. "So you have the seats."

## On the Plane with Srila Prabhupada

- A couple of weeks later they drove from Detroit to New York. The speaker frames everything that followed as a **pastime** — because everything Prabhupada did was a pastime, and his grace was that unqualified disciples were allowed to participate in his devotional service to the Lord.
- **Prabhupada's analogy:** Prabhupada once said, "Lord Ramachandra conquered Lanka and Ravana with monkeys and bears; in the same way, I am conquering this world with my disciples." It was his grace that allowed unqualified disciples to participate in his pastimes of spreading Krishna consciousness.
- At the airport, security stopped Prabhupada and wanted to see what was in his white bag; the speaker opened the bag, and they were led through. During this time he received **three instructions** about how the living entity becomes covered, and how we awaken our spiritual qualities by engaging in devotional service.

### Preparing Prasadam: The Oranges

- They went up the steps to the plane. It was just the speaker and Prabhupada at first (his secretary **Pradyumna** had not yet boarded, and one other person; four in all). The speaker was very nervous, having had little personal association with Srila Prabhupada. Though Prabhupada was small in stature, he seemed as big as a giant because of his pure devotional qualities.
- Prabhupada looked at him and said, "So, Indradyumna, is there some prasadam?" He froze — it was not his service to arrange Prabhupada's prasadam (that was the secretary's).
- But someone had handed him, at security, a plastic bag with **oranges** and a **little knife** (in those days one could carry a small knife and a plate on the plane).
- With his hand shaking — actually preparing prasadam for his spiritual master — he cut the oranges into sections (there were three oranges), put them on the plate, and handed them to Prabhupada.

### "Maha Maha Prasada": The Orange Peels

- Prabhupada ate, sucking all the juice out and taking the peels, going through every single one, and asked for some water. Then he said, "So, you're going to take prasadam?"
- The speaker looked in the bag — **no oranges left**. In his foolishness, "ever the fool in front of the guru," he said, "Srila Prabhupada, there's no prasadam left — you ate it all."
- Prabhupada looked at him, smiled, and said: *"No, Indradyumna, there's maha maha prasada"* — meaning the orange peels. (Another thing captured in his heart, to remember with eyes closed in difficult times.)
  - In those days, to get any *maha* from Prabhupada was almost impossible — the secretaries and senior sannyasis like **Brahmananda** and "the big boys" came first, and juniors had to come afterward "like a little mouse."
- Overwhelmed and joyful, he picked up an orange peel and put it in his mouth. **The orange rind stings.** Prabhupada had expected him to suck a little juice out and then throw the rinds away; instead the speaker started *eating* the rind — lips burning, tears coming down — but he was in bliss and ate every one.
- He had one left; he looked at Prabhupada, who gestured "What are you doing?" — "Sometimes the mercy of the spiritual master is very sweet; sometimes it's very bitter." So he stopped, took the last orange peel, and put it in his pocket.

### Smuggling the Last Peel; Madhavendra Puri's Sweet Rice

- At London one must **declare** any fruits or vegetables. He wrapped the last peel in cloth after cloth — "there's no way I was going to give up that orange peel to customs and immigration."
- It lasted about **six months**: every day, before honoring his meal, he would take a little bite of Guru Maharaja's *mahaprasada*.
- Later he read in great ecstasy of **Madhavendra Puri**, who received a cup of sweet rice from **Kshira-chora Gopinatha** (delivered by the pujari after the Deity had hidden it under His cloth for him). Madhavendra Puri hid himself elsewhere, not wanting to be recognized as a great devotee; he relished the sweet rice, and over the following months broke down the little clay cup to eat the dried remnants of it.

### The In-Flight Movie: "See Krishna Everywhere"

- Eventually the passengers came, Pradyumna arrived, and three of them sat in the row. The plane took off; after the announcements, everyone settled in and closed the window shades for the **movie**, and the plane became dark.
- While the movie played, the speaker took out his *Bhagavad-gita* (a big edition in those days) — "like a little kid trying to impress his father with his first steps in life." He held the Gita in front of his face, going through verses he knew: **Bhagavad-gita 2.13** (*dehino 'smin yatha dehe...*) and **Bhagavad-gita 18.66** (*sarva-dharman parityajya mam ekam sharanam vraja...*).
- Suddenly he heard **Prabhupada laughing** — Prabhupada was watching the movie (a **Charlie Chaplin** film) and laughing and laughing. It was genuinely funny — simple, pure humor of the old silent movies.
- Afterward the speaker worried, "Uh-oh, we weren't supposed to watch movies." He leaned over to Pradyumna: "You ask Prabhupada why we're watching." "No, you ask." "No, you ask." Pradyumna, being senior and the secretary, went and asked. Prabhupada was very cool and authoritative and gave an amazing answer:
  - *"Krishna is the source of all original things, and Charlie Chaplin's humor is very original."* Prabhupada said he used to watch those silent movies as a little kid. "I was seeing Krishna in the humor of Charlie Chaplin. What were you seeing?"
  - Pradyumna admitted, "Well, not so much." Prabhupada concluded: *"We have to learn to see Krishna everywhere."* The speaker was satisfied by that wonderful answer.

## Arrival in London and the Suitcase

- On landing, the speaker had "**Great Expectations**" (like the childhood book): they would go to the London temple; Prabhupada would lead a kirtan; there would be a wonderful feast; and maybe **Paul McCartney, John Lennon, or Ringo Starr** would be there — after all, the devotees were preaching to the Beatles. He anticipated great ecstasy seeing Prabhupada at the Bury Place temple, and could already hear the kirtan party of hundreds of devotees ("boom, boom, boom").
- Then **Shyamasundar**, who had come to pick them up, reported that one of Prabhupada's suitcases was missing. Someone had to stay behind and wait for it.
- The speaker hid behind a pillar, but heard his name: Shyamasundar suggested, "Prabhupada, maybe that boy Indradyumna can stay behind." Prabhupada said, "Yes, he can stay." His heart dropped, and he started cursing that suitcase.

### Dragging the Suitcase Through London

- He waited about **two hours** until the suitcase was retrieved. It turned out to be the suitcase with **all of Prabhupada's books used for his translation commentaries** — in Sanskrit and Bengali, by **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** and **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** — so it was extremely heavy. Though a strong young man, he could barely lift it; he put it on a cart and dragged it, complaining, "You suitcase, you make me miss Prabhupada's lecture."
- Tired, hot, and depressed after the long flight, it was raining — "typical London weather."
  - **Aside:** "They used to say the sun never set on the British Empire; and because of their sinful activities here in India, now the sun never rises on the British Empire."
- He hailed a taxi in the pouring rain. The driver asked where to; he said, "**Seven Bury Place.**" "Did you have a bad day?" "Yes, I had a really bad day — just get me there really quick." Chanting Krishna, they got stuck in typical London evening traffic; finally arrived as it poured cats and dogs. He paid and got out.

### "Is There Any Prasadam Left?"

- Walking in the door, at least expecting some saved prasadam, he found all the brahmacharis lying on the floor with white paper plates, streaks of fingers going through the ghee, little pieces left of *parikara* / prasadam.
- His first words: "Is there any prasadam left?" They asked, "Who are you?" He explained he was **Indradyumna das** from the Detroit temple, accompanying his temple president to Europe to preach, and had been on the plane with Srila Prabhupada.
- "How was the lecture?" "Oh, you missed the best lecture Prabhupada ever gave — and he led the kirtan, everyone was dancing in ecstasy, and this was the best prasadam we ever had." "Thanks, guys" — "the cherry on top of the cake."
- He asked them to carry the suitcase up to Prabhupada's room; they refused ("we can't even move, we're just relishing all the nectar"). So he took the suitcase himself.

### Entering Prabhupada's Room

- He dragged the suitcase up the steep steps (to about the second or third floor), backwards, "boom, boom."
- He reached a door — black, with brass letters reading "His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta..." Tired and confused, a neophyte, he did not knock; he opened the door, turned around backwards, and dragged the suitcase in.
- Suddenly someone said, "Watch out, you're going to bump into Srila Prabhupada!" He froze, turned around, and there was **Prabhupada right in front of him**, in a beautiful pink and white *gamcha*, getting ready for his massage — "as beautiful as the rising sun," effulgent. He had never seen Prabhupada without his clothes on.
  - **Aside:** One time Prabhupada was crossing the street in London, and a policeman grabbed Shyamasundar and said, "Look, your spiritual master is glowing like the sun." It was obvious even to the non-devotee.

### The Slap and the Benediction

- He immediately fell to the floor, considering he had made a great offense by entering in such an ignoble way, without proper respect and etiquette. He waited on the ground, expecting the worst.
- After a few moments — a **huge, hard slap** right on his back. (Being a young man, he "often wanted to tattoo it on my back — if I ever got a tattoo, it would be that tattoo.") Prabhupada said something, then the speaker found himself at Prabhupada's feet, looking at his lotus toes; Prabhupada turned and walked away.
- Only then did he have the courage to stand. He did not know whether Prabhupada was pleased or displeased, because he had been too nervous to hear what Prabhupada said.
- Prabhupada's servant stood there with his mouth open: "I never saw Prabhupada do that before — he slapped you on the back." The speaker asked what Prabhupada had said. The servant: "Oh, it was so nice, he gave you a nice benediction." "Tell me, what did he say?"
- Prabhupada had said: *"So much endeavor in this material world; when I take you home, back to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime."*

## Concluding Reflections on Service and Endeavor

- (A very senior devotee had told him to stop glorifying himself; he agreed to stop the self-glorification.)
- Those words remain etched in his heart. What was it to carry a suitcase up the stairs for Prabhupada? Yet **Prabhupada appreciated the most insignificant service**.
- When devotees go through great endeavor to spread his teachings, it is not easy.

## The Final Story: Washing Prabhupada's Feet in Paris

- Prabhupada visited them several times in **Paris**, and this is a memory the speaker cherishes in particular.
- Whenever Prabhupada would come, they would renovate and paint the temple, the *didis* would have new outfits, etc. — all of which required a lot of **Lakshmi** (money).
- So the Paris temple president organized a big **book distribution marathon**, with this incentive: the first two devotees to win the marathon would get to **wash Prabhupada's feet** at his initial reception.

### The Three-Month Metro Marathon

- The speaker was determined to do anything to get the honor of washing His Divine Grace's feet. It was a **three-month marathon**, and he and his godbrother **Bhugarbha Prabhu** worked together as a team.
- For three months they went out every morning into the **Paris metro (underground)**, entering when it was still dark to catch the first passengers on their way to work, and did not come out until nine or ten at night. They took their lunch and slept on the metro train as it went around the city.
- For three months they **did not see the light of day** — never saw a sunrise or a sunset — so eager were they for the special service of touching His Divine Grace's lotus feet and getting some remnants.
- **Three sources of great mercy** they had heard of: the **remnants of the fruit** of the spiritual master; the **dust from his feet**; and the **water that has washed his feet**. Feeling quite fallen and new and wanting to advance, they aimed for the foot-water.

### Winning, and the "Transcendental Plan"

- They won the marathon (he declined to say who came first). They then had to decide who would wash the feet and who would hold the bowl; they flipped a coin, and the speaker got to **pour the water**, while Bhugarbha held the bowl.
- They procured a **huge silver bowl** and an unusually large **pitcher**. The temple president remarked, "That's a lot of water to wash Prabhupada's feet."
- They had a "transcendental plan," inspired by the saying "**beg, borrow, or steal to get the nectar of the holy name.**"

### The Reception and Collecting the Charanamrita

- Prabhupada came; it was a wonderful reception. They waited by the **Vyasasana**. When Prabhupada sat, the speaker carefully placed his feet into the big silver bowl — even Prabhupada had big eyes seeing how huge the bowl was — and they put rose petals on his feet.
- The **guru-puja** was going on simultaneously, so the devotees were engaged. The speaker poured the water very slowly from the big pitcher; halfway through the guru-puja the bowl had filled up, and they had to pull Prabhupada's feet out of what was almost "a little pond of water." They dried his feet.
- On a signal — "one, two, three, go" — they took the bowl and disappeared into the crowd, up the stairs to a first-floor office. Instead of throwing the water onto the devotees, they **kept it**.
- Some brahmacharis (about ten) saw what they were doing and started following. They ran. They had earlier made a **copy of the office key** without the temple president's knowledge (and misplaced his original key). They opened the office, went in, and slammed the door. The brahmacharis ran into the door: "Let us in! Share the nectar! Share the nectar!"

### Relishing the Nectar

- Inside, leaning against the banging door, they had a huge bowl of liquid nectar — **Prabhupada's charanamrita**. They had also indulged in buying **two silver cups**, reasoning that what was going into them was worthy of silver. He polished the cups.
- The kirtan roared downstairs and the brahmacharis pounded the door, but the two felt they "had all the time in the world" — they had worked hard for three months and received this special grace. Bhugarbha was a very erudite, scholarly devotee, and they had discussed this matter many times; they sat very calmly.
- "May I do the honors?" — he poured into Bhugarbha's cup, then his own, and they looked at each other and said "**Srila Prabhupada ki jaya!**" Then they simply dipped their cups in and relished.
- He did not know how it was possible for a human being to consume that much liquid, but they could not move afterward. At the bottom were **two drops**; they split the drop in half with their fingers ("*santushti*").
- He believes it is only because of that mercy that he still finds himself in this Krishna consciousness movement, allowed to render service — "although I can't explain why I'm still here."

### The Aftermath

- They sat reflecting on their good fortune while the brahmacharis kept calling, "Let us in, we want some of the mercy."
- Finally the speaker took the key and opened the door; the brahmacharis came tumbling in: "Where is it? Where is the nectar?" He turned to Bhugarbha, "What nectar?" Bhugarbha: "Haven't got a clue." The brahmacharis saw the bowl was empty and looked at the big smiles on their faces.
- The **temple president** walked in: "What's going on in here?" "Nothing — we're just getting ready to go down for the lecture." "Get down there immediately." They walked downstairs, and Prabhupada delivered a wonderful lecture.
- In this way they received some **exceptional mercy** from His Divine Grace.
