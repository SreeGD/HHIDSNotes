# You are not the body

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 7 · Festivals and Kirtans · 47:59

## Summary

Speaking on Bhagavad-gita 2.18 from Srila Prabhupada's Bhagavad-gita As It Is, Indradyumna Swami frames the class as a 'refresher course' or 'back to square one' on the foundational truth that we are not the material body but the eternal spirit soul. He distinguishes jnana (mere memorized knowledge) from vijnana (realized knowledge), stressing that truly realizing 'I am not the body' takes many years or lifetimes and, once realized, frees one from all disturbance of mind and senses. Although Prabhupada calls the body 'unimportant' to shake up materialists, the Swami clarifies this must not be carried too far: the human body is precious because its intelligence lets us understand God and revive our eternal relationship with Him, so we maintain it in service just as one cares for a car that carries one to work and home. He illustrates the soul's power with the sun, which though tiny illuminates the entire vast universe, just as the minute soul in the region of the heart spreads consciousness throughout the whole body, proving spirit is superior to matter. Addressing why such a powerful soul falls under maya, he gives two answers: the jiva is tatastha-shakti (marginal energy) poised between the material and spiritual worlds, and when the soul becomes rebellious the Lord places it under maya, dramatized by Prabhupada's boyhood 'playing hooky' story of the man Damodar who dragged truant boys to school by rope. He warns of maya's two great tricks, thinking 'I am the body' and thinking 'I am God,' and in the Q&A explains that among the nine processes of devotional service, hearing and chanting are most effective, because a sleeping (conditioned) soul can only be awakened by sound, as shown by the potency of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra to protect a devotee from every temptation.

## Key Points

- The lecture is on Bhagavad-gita 2.18: the material body is perishable, but the soul is indestructible, immeasurable and eternal; realizing 'I am not the body' takes years or lifetimes and, once realized, ends all agitation of mind and senses (distinction between jnana and vijnana).
- Story of Sanatana Goswami, whose body was covered in oozing sores, deciding to give up his life under the wheels of the Jagannatha Ratha-yatra cart (origin of the English word 'Juggernaut'); Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who lovingly embraced him anyway, forbade it, saying the body now belonged to Him and He had plans for it in spreading the movement.
- The body is called 'unimportant' only to correct materialists; in fact the human form is most valuable because its God-given intelligence lets us understand the soul, God, and our eternal relationship with Him, so we care for it in service just as we care for a car that carries us to work and home.
- Sun analogy: as one tiny sun lights up the entire vast universe, the minute soul situated in the region of the heart spreads consciousness through the whole body, showing spirit is more powerful than matter (also illustrated by the immense body of an elephant moved by one tiny spiritual spark).
- Two explanations for how the powerful soul becomes entangled in maya: the jiva is tatastha-shakti (marginal energy) between the material and spiritual energies, and the rebellious soul is placed under maya by the Lord (Prabhupada's 'playing hooky' story of Damodar roping truant boys back to school); the guru-and-tree story teaches that maya does not hold us, we hold on to maya, and must simply let go.
- Maya's two great tricks are the thoughts 'I am the body' and 'I am God'; Prabhupada's example of the yogi who glimpses the Supersoul and mistakes it for himself shows why a bona fide Vaishnava guru is needed to become a bhakti-yogi, the highest of all yogis.
- Of the nine processes of devotional service (shravanam, kirtanam, etc.), hearing and chanting are supreme, since a sleeping/conditioned soul is awakened only by sound (the burglar analogy); this potency of the holy name is shown by Pariksit Maharaj hearing Srimad Bhagavatam, the Shikshashtakam and 'kirtanad eva krishnasya' verse, and Haridasa Thakura, who by chanting 300,000 (three lakh) names daily converted both a hired prostitute and Maya Devi herself into his disciples, unlike Hercules who had to be tied to the mast to resist the temptresses' songs.

## Scriptural References

- Bhagavad-gita 2.18 (antavanta ime deha nityasyoktah saririnah / anasino 'prameyasya tasmad yudhyasva bharata) — the main verse read and explained: the material body of the eternal, indestructible, immeasurable soul is sure to perish, therefore fight.
- Vedanta-sutra — cited via Prabhupada's purport: the living entity is qualified as 'light,' being part and parcel of the supreme light (as the soul's presence maintains the body, so sunlight maintains the universe).
- Bhagavad-gita 2.20 (na jayate mriyate va kadacit... na hanyate hanyamane sarire) — paraphrased: the soul is unborn, undying, primeval, and is not slain when the body is slain.
- Bhagavad-gita 9.22 (yoga-kshemam vahamy aham) — quoted for the promise that the Lord provides whatever His fully surrendered devotee needs and protects what he has.
- Bhagavad-gita 6.47 — cited for the teaching that of all yogis, one who worships Krishna in loving devotion is the highest.
- Bhagavad-gita 7.1 (tac chrinu — 'just hear') — Krishna's instruction to Arjuna to hear from Him with an attached mind and without doubt ('Tata Srinu... this Gita is enough').
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.1.1 — referenced for Krishna as all-knowing and self-effulgent (abhijnah svarat), present in the heart of Sanatana Goswami; and for the 'first verse' description of maya's bewildering illusion (seeing water on land and land on water, tejo-vari-mridam) that can confuse even demigods.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23 (shravanam kirtanam vishnoh smaranam pada-sevanam / archanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam) — the nine processes of devotional service, with hearing and chanting singled out as most effective.
- Siksastakam verse 2 (namnam akari bahudha nija-sarva-shaktih... durdaivam idrisham ihajani nanuragah) — 'the holy name alone can render all benediction... hundreds and millions of names like Krishna, Govinda... no hard and fast rules,' and the aspiring devotee's lament of having no attraction (speaker refers to it as 'Siksastakam has stated').
- Srimad Bhagavatam 12.3.51 (kaler dosa-nidhe rajann... kirtanad eva krsnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet) — 'this age is an ocean of faults, but there is one saving grace': liberation simply by chanting Krishna's name (the speaker attributes it to the 11th Canto).
- Vedas (paraphrased) — 'by sound one is conditioned and by sound one becomes liberated,' cited for the potency of transcendental sound / the hearing process.
- Hare Krishna maha-mantra (Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare) — chanted throughout and recommended as the sufficient means to awaken the soul and resist all temptation of maya.
