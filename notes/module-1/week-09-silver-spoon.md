# Silver Spoon

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 9 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:05:42

## Summary

Indradyumna Swami recounts his early days of coming to Krishna consciousness as a young man named Bhakta Brian in Ann Arbor around 1969-70, built around the famous 'silver spoon' story from the book Radha-Damodara Vilasa. He first heard of Krishna at a museum's Rasa-lila paintings, then met four sannyasis doing sankirtan on a campus lawn (Vishnujana Swami, Brahmananda, Gargamuni, and Subala Swami), who stayed with him, gave him his first taste of prasadam, and lectured to just him and his wife morning, noon, and night. When he challenged Vishnujana Swami's attachment to a silver spoon (the spoon of an early ISKCON deity of Krishna that Srila Prabhupada had lovingly given him), Vishnujana taught that we should not become detached from everything but rather detached from matter and attached to Krishna; the sannyasis quietly departed, leaving the spoon with a note quoting Srimad Bhagavatam's analogy of sticks and leaves joined and scattered by a river's waves, and Brian became deeply attached to it as his lesson in spiritual attachment. He narrates further turning points: being 'fired' from his gardening job while praying on Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura's appearance day (which he took as the grandfather-guru's mercy freeing him for the temple), Srila Prabhupada's Detroit visit where the words 'you are not these bodies' penetrated his heart, and a personal letter plus a ten-dollar book-fund exchange with Prabhupada. Finally he travels with Prabhupada to London, receiving Prabhupada's dhoti as maha-prasad along with the personal instruction 'preach boldly and have faith in the holy names.' Threaded through humorous episodes (Subala's shoes miraculously provided, Brahmananda eating six plates, the burning orange peel, threatening a rude customs officer, and Prabhupada laughing at a Charlie Chaplin film) run the teachings that even a moment's association with a pure devotee gives a higher taste, that pleasing the spiritual master pleases Krishna, and that loving devotional exchanges make it easy to give up material attachments. The class closes with Prabhupada slapping his back at the London temple and telling him, 'So much endeavor in this material world, but when I take you back to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime.'

## Key Points

- The silver-spoon lesson from Vishnujana Swami: the goal is not to renounce everything but to become detached from the material world and attached to Krishna; a gift from a Vaishnava (the deity's own spoon that Prabhupada gave him) reminds one of Krishna, so such spiritual attachment is favorable.
- The verse 'lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi hoy' — even a moment's association with a pure devotee of the Lord awards the higher taste and can fix one on the path of Krishna consciousness, just as a moment's association with a materialist can deviate one.
- The departure note quoted Srimad Bhagavatam (Eleventh Canto): souls are brought together and again separated by the force of time and past pious and impious activities, like sticks, twigs, and leaves joined and scattered by the waves of a river.
- Hearing transcendental truth ('you are not these bodies') and the Hare Krishna maha-mantra from the lips of a pure devotee (shuddha-bhakta) has a uniquely powerful, penetrating effect that uproots material desires.
- Prabhupada's Detroit teaching on the yoga-bhrashta: a strict devotee who does not finish the course of Krishna consciousness within his lifetime takes birth immediately in a family of devotees, so children of devotees are very special and should be raised carefully — whereas one who leaves for material desires must first pass through the heavenly planets.
- Take the spiritual master's instruction as one's very life and soul — Prabhupada gave his dhoti as maha-prasad with the order 'preach boldly and have faith in the holy names,' just as Prabhupada himself took Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura's instructions (spread Krishna consciousness in English, print books) as life and soul, as noted in Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's Bhagavad-gita purport.
- Pleasing the spiritual master pleases Krishna, and then one's life is perfect — illustrated through the loving exchanges (the ten-dollar book-fund offering, Prabhupada's letter, the maha-prasad oranges where the disciple acts a 'fool,' and Prabhupada seeing Krishna as the source of all originality even in Charlie Chaplin's humor).
