# Chanting is our life and soul

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 2 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:24:24

**Themes:** holy name · japa · sankirtana · humility · yuga-dharma · higher taste

## Summary

Given on the second day of a devotional festival (the Nama Ruchi Festival at the Phoenix Jagannath temple, 2019), this lecture by Indradyumna Swami glorifies the chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra as the yuga-dharma, the prescribed and simplest yet most sublime process for awakening dormant love for Krishna in the age of Kali. He grounds the teaching in the famous 'harer nama' verse, explaining that we should chant humbly like a beggar begging for mercy, in the mood of the third verse of Siksastakam ('trinad api sunicena... amanina manadena'). He recounts his friendship with an advanced Gaudiya Vaishnava sadhu named Fakir Mohan, who lectured for ten years on a single verse about the holy name and spent two hours in preparatory prayers, offering arati to his beads, before chanting japa slowly and with attachment. He stresses that the goal of japa is not to 'finish rounds' but to develop taste, character and love, and that one should chant japa softly, kirtan loudly, and harinama-sankirtana at the top of one's lungs, as Haridasa Thakura did. Congregational chanting is powerful because many voices praying together, like a crowd petitioning a mayor, more surely attract the Lord's attention, and love is a two-way street in which Krishna reciprocates. Through stories of Narottama Dasa Thakura receiving prema through Mother Padmavati, Bhaktivinoda Thakura's assurance that the saints hear and reciprocate our prayers, the woodsman in the kalpavriksha forest, the three sadhus questioning Vyasadeva about the best age, and the 'footprints in the sand,' he shows that Krishna is always present as the Supersoul, more eager for our return than we are, carrying us through our difficulties. He concludes that in Kali-yuga one need only add chanting to one's life to attain perfection, because by the higher taste (param drstva nivartate) the lower tastes are given up, and urges devotees to keep chanting, avoid offenses and criticism, take shelter of the association of Vaishnavas, and say thank you to Krishna through the yajna of the holy name.

## Chanting as the Simplest Way to Awaken Devotion

- Welcome back to the second day of this festival of devotion, which we are celebrating in the most appropriate way — the easiest, simplest way to awaken our devotion for Krishna.
- Just as in our yajnas we say **"yajne vai vishnuh"** to make it clear that our sacrifices are meant to please not the demigods but the Supreme Personality of Godhead, **Svayam Bhagavan Krishna** — so our love is awakened for Vishnu, for Krishna, for Radha-Shyama, through the chanting of the Hare Krishna Maha-mantra.
- This is made clear in the **Brihan-naradiya Purana**. (In spiritual circles, when you speak you must always give a shloka to show your statement is authorized.)
  - — **"harer nama harer nama harer namaiva kevalam / kalau nasty eva nasty eva nasty eva gatir anyatha"**: in the age of Kali the simplest yet most sublime process of achieving devotion for Krishna is by chanting Krishna's sweet holy names.
- Krishna is hiding in our hearts, covered over by so many mundane qualities. Here we are doing the **yuga-dharma** — not only chanting but also talking about it — and we can talk unlimitedly about the holy names, because their glories are unlimited.

## Fakir Mohan: Chanting Like a Beggar

- The speaker had a friend (senior "by many light years"), a Gaudiya Vaishnava named **Fakir Mohan**. His Guru Maharaj gave him the name "Fakir," meaning a holy person or beggar — one who begs not for material sustenance but for **mercy**.
- He was a very advanced devotee who offered obeisances to every Vaishnava he saw, never expecting honor for himself but seeing everyone as more advanced than himself.
  - — **Siksastakam** (verse 3, "trnad api sunicena… amanina manadena kirtaniyah sada harih"): only in that humble consciousness can one actually chant the holy name like a beggar.
- When we chant, we chant like a beggar saying "mercy, mercy, mercy." You are a beggar when you lack something and have no means to acquire it except mercy.
  - In India: **"bakshish."** Prabhupada told the young devotees, "You should give to the beggars — not the ones who approach and disturb you, but those sitting humbly, depending on God's mercy" (the widows of Vrindavan, the sadhus).

## Speaking for Ten Years on One Verse

- Fakir Mohan was affiliated with ISKCON, greatly appreciated ISKCON devotees, and appreciated Prabhupada's contribution in taking the holy names to the Western countries. He gave class every morning and every evening — on the **same verse for ten years.**
- Precedent: **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakur** once spoke on the **first verse of the Srimad-Bhagavatam for one month**, because everything is contained in that verse.
  - Prabhupada said all our philosophy is contained in the **First Canto** of the Bhagavatam — if you were stranded on a lonely desert island with only the First Canto, you could become Krishna conscious. That is what Prabhupada brought when he came to America: the First Canto.
- The location: **Dhaka** (in present-day Bangladesh — formerly East Pakistan, before that East Bengal). Many of Mahaprabhu's pastimes took place in East Bengal:
  - Rupa and Sanatana Goswamis were born there; Haridas Thakur had many pastimes there; it is the birthplace of Advaita Acharya.
  - It is unfortunate that the British just drew a pencil line down the map to partition Hindu and Muslim.
  - Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati declared Dhaka the **"second Vrindavan"** because of so many Radha-Krishna temples, sadhus, Vaishnavas, Vaishnavis, and big festivals (mahotsavas) — non-different from Vrindavan. Today all one hears there is the call to the mosque.
- The **Vyasasana** where he spoke for one month is still there.

## Praying at the Holy Places: Keturi and Narottama Das Thakur

- The speaker went to Bangladesh wanting to visit **Keturi**, the birthplace and pastime-place of **Narottama Das Thakur**, one of his great heroes — a very big preacher yet a deep *rasika* devotee. "I want to be just like him, in a humble way — a big preacher, but understanding the deeper mellows of pure devotional service."
  - Prabhupada wanted us both to **distribute books** and to **cry when we chant Hare Krishna** — we have to work on both.
- At the Vyasasana in Dhaka he placed his head and, with the permission of his spiritual master, requested something from Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakur — because one cannot jump over the authorities; we must go through the authorized line.
  - Prabhupada once said, "You cannot read the books of my spiritual master — you read my books, then you will understand" — not that it is forbidden, but to get a clear understanding.
  - His prayers to Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati: that when he gives class his words would somehow touch the audience so they would want to become more Krishna conscious; to become a good disciple of his Guru Maharaj; that all obstacles on the path of devotion be removed; to be a qualified speaker; and that maybe in some distant life he would be awarded **one tear gliding down his cheek** from *nam-ruchi* — attachment and taste for chanting. These are far-distant goals, "the far horizon."
- Encouragements about slow, steady progress:
  - Confucius: "the longest journey begins with the first step." Better to move slowly in the right direction than to run hell-bent for leather in the wrong direction.
  - The **Brahma-samhita** says we make progress in **increments** — inch by inch, "weensy-beensy" — but permanent. In material life you make huge leaps and bounds, but when you die (German "kaputski") it's finished, all gone.
  - Holy places are full of transcendental energy. In **Vrindavan**, whatever devotional service you do is magnified a thousand times; these temples are like "satellite temples." You must plan to go to Braj (and Mayapur) as many times as you can in your lifetime, because you can make **quantum leaps** there. (Neil Armstrong on the moon: "a small step for man, but a great leap forward for mankind.")

## The Kalpa-Vriksha Tree and the Prema of Narottama

- **How Mahaprabhu deposited His prema for Narottama Das Thakur:** He left His *prema* with **Mother Padmavati** (the Padma River personified). He called her forth from the river and said, "Hold this prema for a young boy named Narottama. After My disappearance he will be born and come to bathe in the river; when he bathes, give him this prema, because it is a great mission to continue the Sankirtan movement."
  - You can only be a participant in the Sankirtan movement if you are empowered by **Gaura-shakti** — the mercy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- **The original "second generation"** of the movement was the trio: **Narottama, Srinivasa Acharya, and Syamananda** — the speaker's favorite heroes. ("Sorry guys, you're the 54th generation.")
- As a young boy Narottama was forbidden to bathe in rivers because an astrologer said that if he ever entered a river he would go mad. But **Lord Nityananda (Nithai)** appeared to him in a dream: "Wake up, young man, go down and bathe in the Padma River." Though his mother said no, he went and bathed, and Mother Padmavati gave him the liquid prema.
  - Aside: the speaker recently named a disciple's newborn in Mayapur **Premamrita** — after Radha-kunda, whose water Rupa Goswami describes as *premamrita*, liquid love, liquid nectar. Narottama was bathed in this premamrita and went **mad with love of God** ("You're going to go mad — go mad with love of God").
- Narottama bathed under a tree overhanging the Padma River — a **Kalpa-vriksha (desire tree)**, still standing, 400–500 years old, which witnessed that pastime.
  - The speaker went there with **Radhanath Swami**; they chanted japa under the tree and performed arati to it and offered it a big feast (108 preparations at noon), with decorations.
  - Suddenly a storm came, and a big branch crashed down right in front of the speaker ("would have been good if it hit me on the head"). The pujari ran for the branch, but the speaker put it on his chadar, saying in Polish **"Dziękuję bardzo"** (thank you very much), and quoting the Bhagavad-gita on "mercy which comes of its own accord." He carried the branch back to Europe, where a disciple who is a woodcarver carved japa beads out of it. He likes to chant japa on them — feeling very close to Narottama Das Thakur and his mercy.

## Why the Glories of the Holy Name Are Inexhaustible

- Just as Bhaktisiddhanta spoke one month on the first verse, Fakir Mohan spoke ten years on **one verse about the glories of the Holy Name** — and the speaker, attending his classes every year, never once heard him repeat himself. Why? Because the glories of the holy names are unlimited.
- **Ananta Shesha** (an expansion of Lord Balaram): one of His sacred sevas is that at every moment He is calling out a **new name of Krishna** — since time immemorial, beyond all the creations and dissolutions of the cosmos. Time is infinite; with our small brains we cannot conceive of infinity. Every moment a new name, each indicating a particular characteristic of Krishna. (The speaker started a website for donations called "Sacred Seva.")
- Krishna is unlimited in **name, fame, form, pastimes, and paraphernalia.**
  - — **"nama cintamanih krsnas caitanya-rasa-vigrahah"** (Padma Purana / Chaitanya-charitamrita): the name, fame, form, paraphernalia, and pastimes of Krishna are all unlimited.
  - He is the Supersoul; we are the jiva soul, and our pastimes are very limited (get up, take a shower, go to work, come back, go to sleep). Krishna's pastimes are unlimited **and** all-attractive.
  - Even **Cupid** (French "le cupidon," material love personified, shooting arrows into everyone's heart) is himself attracted to Krishna — **Madana-Mohana**, the enchanter of Cupid. And Krishna is attracted to **Radharani** — **Madana-Mohana-Mohini**. She is Bhakti Devi; she holds the prema for Krishna.

## Fakir Mohan's Way of Chanting Japa

- Every day, three hours morning and three hours night in Vrindavan (after Kartik), the speaker would listen to Fakir Mohan, who never repeated himself. This is **Gaura-shakti**; this is **atma-nivedanam**.
  - — The **nine processes of devotional service** (Prahlad Maharaj, **Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.5.23**): *"sravanam kirtanam visnoh smaranam pada-sevanam / arcanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam"* — culminating in full surrender, atma-nivedanam.
- For such a pure-hearted soul, he becomes an **instrument**.
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 11.33** ("nimitta-matram bhava savyasacin"): Krishna tells Arjuna to be but an instrument in His service; don't let your false ego get in the way.
  - "Lust means for you; love means for Me," Krishna says. If you have prema, bhakti, love for Krishna, He will use you as an instrument in His service — what could be more wonderful?
- Fakir Mohan had such a pure heart because he was always chanting Hare Krishna from boyhood. He joined the math (monastery) at five, having run away from home. When his parents came to find him at fourteen he said, "I don't know you. Krishna is my mother, Krishna is my father" — quoting the verse by Bhaktivinoda Thakur ("Krishna is my father, Krishna my mother, my soul"). So Gaura-shakti flowed from his heart.

## Preparing to Chant: Two Hours Before Touching the Beads

- To chant japa with Fakir Mohan, you first had to sit for about an hour while he recited many prayers about the glories of the Holy Name, just to get into the right mood.
  - This is like preparing before the deity curtain opens in the morning: we prepare ourselves because we are about to have God's darshan — and, more importantly, God is going to see us. (Especially Jagannath, who doesn't blink — He doesn't want to miss anything.)
- We think we're doing well to have ten rounds done in an hour, but it is not about **finishing** rounds; it is about **chanting** a round. More comes out of the bead bag than out of this finger: purification, good character, attachment to Krishna, and love of Krishna.
- Fakir Mohan's routine: before even touching his bead bag he chanted for one hour, so many prayers, until his hands shook and his eyes watered. Then he placed his beads on the altar and offered a **full arati to his beads.** Then another 45 minutes chanting prayers glorifying japa. After two hours he would finally pick up his beads and chant in full concentration, slowly: "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare…"
- The speaker learned from him and now takes three hours to chant his rounds. "We don't want to *finish* our rounds — we're upset when we have to finish." Like a child playing outside when it gets dark and mom calls him in for dinner ("No, I don't want to come home") — that is the mood we should have when we finish our japa.

## "Why Minimum Sixteen?" — Chanting Beyond the Minimum

- At initiation you take vows: the four regulative principles and a **minimum of sixteen rounds.**
- **The godsister and Prabhupada:** one of the speaker's godsisters was initiated in front of Srila Prabhupada in America. She vowed no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling, "and I promise to chant a minimum of sixteen rounds every day." Prabhupada leaned over and said, **"Why minimum sixteen?"** — and she started crying.
  - Like the Sunday feast: is there a limit on how many sweets? When you're young, your metabolism is strong, and you work hard for Krishna, you want "another one, another one." (When **Brahmananda Swami** joined the movement, Prabhupada said he could eat 27 chapatis — he was a big man.)
- **Softly, loudly, and at the top of your lungs:** Fakir Mohan chanted japa softly. Japa is meant to be chanted fairly softly — but at least loud enough that the person next to you can hear, because sometimes you have to correct others ("What are you chanting? Listen to yourself"). So:
  - **Japa** — chant softly.
  - **Kirtan** — chant loudly.
  - **Harinam Sankirtan** — chant at the top of your lungs.
- **Haridas Thakur** chanted 300,000 names of Krishna daily: 100,000 softly, 100,000 loudly, and 100,000 at the top of his lungs — so that all other living entities could hear.
  - When Mahaprabhu asked him how all the fallen souls, even the bushes and creepers, would be delivered, Haridas said, **"By the loud chanting of the holy names, my Lord."** This is the devotee's vision — more concerned about others' deliverance than his own. We are already delivered and on the path; but what about all the people in the cities of Kali-yuga who have never heard even one syllable?
  - "Krishna" means He who delivers you from **samsara** — the repetition of birth and death; this is one description of the meaning of Krishna's name.

## Congregational Chanting: One Voice, Many Hearts

- We begin the day with japa, then come together for guru-puja, then open the deities with kirtan. It is good because it is **one voice, an assembly requesting mercy** — it is easier for the one you appeal to to hear an assembly than a lone voice crying in the wilderness.
- When many chant together very loudly, Radharani says, "It's Durban" — the farthest forest, the farthest place from where we are — "Krishna, what are they singing? Some rock and roll? Some punk music? No, they're singing your name." And then He responds.
- **Municipality analogy:** if everyone has a complaint (say, the roads aren't being paved) and you stand alone outside the mayor's office where a big meeting is going on, windows closed — no one hears you. But if two or three hundred thousand people come together chanting "We want the roads paved!" the city council responds. That is **congregational chanting.**
- — **Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.5.32** ("krsna-varnam tvisakrsnam sangopangastra-parsadam / yajnaih sankirtana-prayair yajanti hi su-medhasah"): people chanted Hare Krishna even before Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, but mainly sadhus alone in the forest. Mahaprabhu told all the sadhus of Kali-yuga to "all get together now" — the idea being that we are one voice, many hearts begging, "mercy, mercy, mercy." Don't think Krishna doesn't hear you.

## Do the Saints of Yore Hear Us? (And "Be Careful What You Ask For")

- A disciple once asked **Bhaktivinoda Thakur:** when we pray to the saints of yore — Valmiki, Agastya Muni, Kapiladev, so many sadhus and incarnations who may have appeared millions of years ago — do they hear us? (The *Bhagavata Purana* — "purana" means very old, very ancient — goes back so far that people have no memory of it.)
- The speaker identified with the question, because he prays to Prahlad Maharaj (a great devotee of Lord Nrisimhadeva) and to Valmiki before reading the Ramayana, wondering, "Did he ever hear little old me on the earth planet?" Reading a book of Bhaktivinoda Thakur's sayings, he found the question on one page and the answer on the next — and savored the moment for ten minutes before turning the page.
  - **The answer:** Yes, they hear you. And not only do they hear your prayers — they **reciprocate** with your prayers. "Just be careful what you ask for."
- **The woodsman story** (told by Prabhupada). A woodsman lived on the outskirts of Brajmandala, cutting and selling trees — hard work. One day he wandered into Brajmandala (the twelve forests of Vrindavan — "Vrindavan" means Vrinda's forest, given to Srimati Radharani). Having cut ordinary trees all his life, now he was cutting **Kalpa-vriksha** (desire trees) — not fairy tale or mythology but axiomatic fact: trees that fulfill your desires to serve Krishna (one tree can give pears, apples, watermelons, passion fruit — and a silver plate to serve them on).
  - Tired, he thought, "I wish these trees would just chop themselves up into a big pile." Because he was praying in a Kalpa-vriksha forest, it happened — the whole forest.
  - "Far out!" So he wished all his friends were there having a big party drinking wine — and they appeared, drinking wine. He wished for **kulfi** (ice cream in Hindi) — it appeared. He wished for rice, dal, and chapatis stretching for one kilometer — it appeared.
  - Then it grew dark, and he thought, "This is a dangerous time; surely a tiger will appear and eat me, I should be careful." A tiger appeared and ate him. End of story.
  - The moral: be careful what you wish for in Krishna consciousness — be careful what you ask for when you chant.

## What We Actually Ask For: Not Even Liberation

- We ask only for one thing — not dharma, artha, kama, or moksha; **not even moksha.**
- **Prabhupada and the hellish planets:** a godbrother once asked Prabhupada (an improper question to a spiritual master), "Where are you going to go when you die?" Prabhupada replied, "When I die, I plan to go to the **hellish planets and continue preaching Krishna consciousness.**" The speaker's respect for Prabhupada kept piling up — even the way Prabhupada drank from a cup (pouring the water in without touching his lips, seemingly against the laws of nature), and the way he played kartals, which devotees still see in dreams ("I see his hands").
- We want not even liberation — only some simple service to Krishna in the spiritual sky.
- In kirtan, we may not be thinking like that (the mind can be your best friend or your worst enemy). But others in the kirtan party *are* thinking, "Mercy, give me seva, give me service to your lotus feet" — and their prayers are more powerful than yours, because **spirit is more powerful than matter**; a spiritual desire is more powerful than a material desire.
  - We wonder how to overcome the lust, anger, and greed burning in the heart — the answer is to **awaken a spiritual desire.** If you don't have a desire, Prabhupada said, **"desire to desire that desire."** Just desire to be a good disciple, a good devotee, a good resident of Vrindavan.
- How do you get there? **"harer nama, harer nama, harer namaiva kevalam"** — say it three times, because in Vedic culture repeating something three times gives the emphasis. In the age of Kali, sixteen rounds — Prabhupada said in a Bhagavatam purport that this is the **most important instruction of the spiritual master.** Mahaprabhu said: come together and do congregational chanting, and then take the chanting to the people.

## The Holy Name Is a Person

- How do you get mercy there? Because **Sri Nam gives you mercy.** The Name is a person — Nam Prabhu, Sri Nam Prabhu — not just a sound.
  - Prabhupada said that when you chant Hare Krishna, **Krishna is dancing on the tip of your tongue.** He is so close — always close.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakur** said that from time to time an aspiring sadhaka must step away from active duties, go to a quiet place, and just chant the holy names — detach from everything, sit alone, and let the world go by.
  - Don't worry about the world; it will be here with all its imperfection. Krishna takes care of it, fulfilling everyone's nasty desires, hoping they will come to their senses one day. You go to a quiet place and pray.
- The same is in the Bible. — **Matthew 6:6:** Jesus says, "Whenever you pray, go to your room, close the door, and pray to your father in private."
  - Bhaktivinoda Thakur was raised as a practicing Christian (English school), but became a deeper seeker; he searched **seventeen years** for a copy of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* by Krishnadas Kaviraj Goswami and finally found an old palm-leaf manuscript — because nothing compares to Vaishnava philosophy. (Aside: he is Lalita in the spiritual sky.)

## Then We Go on the Street: Love Is a Two-Way Street

- After chanting privately, we go out on the street, because Sri Nam is a person and His glory is meant to be spread everywhere. Just as you like people to praise you (in school the teacher praises the best student), Krishna — the Supreme person, but without any material contamination — loves to hear your sentiments and loves to return them.
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 4.11** ("ye yatha mam prapadyante tams tathaiva bhajamy aham"): "As all surrender to Me, I reward them accordingly." Love is a two-way street.
- **Hanuman's example:** if you want the embrace Hanuman got, you must have the bhakti of Hanuman. Hanuman brought the herbs from the Himalayas to cure Lakshman, jumped over the ocean to Lanka, had his tail set on fire and burned the city, found Sita, and returned.
  - When he reported back, he said, **"Found I, Sita"** — not "I found Sita." Vishwanath Chakravarti Thakur explains: if he had begun with "I," it would be all about him, and meanwhile Ram wouldn't know whether Sita had been found. "Details are a sign of love" (Prabhupada). By saying "Found — Sita — I," he gave Ram the crucial news fastest.
- When we chant all together with one voice, it is not just the loudness of the kirtan but the **intensity of many devotees praying together** that attracts the Lord's attention. So, since love is a two-way street, to have that reciprocation we must eventually chant with love.
- Different relationships bring different reciprocation: the gopis love Krishna in intimacy (dancing, kissing); a servant's relationship gets a handshake, "good boy." But when the service is great, the master may give the servant an embrace or a slap on the back.
  - Prabhupada once slapped the speaker on the back and said, "So much endeavor in this material world — when I take you back to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime." (Whatever the spiritual master says is pertinent to all disciples and grand-disciples.)
  - Ram said to Hanuman, "You saved Sita, you burned down Lanka, you brought the herbs — what can I give you?" Hanuman said, **"Can you give me a hug?"** Spiritual life is that simple. Ram embraced him, and Hanuman was completely satisfied (*santushti*).

## The Best Age: Three Sadhus Question Vyasadeva

- How do we attain the stage of reciprocation with Krishna? It is amazing that one can attain pure prema in the age of Kali.
- **The story of the three sadhus** arguing over which is the best age for spiritual progress (long white beards, big rudraksha beads, thin, sitting by the Ganges):
  - The **first** said **Satya-yuga** — the golden age of enlightenment, where 99.999% of people are spiritually advanced and need only a little meditation for 300,000 years.
    - *(Aside on "late":* in Vrindavan/Braj, saying someone is "late" means he has passed away. The speaker once asked about a devotee friend near the Radha-Gopinath temple and found his room empty; the cloth merchant Satya Narayan — the one with six fingers who does all the devotees' sewing — explained the man was "gone." The speaker cried and cried, and Satya Narayan consoled him.*)*
  - The **second** said **Treta-yuga** — a shorter time; you do some agnihotras (fire sacrifices) with your wife, without all the sannyasi austerity.
  - The **third** said **Dvapara-yuga** is best, because Svayam Bhagavan Sri Krishna appears on earth and His deity form is established in the temples, so you never feel separation and can always be with the Lord happily.
- They argued until push came to shove — eyes going red, top-knots falling down. But being gentlemen, they agreed to take the question to a higher authority (the Aryan way to settle an argument), since the whole world should know the answer forever. So they went to the author of the Vedas, **Srila Vyasadeva**, who lives in the Himalayas near Badrinath.
  - *(Aside:* the speaker recently visited Vyasadeva's cave, doing the Char Dham — Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, and Badrinath — "by helicopter, modern sadhus." At Badrinath you hike a kilometer or two into the mountains to a cave right on the Chinese border where Vyasadeva wrote the Vedas, with a little kund nearby.*)*
- They reached the cave, but Vyasa was in the bathing tank, submerged for a long time (mystic powers). While waiting they bickered over who would ask the question ("I'll win!" "No, I'll win!").
  - *(Aside:* in days of yore people were bigger — riper fruits, better nutrition, cleaner air; because of Kali-yuga we get smaller every day, and at the end of the age people will look like dwarfs.*)*
- Finally one saint addressed him: "Srila Vyasadeva, you are a *shaktyavesha* incarnation of the Supreme Personality of God — the authority. We three have a question: which is the best age to be born in to make spiritual progress? Is it Satya-yuga? Treta-yuga? Or obviously Dvapara-yuga?"

## Vyasadeva's Answer: Kali-Yuga Is the Best Age

- As he was going back down into the water, Vyasadeva said:

## How Do We Know Krishna's Method? The Footprints Story

- How do we know Krishna's method? Because He says so. And to illustrate that He is always with us — the **Footprints story.**
