# Srila Prabhupad Glorification

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 12 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:15:54

**Themes:** srila prabhupada · pure devotee · deity worship · love in separation · guru and disciple · sankirtan mission · transformation by mercy

## Summary

Indradyumna Swami offers a Srila Prabhupada glorification composed entirely of personal reminiscences from the early 1970s, beginning with first seeing Srila Prabhupada at the Detroit airport in 1971, where devotees wept while he, a new devotee, worried he was too materially attached to cry, and where Prabhupada's words 'you are not this body' pierced his heart. He recounts his eight-month wait for initiation, giving his emergency ten dollars to the Bhaktivedanta Book Fund with a letter, and receiving a reply promising that Prabhupada would in due course accept him as a duly initiated disciple. He tells of arranging to fly with Prabhupada to Europe, confronting a rude customs officer over Prabhupada's white bag, walking alone with Prabhupada who three times explained that people in the material world have no good qualities because of the modes of nature but recover them by reviving Krishna consciousness, honoring orange-peel maha-prasadam on the plane, and watching a Charlie Chaplin film that Prabhupada justified because 'Krishna is the origin of all original things.' Further stories cover being left behind in London to carry Prabhupada's heavy bag of Acharya commentaries and receiving the blessing 'so much endeavor in this material world, but when I take you back to Godhead everything will be easy and sublime,' plus receiving one of Prabhupada's dhotis with the instruction to 'preach boldly and have faith in the holy names.' In France he describes the poor Paris temple, the installation of Radha Paris-Ishvara where Prabhupada lovingly treated the marble Deity as directly Radharani, drinking all the charanamrita after winning a book-distribution marathon, and seeing Prabhupada shed torrents of tears while singing 'Jaya Radha-Madhava' in separation from Krishna. He relates preaching to and converting a French Navy admiral (told to read the ninth chapter of the Bhagavad-gita) and the transformation of a filthy, drug-addicted homeless boy nicknamed 'Pigpen' whom Prabhupada blessed with 'it's all right, my boy,' and who became the initiated book distributor Rasada Das. The talk closes on Prabhupada as the perfect gentleman and expert preacher who could touch any heart, redirecting a woman's question from her past life to her next, yet who could also thunder in righteous anger 'you are not God, you are a dog' when Krishna was blasphemed.

## First Sight of Srila Prabhupada — Detroit, 1971

- The speaker first saw Srila Prabhupada when Prabhupada arrived in Detroit in 1971 to visit the temple. It was a huge airport reception.
  - Devotees came from everywhere — Chicago, New York, St. Louis, and even some from the West Coast.
  - Prabhupada had been away for some time; many present, including the speaker (a devotee of only a few months), had never seen him. Satsvarupa Maharaja describes the reception nicely in the *Prabhupada-lilamrita*.
- **The camera moment:**
  - In those days there were very few photos of Prabhupada — the speaker had only ever seen a few black-and-white ones. A photograph of Prabhupada was rare and treasured. During japa sessions, if a devotee had a photo, they would all sit around it and chant.
  - The speaker was eager to get his own photo. As Prabhupada turned the corner off the plane, the speaker looked through his camera and thought, "Prabhupada looks just like he does in the photographs."
- **Everyone else broke down crying:**
  - All around, devotees — especially those who had Prabhupada's association before — broke out crying, fell to the ground, sobbing and weeping.
  - His godbrother **Balalasva Prabhu**, who had been a hero to him (a good kirtan leader, preacher, leader — a "tough devotee"), was on the ground crying "Prabhupada, Prabhupada."
  - The speaker thought: "Why am I not crying? I must have so many material desires in my heart that I can't cry when I first see my spiritual master." He put his camera in his bag and fell on the ground.
- **Prabhupada's entrance and first lecture:**
  - Prabhupada embraced Kirtanananda Maharaja and patted Bhagavan das on the head — "very, very sweet." He was taken to a chair set up nearby and began lecturing.
  - The speaker was mesmerized just to see him after months of meditating on him. He remembers only one sentence, which godbrothers (Bhakti Bhringa Govinda Maharaja, Pancha-gauda Prabhu, and others) all independently recall as the same line:
    - **"Please believe me when I tell you, you are not this body."** — the core teaching of *Bhagavad-gita* 2.13 (*dehino 'smin yatha dehe* — the soul is distinct from the body). Prabhupada leaned over and said it, and it entered the speaker's heart and convinced him: "I'm not the body, I'm a spirit soul." He had read and heard it many times, but hearing it from Prabhupada was different.
  - Principle drawn: as Prabhupada said, **the chanting of Hare Krishna is always beneficial, but especially potent when heard from the lips of a pure devotee.**

## The Child Devotees — Kartals, Bhagavad-gita, and "Prabhupada! Prabhupada!"

- **Vaishnava das playing kartals (Detroit):**
  - The first morning Prabhupada came down to give class, Bhagavan's wife Krishna-bhamini was standing near Prabhupada's Vyasasana with her first child, **Vaishnava das** (about one year old).
  - The child had little kartals and was playing them sweetly in time with the kirtan. Prabhupada stopped, said "Oh, he's very nice," and sat on the Vyasasana. Instead of chanting *Jaya Radha-Madhava* himself, he spoke about the boy: "This boy plays the kirtan so nicely — this could only be because he was a devotee in his last life."
  - Krishna-bhamini beamed with a mother's pride. Prabhupada then quoted **Bhagavad-gita** on how the unsuccessful yogi takes birth again — on higher planets, or into a family of wealthy merchants, or (best) into a family of brahmanas and devotees (the theme of *BG 6.41–42*, *shucinam shrimatam gehe*). He said: "These children are very, very special. You must take very good care of them."
- **His own son Gauras-shakti (New Mayapur chateau, later):**
  - The speaker, then a grihastha, had his son (about one to one-and-a-half years old) on his shoulders while chanting japa around the castle (a chateau at New Mayapur). Prabhupada's quarters were upstairs.
  - Prabhupada looked out his window; the son, **Gauras-shakti**, saw him and shouted "Prabhupada! Prabhupada!" repeatedly, nearly falling off his father's shoulders. Prabhupada waved to him.
  - That night at darshan Prabhupada brought up the incident: "There was one young boy going around with his father around the castle, and he saw me and started chanting 'Prabhupada, Prabhupada.'" He said, "These children — I don't know who they are. Maybe there is some connection from a previous birth. They are so enthusiastic when they see me."

## Aspiring for Initiation — The $10 Donation and Prabhupada's Reply

- The speaker had been a devotee about eight months and badly wanted initiation, but temple president Bhagavan had a strict one-year policy — he said none of the devotees he had recommended had ever left. This was unusual for the time, when people were being initiated after six, four, or even one month (sometimes people walked off the street, were told "you have to get initiated by a pure devotee," sat down for initiation, and later many left).
- Denied authorization, the speaker still wanted Prabhupada to know he was his aspiring disciple. As Prabhupada was leaving the Detroit temple for his car:
  - The speaker ran upstairs to give a donation to the Bhaktivedanta Book Fund (BBT — then called simply the "book fund," not yet incorporated). When he had joined, he gave all his money (about $2,000–$3,000) to the temple, but was allowed to keep $10 as emergency security. He took that $10 and wrote a short letter:
    - "Dear Srila Prabhupada, please accept my humble obeisances. All glories to you. Thank you so much for coming to Detroit... I learned so much during your visit, and I became very attached to you, and I'm very sorry to see you go. I'm going to feel so much your separation when you go."
  - He ran downstairs and yelled "Srila Prabhupada!" loudly from the door as Prabhupada (30–40 yards away) was getting into his car. All the devotees stopped and looked. The speaker offered obeisances and gave the letter. He watched Prabhupada tuck it into his kurta pocket, sticking out, and worried whether Prabhupada would read it.
  - Reflection: even short encounters with Srila Prabhupada leave such an impression — **"lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya"** (*Chaitanya-charitamrita* — by a moment's association with a pure devotee, all perfection is achieved).
- **The reply, two weeks later:**
  - When a letter came from Prabhupada it was a special event. The temple president would not immediately say who it was addressed to; the conch would blow, and everyone would drop their service (kitchen, cleaning, preparing books for sankirtan) and assemble in the reception room to hear "a few drops of nectar." No one thought a letter was only for one person — whatever Prabhupada said was for the benefit of all, so every devotee hung on every word.
  - Bhagavan announced the letter was addressed to "Bhakta Bryan" (the speaker's name before initiation). Hands shaking, he came forward and read (letter later lost, recalled approximately):
    - "My dear Bhakta Bryan, please accept my blessings. I want to thank you very much for your donation of $10 towards my book fund. I have information from the authorities in your temple that you are doing very nicely in Krishna consciousness. So my request to you is that you always follow in the footsteps of advanced devotees. And in due course of time, I will be very, very happy to accept you as my duly initiated disciple."
  - The speaker was elated — this gave him great spiritual strength.

## Going to Europe — Getting on Prabhupada's Flight

- Some months later Prabhupada wrote to Bhagavan das asking him to go to Europe to help the new center in Paris (where Varivala, Lochanananda, and Umapati Prabhu — now Umapati Swami — were serving). Prabhupada wanted a GBC man to organize the preaching.
- Bhagavan needed an assistant who could handle travel. The speaker raised his hand, having traveled in Europe (France, Germany, Spain). Bhagavan agreed to take him — but on one condition: **that he get them on the same flight as Srila Prabhupada**, who was flying to London in about a week.
- **The airline ticket story:**
  - The speaker called New York to learn Prabhupada's flight, then called the airline: "Do you have an A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami — a Mr. Swami — on this flight?" Yes (his servant was also listed). "I'd like two seats on that flight." The agent said the flight was completely booked; his heart dropped.
  - "Isn't there anything you can do? This is my spiritual master." "You're what?" "He's my spiritual master... my guru... my teacher... my spiritual teacher. In Christianity you have Jesus Christ, and he's your life and soul." The agent went to check with her supervisor and returned: "Sir, we've made an arrangement so you can be with your guru on the flight. We've got two seats for you."

## New York (Henry Street) and the Airport Departure

- They drove to New York; the temple was on Henry Street. The speaker recalls Prabhupada giving initiations, meeting devotees he had heard about — Bhavananda, Vishnujana Maharaja leading kirtans and harinam on the streets daily.
  - Bhavananda had made a "pop art" Vyasasana of many colors for Prabhupada — impressive, but Prabhupada kept slipping on it; the speaker wanted to come hold the cushion.
- **The white bag at passport control:**
  - The travelers were the speaker, Bhagavan, Srila Prabhupada, and Pradyumna. They left early and avoided the traffic jam; hundreds of devotees arrived to see Prabhupada off, making the scene chaotic, so check-in ran late.
  - At hand-luggage inspection, standing right next to Prabhupada, the officer demanded to see inside Prabhupada's famous little **white bag** (which held his important papers, glasses, kartals, documents, and medicines — a bag the speaker had always been curious about). Prabhupada struggled with the old Indian-make locks.
  - The officer became rough: "Open that bag." The speaker leaned over the counter into his face: **"If you speak like that again to my spiritual master, I'm going to break your face."** The man stepped back: "I thought you people were non-violent." The speaker: "But you're speaking to a pure devotee; you should know the proper etiquette."
  - "Okay, young man, you open the bag." The speaker turned it around and it opened easily. He became mesmerized peering into "confidential insight into Prabhupada's possessions" — the glasses, the pen. The officer had to remind him, "Excuse me, I want to see in the bag," then said "Okay, thank you."
- **Alone with Prabhupada in the hallway — the three-fold instruction:**
  - The speaker and Prabhupada were the first two to walk to the plane, alone in the empty hallway — the first time the speaker was ever alone with him. Prabhupada turned and taught the same point three times, each time asking "Do you understand, Indradyumna?":
    1. "In this material world these people have no good qualities... because they are associating with the modes of nature, they have no good qualities."
    2. "You just see — hovering on the mental platform, no Krishna consciousness, all the bad qualities."
    3. "Because they are associating with these modes of nature, they're going downwards, downwards, downwards. But if we can revive their Krishna consciousness, they'll [be full] of all good qualities."
  - On the third repetition it entered his heart fully — "a mystical experience." There was special potency in hearing the knowledge three times.

## On the Plane — Prasadam and Charlie Chaplin

- Seated next to Prabhupada with no other passengers yet, the nervous speaker chanted loudly "Hare Krishna..." hoping Prabhupada would notice his good rounds. Prabhupada just looked out the window.
- **The oranges (maha-maha-prasadam):**
  - Passengers arrived; Prabhupada asked, "So, prasadam?" (Pradyumna was acting as both secretary and servant.) Someone had given oranges "for Prabhupada." With a little plastic knife the speaker cut them; Prabhupada sucked the juice from three oranges and set them down.
  - Prabhupada then asked, "So, Indradyumna, you're going to take prasadam?" As a neophyte the speaker said, "Srila Prabhupada, there's no more prasadam." Prabhupada replied, **"There's maha, maha-prasad."** Overjoyed to be ordered to take his spiritual master's remnants, the speaker put a whole orange peel in his mouth and chewed — sour, lips burning, tears in his eyes — "This is maha... maha-prasad," while Prabhupada watched.
- **The Charlie Chaplin movie:**
  - The plane took off; passengers were asked to close windows for a movie. In the dark the speaker made a show of reading his *Bhagavad-gita* instead — reciting **"dehino 'smin yatha dehe..."** (*BG 2.13*) and **"sarva-dharman parityajya..."** (*BG 18.66*) to demonstrate "I'm not watching this movie."
  - Then he heard Prabhupada chuckle and laugh heartily — Prabhupada was watching the movie (a humorous Charlie Chaplin film). Pradyumna was laughing too; the speaker also laughed. It was very funny.
  - Puzzled ("I thought we weren't supposed to watch movies"), he pulled Pradyumna to the back of the plane. After a back-and-forth over "you go ask Srila Prabhupada" / "no, you go," Pradyumna finally went and returned smiling with Prabhupada's answer:
    - **"Generally we don't watch movies, but Charlie Chaplin's humor is very original, and Krishna is the origin of all original things."**
  - Note: as a young boy, Prabhupada himself enjoyed the innocent humor of Charlie Chaplin.

## Arrival in London — The Lost Bag, the Missed Reception, and the Slap on the Back

- The speaker was excited landing in London — Malati, Shyamasundara, Mukunda, Janaki, Guru das and others were building a new center, and the Beatles had taken interest in Krishna consciousness, so he expected an extra-special reception (perhaps Prabhupada meeting the Beatles).
- **The lost bag:** One of Prabhupada's most important bags did not arrive — a big, heavy suitcase full of the **commentaries of the Acharyas** that Prabhupada used for his writing. Shyamasundara (upset) said someone had to stay behind. The speaker tried to hide behind a pillar, but was assigned: "Indradyumna can stay and bring the bag to the temple." Prabhupada agreed it was very important.
  - He watched Prabhupada walk out to the kirtan and reception while he waited about an hour and a half. The bag was finally found — so heavy he could hardly lift it. It was raining, cold, and he was miserable, having missed the reception.
- **The taxi:** He took a taxi to "Sevenbury Place" (Bury Place). The driver noticed his misery: "Are you all right, son?... Anything I can do to help you?" "No, I missed the reception of my spiritual master." The driver recognized the devotees ("the ones that sing down on Oxford Street") and said, "There's always a better day."
- **No prasadam, no help:** At the temple the devotees sat around empty paper plates with turmeric stains and ghee finger-marks. There was no prasadam left ("You missed the prasadam... you missed the reception — it was so ecstatic"). No one would help carry the heavy bag up the steep stairs because they were all "so full." The speaker got angry at that bag — but later understood it was "the source of all good fortune" for him.
- **Opening Prabhupada's door (backwards):**
  - Exhausted, hungry, unshowered, he hauled the bag up (perhaps the third floor). Seeing the door marked "His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami," he did not knock — in his excitement and confusion he opened it and began dragging the suitcase in backwards.
  - Nanda Kumar (Prabhupada's London servant) warned, "Hey, watch out! You're going to bump into Srila Prabhupada!" The speaker dropped the bag, turned, and found himself right in front of Prabhupada. He fell down offering obeisances — **"nama om vishnu-padaya krishna-preshthaya bhu-tale, srimate bhaktivedanta..."** (the pranama mantra to Srila Prabhupada).
  - **The slap on the back:** As he offered obeisances, Prabhupada slapped him on the back — he couldn't tell if it was out of love or anger, but "either would be a blessing." Prabhupada said something softly he couldn't hear, then walked away.
  - Afterward Nanda Kumar, mouth open, told him what Prabhupada had said: **"So much endeavor in this material world. When I take you back home, back to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime."**
- **Lasting value:** Devotees receive many instructions from Prabhupada's books, but a personal instruction or blessing is treasured forever. Through years of difficulty — the movement's spreading, opposition, the daily austerity of sankirtan in cold and heat — the speaker would recall these words as strength: struggle now to push on the sankirtan movement, because in the end Prabhupada will take everyone back home, back to Godhead, where everything is easy and sublime.

## London — Prabhupada's Room, "Preach Boldly," and the Gift of His Dhoti

- A couple of days later Prabhupada called for the speaker and Bhagavan (before they left for Paris) to encourage them. Nanda Kumar admitted them; Prabhupada sat behind a low desk.
- Prabhupada preached on the importance of the **sankirtan movement**, expressed appreciation that they were going to help it, and said Krishna would give them all facility. Looking at the speaker, he gave the instruction:
  - **"My instruction is: preach boldly and have faith in the holy names."**
- **The dhoti:** "I'll give you something to encourage you." From his drawer Prabhupada gave Bhagavan das something (the speaker doesn't recall) and handed the speaker one of his own **dhotis**. The speaker took it to his heart — now he had a treasured instruction plus Prabhupada's own dhoti.
  - Though a grihastha, he wore that dhoti "like a charter." When a sannyasi objected, "You're a householder, you can't wear saffron," he replied, "Maharaja, yes I can — this is Prabhupada's dhoti. No one's going to make me take it off." He wore it until it wore out.

## Paris — The Collapsing Altar and "Expert at Sense Gratification"

- Arriving at the Paris temple (Fontenay-aux-Roses), used to America's big organized temples and deities, they walked into a cold, old building with no heat. **As they entered the temple room, the altar collapsed** — it was made of cardboard boxes and a couple of sticks, holding a picture of the Panca-tattva. They wondered, "Where did Prabhupada send us?" But the kirtan (led by Lochanananda) was ecstatic and very nice.
- A couple of weeks later Prabhupada came to Paris. There was a program at the American Center downtown; Hansadutta brought all the German devotees. There were some problems outside (ruffians) that they didn't want to tell Prabhupada about.
- **Morning walk:** The speaker was impressed by the nice French houses and gardens. Prabhupada was not impressed — he looked and said of the French, **"They're so expert at sense gratification."**

## The Installation of Radha–Paris-ishvara and the Conviction About Deity Worship

- On a later visit (1973–74) Prabhupada came to install **Radha–Paris-ishvara**. It was a very simple installation — Radha and Krishna were bathed in two large buckets.
- Standing next to the Vyasasana, the speaker watched as Srimati Radharani was bathed. On her marble cheek was a deep rose color; Prabhupada assumed it was from the abhisheka (ghee mixed with turmeric or some color).
  - Prabhupada got up from his Vyasasana and walked "with so much concern and so much care and love" toward the Deity of Radharani. He folded his hands and gently tried to wipe the color from her face — but it was the natural color of the marble. He kept tenderly trying to remove it.
  - The speaker realized: Prabhupada does not see the Deity as marble; he sees that Radharani is actually there and treats her with love and devotion. This one incident convinced him deeply about Deity worship — as the pujaris pray: **"My dear Lord, you are not a statue; you are directly the son of Maharaj Nanda."** (Though he didn't know that quote then, Prabhupada's intimate dealings convinced him the Deity is not stone but actually Krishna and Radha.)
  - Supporting instruction: when Prabhupada installed the small Radha-Krishna in Los Angeles he said, **"If you see these Deities as brass, they'll remain brass to you forever. But if you follow the rules and regulations of scripture and worship them with love and devotion, one day these Deities will speak to you."**
- **Principle — great things and small things of a pure devotee:** Prabhupada did monumental things worldwide, but because he was a pure devotee even his little acts were wonderful — **as Krishna is greater than the greatest and smaller than the smallest** (*anor aniyan mahato mahiyan*), his pure devotee, by the Lord's grace, can do huge things, and even the small things (imbued with pure devotion) are significant, endearing, and captivating — like the way Prabhupada moved his fingers and chanted when playing kartals, always in pure devotion for Radha and Krishna.

## The Marathon to Wash Prabhupada's Feet — and the Charnamrita

- The next year, having worshiped Radha and Krishna for a year, the devotees were enthusiastic. They had new outfits, a repainted temple, and a fundraising marathon to renovate for Prabhupada's arrival — distributing a small pamphlet on chanting Hare Krishna in the Paris metro/underground.
- Bhagavan announced a competition: **the first two to win the marathon would get to bathe Prabhupada's feet.** The speaker and his godbrother Bhagavata teamed up.
  - For one month the speaker did not see sunlight — up before dawn to chant rounds, into the metro until 11 p.m., sleeping three to four hours a night, sometimes on metro chairs. He was determined "beg, borrow, or steal" to get the mercy, because he had heard the dust of the lotus feet, the water washing them, and the remnants of prasadam of the spiritual master are very conducive to advancing in Krishna consciousness.
  - They won, went out extra days distributing pamphlets and small books, and got an extra-big silver bowl and pitcher for washing Prabhupada's feet — because they had a plan.
- **The plan:**
  - Prabhupada arrived (big reception at Orly Airport), saw the Deities, and sat for guru-puja. The speaker held the bowl; Bhagavata poured the warm water with rose water and petals over Prabhupada's feet. "The perfection of my life — touching the feet of a pure devotee." They collected liters of charnamrita.
  - With the kirtan raging, they ran the bowl upstairs to the president's office (the speaker had the key). A group of brahmacharis chased them (normally the charnamrita is thrown on everyone's heads), but they wanted every drop. He slammed and blocked the door as the brahmacharis banged: "We want the nectar! We want the nectar!"
  - With brand-new cups, the two of them slowly and reverently drank the entire bowl of charnamrita, savoring every drop, remembering the mercy in the water that washes the lotus feet of the spiritual master — "I don't know how two young men could drink that much." At the last couple of drops they finished and sat back.
  - Reflection: **"I think that's probably the reason I'm still in the International Society for Krishna Consciousness — we got that mercy."**
  - He opened the door; about 20 brahmacharis tumbled in expecting resistance, demanding "Where's the charnamrita?" The two feigned ignorance ("Oh, sorry, boys — beg, borrow, or steal, you've got to get the mercy"). When Bhagavan came asking what they were doing, they said "Nothing, we're coming back down."

## Prabhupada Singing Jaya Radha-Madhava — Tears of Love

- After the kirtan Prabhupada took his kartals and began singing **"Jaya Radha-Madhava, kunja-bihari, gopi-jana-vallabha, giri-vara-dhari..."** (Bhaktivinoda Thakura's song describing Krishna's Vrindavan pastimes).
- Hearing Prabhupada sing about the spiritual world — Radha and Krishna, Giri-Govardhana, the Yamuna — everything around them stopped; they were mesmerized, entering the spiritual world through Prabhupada's transcendental sound. Prabhupada gazed at Radha–Paris-ishvara.
- Suddenly Prabhupada began shaking on "kunja-bihari"; the speaker feared he was sick. Sitting close, right before the Vyasasana, he saw Prabhupada's eyes well up and overflow with tears flowing "like torrents of rain" — like the mood of **Shikshashtakam verse 7**: "O Govinda, feeling your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more; tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain" (*yugayitam nimeshena...*).
- Reflection: "Here I am in the material world and Prabhupada is in the spiritual world — he's in Vrindavana. And by his causeless mercy I'm getting a glimpse of what it's like to be there, to have love for Radha and Krishna." Forever etched in his heart. Of everything that convinced him about Krishna consciousness, seeing the symptoms of love manifest was the most convincing — still far beyond him, but convinced by that one incident. Prabhupada then opened his eyes, said "chant Hare Krishna," gave a very short lecture, and went upstairs.

## Geneva — The Swiss Chocolate

- In Geneva, after the reception, Guru Gauranga had bought two bars of Swiss chocolate (light and dark) and asked the speaker to offer them to Prabhupada, since "Swiss chocolate's the best chocolate."
- In his room Prabhupada was joking about the room's layout. The speaker offered the chocolate: "Guru Gauranga says Swiss chocolate is the best." Prabhupada tried a piece of the light one: "Oh, very good." He pointed to the other; told it was dark and maybe a little bitter, Prabhupada said, "No, no, we'll try," took a little, and said, "All right, Hare Krishna."

## The French Admiral — Corsica to Paris

- On one occasion Prabhupada came to Paris quite suddenly (he had been ill in England). Bhagavan had gone to beg him to come, speaking of the devotees' intense service and love — many books published and distributed, deity worship going nicely — without pressuring him. After Bhagavan left the room, Prabhupada turned to a secretary and said, **"All right, we will go — Bhagavan is the supreme controller"** (a play on Bhagavan's name).
- **Finishing the lecture:** The speaker's traveling sankirtan party was on Corsica (a French Mediterranean island), distributing books. Word came that Prabhupada had arrived in Paris and they were to come immediately, but the speaker was mid-lecture at a yoga club. ## Mayapur — A Question on Seeing Krishna Everywhere vs. Feeling Separation

- Arriving late to Mayapur (still doing traveling sankirtan up to the last minute), the speaker found Prabhupada giving darshan on the lawn and asking for questions. Wanting Prabhupada to see and know him as his disciple (intimate association was rare amid so many devotees), he raised his hand:

## Prabhupada the Perfect Gentleman — and When He Became Angry

- **The perfect gentleman:** Prabhupada was willing to preach to anyone, always had time for anyone who came, was the perfect listener, and gave expert advice according to each person's level — so expert he could touch the heart of anyone, even the antagonistic.

## Glossary

- **darshan** — The audience or sight of the Lord or the spiritual master.
- **charanamrita** — The sanctified water that has bathed the feet of the Deity or spiritual master.
- **maha-prasadam** — The remnants of food taken by the spiritual master or a pure devotee.
- **sankirtan** — The congregational chanting of the holy names and the mission of book distribution.
- **prema-bhakti** — Pure, ecstatic love of God, the highest goal of devotional service.
- **vipralambha** — Loving sentiments felt for the Lord in separation.
- **archana (deity worship)** — Worship of the Lord in His Deity form on the altar.
- **Vyasasana** — The elevated seat from which the spiritual master speaks.
- **guru-puja** — The ceremonial worship of the spiritual master.
- **dhoti** — The traditional cloth garment worn by men; Prabhupada gave the speaker one of his own.
