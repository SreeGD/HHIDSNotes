# Birth and death Cycle

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 6 · Foundations in Bhakti · 23:09

**Themes:** soul and body · reincarnation · material duality · love of god · self-realization · human life

## Summary

Speaking at an outdoor cultural festival (in Poland, having just returned from a Ratha-yatra in Berlin), Indradyumna Swami introduces the audience to the philosophy of the Bhagavad Gita as the deep spiritual wisdom of India, spoken by Lord Krishna 5,000 years ago. He explains that the human form of life is special because God has given us advanced intelligence, whose ultimate purpose is not merely science and technology but self-realization, understanding that we are not the material body but the eternal spirit soul within it. Using the analogy of a car and its driver, he points out that we know everything about the machine (the body) but nothing about the driver (the soul), and notes that even intuitively we say 'my hand' or 'my body,' showing the body belongs to us but is not us. He teaches that the soul is eternal, originated in the spiritual world with God, and has taken countless births in the material world chasing a happiness it cannot find here, because the material world is a place of duality where happiness always comes paired with equal distress. Since the soul's intrinsic nature is happiness that can only be fulfilled by reuniting with God, trying to be happy here is like a fish taken out of water or a lost child who wants only her father and not toys or gifts. He states that real progress in life is measured by how close we come to God, and that once we become fully self-realized we are freed from the repeated cycle of birth and death. He concludes that the whole festival, its atmosphere, books, and especially the congregational chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra, is meant to purify and elevate consciousness, presenting a 5,000-year-old culture of happiness from within.

## Introduction: The Wisdom of India at the Festival

- The talk is given from the festival stage while the technicians prepare for the final performance; the speaker uses the interval to share "another treasure" that has been "sculpted."
- So far at the festival the audience has seen a lot of music, beautiful bands, theatrical performances, martial arts, and tasted nice food at the restaurant.
- But there is another very important part of India's culture to share this evening: **the philosophy of the ancient sages of India — the wisdom of India.**
- That deep spiritual wisdom is found in a very ancient scripture called the **Bhagavad-gita**, which means **"the song of God."**
- It was spoken by **Lord Sri Krishna** when He was present on this planet **5,000 years ago**.

## The Importance of the Human Form of Life

- Like all the great authorized scriptures of the world — the Bible, the Koran, the Torah — the Bhagavad-gita first of all stresses the **importance of the human form of life**.
- There are so many species of life around the world, and we should respect all of them, because essentially they are all God's children. But special significance is given to the human form.
- **Why?** Because in the human form God has given us the gift of **advanced intelligence**. Everyone agrees man is more intelligent than the animals, birds, and fish.
- But why has God given us this intelligence — simply for science, technology, and advanced medicine? "Yes and no." Ultimately, the Lord has given us intelligence so that we can **understand our deeper spiritual nature within**.
- This is the **first step in the science of self-realization**: to know who we are.

## We Are Not the Body: The Soul

- The first teaching of the Bhagavad-gita: **Krishna explains that we are not these material bodies.**
- Most of us think we are the body — you look in the mirror in the morning and think "Oh, I'm looking good," or "Oh, I'm not looking so good." But that is a **misconception**.
- The body is made of matter, but **you are spirit — you are spirit soul. The soul lives in the body.**

### The Car-and-Driver Analogy

- Analogy to make it clear: it is like a **car and the driver of the car**. The car is a machine; the body is a machine; the driver is the soul.
- Just as, if one of your friends goes by in her Toyota and you say, "Oh, there goes my friend," and someone asks, "Oh, the Toyota?" — No, not the Toyota; the **person inside driving** the Toyota. The body is like the Toyota, and the soul is like the driver.
- Unfortunately, we know so much about the **machine** (the body), but we know nothing about the **driver** (the soul).

### We Intuitively Know We Are Not the Body

- Intuitively, we already know we are not the body, because we don't say "I hand," "I arm," "I head," "I body." We say "**my** hand, **my** arm, **my** head, **my** body" — it belongs to me.
- But if asked to come up on the stage and speak about who you really are — the spirit soul — how long could you speak? Most cannot speak for more than **five minutes** about who we really are. Yet that is the most important thing in life to understand.
- We may be very advanced materially, but spiritually we are back at first or second grade. That is why a book like the Bhagavad-gita is very important — it goes into the science in detail.

## The Soul Is Eternal; Where We Came From

- **The soul is eternal** — there was never a time the soul did not exist, nor in the future shall it ever cease to be. (Cf. **Bhagavad-gita 2.12** / **2.20**, the eternality of the soul.)
- Where did we start? **We started in the spiritual sky.** At one time we were all with God.
- Which God — the Christian God, the Hindu God, or the Muslim God? That is another misconception: **there is only one God, but He has many names.**
  - Just as there is only one **sun**, but in different parts of the world they call the sun by different names — so there is only one God with many names.
  - In India, if you want to talk about God, you say **Krishna** — that is how people call God in India. **Krishna means "the most beautiful person."**
- Originally, that is where we were — with God. But somehow or other we had "the bright idea" to come to the material world to try to be happy, and we have been trying to be happy here for many, many births.

### Reincarnation

- When you die, it is only the **body** that dies; the **soul continues on** — that is called **reincarnation**.
- Analogy: just as throughout your lifetime you may sell several cars — the car changes, but you remain the same person — so, looking at the bigger picture since we left the kingdom of God, we have changed the body many times.
- This is **not your first life, and it may not be your last life**. We have changed our body hundreds, thousands, or millions of times since we left the spiritual world — from one body to the next, to the next, to the next.

## The Search for Happiness

- What are we trying to achieve in all these changes of body? **We are trying to find happiness.**
- Just as the intrinsic nature of salt is that it is salty, and the intrinsic nature of sugar is that it is sweet, the **intrinsic nature of you, the soul within the body, is happiness**. By nature, you are happy.
- But are we completely happy here in the material world? Even if you are very wealthy, very famous, very beautiful, and well educated — all the things we think will bring happiness — is such a person actually happy? **Not if he is intelligent.**

### The World of Duality

- Although we may have those things, we must understand this is a **world of duality**: there is black and white, heat and cold, up and down, happiness and distress. **You cannot have one without the other.**
- Analogy: a **coin has two sides** — in America we say "heads and tails." By having that coin, you have the heads, but you also have the tails. You cannot have one without the other.
- So in this world, **you cannot have happiness without an equal amount of anxiety.**
- Not that there is no happiness here — "We're all on our vacation, and it didn't rain today, so we had a good day on the beach, so we're happy." But you won't be happy if you got a sunburn today, and you won't be happy if it rains tomorrow (and it probably will, because it's a very rainy summer). **We want to be happy, but we are in a place where you are only happy half the time.**

### The Fish Out of Water

- We are like a **fish out of water**. A fish is happy in the water. Take the fish out of the water, bring him into Reval, and give him a drink, a cigarette, a pretty girl, a diamond ring — "Enjoy, Mr. Fish." It is not his nature.
- And neither is it your nature — because **you are not this body** (the body is here today and gone tomorrow); **you are the soul**, and the soul by nature requires a **different type of happiness** than this world can give.
- Actually, the soul is only really happy when the soul is **reunited with God**, because ultimately the happiest relationship is a **loving relationship with God**.

### The Lost Little Girl on the Beach

- Analogy: you are walking down the beach with your family, and your little daughter gets separated. She plays in the sand for a few minutes, then realizes she has lost her family.
- Someone comes along: "Don't cry, little girl, I'll give you a big ice cream cone." — "No, I want my daddy."
- "Don't worry, I'll buy you a new dress." — "No, I want my daddy."
- "Don't worry, little girl, I'll buy you a beautiful gold necklace, everything will be okay." — "No, I want my daddy."
- You can offer her the whole world, but it does not mean as much to her as the relationship with her mother and father.
- The same is true with you and me: we can have all the comforts of the world (nothing wrong with that), but that won't make us as happy as being **reunited with God**. **And that is the purpose of life** — real progress can be measured by how close, in this lifetime, we come to God.

## The Goal: Ending the Cycle of Birth and Death

- It is not easy, because we are all very busy.
- **The ant hill:** the speaker was in the forest, walking, and saw a big ant hill with thousands of little ants busily building the house. Similarly, we are also very busy, and we do need to be busy to maintain this body.
- But we cannot forget the ultimate goal: to understand our deeper spiritual nature, to understand God, and our very deep, intimate, loving relationship with Him.
- **Once you understand that fully, you don't have to take birth again** — the cycle of reincarnation (birth, death, birth, death, birth, death) is finished.
- When, at the end of life, you are fully **self-realized**, you can speak for hours about who you are as a soul; you can stand on the stage and speak for hours about the personality of God; and you can go on for days, weeks, months, and years speaking about that intimate loving relationship. When you come to that point, there is no need to take birth again in this world.

## The Festival as Food for Thought

- It is a natural culture. There is something special about the atmosphere here — very festive, very colorful, very joyful, and pure culture.
  - **"We're not selling any beer or wine here."** The speaker stood next to one man who said, "Everyone seems happy here — but there's no beer." No, because this is presenting a very elevated culture — a **culture of happiness from within**.
- It is a great culture, over **5,000 years old**, and again it is based on this ancient scripture, the Bhagavad-gita.
