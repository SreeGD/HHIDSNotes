# Becoming a Devotee

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 3 · Foundations in Bhakti · 50:48

## Summary

This morning lecture is based on Srimad Bhagavatam, Canto 1, Chapter 15 ('The Pandavas Retire Timely'), Text 36, which describes how, as soon as Lord Krishna left this earth, the age of Kali became fully manifest to create inauspicious conditions for those endowed with a poor fund of knowledge. Indradyumna Swami reviews the grim predictions of Kali Yuga found in the Twelfth Canto of the Bhagavatam, that religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, tolerance, mercy, lifespan, physical strength, and memory all steadily diminish, and that famine, excessive taxation, and drought will eventually force people to survive on leaves, roots, flesh, fruits, and flowers, stressing that because these are shastra (apaurusheya, not composed by any mundane being) they are certain to come true, unlike the subtle guesses of Nostradamus. He warns that the worst prophecy is that eventually there will be no devotees, and recounts how Srila Prabhupada told a disciple who wished to keep taking birth to distribute books, 'Don't come back, this Kali Yuga is only going to get worse,' while Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura said he saw no reason why all his disciples could not return to Godhead in one lifetime if they were very serious and strict. The heart of the class, however, is the positive alternative: Kali's influence acts only on those not fully surrendered, and one neutralizes it by taking shelter of the Lord. Through several stories, Buddha teaching a grieving mother that death is universal by sending her to beg rice only from a house where no one had died, the French philosopher Jean-Paul Sartre who analyzed material suffering brilliantly (his books reading like a summary of the Gita, 'duhkhalayam asasvatam') but, offering no solution, committed suicide, and Midwestern tornado survivors who lived because they knew where to take shelter, he shows that the sadhu both exposes the futility of material life and gives the remedy. He explains that the Lord's real protection is not shielding the body but protecting the devotee from maya so he never forgets Krishna's lotus feet, illustrating this with Prahlada Maharaj's fearlessness before Nrisimhadeva, Lord Shiva's statement while watching the battle at Lanka ('if Krishna wants to protect a person, who can kill him; if Krishna wants to kill him, who can protect him'), and Krishna's own promise in the Bhagavad-gita that 'My devotee never perishes.' He concludes with Draupadi, who was rescued only when she let go of her sari and raised both hands crying 'He Govinda!', teaching that full protection comes in exact proportion to full surrender, and that strict, serious devotees who help spread Krishna consciousness can expect protection, mercy, and love in their lives.

## Key Points

- Based on Srimad Bhagavatam 1.15.36: when Krishna departed the earth, the age of Kali became fully manifest, but Kali's influence affects only those not fully developed in God consciousness, and it is neutralized by taking shelter of the Lord.
- The Kali Yuga predictions in the Twelfth Canto are shastra (apaurusheya) and therefore certain: religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, lifespan, physical strength, and memory decline, and famine, heavy taxes, and drought will force people to eat leaves, roots, and flowers; the worst prophecy is that eventually there will be no devotees.
- Srila Prabhupada told a disciple who wanted endless rebirths to distribute books, 'Don't come back, Kali Yuga will only get worse'; Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati said all his disciples could go back to Godhead in one lifetime if very serious, meaning strict in the rules and regulations, chanting Hare Krishna, and helping spread the movement.
- The sadhu exposes the futility of material life and then gives the solution: contrast Jean-Paul Sartre, who dissected material suffering like a summary of the Gita but offered no remedy and committed suicide, with Buddha's grieving mother sent to beg rice only from a house where no one had died, learning that death is universal and a lesson in renunciation.
- Real protection is not preserving the body but being shielded from maya so one never forgets Krishna's lotus feet; this is the devotee's true prayer, illustrated by Prahlada Maharaj, who feared not Nrisimhadeva's ferocious form but only falling under the material energy that even Brahma, Shiva, and Indra can succumb to, our whole aim being smaranam (shravanam kirtanam vishnu smaranam), remembering the Lord in love.
- Lord Shiva at the battle of Lanka declared 'Rakhe Krishna mare ke, mare Krishna rakhe ke' (if Krishna wants to protect, none can kill; if He wants to kill, none can protect), establishing Krishna's supremacy over even Shiva, echoed by the Gita's promise 'kaunteya pratijanihi na me bhaktah pranasyati' ('My devotee never perishes'), with examples of Pariksit protected in the womb, Kunti, the Pandavas, Ambarisha, and Prahlada.
- Protection comes in proportion to surrender: Draupadi was saved only when she released her sari and raised both hands calling 'He Govinda!'; like a government equipping its soldiers with the best supplies, devotees engaged in the Lord's important mission of sharing their good fortune receive special protection, mercy, and love, so a sincere devotee, whose heart is being purified, need not fear the anomalies of Kali.

- Srimad Bhagavatam, Eighth Canto — the Deliverance of Gajendra (Gajendra-moksha), cited that praying to protect the body in order to continue Krishna's service undisturbed is pure bhakti, not selfish.
- Brahma-samhita — a verse cited (verse number not specified by the speaker) that the devotee advances in remembrance of the Lord in small increments, little step by little step.
