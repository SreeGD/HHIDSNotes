# The Life and Time fo Srila Narottam Das Thakura Part 2

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 75 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:34:28

**Themes:** preaching opposition · conversion · holy name · dacoits · separation · kirtan

## Summary

Continuing his series on Srila Narottama Das Thakura, Indradyumna Swami recounts how, after the historic first Gaura-purnima festival at Kheturi, Narottama and his intimate companion Ramachandra Kaviraja (described as one soul in two bodies) began preaching Lord Chaitanya's message in earnest, meeting fierce opposition from caste brahmanas who resented that Narottama, a Kayastha by birth, was initiating brahmanas. Through a series of dramatic conversions he transformed his opponents: a proud karma-kandi brahmana afflicted with leprosy was cured limb by limb while dancing in Narottama's kirtan; two goatherd boys, Hari Rama and Ramakrishna, bound for a Durga sacrifice, overheard the two Vaishnavas loudly discussing philosophy, freed their animals, and became empowered preachers who, with the king's physician Balaram, defeated the undefeated debater (digvijaya) Murari; and the learned brahmana Ganga Narayan Chakravarti surrendered after Narottama appeared to him in a dream. When hundreds of brahmanas led by Rupa Narayan and King Narasingha marched on Kheturi to defeat him, Narottama's disciples, disguised as a betel-nut seller and a clay-pot seller, defeated the pundits in Sanskrit, and Durga herself appeared in the brahmanas' dreams affirming Krishna's supremacy and demanding their surrender. The speaker dwells at length on the dacoit-king Hari Chandarai, who, tortured from within by a Brahmana-rakshasa ghost, was freed by Narottama's mere glance; after becoming a fixed chanter of the holy name he was imprisoned in a filthy pit and condemned to execution by a maddened elephant by a vengeful Yavana king, but, praying to his guru, he seized the charging elephant's trunk and killed it, melting the king's heart, and afterward converted whole bands of dacoits. Narottama's mission spread even to tribal Manipur, whose king (converted by Ganga Narayan) decreed universal Vaishnavism, and Indradyumna Swami interweaves personal reminiscences of Srila Prabhupada's glance, a stoning by Maoist youths at a modern Manipur festival, and a practical letter of Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura found in the Jaipur archives asking the king to protect pilgrims from dacoits. The lecture closes with Narottama's overwhelming separation on learning of the deaths of Ramachandra Kaviraja and Srinivasa Acharya, during which he composed his famous song of separation from the Vaishnavas; his own passing on a funeral pyre, from which he miraculously returned to life bearing a shining sacred thread to save the blaspheming brahmanas at Ganga Narayan's compassionate plea; and his final disappearance, when his body melted into milk in the Ganges, leaving the humble Dudh (milk) Samadhi, after which he twice reappeared amid the mourners' kirtan to console every Vaishnava and promise he would always be with them.

## Recap of the Previous Sessions

- Tonight the speaker continues with the glorious pastimes of the great acharya **Srila Narottama Das Thakura.**
- The first morning, **Chaturatma Prabhu** covered Narottama's pastimes up until the books were stolen in **Vishnupur** — the first traveling Sankirtan party, the first book distributors, and how the books were stolen.
- Last night the pastimes continued (in brief — "one could speak for hours, days, or months about the lives of such great acharyas"), up to the end of the famous festival in **Keturi** — the very first Gaura Purnima festival, when all the Vaishnavas assembled. As predicted by the Lord to **Mother Janhava**, the **Panchatattva appeared** and had an ecstatic kirtan with all the devotees; then the deities were installed, and the festival ended with the wonderful feast cooked by Janhava Mata.

## Preaching Begins in Earnest — and So Does Opposition

- The Shastra relates that after the Keturi festival, Narottama Das Thakura and his very dear, intimate friend **Ramchandra Kaviraj** — described as "one soul and two bodies" — began their preaching in earnest.
- After Mahaprabhu disappeared, the devotees felt so much lamentation and separation from Lord Chaitanya that "they couldn't pick up a kartala, they couldn't pick up a drum, they couldn't stand up to dance, they couldn't sing — they were so devastated." For all practical purposes the Sankirtan movement slowed down, and many devious sects, schisms, and different philosophies arose.
- One purpose of the Keturi festival was that the elders and all the **Mahants** would meet, discuss, and write down the actual teachings of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — which they did. So the stage was set, and Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj went out to preach "the divine command."
- **But there is always opposition when you preach Krishna consciousness.** One should not think that opposition means something is wrong with the preaching.
  - Prabhupada once told the German devotees, **"If there's no opposition, that means there's no preaching."**
  - "There were no demons in Vrindavan until Krishna came." It was a peaceful little pastoral scene — but as soon as the Supreme Absolute Truth appeared in Vrindavan, the demons started coming. So opposition is healthy — a sign of success.

## Everyone Here Is Inimical

- We are preaching in a place "where everyone has come to forget Krishna" — everyone is inimical to a greater or lesser degree. From **Brahma** down to the **indragopa** (the smallest insect/germ), everyone is here.
- One time a devotee said to Mahaprabhu, "You have so much bhava, so much love for Krishna." Lord Chaitanya replied (in humility, being the embodiment of pure love): **"If I had any love, any bhava, any bhakti for Krishna, I wouldn't be here — I'd be there, where love reigns supreme."**
- Anyone who comes to this world has come because of "the cardinal sin," as the Christians say — "they've turned their face away from God, they've turned their back on God." Even **Brahma** — although our Brahma is an exception, being a great devotee.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** said, "I would rather take birth in the house of a devotee as an insect than become a non-devotee Brahma." The position of Brahma is described as the first birth as one falls from grace — there are no sinful activities before it, "just some misuse of independence" — and from there it's all downhill.
- So everyone is inimical, and we should expect opposition.
  - Prabhupada said, **"Don't expect things to go right. Expect things to go wrong. Don't be surprised if things go wrong — be surprised if things go right."**
  - Preaching is a little difficult — "it's like drinking hot sugar juice: it burns the lips, but at the same time it's so sweet you can't stop."

## The Leprosy of the Karma-kandi Brahmana

- As a result of the powerful preaching of Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj, the local Brahmanas around Keturi — addicted to **karma-kanda** and **jnana-kanda** teachings, which they said were meant only for a select few — became very upset.
- One day a very learned, powerful Brahmana came to Narottama: "I am a karma-kandi Brahmana. When I saw you, a **Kayastha**, preaching spiritual knowledge — who are you to preach? You're not a Brahmana. And then I heard you were initiating people. I won't tolerate you."
  - (Narottama was not born in a Brahmana family; he was **Kayastha** — "something in between, like Vaishya and Kshatriya, a sub-category.")
- This Brahmana, described as a beautiful, relatively young man, woke up the next morning to find his body diseased with **leprosy.** So he fell at the feet of Narottama Das Thakura and begged forgiveness.
  - This is the process: if we offend a great devotee of the Lord, we must go personally to that devotee and beg his forgiveness — and the Vaishnava should always forgive.
- Narottama, the great compassionate devotee, put his feet on the head of that Brahmana and said, "Stand up and dance." The man was embarrassed — leprosy all over his body, and he's supposed to dance in public. But Narottama said, "Dance!"
- Narottama started singing his special flavor of kirtan — so empowered that it could melt even the most steel-framed, icy-cold, mundane, materialistic, offensive heart. As he sang, the Brahmana danced and danced and danced — and, to the amazement of all the Vaishnavas, the symptoms of his disease began to disappear: one finger, then another, then another, then the other hand; his ears, his nose, his toes — all became cured, and he again looked beautiful.
- All the Vaishnavas felt so much ecstasy at this miracle that they got up and started chanting. The kirtan went on all night. At the end, the Brahmana fell at Narottama's feet: "Please accept me as your disciple." Narottama picked him up, embraced him, and said, "I accept you." **That was the initiation ceremony.**
  - Aside on initiation: "Nowadays we have the Agni-hotra... the channa-mantra." But really "it's a question of the heart in the long run." The ceremony is meant to create an impression of how serious our vows and commitment are; "really, it's an affair of the heart." That Brahmana was thus a duly initiated disciple, and he himself became a powerful preacher.

## The Two Goat-herd Boys — Hari Rama and Ramakrishna

- Sometime later, Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj did everything together — their sadhana, their prasadam, their preaching, their rest.
- One late morning they were chanting their rounds down by the bank of the Ganges (they taught by example) and saw two young boys, about 11, 12, or 13 years of age, herding goats and sheep.
- It was the time of year for **Durga Puja**, when they often offered flesh to Durga. Narottama and Ramchandra understood that these boys were herding the sheep and goats to the temple for slaughter, and took compassion on both the animals and the boys.
- What they did: they walked near where the boys were, and began speaking philosophy back and forth in an animated, quite loud way, so the boys could overhear. "Here you have two very deep, very advanced Maha-bhagavata devotees speaking philosophy" — powerful, purifying, inspiring. By hearing them, the boys became convinced of Gaudiya Vaishnava philosophy, including how we are kind to the lower species of life.
- The boys wandered over. Narottama asked their names. One: "My name's Hari Rama." The other: "My name's Ramakrishna." "We are the sons of **Shivaya Acharya**, a great devotee of Durga. Our father told us to bring these animals to sacrifice on Durga Puja a few days from now. But by hearing from you two great souls, we realize how sinful this is — so we're letting all the animals go." And they chased and freed all the goats and sheep.
- "But now, if we go back to our father, King Shiva, we'll be severely punished. So we can only take shelter of both of you. Please accept us." Narottama and Ramchandra: "Okay, come on."
- They took the boys back to their ashram, enlightened them in the philosophy of Krishna consciousness, and taught them the art of chanting **Hare Krishna.** In the association of such great souls spiritual life is accelerated — **Chaitanya-charitamrita** — *"sadhu-sanga, sadhu-sanga — sarva-shastre kaya / lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya"* — "even a moment's association with a devotee brings all perfection."
- After a few days these goat-herd boys became so advanced and surrendered that **Ramchandra Kaviraj initiated Hari Rama**, and **Narottama Das Thakura initiated Ram Krishna.**

## The Boys Convert Their Father's Court

- The boys said, "Now we have to go home." Narottama: "But you were afraid." "We're not afraid to go home now. We want to face our father — he's a great worshiper of Durga — we want to set things straight."
- On the way, they met the king's (their father's) **physician**, who had been out looking for them: "Your father has been wondering where you are. Four or five days ago he sent you to get the goats for slaughter. Where are the goats? Where have you been?"
- Hari Rama said, "We have to discuss something." They sat down, and Hari Rama shared the philosophy heard from Narottama and Ramchandra with **Balarama** (the father's doctor) — and convinced him in forty-five minutes. Balarama was also a great devotee of Durga, yet these young boys converted this well-educated, intelligent doctor to Gaudiya Vaishnava philosophy in forty-five minutes. "They were now empowered to preach Krishna consciousness."
- When they got home, their father was flying in rage, screaming: "Where are the goats? Where is the blood and the flesh? And what is this rumor that you've been associating with the sentimental Bhaktas, the Vaishnavas, who worship Vishnu, Krishna, as supreme? Don't you know that **Durga Ma, Bhavani**, is supreme? I'll set you straight — talk with some of the brahmanas in the court."
- Hari Rama: "Bring them here." The king called the pundits: "Set these boys straight." The discussion went on for some time, and then the pundits turned to the king: "Something's changed in these boys — they're empowered. We were totally defeated. In fact, we don't think anyone can defeat this Vaishnava philosophy."
- The king, furious: "You're finished! Throw you out of my court!" He couldn't tolerate that Vishnu or Krishna was said to be supreme over Durga, and he was angry that Narottama and Ramchandra had polluted his sons' hearts.

## The Defeat of the Digvijai Murari

- The king declared, "I'll call a **Digvijai** to debate with you." (A Digvijai is a pundit who has conquered all other pundits in debate.) His name was **Murari**, and he lived in the town of **Matila**, some distance away. "He'll defeat you, and then you'll surrender." Ramakrishna: "Yes, bring him here."
- A debate was arranged. For a little fun, Ramakrishna and Hari Rama sent the court physician, **Balaram**, to debate: "You debate with him." "Me?" "Yes — we explained the philosophy to you."
- So Balaram — who had heard only 45 minutes of philosophy, but from the mouths of boys blessed by Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj — sat down with a Digvijai who had never been defeated in his entire life, was in his 60s, and wandered around India debating.
  - In those days, if you defeated someone, they became your disciple; if they didn't want to, they had to take their life — suicide.
- The Digvijai sat down with great confidence. Within no time, Murari was humiliated, shamed, and defeated in philosophy by Balaram the court doctor. So embarrassed was he that he took off his fancy dress and became a beggar, begging door to door; no one respected him anymore.
- The father was upset — the court brahmanas couldn't defeat his contaminated sons, and now the Digvijai was defeated. With no one else to turn to, he went to sleep, and **Durga appeared to him**: "Vishnu is supreme. I'm His humble servant, just doing my thankless task of keeping the conditioned souls in this material world. Lord Vishnu, Lord Krishna, is my worshipable Lord. And if you don't accept Him as your worshipable Lord, I'll make sure that your head rolls."
- King Shiva woke in the middle of the night, perspiring, and the next morning he also went to Narottama Das Thakura, begged forgiveness, and asked to become his disciple. "This was powerful opposition, but by the pure-heartedness and determination of these Vaishnavas, they were successful."

## Ganga Narayan Chakravarti Surrenders

- At **Gambila**, on the bank of the Ganges, there was another Brahmana — **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** — very high-class, learned, and pure. He used to see Hari Ram and Ram Krishna out herding the cows, but then heard how these two boys had been converted, initiated by Narottama, and could now defeat great Brahmins and Digvijais. He was astounded: "Narottama — from where is he getting his mercy, his blessings? He turned two goat herdsmen into saints."
- He began to lament: "I've been practicing this **jnana-karma** all my life, and I haven't achieved the status of these two young boys who became saints in a matter of days. Who is this Narottama Das Thakura? I'm wasting my life. I want to go and surrender to him."
- In that mood he fell asleep, and Narottama appeared in his dream: "Tomorrow I'll be very happy to meet you down at the bank of the Ganges at 12 noon." Then he disappeared.
- Ganga Narayan woke excited and waited with great expectation. At noon he ran to the bank of the Ganges — and there he found **Hari Rama and Ramakrishna** doing their bhajan. He immediately bowed down to the goat herdsmen, "in the eyes of the masses": "Where is your guru, the great saint Sri Narottama Das Thakura? Do you think he would accept such a fool as me?" Hari Rama pointed: "Gurudeva is coming." Looking up the road, Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj were walking there.
- As they came close, Ganga Narayan Chakravarti fell down at their feet and begged to become their disciple. Narottama immediately picked him up, embraced him, and accepted him. "But what will all the brahmanas think? You're the head of the brahmanas in this area, and you're surrendering to me." Ganga Narayan: "I don't really care what they think." Narottama asked him to go stay with **Govinda Chakravarti** and learn Krishna consciousness.

## The Brahmanas Rebel — and the Merchant Disguise

- This news spread very quickly, because Ganga Narayan was a powerful personality. Then the rumor spread that another of the great acharyas, **Jagannath Acharya**, had also surrendered to Narottama. He was converting all the brahmanas.
- The leaders of the brahmanas became very upset. The supreme leader, **Rupa Narayan**, went to the big king in that area, **Raja Narasingha**: "You're the king; you have to set things straight. We brahmanas are the gods on earth — when we eat the offerings, it is as if Vishnu is eating through us; we disseminate spiritual knowledge and set a nice example; we must be worshiped. But this Narottama is causing so many problems that brahmanas are going and worshiping him — a man from the lower caste. Our whole culture will be finished — brahmanas surrendering to the lower classes?"
- The king was very equipoised and diplomatic; he didn't take sides. Raja Narasingha: "If you're so confident that you are right and he is wrong, we'll settle it in a civil way — we'll have a debate, and you can prove that he is wrong." Rupa Narayan: "Okay. I'm the head of the brahmanas; we'll get that Digvijai (now a beggar), gather all the brahmanas, go to Keturi, have a public debate with Narottama, and defeat him."
- A big procession started out — hundreds of brahmanas, the king at the front, Rupa Narayan right behind him, then the Digvijai and all the learned brahmanas.
- The word reached Narottama in Keturi. He smiled: "We're going to have a very nice debate." But his disciples, **Ramchandra Kaviraj and Ganga Narayan Chakravarti**, didn't want their spiritual master to have to go through all this. "Why should our Guru Maharaj have to take on these fools and rascals? We'll do it ourselves."
- What they did: they went outside Keturi some distance to a little roadside market and posed themselves as simple merchants (though they are the highest-order **Pukka Vaishnavas**). They put on the Vaishya dress:
  - **Ramchandra Kaviraj** pretended to sell **betel nuts.**
  - **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** (the great Brahmana turned Vaishnava) set up a stall selling **clay pots** (for water or yogurt).
- The grand procession came on the way to Keturi and stopped there for refreshments — a halfway rest stop (like the road from Delhi to Vrindavan, where the taxis have their favorite chai stop).

## Defeated by the Pot-seller and the Betel-nut Seller

- The pundits sent their students to get refreshments and betel nuts. The students asked the price of the betel nut and the clay pot, and Ramchandra Kaviraj and Ganga Narayan **replied in Sanskrit.** The common people in Bengal don't speak Sanskrit, so the students were shocked: "How could you low-class merchants know Sanskrit?"
- With great pride, they said, "Because we are disciples of **Narottama Das Thakura.**" Those students, having heard of this Kayastha who initiates brahmanas, started blaspheming him: "We've heard of him — he's a great offender, destroying our culture; he's of a lower caste and he's initiating brahmanas; he's just a sentimental bhakta."
- Ramchandra Kaviraj and Ganga Narayan started defeating them in Sanskrit — quoting so many references from Shastra, giving logic and reasoning, debating — and the students were completely defeated. They went back and reported, "We have a problem here." The pundits pushed them aside: "We'll go debate with this pot-seller and betel-nut seller." They went — and were also defeated.

## The King and Rupa Narayan Reconsider

- **Rupa Narayan** (head of the community) and **King Raja Narasingha** were watching this whole thing and, with their own eyes and ears, saw these pundits defeated by the betel-nut and pot sellers.
- The king (who had been asked to resolve the crisis) concluded: "If the simple betel-nut-leaf sellers and clay-pot disciples of Narottama Das Thakura have defeated us, what hope do we have challenging Narottama himself? I think it's better to surrender to Narottama."
- Rupa Narayan: "But what will we tell all the pundits? They're all following me. What am I going to tell them if I surrender?" The king: "Well, think about that." "Some problems are solved immediately; some take a little time; some take a long time to solve." The king couldn't come up with an immediate answer.

## Durga's Threat and the Silent Procession of Surrender

- That night everyone went to sleep, and **Durga appeared in the dream of every one of those brahmanas**, threatening: "Surrender to Narottama, or die." Being intelligent brahmanas, they concluded it was better to surrender.
- But they had a dilemma: their leader, Rupa Narayan, might have the king chop off their heads. Still, they figured Durga was more powerful. So they went to Rupa Narayan and the king: "We've thought about it — remember how they were speaking the other day — and we're thinking that maybe we want to surrender to Narottama." The king: "Well, okay." And it was resolved.
- They put away the palanquins and chariots, sent the horses back, and all walked to Keturi on foot — very quiet and silent, with their heads bowed, a silent procession with no fanfare or opulence. In front was Rupa Narayan, behind him the king, and then the hundreds of Brahmanas in order of their importance.
- They walked into Keturi and asked, "Where is Narottama Das Thakura?" Narottama came out of his kutir. Rupa Narayan and King Narasingha fell at his feet, touched his feet, begged forgiveness for their offenses, and asked to become his disciples. "Okay, stand up." He embraced and accepted them. "But I want to train you in this Krishna consciousness, so stay with me here for a while."
- They lived there for some time with Narottama and Ramchandra Kaviraj, enlightened in the teachings of **Sri Rupa Goswami** and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- After some time, King Narasingha's wife (the queen) showed up: "Where have you been? You didn't come home." He shared and convinced her, and the next morning the queen went and surrendered to Narottama, and he accepted her as his disciple. By this time he had many, many disciples. "In this way he defeated the Brahmanas — he converted them all."

## The Dacoits of Keturi — and Vishvanatha Chakravarti's Practical Letter

- In that area of Keturi lived many **dacoits** ("we shouldn't be surprised — dacoits are everywhere").
- **Digression:** One of the speaker's disciples, **Nila Madhava**, was doing PhD research into Gaudiya Vaishnavism and had access to the museum in **Jaipur**, where confidential papers, records, and old letters are kept. In his research he found a letter from **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakur** to **Jai Singh**, the king of Jaipur.
  - In it, Vishvanatha wrote: "You have to stop all these dacoits on the road from Vrindavan. All the pilgrims coming from Vrindavan to take darshan of the Goswami deities — **Govindaji, Damodar, and Gopinath** — are being attacked by dacoits. You're the king — do something about it."
  - "I thought that was amazing. We hear of Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakur writing these eloquent shastras that go very deep into the esoteric philosophy — but here's just a practical letter he wrote to the king: 'There are so many dacoits on the road down from Vrindavan to Jaipur; please get rid of these guys.'"
- So here in Keturi, despite such elevated souls as Narottama, Ramchandra Kaviraj, and Ganga Narayan Chakravarti and their converted pure-hearted Vaishnavas, there were dacoits. "That's India."

## Hari Chandarai, King of the Dacoits, and the Brahmana Rakshasa Ghost

- The most infamous of all those dacoits was named **Hari Chandarai.** Born a Brahmana, he had fallen down; he was a master of every weapon imaginable, "his heart as cold as ice and hard as steel." He'd murder anyone for any little thing. All the dacoits feared him; he got the infamous title "the king of the dacoits." He assembled an army of dacoits and went through the area plundering villages — raping the women, killing the men, taking their wealth. No one could stop him.
- But he got some **instant karma** ("as John Lennon had that song"). Because of his offenses to innocent people, the Shastra describes that eventually a ghost entered his body and began torturing him from the inside. And this was no ordinary ghost — it was a **Brahmana Rakshasa ghost.**
  - (Very sinful Brahmanas, or those who fall down or commit suicide, become Brahmana Rakshasa ghosts — difficult to get rid of.)
  - **Aside on these ghosts:** Sometimes you can see them. "In Mayapur, in the old days, devotees would see them, and the local Brahmanas would confirm it." They walk at dusk down the road, "and you can recognize them because they have **chicken feet** — human bodies, but feet like chickens." They become more manifest at dusk, "when things are neither here nor there and kind of hazy." Devotees who saw people with chicken feet were terrified, and the locals said, "This is a Brahmana Rakshasa — you lickety-split the other way."
- This Brahmana Rakshasa ghost, entering Chandarai's body because of his sins, tortured him so much that he couldn't do anything — just withering in pain, rolling on the ground. His father brought all kinds of doctors, mystics, and exorcists — no one was successful; all concluded it was a Brahmana Rakshasa ghost.
- In desperation, Chandarai began calling out for the mercy of **Narottama Das Thakura.** He told his father he aspired to get the mercy of this great soul, so his father sent a message to Keturi: "My son is very fallen, but he's wanting your mercy."

## The Four Kinds Who Turn to God — the Astronaut Story

- It happens sometimes. There are four types of people who surrender to Krishna: the **curious**, those **looking for knowledge**, those **looking for some material facility**, and the **distressed.** Prabhupada said that of the four, mainly it's those in distress who turn to God.
- **Anecdote:** In the '60s the Russians sent the first Sputnik with a manned astronaut into outer space, beating the Americans. Khrushchev, through the radio (broadcast across Russia), asked, "So, do you see God?" The astronaut looked out the little window: "Nope, I didn't see him." Khrushchev: "So we can conclude there is no God." But later there was a mechanical failure, and the Sputnik started crashing toward the earth. The last radio contact was from the astronaut: **"My dear God, please save me."** So in distress, someone might turn.

## Narottama Prays and Receives Chaitanya's Order

- The first messenger taking Chandarai's message to Keturi thought, "There's no way I'm going to deliver this message. This guy's not going to get any mercy — he's been the source of misery to so many people that he doesn't deserve the mercy of Narottama." So he tore up the message and threw it away. The father waited and waited, but no answer came, so he sent another messenger, who did his duty and took the message to Narottama.
- Narottama knew about this king of the dacoits. He called in his confidential associates — **Ramakrishna, Hari Ram, and Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** (now Pukka Vaishnavas): "What should I do? Should I go?" They said, "You're our spiritual master; you have to decide."
- So one evening Narottama sat down, went into deep meditation and **samadhi**, and prayed to Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. He had a vision, and Lord Chaitanya appeared: **"Yes, you go. And through you, I will personally deliver this dacoit — to prove the power of the holy names and give faith to the masses."**
- He wanted to give people faith that this is the cure for the disease of material life. Analogy: if a doctor cures a simple infection, that's not a big thing, but if someone finds the cure for cancer or malaria, "that's going to be big news; it's going to spread very fast." So by delivering someone like the king of the dacoits through the power of the holy name, people would listen: "Whoa, this is something."

## The Power of the Glance — Prabhupada's Darshan

- Narottama went there and found the dacoit withering in pain on the floor, screaming, tortured from within by the Brahmana Rakshasa ghost. Narottama spoke to the ghost, not to the dacoit: **"In the name of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, I order you to leave."**
- The ghost said: "I thought you'd never come. I was waiting for you, Narottama. Due to my despicable karma, I had to live within the body of this horrible dacoit. And because you glanced upon us, both of us are now free. This is the power of your glance — you just glanced at us, and we were free."
- **Digression on the spiritual master's glance:** Some of us remember that when Prabhupada was here, if he just so much as glanced at us, we felt blessed. "That was the reward for everything we'd done for him — we'd abandoned our families, given up material life, stopped all sinful activities, and were working day and night to spread the Sankirtan movement with no remuneration, just the satisfaction of serving Guru and Gauranga. We didn't desire anything in return. But when Prabhupada happened to glance at you, you just felt — 'that's all I need.' Just one glance, to be recognized by the spiritual master; that's all we wanted. And it was enough. It carries you through lifetimes, back home, back to Godhead."
- **Personal story (New Mayapur):** Prabhupada was giving darshan on the grass outside the chateau. The speaker sat in the front, wanting Prabhupada to see him, "just to know that I existed." But Prabhupada never looked at him; the speaker moved this way and that — nothing. Then Prabhupada asked, "Are there any questions?" The speaker thought, "Here's my chance," and raised his hand — but he didn't actually have a question; he just wanted to be recognized. Put on the spot, he improvised:
  - **The question:** "Srila Prabhupada, you were saying that a pure devotee of the Lord, due to his pure vision and his love, sees Krishna everywhere. But then in the **Shikshashtakam** prayers we hear Lord Chaitanya saying, 'O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I'm considering a moment to be like twelve years, and tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain.' It appears contradictory, Prabhupada, that the pure devotee is seeing Krishna everywhere and at the same time experiencing separation. I don't understand."
  - Prabhupada looked at him for a long time (the speaker felt Prabhupada was looking into his heart) and finally said: **"The answer to your question is, actually, you can't understand."** "I really felt like a fool. But then he said, 'But one day, when you're very advanced, then you will understand.' 'Oh, thank you, Prabhupada.'"
  - The point: "You want to be noticed. Just a glance is enough."

## Chandarai Is Delivered and Initiated

- The ghost continued: "I was so despicable — my sins were such that I was placed in the body of this rascal. But you just glanced at us, Narottama Das Thakura, and by your glancing we've been freed from all our sins, and all our material desires have been removed. My prayer to you is that one day, when I'm freed from this ethereal, ghostly form, I'll take birth in the Holy Dham of Keturi and take shelter of your lotus feet." Then, "poof, he was gone."
- The dacoit king woke up, and everyone shivered with fear ("Oh no, he's waking up"). But he was very humble. He turned to all the Vaishnavas and paid his obeisance with the **Vaishnava-pranama** — *"vanchha-kalpa-tarubhyash cha kripa-sindhubhya eva cha / patitanam pavanebhyo vaishnavebhyo namo namah"* — "I offer my respects to the Vaishnavas, who are like desire-trees, oceans of mercy, and the deliverers of the fallen souls." Then he saw Narottama Das and fell at his feet: "Please accept me as your disciple." Narottama: "Okay." He picked him up and embraced him.
- **The real adventure:** Krishna says in the Gita that He is adventure. "What is the adventure? Not a trek to the Rocky Mountains, not skiing on Mount Kilimanjaro. The real adventure is preaching Krishna consciousness. Do it, and there is satisfaction."

## The Yavana King's Revenge — the Pit and the Elephant

- The exploits of Narottama were spreading by word of mouth. Some distance away was one **Yavana king** ("Yavana, Mleccha, Rakshasa — that was us before Krishna consciousness"), who previously had had bad relations with Chandarai the dacoit king.
- Chandarai wasn't a king, but he had an army of dacoits, and was so powerful that he kept defeating this real Yavana king, who even had to pay taxes to the dacoit king — very embarrassing. Now the Yavana king heard that Chandarai had changed — become a simple, humble Vaishnava, a disciple of Narottama, "not a powerful dacoit anymore, just a simple bhakta with a bead bag." "Now I can get my revenge."
- He sent his best soldiers, who found Hari Chandarai taking his bath in the Ganges, dragged him out of the water, and took him back to the Yavana king. The king had his soldiers dig a very deep hole, put the humble bhakta (completely transformed, always chanting Hare Krishna) in an **iron cage**, and lowered him to the bottom of the pit, then went away. Sometimes they would come and pour scalding water down into the pit and throw garbage in; his only foodstuff was rotten garbage, and some people would even pass stool and urine into the pit.
- The whole time, Hari Chandarai remained completely aloof. By the mercy of Narottama, he had become "mad with chanting the holy name" — "this type of madness we want." He just kept chanting Hare Krishna.

## The Tantric's Kali Mantra Refused

- His father was so aggrieved that he put out a public notice: he would pay a huge sum of money to anyone who could free his son from this embarrassing, painful condition.
- A **tantric mystic** came: "I can do it — you pay me when I do it." (Aside: "This is not mythology; this is all true historical accounts. We think we have it bad when we go out on Sankirtan on a hot day. Look what these Vaishnavas went through to spread Krishna consciousness — and they weren't even aspiring devotees, they were fully self-realized, fully blossomed Vaishnavas.")
- The tantric dug a hole and popped his head through the earth near the cage: "I've come to rescue you. It's not going to be easy — we need the grace of Mother Durga/Kali. I'll give you a mantra to the goddess Durga/Kali; you chant it, and we'll free ourselves."
- When Chandarai heard he was supposed to chant a mantra to a demigod, he refused: "Don't you understand that I am a disciple of Narottama Das Thakura? I've been initiated into the Krishna mantra. Why would I, in my wildest imagination, glorify some demigod? The demigods are all servants of Krishna; they get their power from Krishna. I'm very happy just to sit here and chant Hare Krishna. My guru has given me a mantra; I'm not going to accept a mantra from anyone else. I'm a faithful, loyal disciple; I will not chant some Kali mantra. Go tell my father, who sent you: I'm very happy chanting Hare Krishna. My only suffering is that I'm feeling separation from my Guru Maharaj — from the lotus feet of that great acharya, Narottama Das Thakura, who delivered me." The mystic left and went back to the father.

## The Public Execution and the Elephant

- After a few days the Yavana king became very upset, hearing that although he was torturing and starving Chandarai, he remained happy in the hole. "Bring up the cage! We're going to have a public execution." He announced that Chandarai would be decimated by a wild elephant, and called all the townspeople. (Chandarai was famous — previously infamous, now famous by the mercy of Narottama.)
- They pulled up the cage, put him in iron shackles, and dragged him to the marketplace. They made a male elephant very angry, ferocious, and mad, and let him loose. The elephant charged toward Chandarai, who was lying on the ground, unable to move after so long in the cage — to stab him with its tusks and stamp on him (one way they tortured people in those days).
- Chandarai saw the mad elephant coming and prayed to his Guru Maharaj: **"Narottama Das Thakura, I'm your servant — in happiness, in distress, in heat, in cold, in life, and in death. You do with me as you wish. I'm your surrendered soul."**
- Just when the elephant came to grab him with its trunk and break his neck — Chandarai stood up, grabbed the elephant's trunk, swung the elephant around in front of everyone's eyes, snapped off its trunk, and the elephant fell dead to the ground.

## The Yavana King's Heart Melts

- The Yavana king was astounded: "Bring him here. Take off the iron shackles." He had Chandarai sit on the asana with him. "I want to know how you did that. How did you survive for so many weeks in that bottomless, filthy pit? How did you kill that wild elephant and break off his trunk?"
- Chandarai: "I prayed to my Guru Maharaj — that's all I can say." The king: "But when I had you under the ground, why didn't you beg for mercy to get out? Why did you tolerate that situation?" Chandarai: "I understood it was my karma. I was prepared to receive the sinful reaction of all those poor souls whom I offended, and I accepted it as my purification."
- The king's heart was melted; he was so impressed. "I'm sending you home." He gave him great honor and distinction — a **turban of honor** and a special procession all the way back to Keturi.
- Back home, Chandarai chastised his father: "Why did you send that mystic tantric who tried to give me a mantra to Kali?" The father wept: "But you're my son — I just wanted to save you." Chandarai: "You have to understand: **if Krishna wants to kill you, no one can save you; and if Krishna wants to save you, no one can kill you.**"
- Aside: "Again, this is not a bedtime story. This should give us great faith in the Sankirtan movement of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — and there are also modern-day pastimes of how Mahaprabhu works through the lives of His devotees to save the conditioned souls."
- The two of them stayed there and worshiped the beloved deities of Narottama Das Thakura in Keturi, and it is described they also ate immense amounts of the Mahaprasadam that those deities were eating.

## The Gang of Dacoits Converted — "Bring Me All the Dacoits"

- Another gang of dacoits heard the story of Chandarai and couldn't believe it — that the king of the dacoits had become a very humble Vaishnava. They knew him. So they came to Keturi to see if it was true, and met Chandarai. "Let's sit down and talk for a little bit." After a few hours he converted all of those dacoits into Vaishnavas and took them to Narottama Das Thakura; they surrendered, and Narottama initiated them.
- And what instruction did Narottama give them? **"Go out and look for more dacoits."** ("Go out — as Bhaktivinoda Thakura was saying, 'preach the divine command' — and find more dacoits. Birds of a feather flock together. Bring all the dacoits in the land to me.")
- The country was infested "like with wasps" — hundreds of gangs of dacoit bands. These converted dacoits went out, brought the other dacoits back, and they became devotees. So all the dacoits in Keturi were transformed into devotees by the mercy of Narottama Das Thakura.

## Virachandra, Son of Nityananda, Comes to Keturi

- This amazing thing reached the ears of **Virachandra**, the son of Lord Nityananda. He heard that Narottama was in Keturi, delivering all these dacoits and lowborn people and making them into wonderful Vaishnavas. "I have to witness this with my own eyes."
- The very son of Nityananda went to Keturi. **Santosh** (the reigning ruler) received him very nicely, set him up, and gave everything needed for his puja; all the pujaris were there.
- Virachandra had received a deity from the hand of his father, Nityananda Prabhu — a **Govardhan Shila** (Nityananda had picked one up from Govardhan Hill and given it to his son to worship). It is said that Virachandra would never eat anything that was not first offered to that Govardhan Shila — you could invite him to your house, but first the prasad had to be offered to his Govardhan Shila.
- Virachandra came to meet Narottama while there was a kirtan going on — led by Narottama, his special kirtan, his special type of dancing. And when Virachandra, the son of Nityananda, came to that kirtan, he too began to dance in ecstasy.

## The Whole Region Drowns in Ecstasy

- This converting-into-Vaishnavas went on, and suddenly Narottama had thousands and thousands of disciples. He demonstrated the power of the **Maha Mantra.** People had so much faith that this mantra, Hare Krishna, could deliver anyone and everyone, that spontaneously people began chanting Hare Krishna, and "the whole Keturi region became drowned in ecstasy."

## Manipur Converted — 450 Years Ago

- Narottama sent **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** to a very tribal place where only tribal people lived, worshiping various strange demigods and demigoddesses — a place called **Manipur** — "just tribals, wild, beastly-like people." He sent Ganga Narayan up there alone — "just like Krishna sent Prabhupada alone to this beastly, wild place called America."
- Ganga Narayan went there, met the **king of Manipur**, and converted him. The king made a decree that everyone in his country had to become a Vaishnava. As a result, everyone became Vaishnava, took on the same style of kirtan as Narottama Das Thakura, stopped the worship of demigods, and installed the deities of **Radha-Krishna, Jagannath-Subhadra-Balaram, and Gaura-Nitai — 450 years ago.**
- Even today, if you go to Manipur — to **Imphal**, the capital, and to the outlying region — there's only one style of temple. "In India there are so many styles of temples: some worship Krishna alone, some Radha-Krishna, some Jagannath, some demigods. But in Manipur all the temples are the same — in every temple there's Radha-Krishna, Jagannath-Subhadra-Baladeva, and on the other side Gaura-Nitai."
- **The speaker's own visit:** He saw it, going with **Sri Prahlad**, invited there with Prahlad's **"Village of Peace"** band to convert the teenagers back to their culture. They went to the most far-off, distant places in Manipur; people were surprised: "You're coming here? This is the tribal areas." Visiting a temple: Radha-Krishna, Jagannath-Subhadra, Gaura-Nitai.
- **The Maoist attack:** They did one concert in a big fenced-off area, and all the young people came. But because the Maoists/communists were also "preaching" there, the youth had been converted to Maoism, "their worshipable deity Lenin and Marx." They had a plan — big rocks in their pockets. As soon as the band started ("Village of Peace, Village of Peace"), a huge volley of rocks flew — it broke the drummer's bass drums, smashed the bass guitarist's guitar, and hit one devotee in the shoulder; Prahlad was ducking the rocks. "These kids were angry, they wanted blood." Within moments the band had to jump down and hide under the stage. The police came and tried to move them to shelters as the rocks broke the windows. The army had been in trucks nearby (expecting trouble) — about 300 soldiers charged the roughly 1,000 kids with lathis and sticks; it was a big fight. They put the devotees in jeeps and drove them out of town.
- "These are the type of places Narottama sent his preachers to — but they were successful, and generally converted the people."

## Narottama Sends the Disciples Home — Losing Ramchandra Kaviraj

- His mission was successful. One day Narottama, in a very grave mood, called his senior men — **Ganga Narayan, Rupa Narayan, Hari Ram, Chandarai, and King Narasingha**: "Go to your homes and stay there until I call you back." They didn't understand what was going on; it was very difficult for them to leave his association, but they left.
- Then Narottama went to a private place and had a long conversation with **Ramchandra Kaviraj** — and "to this day no one knows what they talked about."
- Ramchandra Kaviraj, his very dear friend, was called to **Yajigram** to meet his Guru Maharaj, **Srinivas Acharya.** Word came back to Keturi that Srinivas Acharya wanted to take Ramchandra Kaviraj with him to **Vrindavan** — "because they're all Brajabasis, like us: they do their service and then go back to Vrindavan as our real home."
- Srinivas sent a message that he was taking Ramchandra Kaviraj. When Narottama heard, "his whole life fell apart" — he couldn't imagine being separated from Ramchandra Kaviraj for any amount of time. But he had no choice but to let him go, because Srinivas was a little senior.

## The Departure of Ramchandra Kaviraj and Srinivas Acharya

- They went to Vrindavan, and somehow, a couple of months later, the news arrived in Keturi that after coming to Vrindavan, **Ramchandra Kaviraj gave up his life.** Then **Srinivas Acharya**, hearing that his most beloved disciple had left, could not bear the separation, and in that mood he too gave up his life.
- They were placed in **samadhi** near to each other. "There's that temple near **Gopishwara Mahadev**, down near the **Braja Mohan temple**, with a courtyard where you can visit the samadhi mandir of Ramchandra Kaviraj, the samadhi mandir of Srinivas Acharya, and his daughter **Hemlata Goswamini** — a very nice little courtyard where we often have our programs when we do Braja Mandala Parikrama."
- No one wanted to tell Narottama, because they knew how devastating it would be — he was already lamenting in great separation. Finally the disciples realized somebody had to say something, so **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** was deputed to go and tell Narottama that his most beloved friend Ramchandra Kaviraj had left this world — and his other friend, with whom he had started the whole thing (studying in Vrindavan with Srinivas and Shyamananda, taking the books) — that they had departed. "Close friends, deep relations."
- It took him days to work up the courage. One morning he walked up the hill (Narottama had a little bhajan kutir upon the hill), approached very slowly, and stood outside the hut for a long time getting up his courage. Finally he said, "Guru Maharaj, can I speak to you?" By the tone of his voice, Narottama said, "What's wrong?"
- Without going inside — he couldn't bear to see Narottama plunge into grief — Ganga Narayan said, "My dear lord and master, Ramchandra Kaviraj has left this world." He could hear the cry of grief come from Narottama's heart as he fell to the ground sobbing and weeping. Narottama said, "Is that all?" Ganga Narayan: "Guru Maharaj, Srinivas Acharya has left this world." And Narottama plunged into the deepest depths of lamentation, suffering, and crying. Ganga Narayan didn't know what to do, so he left.

## The Song of Separation

- Soon after, the devotees saw Narottama run into the jungle, where alone he was weeping and weeping. There in the jungle he composed his **famous song of separation from the Vaishnavas** — the song we sing on the disappearance day of great acharyas, the spiritual master, and our devotees. "That song just brings you into a certain mood — that was the melody he also presented."
- After some time the devotees heard him crying day after day, so they went in to give him some solace; but seeing him in that condition and hearing his crying, they also fell on the ground. "This huge group of devotees, just lamenting and lamenting."

## Narottama Prepares to Leave — the Funeral Pyre

- One week later he brought all his disciples out of the forest and took them immediately to the bank of the Ganges: **"Build my funeral pyre."**
- The devotees were also feeling separation from Ramchandra Kaviraj and Srinivas Acharya, but when they heard their spiritual master indicate that he was leaving, no one could move. Understanding that they had to follow their spiritual master's instructions, they began building the pyre.
- For three days Narottama didn't speak, and gradually his life faded away from his body. His disciples fell into their own lamentation and grief, took the body of their Guru Maharaj, and placed it on the funeral pyre. They were performing their rituals and were about to light the fire.

## The Blasphemy of the Envious Brahmanas

- Suddenly some envious brahmanas came by ("here we go again"), stopped, and began blaspheming: "You see, this is the result of his sinful actions — initiating brahmanas, thinking he can preach spiritual knowledge. He's created such a disturbance in our culture, a lowborn man initiating brahmanas; therefore he got the fever of death, couldn't speak, and died."

## The Unique Quality of a Vaishnava — Compassion

- Hearing this blasphemy, **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** manifested the most unique quality of a Vaishnava. Vaishnavas are the repository of all wonderful qualities, but their unique quality — which sets them apart from all other classes of transcendentalists — is their **compassion.**
- We see this revealed in the hearts of great devotees:
  - **Dhruva Maharaj**, who wouldn't go back to Godhead without his mother.
  - **Prahlad Maharaj**, who, after all was said and done, had compassion for his father: when Lord Nrisimhadev asked, "What benediction can I give you?" Prahlad said, "Look at my dad — what You did to my father, what about him?" Nrisimhadev smiled: "Don't worry — by the touch of my hand, he's been liberated, along with ten generations before and ten generations after."
  - **Vasudev Datta**, whose mood was: "Don't just free me from my sins, Mahaprabhu — let me take on the sins of all the conditioned souls in this universe. Let them go back to God; let me stay here as the one person who suffered the sins of all humanity."
  - **Jesus Christ**, who was kind enough to accept the sins of his disciples, and in whose heart we see, "Forgive them, Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do."
- "This is so beyond us." **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** prayed: **"When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest, and with a lowly heart I'll go out to preach the divine command?"** This is what we are aspiring for.
- You can't really have that kind of compassion if you still have material desires, because then you'll be a little jealous of those who have a successful material life — you can't be compassionate if you're thinking "they have something." So, to really get this compassion, we have to come to the stage of cleansing the heart of all **anarthas** — **anartha-nivritti.** When the heart is completely cleansed of all material desires, then we can feel selfless compassion for the sufferings of the fallen conditioned soul. This type of compassion we see in the hearts of great devotees.

## Ganga Narayan's Prayer and the Miracle

- Ganga Narayan stood there listening to the brahmanas blaspheming his spiritual master, thinking: "These poor brahmanas — what kind of sinful reaction are they going to get? How they're going to suffer in hell for unlimited lifetimes by blaspheming my Guru Maharaj." He was thinking of *their* welfare.
- Blaspheming a Vaishnava is the **first offense to the holy name** — even to think ill of a Vaishnava could be more serious than to openly blaspheme. "You may have some reasoning in your mind, but this logic and reasoning doesn't stand in front of Krishna, who loves His devotees."
- So Ganga Narayan (himself another great acharya) went to the funeral pyre, touched the feet of his Guru Maharaj, and prayed to the dead body: **"Guru Maharaj, don't let these brahmins go to hell for their offenses to you. Please save them."**
- And suddenly, one of the most miraculous things in Gaudiya Vaishnava history happened, on the bank of the Ganges, in the presence of all the Vaishnavas, the disciples of Narottama, and those blasphemous Brahmanas: **life came back to the dead body of Narottama Das Thakura.** The symptoms of life and consciousness returned; his body became as effulgent as the sun; and a mystical sacred thread appeared on his body, shining like millions of suns.
- The Brahmins stood back, watching: "He's coming back to life — and he has a Brahmin thread." Narottama sat up, and from the heavens a shower of flowers came down. Narottama said, "Let us go back to Keturi." He climbed off the funeral pyre, and with his disciples walked back to Keturi.

## Deeper into Separation — Prayers to the Deities and Associates

- From that time on he was a little different — he remained very deeply immersed in a mood of separation. When he arrived back, he came before his beloved deities (the golden Gauranga deity and the five others he had installed) and prayed:
  - **"O Gauranga Vallabhi Kanta, please be merciful to me, a very materialistic person. O Radha-Krishna, O Radha-Kanta, Braja Mohan, please free me from the agonies of this material world. O Radha-Raman, I lie at your feet — please bless me that I can remember you in life and in death."**
- From that time on, all he did was serve his deities; he retired.
- One day his disciples saw him running into the forest again and followed him. He began praying — a long prayer invoking the associates of the Lord (as described in Shastra):
  - "O Lord Gauranga Sundar, the moon of Navadvip; O Lord Nityananda; O life of Padmavati; O Advaita, the husband of Sita; O Pandit Gadadhar, Lord of love; O Srivas, the ocean of mercy; O Haridasa; O Vakreshvara; O Sri Rupa, Ramananda, Acharya, Gopinath, Kashishwar, Govinda Ghosh, Mukunda, Vasu Ghosh, Shankar Pandit; O Sri Rupa; O Sanatana; O dwellers of Radha Kunda; O all devotees of Lord Gauranga — all of you, be merciful to me. I fall at your feet. Do not neglect me, because I am a great sinner. Please help me to fulfill my desires."
- He was crying, rolling in the dust, praying to his deities, to the **sakhis**, and to the **manjaris** to help him stay under the lotus feet of Srimati Radharani. Finally he prayed to **Vrindavaneshwari, Srimati Radharani** herself: "Krishna is yours; you are always engaged in His service. Please give me the chance of worshiping you both. I want to place you on a jeweled throne; I will fan you; I will offer you betel nuts — and all my desires will be fulfilled."
- In this deep mood of separation — from Ramchandra Kaviraj, Srinivas Acharya, Lord Chaitanya, Lord Nityananda, the Panchatattva, and all the great devotees who had left — he felt his heart being pierced.

## The Last Act of Mercy — Accepting the Blasphemers

- In that state, **Hari Ram and Ram Krishna** went and gathered all those brahmanas who had blasphemed him at his funeral pyre and brought them to Narottama Das Thakura: "Guru Maharaj, please accept these fallen sinners as your disciples."
- As a last act of mercy, he accepted all those brahmanas — who had been converted by seeing his compassionate nature — as his disciples. They had one last big festival at Keturi, honoring the initiation of those converted brahmanas.

## The Dut Samadhi — Narottama Melts into the Ganges

- Just when the festival finished and everyone had gone home, Narottama collected a few of his very intimate and close associates and disciples, and they went down to the bank of the Ganges. He stood there with his hands folded in great reverence, then walked into the Ganges up to his waist. ("This place we actually visited — the exact spot.")
- He turned to **Ramakrishna and Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** and said, "Massage my body." So those great saints started to massage his body with the water of the Ganges — and as they massaged, his body began to melt into milk and merge with the waters of the Ganga.
- Seeing this happen, their hands were trembling; they wanted to stop, but they had received the order of their guru. The more they massaged him, the more he disappeared, until finally there was nothing left of his transcendental form — just the milk in the water.
- Fortunately, one of the Vaishnavas present, mad with grief and lamentation, took a pot and dipped it into the water — "and they have the **Dut Samadhi** (the 'milk samadhi') of Narottama Das Thakura there."
- Just as Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu disappeared into the form of **Tota Gopinath**, or Lord Nityananda disappeared into the form of **Bankim Rai**, so Narottama's disappearance pastime was to disappear into the Ganges. His disciples took that sacred, divine milk mixed with water and made a samadhi called the **Dut Samadhi.**
- **The speaker's visit:** He went looking for it everywhere, expecting a beautiful samadhi mandir like those in Vrindavan. Finally they found it: one humble, tiny little mound — carved, but not fancy, "about this big." He found two or three there, with writing on them. He asked someone what it said: "**Dut Samadhi** — Guru Maharaj Narottama Das Thakura Mahasaya, Dut Samadhi." They dusted it off, and it read: **"The Dut Samadhi of Siddha Narottama Das Thakura Mahasaya"** — just there, under a tree, on the bank of the river, in an undeveloped area in a village.
- Right next to it was the samadhi of his closest, most favorite disciple, **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti** — also a little smaller than Narottama's, under a tree. They all fell down into the dust, took the dust, and prayed. And then they found the house of Ganga Narayan Chakravarti — where they found the golden deity of Lord Chaitanya.

## Narottama Appears Again — Consoling the Devotees

- After he passed away, the water of the Ganges became turbulent, and the demigods showered flowers. All the devastated devotees returned to Keturi to honor his departure, crying and crying. Devotees came from everywhere — "almost as big as that original Gaura Purnima festival" — from Braj, from Orissa, from Bengal, from South India; everyone wanted to honor this great Vaishnava acharya, Narottama Das Thakura.
- They had kirtan after kirtan, going for hours and days. Suddenly, in the midst of one of the biggest kirtans, Narottama Das Thakura — unable to bear seeing the suffering of all his followers — **appeared and began dancing with them**, just like the Keturi festival all over again; everyone went mad with ecstasy. But then he disappeared, and now they were even more attached, and really fell apart, going on crying.
- And then he **appeared a second time**, went to each and every Vaishnava at that festival, spoke with them, consoled them, and told each one that **he would always be with them** — and then he disappeared.

## Closing
