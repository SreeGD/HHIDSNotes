# The Life and Time fo Srila Narottam Das Thakura Part 1

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 73 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:21:25

## Summary

Delivered on the appearance day of Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura at the Alachua community, this evening class (Part 1) traces how Narottama, understood to be Vilasa Manjari, an intimate maidservant of Radha and Krishna, fulfilled the mission entrusted to him by Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Indradyumna Swami frames the day as one of ISKCON's most important, since it is only through great acharyas that we receive knowledge of Krishna, and he stresses that a genuine devotee is simultaneously a deep rasika and a bold preacher, combining purity ("preaching is the essence, books are the basis, purity is the force") with the mission of sankirtana; real purity, he says, is not mere freedom from material desire but an intense desire to please one's guru and Radha-Krishna. He recounts how Narottama was foretold by Mahaprabhu, who called out "Narottama" during kirtana on the banks of the Padma; how, trained in Vrindavan by Jiva Goswami alongside Srinivasa Acharya and Syamananda, the three carried the Goswamis' only hand-copied manuscripts toward Bengal, where they were stolen by King Bir Hambir; and how Narottama returned to his birthplace Keturi, whose regent, his nephew Santosha Datta, surrendered the entire kingdom to him. The heart of the class narrates Narottama's pilgrimage to obtain the association and blessings of Mahaprabhu's few surviving associates, at Navadvipa, Shantipur (Achyutananda), Kadaha (Jahnava Mata and Virachandra), and Jagannath Puri (Tota-gopinatha and Haridasa Thakura's samadhi), during which repeated dreams and visions of Lord Chaitanya promise that through Narottama a new, heart-melting style of kirtana will flood the universe and that anyone who takes shelter of him will easily attain prema-bhakti. Maharaja tells the vivid stories of Gadadhara das fearlessly inducing a cruel Muslim Kazi to chant Hari, of Lord Nityananda greeting Narottama at Ekachakra disguised as a brahmana, and of Narottama recovering a solid-gold deity of Gauranga (with Vishnupriya) buried in a snake-infested granary, a deity the speaker says he personally saw at Ganga-narayana Chakravarti's home near Mayapur. The narrative climaxes at the great Gaura-purnima festival at Keturi, where Srinivasa Acharya (aided by Narottama's new co-worker Ramachandra Kaviraja, whom Srinivasa gave as "one soul in two bodies") installs six deities named by Narottama, Jahnava Mata composes the famous naming-song, and Narottama's unprecedented kirtana of separation, empowered by Jahnava's merciful glance, so intensifies the mood that the Panchatattva Themselves appear and dance among the devotees, followed by a festival of colors and Jahnava's feast. The class closes with Jahnava Mata, glorified as the compassionate potency of Nityananda, nonviolently converting bands of dacoits and hostile brahmanas who had plotted to kill her, after which Maharaja pauses the story to continue the following night.

## Key Points

- A true devotee is both a deep rasika, relishing the mellows of pure devotion, and an active preacher: "preaching is the essence, books are the basis, purity is the principle and purity is the force"; real purity is an intense desire to please guru and Radha-Krishna, not merely the cessation of material desire.
- Narottama Dasa Thakura (Vilasa Manjari) was predicted by Lord Chaitanya, who called his name in kirtana on the Padma; he was trained in Vrindavan by Jiva Goswami with Srinivasa Acharya (given the title Acharya) and Syamananda (Pandit), receiving the title Mahasaya.
- Deep, Krishna-centered friendship among godbrothers and godsisters is essential for spiritual life; the bond among Narottama, Srinivasa, and Syamananda models friendship rooted in service to guru and Krishna rather than in sense gratification.
- Narottama toured the holy places to receive the association of Mahaprabhu's remaining associates; through a series of dreams and visions Lord Chaitanya promised a new style of kirtana through him and that whoever takes shelter of Narottama easily attains prema-bhakti.
- He recovered a golden deity of Gauranga and Vishnupriya from a snake-infested granary in Keturi, and received Ramachandra Kaviraja from Srinivasa as his intimate co-preacher.
- At the Keturi Gaura-purnima festival, Srinivasa installed six deities named Gauranga, Vallabhikanta, Sri Krishna, Vrajamohana, Radharamana, and Radhakanta (celebrated in Jahnava Mata's song); Narottama's kirtana of separation grew so intense that the Panchatattva (Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhara, Srivasa) appeared and danced, followed by a festival of colors.
- Jahnava Mata, wife of Nityananda and foremost Vaishnava of the age, is glorified as Nityananda's compassionate potency: she cooked and fed all the assembled devotees, and later converted would-be assassins (dacoits and Durga-worshipping brahmanas) into surrendered disciples.
