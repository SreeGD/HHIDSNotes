# How to enter Vrindavan(Power of Pilgrimage)

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 70 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:37:26

**Themes:** gokula and goloka · playground of god · akrura's example · humility · braja-renu · following a pure devotee

## Summary

Speaking at the Radha Gopinath temple in Chowpatty, Mumbai, on the eve of the Ayodhya Rama temple opening, Indradyumna Swami lectures on Srimad Bhagavatam 10.13.60, which describes Vrindavan, the 'playground of God,' as a transcendental abode free of hunger, anger, and thirst, where naturally hostile creatures such as tigers and cows live together in friendship because the sole aim of life there is Krishna's satisfaction rather than sense gratification. He explains that Goloka in the spiritual world and Gokula on earth are non-different: when Krishna descends (once in a day of Brahma) He brings His whole entourage with Him, so the Vrindavan a short train-ride from Mumbai is identical to the eternal abode, a point he supports with Jiva Goswami's derivation of Goloka as the 'planet of cows,' Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati's commentary on Brahma-samhita, and the principle that a pure devotee's words are as good as scripture. The heart of the talk is that one cannot enter Vrindavan merely by buying a ticket; following Krishna Book chapter 38, he holds up Akrura's journey (SB 10.38) as the model, marked by genuine humility ('what have I done to deserve this?'), meditation on serving Krishna, confidence in the Lord's mercy over one's disqualifications, and, on arrival, bowing down and rolling in the sacred dust. He stresses humility as the crest-jewel of devotion, illustrated by Prahlada, by Krishnadasa Kaviraja calling himself lower than the worms in stool, and by Mahaprabhu's 'trinad api sunichena' verse, which the Lord urged everyone to string on the thread of the holy name and wear at the neck. Throughout he weaves Srila Prabhupada pastimes to show that love is the essence and that following a pure devotee is how one truly enters the dham: the samosa relished as being made 'by the recipe of Mother Yasoda,' the ocean waves heard as the gopis' heartbeats in separation from Krishna, and the 'speck in your eye' (material desire) that must be removed before one can see Vrindavan as it is. He closes by urging devotees to visit Vrindavan often or, failing that, to make their own homes Vrindavan through the nine processes of devotional service and by hearing and chanting about the holy dham, noting that Vrindavan purifies even sinners and that Prabhupada carried its mood to the whole world.

## Key Points

- Class verse SB 10.13.60: Vrindavan is the Lord's transcendental abode, free of hunger, anger, and thirst, where inimical creatures live in friendship because the only aim is Krishna's satisfaction, not sense gratification (Prabhupada's purport: sadhus feed cows' milk to tigers).
- Goloka (spiritual) and Gokula (earthly Vrindavan) are non-different: Krishna descends once in a day of Brahma bringing His whole realm with Him, so the nearby Vrindavan is identical to the eternal abode.
- One cannot enter Vrindavan just by purchasing a ticket; the correct mood is required, and it is learned only by following a pure devotee, Akrura 5,000 years ago and Srila Prabhupada today.
- Akrura's journey (SB 10.38) is the template: deep humility, meditation on serving Krishna, trust in the Lord's mercy over one's disqualifications, and rolling in / smearing the sacred dust (braja-renu) of Vrindavan on arrival.
- Humility is the crest-jewel of a devotee; one sees Vrindavan truly only when the 'speck' of material desire is removed and the eyes are anointed with love (Brahma-samhita 5.38, premanjana-cchurita).
- Vrindavan purifies even sinful, atheistic visitors; devotees should go often (SB 5.13.8, 4.29.39, 4.8.42), and may make their own home Vrindavan through the nine processes of devotional service and hearing/chanting about the dham.
- Numerous Prabhupada pastimes drive home that Krishna consciousness is 'love masquerading as a philosophy': Mother Yasoda's samosa recipe, ocean waves as the gopis' heartbeats, and 'you are not these bodies.'

## Notable Stories & Analogies

- Goloka, the planet of cows — the Swami tells a Christian man his idea of heaven is a planet of cows, prompting an astonished 'Excuse me?'
- The Zulu king's name for God — 'Akulukula,' meaning 'He who owns the most cows,' over which the Swami and the king bonded, with a promised Food for Life for 60 million Zulus.
- Bhaktisiddhanta's three-day dictionary — at age seven he locked himself in a room with a British dictionary and emerged speaking fluent English.
- Mother Yasoda's samosa — Prabhupada revealed that the samosa Tamal Krishna Goswami called the most delicious ever was made 'by the recipe of Mother Yasoda.'
- Ocean waves as the gopis' heartbeats — on a California beach Prabhupada told the devotees the crashing waves were the sound of the gopis' hearts in separation from Krishna.
- The speck in your eye — a devotee who saw only filth and mosquitoes in Vrindavan was told the speck was his desire for material enjoyment, not dust on his glasses.
- Krishna won't wear shoes — He refuses Yasoda's shoes because His 900,000 cows go barefoot, so His lotus feet leave prints that make Vrindavan more glorious than Vaikuntha.
- Akrura rolling in the dust — reaching Vrindavan at sunset and seeing Krishna's footprints, Akrura exclaims 'this is the dust from my master's feet' and rolls in the braja-renu.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.13.60 (the class verse) - Vrindavan free of hunger, anger, thirst, where inimical humans and fierce animals live in transcendental friendship; with Prabhupada's purport (sadhus supply cows' milk to tigers; no envy in Braj).
- Bhagavad-gita 8.15 - 'duhkhalayam ashashvatam,' the material world as a temporary place full of misery (theme that half of Prabhupada's lectures addressed material existence).
- Brahma-samhita 5.38 - 'premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena,' eyes anointed with the salve of love see the Lord and Vrindavan as they are ('speck in your eye' story).
- Brahma-samhita, verse 2, with Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura's commentary - cited to establish that Gokula and Goloka are non-different ('there is practically no difference between Gokula and Goloka').
- Sikshashtaka, verse 7 - feeling separation from Krishna, a moment like twelve years, tears flowing like torrents of rain (vipralambha-bhava; why Krishna Book gives solace).
- Sikshashtaka, verse 3 - 'trinad api sunichena,' chant humbly, lower than the straw in the street, more tolerant than a tree, offering respect to others.
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi-lila 5.205 - Krishnadasa Kaviraja: 'more sinful than Jagai and Madhai, lower than the worms in the stool' (exemplary humility).
- Chaitanya-charitamrita, Antya-lila (Sikshashtaka narrative, approx. 20.65) - Mahaprabhu raising his arms: string this verse on the thread of the holy name and wear it on your neck for continuous remembrance.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.38 - Akrura's journey to Vrindavan: his humility, his thought that a soul swept in the river of time may sometimes reach the shore, and his plan to alight, bow to Krishna-Balarama and all residents of Vraja.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.38.17 - Akrura's meditation: 'When I have fallen at Krishna's feet, the Lord will place His lotus hand upon my head... that hand removes all fear.'
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.21.10 (gopis' venu-gita) - 'Vrindavan is spreading the glory of the earth, having obtained the treasure of the lotus feet of Krishna... the peacocks dance madly at Govinda's flute.'
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.33 - Krishna's hand acquiring the saugandhika-lotus fragrance through contact with the faces of the gopis (rasa-lila; cited re Akrura's longing for Krishna's lotus hand).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.24.24 - Krishna: 'our home is not in the cities or towns or villages; being forest dwellers, we always live in the forest and on the hills' (quoted on the book's back cover).
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.5 - Prahlada to Hiranyakashipu ('O best of the asuras'): abandon household life, the dark well of suffering, and go to the forest / Vrindavan to take shelter of the Lord; Prabhupada's purport (kalau shudra-sambhavah) invites the world's elderly to Vrindavan.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.9.8 - Prahlada's humility: 'How is it possible for me, born in a family of asuras, to offer suitable prayers... when even the demigods headed by Brahma could not satisfy the Lord?'
- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23 - the nine processes of devotional service (shravanam kirtanam vishnoh smaranam pada-sevanam / archanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam), by which one can make one's own home Vrindavan.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 5.13.8, purport - 'One must go to Vrindavan forest and take shelter of Govinda'; ISKCON built the Krishna-Balaram temple to invite members and outsiders to a spiritual atmosphere.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 4.29.39, purport - in a holy place like Vrindavan, if one gets the chance to hear from pure devotees, cultivation of Krishna consciousness becomes very easy.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 4.8.42, purport - unless very advanced, one is recommended to live in a holy place like Prayag, Mathura, or Vrindavan (so neophytes may also live there).
- Srimad Bhagavatam (purport, verse number uncertain; speaker said 'Bhagavatam 3:24', likely 3.2.4) - any person, even if sinful, going to Vrindavan at once contacts an atmosphere of spiritual life and automatically chants the names Krishna and Radha.
- Krishna Book, chapter 38 (Prabhupada's summary study of the 10th canto) - 'One cannot go to Vrindavan by purchasing a ticket; the process is shown by Akrura'; and 'as soon as one reaches the boundary of Vrindavan he should immediately smear the dust of Vrindavan all over his body without thinking of his material position and prestige.'
- Padyavali (Rupa Goswami) - two prayers: 'I am drowning in the fathomless whirlpool of material existence... please extend your hand'; and 'the elephant of my mind is drowning... please rescue it with your glowing Sudarshan chakra.'
- Stavamala / prayer to Vrindavan (Rupa Goswami) - 'O Vrindavan, was there ever anyone who did not quickly attain transcendental bliss by serving you?... disclose to me the best way to receive the darshan of Sri Sri Radha and Krishna.'
- Vrindavana-mahimamrita (Prabodhananda Saraswati) - do not walk or jog but run to Vrindavan.
- Sri Mukta-charita (Raghunatha dasa Goswami) - closing verse: bowing to the guru by whose causeless mercy one obtains the holy name, the divine mantra, the service of the son of Sachi Mata, the association of Svarupa Damodara, Rupa and Sanatana Goswami, Mathura, Vrindavan, Radha-kunda, Govardhan Hill, and the desire for the loving service of Sri Radhika and Madhava.
- Traditional Vishnu prayer - 'namo brahmanya-devaya go-brahmana-hitaya ca, jagad-dhitaya krishnaya govindaya namo namah,' glorifying the cows and brahmanas as dear to Krishna.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura, celebrated song quoted in Krishna Book chapter 38 - 'When my mind will be purified after leaving the contamination of material sense enjoyment, I shall be able to visit Vrindavan.'

## Glossary

- **Goloka** — the topmost planet of the spiritual world, Krishna's eternal abode
- **Gokula** — the earthly Vrindavan, non-different from Goloka
- **Viraja** — the river dividing the material world from the spiritual world
- **braja-renu** — the sacred dust of Vraja
- **Ajita** — 'unconquerable,' a name of Krishna
- **vipralambha-bhava** — the exalted mood of separation from Krishna
- **premanjana** — the salve of love that anoints the devotee's eyes to see the Lord
- **guru-nishtha** — firm faith in the spiritual master
- **saugandhika** — the fragrant white lotus of Lake Manasarovara
- **amala-bhakti** — pure, unalloyed devotion
