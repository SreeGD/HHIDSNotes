# Srimati Radharani

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 78 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:25:47

**Themes:** radha-tattva · hladini shakti · selfless love · mahabhava · compassion · sankirtan

## Summary

On the appearance day of Srimati Radharani (Radhastami), Indradyumna Swami glorifies Radha as Krishna's pleasure potency (Hladini Shakti) and as Bhakti-devi, the one in charge of devotional service whose recommendation is needed to serve Krishna; she is also present within the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. Though asked to speak on Sankirtan, he devotes the class to Radha-tattva, noting that Sukadeva Goswami only alludes to her in the Bhagavatam (as 'Aradhana') lest he collapse in ecstasy and fail to finish reciting to Pariksit, while later acharyas elaborated her glories, so that one may speak of her only in humility and by hearing from them. Citing the Brahma-vaivarta Purana and the Govinda-vrindavana Shastra, he describes how Radha manifested from Krishna's left side when Krishna, enchanted by his own reflection under a kadamba tree, desired to relish himself as a woman, and how the divine couple reunite as Lord Chaitanya. Through the principle that no one enjoys alone (grihasthas raising children despite great trouble, an old couple married 75 years whose secret was 'give more than you take,' and a mother's selfless love), he teaches that the true goal of bhakti is to please Krishna rather than oneself, since the more one gives the happier one becomes. He recounts the pastime of Krishna's headache, curable only by the foot-dust of the gopis who unhesitatingly risked hell to relieve him, and the playful 'name game' at Yavat where Radha rebuffs each of Krishna's names using their Sanskrit double meanings, showing how the Supreme Controller delights in being controlled by his devotee's Mahabhava. Because Radha's compassion leads her to take more pleasure in engaging other gopis and devotees in Krishna's service than in her own union with him, she descends as the compassionate Lord Chaitanya to spread the Sankirtan movement. He concludes that serving Chaitanya's preaching mission is the surest and fastest avenue to Radharani's mercy, quoting Prabodhananda Saraswati's Chaitanya-chandramrita that her ocean of nectar suddenly floods the heart of one devoted to Chaitanya, and closes with the Radhastami custom of revealing Radharani's lotus feet for darshan once a year.

## Key Points

- Radha is Krishna's Hladini Shakti (pleasure potency) and Bhakti-devi, in charge of devotional service; her mercy and recommendation are required to serve Krishna, and she is present within the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.
- Sukadeva Goswami deliberately only alludes to Radha in the Bhagavatam (as 'Aradhana') to avoid ecstatic breakdown; we may glorify her only in a mood of humility (a blade of grass between the teeth) and by hearing from the acharyas, never surpassing the master.
- Radha manifested from Krishna's left side as his own reflected female counterpart, and the two reunite as Lord Chaitanya; God expands as two 'to enjoy,' since no one relishes alone.
- The essence of bhakti is selfless love meant to please Krishna, not oneself ('give more than you take'); against the Western idea that getting more brings happiness, the Vedic principle is that the more you give, especially to Krishna, the happier you become.
- Radha's love is Mahabhava, so great that it controls the Supreme Controller, illustrated by the gopis' foot-dust curing Krishna's headache and the Yavat 'name game' wordplay; her love is wholly spiritual and free of material lust.
- Radha's defining trait is compassion: she takes more joy in engaging other gopis and devotees in Krishna's service than in her own union with him, and therefore appears as the compassionate Lord Chaitanya to spread Sankirtan.
- Serving Chaitanya's Sankirtan and preaching mission is the fastest way to obtain Radharani's mercy (Goshthy-anandi over Bhajananandi); on Radhastami her normally covered lotus feet are turned up for special darshan once a year.

## Notable Stories & Analogies

- Radha born from Krishna's reflection — under a kadamba tree Krishna becomes enchanted by his own reflection in a jeweled platform, desires to relish himself as a woman, and Radha manifests from his left side.
- The couple married 75 years — Indradyumna Swami meets a 90-year-old pair on a plane whose secret to marriage is 'you have to give more.'
- A mother's selfless love — she gives everything to her baby and receives only dirty nappies and sleepless nights, yet her love never diminishes, coming close to pure love.
- Krishna's headache and the gopis' foot-dust — no devotee will risk the offense of giving foot-dust for the Lord's head, until the gopis instantly offer theirs, willing even to go to hell if it makes Krishna happy.
- Russian devotees in Paraguay — Maharaja is astonished to find twenty Russian devotees serving on a farm in landlocked Paraguay, showing devotees are everywhere.
- The Yavat name-game — climbing to Radha's window, Krishna announces each of his names (Hari, Sri Hari, Shyamasundara, Chakri, Madhusudana, Krishna), and Radha rebuffs each using its Sanskrit double meaning (lion, monkey, rain cloud, potter, bee, black snake).
- The playful wedding at Uchagaon — Radha delightedly marries Lalita to Krishna in child's play, and in her bliss her veil falls on a rock that melts, leaving an imprint still visible today.
- Krishna wrestling his cowherd friends — in Goloka's intimate mood Krishna loses the match and must carry his friends around on his back.

## Scriptural References

- Srimad Bhagavatam 7.14.30 (also 7.14.29), Chapter 'Ideal Family Life' - verse read at the start listing sacred pilgrimage places and stating that ISKCON centers where Radha-Krishna deities are worshipped are equally sacred, giving results a thousand times greater.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.28 (the 'anayaradhito nunam' verse) - Sukadeva Goswami's veiled allusion to Radha through the word 'aradhita'/'Aradhana' (the gopis: 'certainly this gopi has perfectly worshiped the Lord'); the name Radha is derived from Aradhana.
- Brahma-vaivarta Purana - states that Sri Radha was expanded from the left side of Krishna.
- Govinda-vrindavana Shastra (quoted by Raghava Pandita Goswami) - a Krishna-Balarama conversation in which Krishna, enchanted by his reflection in a jeweled golden platform under a kadamba tree, desires to become a woman, and Radha manifests from his left side as 'half of Krishna's body, mistress of all potencies.'
- Chaitanya Charitamrita (Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami), 'Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Radha-Krishna nahe anya' (Adi 1.5) - Radha and Krishna combined in the single personality of Lord Chaitanya.
- Bhagavad Gita 4.11, 'ye yatha mam prapadyante...' - 'as they surrender unto me, I reward them accordingly'; cited for not expecting full reciprocation.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6, 'sa vai pumsam paro dharmo...' - the supreme occupation is devotional service to God that is unmotivated and uninterrupted (selfless).
- Bhagavad Gita 16.21 - lust, anger (and greed) as the three gates leading to hell; one must be freed from these to relish Radha-Krishna's love.
- Srimad Bhagavatam 11.5.32, 'krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam sangopangastra-parshadam / yajnaih sankirtana-prayair yajanti hi sumedhasah' - the intelligent worship Krishna (as Chaitanya) through Sankirtana.
- Chaitanya-chandramrita by Prabodhananda Saraswati - when a pious person attains devotion to Lord Chaitanya's lotus feet, the ocean of nectar from Srimati Radharani's lotus feet suddenly floods his heart.
- Bible (attributed to Jesus) - 'it is better to give than to receive' (Acts 20:35) and 'my cup runneth over' (Psalm 23), cited to support the principle of selfless giving.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura - holy days such as Ekadasi and Janmastami are 'the mothers of devotion' for those who properly respect them; and the song line 'When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest, and with a lowly heart I go out to preach the divine command.'
- Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura - his booklet 'What kind of Vaishnava are you?' distinguishing beginner, intermediate, and advanced Vaishnavas, the advanced being marked by compassion.

## Glossary

- **Hladini Shakti** — the Lord's pleasure or bliss potency, personified as Srimati Radharani
- **Bhakti-devi** — Radha as the personification and controller of devotional service
- **Aradhana** — the perfect worship that gives Krishna most pleasure; root of the name Radha
- **Mahabhava** — the highest, deepest ecstasy of love for Krishna, held by Radha
- **Anapayini** — 'never separated'; Radha, who is eternally inseparable from Krishna
- **Madana-mohana** — Krishna who bewilders even Cupid; Radha is Madana-mohana-mohini, who bewilders him
- **Sakhi** — one of the eight principal gopi confidantes assisting Radha-Krishna
- **Manjari** — young maidservant-gopis who witness the most intimate pastimes
- **Aishwarya-bhava** — the mood of awe and reverence toward God, absent in Goloka Vrindavan
- **Braja-bhakti** — the intimate love of God cultivated by the residents of Vrindavan
- **Goshthy-anandi** — a preaching devotee who spreads Krishna consciousness, contrasted with the solitary bhajananandi
