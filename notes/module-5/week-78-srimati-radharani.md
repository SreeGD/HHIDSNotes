# Srimati Radharani

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 78 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:25:47

**Themes:** radha-tattva · hladini shakti · selfless love · mahabhava · compassion · sankirtan

## Summary

On the appearance day of Srimati Radharani (Radhastami), Indradyumna Swami glorifies Radha as Krishna's pleasure potency (Hladini Shakti) and as Bhakti-devi, the one in charge of devotional service whose recommendation is needed to serve Krishna; she is also present within the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. Though asked to speak on Sankirtan, he devotes the class to Radha-tattva, noting that Sukadeva Goswami only alludes to her in the Bhagavatam (as 'Aradhana') lest he collapse in ecstasy and fail to finish reciting to Pariksit, while later acharyas elaborated her glories, so that one may speak of her only in humility and by hearing from them. Citing the Brahma-vaivarta Purana and the Govinda-vrindavana Shastra, he describes how Radha manifested from Krishna's left side when Krishna, enchanted by his own reflection under a kadamba tree, desired to relish himself as a woman, and how the divine couple reunite as Lord Chaitanya. Through the principle that no one enjoys alone (grihasthas raising children despite great trouble, an old couple married 75 years whose secret was 'give more than you take,' and a mother's selfless love), he teaches that the true goal of bhakti is to please Krishna rather than oneself, since the more one gives the happier one becomes. He recounts the pastime of Krishna's headache, curable only by the foot-dust of the gopis who unhesitatingly risked hell to relieve him, and the playful 'name game' at Yavat where Radha rebuffs each of Krishna's names using their Sanskrit double meanings, showing how the Supreme Controller delights in being controlled by his devotee's Mahabhava. Because Radha's compassion leads her to take more pleasure in engaging other gopis and devotees in Krishna's service than in her own union with him, she descends as the compassionate Lord Chaitanya to spread the Sankirtan movement. He concludes that serving Chaitanya's preaching mission is the surest and fastest avenue to Radharani's mercy, quoting Prabodhananda Saraswati's Chaitanya-chandramrita that her ocean of nectar suddenly floods the heart of one devoted to Chaitanya, and closes with the Radhastami custom of revealing Radharani's lotus feet for darshan once a year.

## The Most Auspicious Day: Radharani's Appearance

- We feel very blessed to be together on this most auspicious day — the **appearance day of Srimati Radharani**. For most Gaudiya Vaishnavas, this is the most blessed day of the year.
- We want to glorify Lord Krishna's most beloved, **Srimati Radhika**, who gives the Lord the most pleasure, and by whose mercy alone we are all engaged in the process of devotional service.
- Radharani has many names, one of which is **Bhakti-devi** — meaning she is in charge of the process of devotional service. Anyone who wants to approach her beloved Sri Krishna and engage in his divine service has to get **her recommendation**. So a day like this is a day we cry out for mercy from Srimati Radharani to please engage us in the service of her beloved Lord Sri Krishna.
- The chanting of Hare Krishna is central to our process, and Srimati Radharani is very much present in the Maha-mantra. Prayer: "My dear Lord, my dear energy of the Lord, Radharani, please once again allow me to engage in your **nitya-seva**, your eternal devotional service."
- These days are especially important in Vrindavan, where Radharani is the **queen of Vrindavan**. Prabhupada mentioned in a lecture that when you go to Vrindavan, you don't hear so much "Jai Sri Krishna" as you do **"Jai Sri Radhe."**
- As mentioned several days ago, **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** says that holy days like **Ekadashi** and **Janmashtami** are the mothers of devotion for devotees who properly respect them. So we don't want this day to pass without hearing some of the tattvas and lilas of Srimati Radharani.
- Note on the topic: the assigned subject was **Sankirtan**, but with the audience's mercy the speaker will speak about Srimati Radharani (and will also touch on Sankirtan). About 70 minutes are given.

## The Etiquette of Speaking About Radharani

- Today is the most auspicious appearance of Srimati Radharani; a neophyte, aspiring devotee only **dares** to speak about her transcendental personality.
- In the Srimad Bhagavatam, **Sukadeva Goswami** only alludes to her name, very briefly. Yet his **Ishtadeva** (worshipable deity) was actually Srimati Radharani. From our acharyas we learn that in Krishna-lila, Sukadeva Goswami is one of the **two parrots** who take messages back and forth between Radha and Krishna and sing their glories throughout Vrindavan — and he is very devoted to Srimati Radharani.
- Why, then, did he not speak about her extensively, especially in the Tenth Canto? The acharyas explain: had he described her in detail — elaborating on her transcendental qualities and her pastimes with Sri Krishna — he would have gone into ecstasy and would not have been able to complete his recitation of Srimad Bhagavatam to **Maharaja Parikshit** within the seven days.
- The duty of the student is always to repeat the words of the Master with realization, but never to attempt to surpass the Master — one should not speak more than he has heard from the spiritual master. (We have heard so much from Srila Prabhupada that it will take lifetimes to assimilate.)
- So are we transgressing etiquette by speaking for 60 minutes when Sukadeva Goswami spoke only briefly? No — because our acharyas, who come in the line of Sukadeva Goswami, being self-effulgent, self-realized souls, felt it their duty to give us these details of the spiritual world as an impetus to get out of this world and go back home. They have elaborated on the characteristics and pastimes of Srimati Radharani, so we can speak because we have heard from them.
- But we speak about her divine personality **with a blade of grass between our teeth, in a mood of humility**, realizing we have no realization but speak only out of duty to glorify her transcendental personality.

## Who Is the Girl Next to Krishna? — The Divine Couple

- When people see a picture of Krishna, they ask, "Who's the girl standing next to Krishna?" We say she is the Lord's **eternal consort**. She is **Anapayini** — because she loves Krishna so much, and Krishna loves her, they are never separated. They are the divine couple.
- When we speak about God, about the Absolute Truth, we speak about **Radha and Krishna** — not just "Krishna," but "Radha and Krishna."

## Radha as the Hladini Shakti (How She Manifested)

- In the Srimad Bhagavatam, Sukadeva Goswami refers to her as **Aradhana** — that gopi, that maidservant of Krishna in Goloka Vrindavan who offers the **best worship** to the Lord. The word **Radha** is derived from **Aradhana**.
- From the tattva (philosophical angle), Radha is an expansion of Krishna — an expansion of his pleasure potency, the **Hladini Shakti**. In the **Brahma-vaivarta Purana** it is described that Sri Radha was expanded from the **left side** of Krishna.
- **Srila Raghava Pandit Goswami** quotes the **Govinda-Vrindavana-shastra**, a conversation between Krishna and Balarama in the forest of Vrindavan:
  - Krishna said: "My dear Balarama, please listen, I want to tell you something very wonderful. One day, taking my flute, my heart full of bliss and my form bending in three places, I went under a **kadamba tree** and saw my own form reflected in a splendid golden platform studded with jewels." (Those who have been to Vrindavan and wandered the groves often come across these ornate golden platforms, raised about three feet off the ground — places where Radha and Krishna have their **rasa-lila** dances.)
  - "I became enchanted by my own reflection. Seeing my reflection, my heart became filled with sweet happiness, the nature of which was like conjugal love, which charms the entire world. Then suddenly my heart desired to become a woman; I yearned to enjoy myself as a woman."
  - The Govinda-Vrindavana-shastra continues: as the Lord thought in this way, his heart approached itself, and from the sweetness of his heart came bliss, and from the bliss came himself manifested in a **second form, a female form of transcendental bliss**. At that time a goddess whose form was like nectar, whose fair complexion was like a host of lightning flashes, and who was decorated with glittering ornaments, appeared from the Lord's **left side**. She is known as **Radha**, who was half of Krishna's body and the mistress of all potencies.
- **Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami** explains this simply in **Chaitanya-charitamrita** — Krishna expanding himself as Radha, and then the divine couple again becoming one in the personality of **Lord Chaitanya**. The verse: *Sri Krishna Chaitanya Radha-Krishna nahe anya* — originally the Absolute Truth was one, but then expanded himself as two, as Radha and Krishna.

## Why God Expands as Two: To Enjoy

- Why would God expand himself as two? Why is he not just happy to be one? The answer is simple: **to enjoy**. No one really enjoys alone. If told to go into the forest alone and enjoy, we say, "No, I want to be with the devotees — the more the merrier." We may like to be alone for a few moments to relax, but then we come back to enjoy with family, friends, and countrymen.
- Analogy of the **grihasthas**: Why have children? It is not easy. The wife carries the embryo for nine months and gives birth; the husband works hard for 25 years to make money; and then the child leaves home — "Bye-bye Mama, bye-bye Papa, I'm going off to enjoy with my new wife." Why go to all that trouble? Because, despite the trouble, there is an **inherent joy** in having children around and expanding the family — especially if you are a devotee. Nobody wants to enjoy alone.
- Krishna is the same — the supreme enjoyer. Though capable of being perfectly happy in any situation, he expands as **Radha**. Because she is the expansion of his pleasure potency, we can understand how much pleasure she gives to Krishna — **no one can give as much pleasure to Krishna as Radha.** Therefore we worship her.

## The Nature of Love: Giving, Not Taking

- What is our ultimate goal in devotional service? Not to be happy — our goal is to **give pleasure to Krishna**. That is our philosophy. If Krishna is happy, even if we go to great difficulty to make him happy, then we are happy. It is the nature of absolute love that you care more about your beloved than about your own self.
- Material love is very selfish: "I care more about myself than my partner; if I'm happy and enjoying him or her..." — and when it gets to that point, there is divorce. Real love is **selfless**.
- Story: The speaker met an old couple on a plane — 90 years old, married 75 years. Asked (in an age of divorce "left, right, and center") how they made it 75 years, the man said: "Young man" (the speaker was 60), "the secret to marriage is **you have to give more**." The way to solve trouble in marriage is to give more than you take. That is the nature of love.
- The closest thing to love in this world is the **love of a mother for her child** — she gives everything and expects nothing in return. What does she get back? Dirty nappies, a dirty milk bottle, sleepless nights (the newborn wants to eat three or four times a night, so she sleeps maybe two hours). But does her love diminish? No — because it is **true love, not puppy love**. No material analogy is perfect for something spiritual, but it gives us an idea. The love of the gopis is for Krishna.
- **Srimad Bhagavatam (1.2.6)** puts it philosophically: the supreme occupation for all mankind is that by which they can obtain the service of God — but that service must be **unmotivated and uninterrupted**, selfless — and if it is, the heart is fully satisfied.
- Western culture thinks the more you **get**, the happier you will be; we must get rid of this conception (it can take years, sometimes lifetimes, to get rid of this false ego, this enjoying spirit). The Vedic conception is the more you **give**, the happier you are. As Jesus says in the Bible, it is better to give than to receive.
- The happiness increases depending on **whom** you give to. Humanitarians and do-gooders get a certain selfless happiness by giving charity to the sick, uneducated, and poor. But when you direct that selfless service to the Absolute Truth — to Radha and Krishna — it reaches its perfection, and your happiness knows no bounds. It is **spiritual mathematics**, not understandable by logic and reasoning: by giving everything to Krishna, you achieve full satisfaction.
- We are trained in this idea by serving our **spiritual master** selflessly, because he is Krishna's representative. In his personal service we begin to see how nice it is to serve and please the guru — "I feel so happy" — and he takes that service and offers it to Krishna. That is why we must have a spiritual master.

## Story: Krishna's Headache and the Dust of the Gopis' Feet

- One time Krishna had a terrible headache — like the original migraine — and could not tolerate it. It was Lila: he wanted the world to know the glories of his most surrendered devotees, the gopis of Vrindavan.
- He told **Narada Muni**, "I've got this terrible headache." Narada asked what could be done. Krishna said, "The only thing that will cure my headache is if I get some **dust from the feet of my devotees** and rub it on my head."
- Narada thought this would be easy — there are so many devotees all over the universe. (Aside: devotees are everywhere. The speaker was surprised in **Paraguay** — a landlocked country in the middle of South America — to find a little farm with about 20 Russian devotees serving. "The Russian devotees are everywhere!" — Bolivia, Brazil, America, Canada, China, even the North Pole.)
- Narada went from the heavenly planets to the middle planets to the lower planets, asking for the dust. In every case the devotees refused: "I have to give the dust of my lowly feet to put on the Lord's head? That's a great **aparadha** (offense). I'll go to hell for that." (We are taught not to touch our feet to any sacred object — even neophytes should never move a Krishna-related object with the feet, but pick it up with the right hand.) One could not argue with what they said.
- Narada, who could hear the Lord all the way in Vaikuntha, grew desperate. There was only one group left he had not visited: the **gopis** in Vrindavan, who were busy with their daily chores — milking cows, preparing foodstuffs, washing clothes. Thinking he would have to argue hard with these girls, he approached them and said, "Girls, excuse me — the Lord has a really bad headache, and the only thing that will cure it is the dust from the feet of his devotees."
- Before he could even get "heavy," the girls **immediately put out their feet**: "Take the dust immediately; take it back and cure the Lord's headache." Stunned, he said, "But don't you know that if you give the dust of your feet, you may go to hell?" The gopis replied, **"If Krishna's headache goes away, if Krishna's happy, we can stay in hell. Let him be happy."**
- They gave the dust, and Krishna's headache was cured. Did the gopis go to hell? There is no reference in Shastra that they did. Krishna was simply **testing** his devotees' love. Sometimes he likes to test the quality of their love — like the "fire of ordeal," where gold placed in fire becomes even more brilliant. When devotees are tested, they never waver, and the Lord appreciates their love even more.
- We too are sometimes tested: "Do you want this Maya, or do you want Krishna?" — "Um... Krishna." But do not expect full reciprocation. **Bhakti is a question of the heart** — spontaneous love. As Jesus said, "My cup runneth over with love." As Krishna says (**Bhagavad-gita 4.11**): "All of them, as they surrender unto me, I reward them accordingly."

## Mahabhava: Radharani Controls the Supreme Controller

- That principle — its embodiment — is the heart of **Srimati Radharani**. Her love is not just **bhava**; it is **Mahabhava**. (Just as we do not chant just any mantra but the **Maha-mantra**, and we take prasad but are especially happy when the **Maha-prasad** comes — so with "Maha," Radharani has the greatest love.)
- Her love for Krishna is so great that she **controls** Krishna. In a Russian dictionary, "God" is defined as the Supreme Controller — yet here we see Krishna's devotee, Radharani, controlling the Supreme Controller. This is something amazing you will only find in our Gaudiya Vaishnava lore: Krishna **likes** to be controlled by the love of his devotee.
- In Goloka — and in all the Vaikuntha planets, the official kingdom of God with its master and servant — there is no **Aishwarya-bhava**, no awe and reverence in Goloka Vrindavan. It is all sweet, intimate, loving dealings. Sometimes Krishna's boyfriends challenge him — "What kind of big boy are you? You want to fight?" — and they wrestle; Krishna loses, and has to carry his friends around on his back. This is the sweetness, the fun, of their relationship.

## Story: Radharani Refuses Krishna at the Door (Word-Play at Yavat)

- One time Radharani was living at **Yavat** — where, when she is married (quote-unquote) to **Abhimanyu**, she lives, along with her mother-in-law **Jatila** and sister-in-law **Kutila**, who make it very hard for Krishna to come and visit her.
- Do not get bewildered that Radharani is "married" to Abhimanyu — **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** describes that Abhimanyu is the **shadow of Krishna**. It is all a kind of play, to increase the emotion.
- Krishna tries to sneak in to see Radharani, but in a playful mood she does not make it easy for him either. He climbs the wall, climbs into a tree, and calls into a room:
  - Krishna: "Hey Radha, it's me!" Radha: "Who? Who are you?"
  - Krishna: "**Hari**!" (Hari means "he who takes away all inauspiciousness" — but is also a name for a **lion**.) Radha: "A lion? We don't need any lions — get out of here!"
  - Krishna: "No, **Sri Hari**! Sri means very beautiful — I'm the beautiful Hari, the Personality of God." (But "Sri Hari" is also a name for a **monkey**.) Radha, shaking her finger: "We don't want any mischief here — go away."
  - Krishna: "No, Radha, I'm **Shyamasundara**." (A beautiful name of Krishna — bluish-black like the monsoon cloud; but depending on pronunciation, it can also mean just a fresh **rain cloud**.) Radha: "No, go away — you'll put out all the sacred fires."
  - Krishna: "No, I'm **Chakri**, who carries the wheel!" (But Chakri also means a **potter**.) Radha: "We'll call you when there's a need for a wedding — we'll need the pots. You can go away now."
  - Krishna, getting frustrated: "I'm **Madhusudana**, killer of the Madhu demon!" (Like boys trying to impress girls — but "Madhu" also means a **bee**.) Radha: "No, sorry, all the flowers are asleep; we don't need any bees here, there's no nectar — fly away."
  - Krishna: "**Krishna**!" Radha: "Krishna also means a **black snake** in Sanskrit — look at the Sanskrit dictionary. We don't need you to bite us — go away."
- So here is the Supreme Controller, submissive to the love of his devotee. A very unique — and very sweet — understanding of the love of God.
- Krishna is called **Madana-Mohana** (he can attract even Cupid); Radha is **Madana-Mohana-Mohini** (she is the attractor of the attractor of Cupid).

## The Purity of Their Love; Sakhis and Manjaris

- At the same time, we must be careful not to misunderstand their love — it is not like boy-girl relationships in the material world. Her love for Krishna, and his for her, are **pure and uncontaminated**. How could she have any lusty feelings for Krishna? She does not have a material body — she has a completely spiritual body.
- **Lord Chaitanya** glorified the love of the gopis — those milkmaids, those young girls — always glorifying their love. Though he was a strict **sannyasi** (and sannyasis are not supposed to speak about women), he glorified these women's love for Krishna because it is completely transcendental. In fact, that is all he talks about.
- But to understand these very deep subject matters, we ourselves must be **freed from lust**. You cannot really understand and relish the transcendental nature of Radha-Krishna's love just by hearing about it, unless your heart is freed from one of the three gates leading to hell (lust, anger, greed). We want to understand it, relish it, serve it, and be part of it in the spiritual world.
- The **sakhis** (the eight sakhis) are the ladies who assist in Radha-Krishna's pastimes, but they are not always present when the most intimate pastimes take place. It is the **manjaris** — the sakhis' servants, young girls of about seven, eight, or nine — who actually witness the very beautiful, sweet, intimate pastimes of Radha and Krishna. Not that the sakhis have any lust, but this is to impress upon us the need for **purity** to associate with Radha and Krishna. It is the manjaris who participate in the most wonderful pastimes.

## Our Aspiration: Braja-bhakti, Not Vaikuntha

- One day, in some future lifetime, this is our aspiration as followers of Lord Chaitanya: to go to **Vrindavan**. Lord Chaitanya's mission was not to deliver us to Vaikuntha. Some time after Krishna returned to the spiritual world, he began thinking, "I displayed these pastimes of love, but I did not allow any of the inhabitants of the earth planet or the material world to participate in them. I will go back and get them all to participate in these loving pastimes and experience **Braja-bhakti**, the highest love of God."
- We love any incarnation of God, because it is God — just a part of his personality that he is manifesting. Analogy: the speaker's father was his hero as a boy; the moments he enjoyed most were when his father **played** with him (football, mowing the lawn, going to a movie). He was also interested in his father's work ("Dad, how's business? How was traveling to London?"), even though that was not their intimate dealing — it was still part of his dad's personality. But given his choice, his dad would not even go to work; he would just stay home and play all day — which is why the weekends were so nice.
- So we love to hear about **Ramachandra, Narasimha Bhagavan, Matsya, Kurma, and Vamana** — but in our heart of hearts we are meant, hopefully, to be devotees of **Radha-Shyamasundara**. Therefore, if you go to Vrindavan — **Gokula Vrindavan**, a replica of Goloka Vrindavan in the spiritual sky — it is meant to give us a deep impression whereby we become purified and awaken our love. You will find practically 100% of the sadhakas and sadhus worshipping Radha-Krishna.

## Which Deity to Worship?

- Sometimes disciples ask which deity they should worship. When Prabhupada came to the West, we hear he had a trunk of books and rupees, but also (perhaps the first or second time he came back) he traveled for some time in America with a small set of **Radha-Krishna deities**. He later found it impractical to travel with them and entrusted their worship to some disciples — which is instructive to all of us.
- Worshipping Radha-Krishna is very elevated, so we worship **Gaura-Nitai**, because through Gaura-Nitai we become qualified to worship Radha and Krishna. We may worship **Jagannatha, Subhadra, and Baladeva** — and Jagannatha is also Shyamasundara Krishna. And if we worship **Nrisimha**, it is to remove the obstacles on the path of pure devotion.

## Radharani in Charge of Devotional Service

- This is a very important day because we want to achieve that most exalted stage — and **Radharani is in charge of devotional service**. She is the most qualified, so she is in charge — just as in the temple, the best cook is in charge of the kitchen, the best book distributor is the sankirtan leader, the best brahmachari is in charge of the brahmacharis. The best devotee is put in charge of devotional service.
- It is Radharani who blesses us with service to Krishna. She says to Krishna, "This boy, this girl — they're so sincere. They're serving you better than me" (as Prabhupada said). How can she say this? Because she is the embodiment of the best devotee, and she has **humility**: "That devotee is serving you better; please accept their service." And Krishna says, "Yes — since you are recommending, I accept."

## Compassion: The Advanced Quality of a Devotee

- Devotees are **compassionate**. Of all the transcendentalists (yogis, jnanis, devotees), devotees have a unique quality above and beyond: they are compassionate, eager to share their good fortune with others. You do not see yogis and jnanis going out of their way to help others, but for bhakti-yogis that is all they do.
- This mood of compassion is a very advanced quality, coming into the heart of one who has performed devotional service for many years. To be compassionate you have to be free from **envy** — envying the position of people in this world. If you are rid of that and experience the joy of Krishna consciousness, then you can be compassionate to the fallen conditioned souls.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura**: "When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest? And with a lowly heart, I'll go out to preach the divine command."
- There is that book by **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura**, *What Kind of Vaishnava Are You?* All Vaishnavas are worshipable, because they are attempting to give up selfishness and become selfless in service to God — but there are beginners, intermediate, and advanced. Advanced means one who has a **mood of compassion**, who wants to share.

## Radharani's Compassion and the Playful Marriage

- We would expect to see that compassion in the personality of Srimati Radharani, the best devotee — and we do. We hear from our acharyas that **Srimati Radharani takes more pleasure in engaging other devotees in the service of Krishna than she experiences herself in direct association with the Lord.** In the spiritual world she is always engaging the other gopis — that is her love: to engage the gopis in the services.
- Story: One time they had a **playful marriage** — Radharani married Lalita to Krishna. It was all play, like child's play. Lalita and Krishna were sitting next to each other; the gopis snuck up, and Radharani took the veil of Lalita and the chadar of Krishna, tied them together, put a dot on Lalita's forehead, and performed the marriage. It was all children's play, but Radharani was in so much bliss that her veil fell off onto a rock, and **the rock melted in ecstasy**. To this day you can see the imprint of Radharani's veil on that rock, out at **Uchagaon**, where the wedding was performed. That is the spiritual world.

## Radharani in the Material World: The Mood of Lord Chaitanya

- What about the material world? In the material world, Radharani manifests in the compassionate mood of **Lord Chaitanya**. Remember: *Sri Krishna Chaitanya Radha-Krishna nahe anya* — once the Lord was one; he became two, Radha and Krishna; and the two again became one in the personality of Lord Chaitanya, who came to the material world.
- **Srimad Bhagavatam 11.5.32**: *krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam sangopangastra-parshadam / yajnaih sankirtana-prayair yajanti hi su-medhasah* — the same Lord Krishna came again as **Gauranga Mahaprabhu**, but with the mood and compassion of Radha. Krishna was trying to understand Radha's love, so as Chaitanya Mahaprabhu he is experiencing the love of Radha — and part of Radha's love is her desire for the other devotees to engage in service. The devotee sees everyone as a candidate for devotional service, because essentially everyone is a devotee of Krishna. So Radharani manifests in the mood of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.

## Calling Out for Her Mercy Through Sankirtan

- Therefore we should call out for her mercy. She is looking for candidates — people eager to serve Krishna. That's us, Prabhus. But there are billions of people on this planet, zillions in the universe, so you have to **call out to her** so she can hear — demonstrate your eagerness to let go of material existence and embrace the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna. How? By chanting **Hare Krishna** — and then, like her, **distribute that mercy to others**. Then she sees, "That little devotee — even in his neophyte stage, he's so merciful, trying to help others" — and a flood of mercy comes from Radharani.
- By serving the **Sankirtan movement** of Lord Chaitanya, that is the avenue to getting the mercy of Srimati Radharani. This is stated in scripture — **Chaitanya-chandramrita** by **Srila Prabodhananda Sarasvati**: "When a pious person attains devotion to the lotus feet of Lord Chaitanya, the ocean of nectar from the lotus feet of Srimati Radharani suddenly floods his heart."
- How do you attain devotion to Lord Chaitanya? Devotion comes by **kripa** (mercy). When your spiritual master and Lord Chaitanya (**Guru-Gauranga**) see you serving their movement, you get their kripa. If we want mercy from Srimati Radharani, we engage in this process of helping to deliver the fallen conditioned souls — then the floodgates open very quickly. A **Bhajananandi** gets mercy gradually, but if you want the floodgates to open, become a **Goshthyananandi** — go out and preach.

## Radharani's Lotus Feet: Seen Once a Year

- The verse says the ocean of nectar from Srimati Radharani's **lotus feet** floods the heart. Traditionally, her lotus feet are only seen once a year in our temples. Prabhupada mentions in this connection that in the temples of Radha and Krishna, her feet are always covered by her skirt — but on **Radhashtami** the pujaris turn her feet up for special darshan, for special mercy.
- Personal example: on the day the speaker did his puja to his little Radha-Krishna (Radha-Bala), at the end of the puja he remembered — before offering the lamp, incense, and flower — to turn her skirt up a little and pin it, so you can see her two cute little lotus feet. He took a picture; it is so nice.
- But for the devotee dedicated to preaching the message of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, the mercy of Radharani's lotus feet — where else can you get such mercy? **Nowhere else.** It is the Sankirtan movement.

## Conclusion: All Engaged in This Service

- This should inspire us all to participate in this wonderful movement of delivering the fallen conditioned souls — according to our ability, time, place, and circumstance, big and small — under the direction of Srimati Radharani's favored devotee, **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu**. We are all engaged in this wonderful service.

## Glossary

- **Hladini Shakti** — the Lord's pleasure or bliss potency, personified as Srimati Radharani
- **Bhakti-devi** — Radha as the personification and controller of devotional service
- **Aradhana** — the perfect worship that gives Krishna most pleasure; root of the name Radha
- **Mahabhava** — the highest, deepest ecstasy of love for Krishna, held by Radha
- **Anapayini** — 'never separated'; Radha, who is eternally inseparable from Krishna

- **Manjari** — young maidservant-gopis who witness the most intimate pastimes
- **Aishwarya-bhava** — the mood of awe and reverence toward God, absent in Goloka Vrindavan
- **Braja-bhakti** — the intimate love of God cultivated by the residents of Vrindavan
- **Goshthy-anandi** — a preaching devotee who spreads Krishna consciousness, contrasted with the solitary bhajananandi
