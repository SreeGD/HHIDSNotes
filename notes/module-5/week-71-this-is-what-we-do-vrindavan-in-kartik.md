# This is what we do (Vrindavan in Kartik)

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 71 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:53:28

## Summary

Opening the 2014 Kartik Vrindavan Parikrama at Kokilavan, Indradyumna Swami frames the whole month around Srila Prabhupada's strong desire that his disciples and future generations regularly return to the holy dham for purification and inspiration — a desire so firm that when Rameshwara objected on grounds of cost (about $800 per devotee) and reduced book distribution, Prabhupada dismissed the concern, saying it is more important that his disciples come every year. He grounds this in Rupa Goswami's Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, which names five most-essential limbs of devotional service — associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam, residing in Mathura/Vrindavan, and worshiping the deity with faith — and notes that Prabhupada organized ISKCON so every serious devotee performs all five daily; when a reporter asked what his movement does, Prabhupada simply replied, 'This is what we do.' He presents Prabhupada as the ideal acharya and Vrajavasi who lived simply at the Radha-Damodara temple, visited the seven main temples, followed the six Goswamis' mission, and declared he appeared for two reasons — to establish Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead and to defeat the 'rascal scientists' — insisting that Krishna spoke to him directly. Citing Prabhupada's own words ('here I am in New York, the world's greatest city, but my heart is always hankering after Vrindavan') and several Bhagavatam purports, he stresses that Vrindavan purifies even sinners, echoing Prahlada's counsel to Hiranyakashipu to abandon the 'dark well' of household life and take shelter of Krishna. Explaining why the parikrama begins at the Shani (Saturn) temple, he teaches that demigods like Shani — Yamaraja's elder brother, who delivers 'instant karma' during one's seven-and-a-half-year sade-sati — are servitors of the Lord whose ishta-deva is Nrsimhadeva, and he recounts Shani's prayer begging Nrsimhadeva's merciful sidelong glance, assuring devotees that surrender to Krishna, not gemstones or amulets, is the real protection. The heart of the class is the Kokilavan pastime: to free Radharani from her 'imprisonment' at Yavata under Jatila and her nominal husband Abhimanyu, Krishna imitated the cuckoo birds with his flute and sang Radha's glories so sweetly that the birds fainted from the branches, drawing Radharani out through the pretext arranged by Vishakha and Lalita for a hidden meeting in the grove. He closes by defining the parikrama's mood — 'this is what we do': sit for hours in kirtan and hari-katha to awaken prema, stick with the group rather than chase other temples and sadhus, and remember that a saintly person 'sees through his ears, not through his eyes.'

## Key Points

- Prabhupada's insistence that devotees regularly visit Vrindavan and Mayapur for purification and inspiration outweighed all practical objections (cost, decline in book distribution); this was a foremost reason he built the Krishna-Balaram Mandir.
- The five most-essential limbs of devotion from Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-sravana, mathura-vasa, and worship of the deity with faith); ISKCON is organized so devotees perform all five daily — 'This is what we do.'
- Prabhupada as model acharya and Vrajavasi: lived simply at Radha-Damodara, visited the seven main temples, followed the six Goswamis, said he came to establish Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead and defeat the 'rascal scientists,' and heard from Krishna directly.
- Vrindavan purifies even sinners; Prahlada urged even the demon Hiranyakashipu to take shelter there, and Prabhupada invites the elderly to retire in Vrindavan and disciples to settle permanently as Vrajavasis (letter of 9 November 1975).
- Shani-dev (Saturn), whose temple opens the parikrama at Kokilavan, is a servant of the Lord who worships Nrsimhadeva; protection from the sade-sati period of bad luck comes from surrender to Krishna and chanting, not from amulets or gemstones.
- The Kokilavan pastime: Krishna lured Radharani out of Yavata by imitating cuckoo-bird song on his flute and glorifying Radha, so that the enchanted cuckoo birds fell unconscious, enabling a secret meeting arranged with the help of the sakhis.
- The parikrama's mood is sitting for many hours a day in kirtan and hari-katha to awaken prema for Radha-Krishna, staying with the group; 'the saintly person sees through his ears, not through his eyes.'

- Srimad-Bhagavatam, Prabhupada purport — 'we therefore invite all the elderly persons of the world to come to Vrindavan and stay there in retired life.' Exact verse not specified in the talk.
- Srimad-Bhagavatam, Third Canto purport — a sinful person who goes to Vrindavan 'will at once contact the spiritual atmosphere' and automatically begin chanting Hare Krishna. Exact verse not specified.
- Srimad-Bhagavatam, Fourth Canto purport (speaker cites the 29th chapter) — by going to Vrindavan and hearing from the pure devotees who live there, the cultivation of Krishna consciousness becomes very easy. Exact verse uncertain.
- Nrsimha prayer attributed to Shani-dev (Nrsimha-stuti) — 'You protected your own devotee Prahlada from a raging fire, from being drowned in the deep ocean, from falling from a tall mountain peak, from poison, a mad elephant, and the fangs of a poisonous serpent... please bestow upon me your merciful sidelong glance'; one who chants prayers to Nrsimhadeva never falls under the effect of Saturn.
- Srila Prabhupada's letter, 9 November 1975 — 'Anyone of my disciples who can stay permanently in Vrindavan is welcome... I want that as many of my disciples stay permanently here and become Vrajabhasis.'
