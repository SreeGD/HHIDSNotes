# In Search of Krishna (Kartik Parikrama Day 1)

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 76 · Foundations in Bhakti · 1:03:51

**Themes:** kartik · damodara · rasa-lila · radharani · separation · parikrama · vratas

## Summary

Delivered on the first day of Kartik at the Krishna-Balaram Mandir in Vrindavan, this lecture first glorifies the month of Kartik, also called Damodara, which Indradyumna Swami explains bears this name both because Damodara is the worshipable deity of the year's final month and because it commemorates Mother Yashoda binding baby Krishna to the grinding mortar with a rope (dama = rope, udara = belly); he adds that Krishna was also bound by Radharani's golden belt. He establishes Radharani as 'Kartika Devi,' the presiding goddess of the month, citing Rupa Goswami, Raghunatha dasa Goswami and Sanatana Goswami, and notes that the Brahma-vaivarta Purana enjoins that Radha must be adored on this first full-moon day. The heart of the class narrates the Saradiya (autumn) Rasa Lila, which occurred at midnight on this very day 5,000 years ago and is celebrated again tonight; he first defends the propriety of hearing rasa-lila for sincere sadhakas from authorized sources, quoting Bhagavatam 10.33.39 and Chaitanya-charitamrita that such faithful hearing conquers lust, the disease of the heart. Following Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's Sarartha-darshini commentary, he recounts how the gopis, maddened in separation after Krishna leaves the dance with Radha, imitate and enact his pastimes (including demons, whose roles Yogamaya/Purnamasi assumes per Kavikarnapura), then discover Krishna's footprints marked with a flag, lotus, thunderbolt, elephant goad and barley corn beside Radha's smaller prints. He describes how the different groups of gopis (Vipaksha, Tatastha, Suhrit-paksha) react, quotes the famous verse glorifying the gopi who 'worshipped best' (aradhana, the root of the name Radha, per CC Adi 4.87), and explains that Radha asked Krishna to carry her not out of pride but out of compassion for her friends, after which Krishna vanished, the gopis found the fainted Radharani, revived her, and sang the Gopi-gita on the bank of the Yamuna. Throughout he ties the pastime to the purpose of Parikrama, wandering Vrindavan 'in search of Krishna' in the footsteps of the Goswamis, and to taking vratas during Kartik, illustrating with stories such as Prabhupada telling a Western disciple to 'just wander,' the Polish festival drama where a little girl cried out 'Mommy, I want Krishna,' and Prabhupada's teaching that the Hare Krishna mantra means 'my friend, my friend, my friend.' He closes by reassuring devotees anxious about that night's inauspicious lunar eclipse that worshiping Radharani, the source of all auspiciousness, removes all danger, and mentions the tradition of offering a big pot of sweet rice on the rooftop for Radha-Krishna and the gopis.

## Opening: Hearing About Kartik from Srila Prabhupada

- The most appropriate, relevant and auspicious way to begin is to first hear from **Srila Prabhupada** about the month of Kartik, which has brought the assembled Vaishnavas and Vaishnavis together.
- Quoting Prabhupada's summary of Kartik in **Krishna Book, Chapter 29**:
  - "The appearance of the moon increased Krishna's desire to dance with the gopis. The forests were filled with fragrant flowers. The atmosphere was cooling and festive."
  - When Krishna began to blow his flute, the gopis all over Vrindavan became enchanted; their attraction to the flute's vibration increased a thousand times due to the rising full moon.
  - The red horizon, the calm cool atmosphere, and the blossoming flowers made all the gopis happy and attracted to Krishna's beauty.
- **Tonight is a rising full moon — the first day of Kartik.**
- Invocation: *Om ajnana-timirandhasya jnananjana-shalakaya / chakshur unmilitam yena tasmai sri-gurave namah* and *Sri-chaitanya-mano-'bhishtam sthapitam yena bhu-tale / svayam rupah kada mahyam dadati sva-padantikam* — obeisances to the guru who opens the eyes blinded by ignorance, and to Srila Rupa Goswami who established the desire of Sri Chaitanya.

## The Special Benefit of Kartik in Vrindavan

- There is benefit to observing Kartik anywhere in the world, but a **special benefit** in coming to Vrindavan — particularly to the Krishna-Balaram Mandir on Srila Prabhupada's invitation — the land where the eternal pastimes of Radha and Krishna take place.
- **Adi-varaha Purana** on the glory of the Dham (a beautiful verse): "As sunrise destroys the dark of night, as transcendental knowledge vanquishes misery, as the sight of Garuda frightens snakes, as the force of the wind disperses clouds, as the arrival of a lion causes deer to flee, and as the force of a lightning bolt levels mountains — so the very sight of Radha and Krishna's transcendental abode, Sri Vrindavan, destroys all sins lodged within one's heart."
- Why is such a verse glorifying Vrindavan found in the Varaha Purana?
  - After Lord Varaha killed the first asura in time, **Hiranyaksha**, he came to Vrindavan — actually to **Vishram Ghat** — and sat with **Bhumi** (the earth, his consort).
  - For a very long time he spoke the glories of Sri Vrindavan Dham.
  - This is why, traditionally, many Parikrama groups **start at Vishram Ghat** and begin discussing the glories of Vrindavan.

## Kartik: The Most Favorable Month for Spiritual Practice

- Analogy of the daily cycle: just as **Brahma-muhurta** (the hour before sunrise) is the most auspicious time of day for spiritual practice — quiet, materialists asleep, even the birds still sleeping, a good time to focus on chanting Hare Krishna — so in the yearly cycle of twelve months, **Kartik is the most favorable month**.
- From Shastra: just as the result of giving 10,000 cows in charity can be obtained by offering one Tulsi leaf to Krishna's lotus feet, so the result of bathing in all the holy places in creation can be achieved by **one morning bath in the month of Kartik**. (Playful check with the audience: "So you get the result of giving 10,000 cows in charity.")
- **Padma Purana**: The Lord may offer liberation or even material happiness to a devotee, but once one has rendered devotional service in Vrindavan — particularly in Kartik — such a devotee ends up hankering for **only pure devotional service**.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilasa 16.166** (a sweet, half-joking verse): "A person who, as a joke, worships Krishna in Mathura during Kartik attains the Lord's supreme abode. What then can be said of those who worship the Lord with faith and devotion?"

## The Name "Damodara" — Two Reasons

- Kartik is also called the month of **Damodara** (devotees switch back and forth: "I'm going to Vrindavan for Kartik" / "...for the month of Damodara"). We should know what we are saying.
- **Reason 1 — the worshipable deity of the month.** Every month in the Vaishnava calendar has a worshipable deity:
  - First month of the year: **Keshava** (so it is called the month of Keshava).
  - Second month: **Narayana** (the month of Narayana).
  - Last month of the year: the worshipable deity is **Damodara** — therefore the month of Damodara.
- **Reason 2 — the famous pastime.** Mother Yashoda tied baby Krishna to the grinding mortar with a rope during this month. In Sanskrit **dama** = rope and **udara** = belly; one with a rope around his belly is **Damodara**.
- During the month of Damodara, if one manages to **capture Krishna with the ropes of pure devotion**, he will be released from the bondage of repeated birth and death and go back home to Goloka Vrindavan. The assembled devotees have taken full advantage by coming here.

## Krishna Bound a Second Time — by Srimati Radharani

- Krishna was bound this month by Yashoda's love, but there was **another** time he was bound in this month — by **Srimati Radharani**, with a golden belt.
- **Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami's commentary on Bilvamangala Thakura's Krishna-karnamrita, verse 110**: Having missed a rendezvous with Srimati Radhika, Krishna was passionately frowned upon by her and bound up by her golden belt. He offered the explanation that his delay was due to the great festival of Kartik being celebrated by his mother. Krishnadasa Kaviraja meditates on him who thus became known as Damodara — who, in that bound condition, offered many excellent words of flattery with goosebumps on his body.

## Worshipping Radharani During Kartik

- During this month we glorify both **Lord Damodara** and his beloved **Srimati Radharani**.
- **Satyavrata Muni's Damodarashtaka**: "O Lord, the entire universe was created by Lord Brahma, who was born from your abdomen which was bound with a rope by Mother Yashoda. To this rope I offer my humble obeisances. I offer my obeisances to your most beloved Srimati Radharani and to your unlimited pastimes."
- In **Stavamala**, Sri Rupa Goswami describes Vrindavaneshwari Srimati Radharani as **Kartika-devi** — for Gaudiya Vaishnavas she is the goddess of Kartik.
- In his **Radhika-ashtottara-shata-nama-stotram**, Raghunatha dasa Goswami writes that Radharani is **Damodara's unrivaled beloved who bestows auspiciousness during the month of Kartik**.
- **Sanatana Goswami, Hari-bhakti-vilasa 16.197**: Lord Damodara becomes very pleased if, during Kartik, one worships Radharani's deity solely for her pleasure. Whatever spiritual practices are sincerely performed this month are all blessed by Radharani. ("It's all about you, it's not about me.")

## Taking Vratas During Kartik

- Devotees traditionally take special vows (**vratas**) to obtain the mercy of Damodara and of Sri Radha.
- **Prabhupada in Nectar of Devotion** (his summary study of Rupa Goswami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*): "One should surrender to Krishna in all respects and observe particular vows during the auspicious month of Kartik."
- Examples of vratas devotees take: chant more japa; read more of Srila Prabhupada's books; sleep less; abstain from reading/listening/going on the internet for mundane news.

## The First Day of Kartik: Radha Must Be Adored

- **Brahma-vaivarta Purana, Prakriti Khanda, Chapter 34, verses 90–91**: On the **first day of Kartik during the full moon, Radha, the beloved of Lord Krishna, must be adored.** This is not optional — "this you have to do."
- Since today is that day, it is best to adore her now, as instructed, and to discuss a very special event that Radha, Krishna and the gopis celebrated on the first day of Kartik **5,000 years ago** — the **Sharadiya Rasa-lila** alluded to in Prabhupada's opening verse (Krishna Book, Ch. 29): Krishna playing his flute in the forest, calling out the gopis. It is today. Vrindavan is full of a variety of activities.

## The Rasa-lila: Topmost of Krishna's Pastimes

- The rasa-lila is the topmost of Krishna's pastimes. **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, quoting the Brihad-vamana Purana**, has Krishna himself state its importance: *santi yadyapi me prajya lilas tas te manohara / na hi jane smrite rase mano me kidrisham bhavet* — "I enjoy many pastimes and all of them are enchanting, but rasa-lila is so powerful that as soon as I think about it, I cannot explain what happens to my heart."
- Rasa-lila is very deep; Prabhupada kindly gave a summary of the tenth canto explaining it over many pages.
- Ultimately it is relished mostly by **rasika** devotees (Sanskrit: a connoisseur of *bhakti-rasa*). But hearing about rasa-lila is **not forbidden** for aspiring devotees (**sadhakas**) if they hear it from an authorized spiritual authority.

### "We Know Who We Are and Where We're Going"

- The beauty of our culture: we have full information about God. Spoken with respect for brothers and sisters of other traditions — who, though they turn their backs on the material energy and want to go home to the spiritual world, when asked "Who is God?" can say little more than "He's up there; if I'm bad I go down, if I'm good I go up."
- But how can you love someone you know nothing about? You choose a husband, wife, or friend because of their demeanor, interests, the way they walk, talk, joke, the foods they like — **love is in the details**.
- Though God is unlimited, we have enough details about Krishna from Sri Rupa Goswami and our acharyas that we **can** fall in love with Krishna. One can read Srimad Bhagavatam a thousand times and not capture everything.
- We know the ultimate place (**Goloka**), the ultimate persons who live there (Radha-Krishna, Krishna-Balaram), and their activities. Hearing about them from authorized sources, gradually we can fall in love with Krishna.

### Scriptural Sanction for Hearing Rasa-lila

- **Srimad Bhagavatam 10.33.39**: Anyone who faithfully hears or describes the Lord's playful affairs with the young gopis of Vrindavan will attain the Lord's pure devotional service and quickly become **sober** — conquering lust, the disease of the heart.
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita, Antya-lila, Chapter 5, verses 45–47** ("just like a double whammy"): When one hears or describes with great faith the pastimes of Lord Krishna such as the rasa dance, the disease of lusty desires in the heart and the agitation caused by the three modes are immediately nullified, and he becomes **sober and silent** — silent from mundane gossip, focusing on the holy name.
- In his purport to that same verse, Prabhupada quotes his own spiritual master **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura**: any person seriously inclined to hear about the rasa dance with great faith and an inspired mind is immediately freed from the natural lusty desires found within the heart of the materialistic man.

## The Sharadiya Rasa and the Five Phases (Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura)

- Today is the **Sharadiya Rasa**, which takes place at midnight; it is a tradition to observe it.
- Different kinds of rasa dances:
  - **Sharadiya Rasa-lila** (autumn) — described in **Srimad Bhagavatam**.
  - **Vasanta Rasa-lila** (spring) — described in **Gita Govinda**.
  - Every rasa-lila takes place on a full-moon night, but only the Sharadiya and Vasanta rasas have been recorded in scripture.
- In the Sharadiya Rasa (celebrated tonight), Krishna leaves the rasa dance with his most beloved (Radha). In his **Sarartha-darshini** commentary on **Srimad Bhagavatam 10.31**, **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** explains this rasa-lila unfolds in five phases, including:
  1. The gopis' madness in separation from Krishna — inquiring from trees and deer where he has gone.
  2. Krishna's enjoyment in abandoning Sri Radha (done for a purpose).
  3. The gopis realizing the superiority of Radharani's love for Krishna.

## The Gopis' Divine Madness in Separation

- The pain the gopis felt after Krishna's departure drove them to **pramada** (madness). While the sweet dance was going on, Krishna left — and left with Radha — and the gopis were bereft: "Where is Krishna?"
- This **vipralambha-bhava** (mood of separation) we cannot imagine; we get an idea by Mahaprabhu's mercy through his **Shikshashtaka**: "O Govinda, feeling your separation, I consider a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I feel all vacant in the world in your absence." The more you love someone, the more intensely you feel separation.
- The gopis love Krishna more than anyone has ever been loved, and Krishna loves the gopis more than anyone has ever been loved — *ad infinitum*, always increasing. So when Krishna left, they were heartbroken and became **pramada** — divine madness.
- **Sarartha-darshini**: to console themselves they became absorbed in remembering Krishna — his movements, words, and deeds — and began **imitating** him, eventually losing all distinction between themselves and the object of their love. In that state they declared **"I am Krishna"** — transcendental madness.
- In time their madness subsided somewhat, and they wandered around Vrindavan looking for Krishna. **This is what devotees do.**

### "Just Wander" — Parikrama and the Mood of the Gopis

- Story (early days): A devotee came from the West and asked Prabhupada, "What should I do here in Vrindavan?" Prabhupada famously said, **"Just wander."** There are so many lila-sthans, with different moods and bhavas; by going there you imbibe this variety of loving sentiments.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura said Krishna consciousness is something you **imbibe**, not something you buy or purchase — like catching a disease by association (e.g., COVID); you can catch the transcendental "good disease" of Krishna consciousness by **sanga** (association).
- The Goswamis went out and found these lila-sthans so we could visit them — that is what **Parikrama** means: going to the holy places, wandering in search of Krishna. This is the Gaudiya Vaishnava mood of the gopis: hankering for Krishna. Someday that real hankering will come.

## The Gopis Question Every Creature

- Following in the footsteps of the Brajabasis, the gopis wandered and inquired:
  - First they asked the banyan tree at **Vamshivat** — "Did Krishna pass by here?" No reply.
  - They questioned different trees, the Tulsi plant, various vines and creepers, the earth (the dust of Vraja), and finally some deer.
- Having inquired from every possible creature in the forest for even a hint, they again became despondent and fell into the ecstasy of complete madness.

## Enacting Krishna's Pastimes

- In that state, as read in the Krishna Book, they began **acting out Krishna's pastimes**, which the acharyas say allowed them to taste some fleeting pleasure. Since they could not see Krishna, they performed dramas — just as devotees do at the Sunday feast or festival programs (someone plays Krishna, Balarama, Vishakha, Madhu Mangala; Chaitanya-lila; Gaura-Nitai kirtan) — because dramas help us remember Krishna.
- In those lilas, some gopis even played the parts of Krishna's **demons**. The Bhagavatam says one gopi pretended to be **Putana**, one pretended to be **Trinavarta**, so that another gopi could come and kill Trinavarta — just as someone must play Jagai and Madhai at the Gaura Purnima feast. One gopi played **Kaliya**, another **Vatsasura**.
- **Question:** How could the gopis assume the roles of demons and become antagonistic to Krishna, even in play? The Bhagavatam itself raises this.
- **Answer** — in **Ananda-vrindavana-champu** by Kavi Karnapura: **Yogamaya (Purnamasi), not the gopis,** actually assumes those demon forms, out of her causeless mercy. So the gopis did not have to play any inappropriate role; they just witnessed the mellows and the drama unfold. In Vrindavan everything is to increase **bhava**, to increase love.
- After observing that, the gopis (per Kavi Karnapura) enacted **less ferocious pastimes**: pretending to be Krishna playing the flute, playing games with Madhu Mangala, devouring a forest fire, being bound by his mother (one gopi played little Krishna, one played Yashoda), and one gopi pretended to lift Govardhan Hill. All so they would not forget Krishna — remembering his pastimes, which are not different from him. *Shravanam, Kirtanam* bring Krishna *Smaranam*.

## Always Remember Krishna, Never Forget Him

- Always remember Krishna, never forget him — the nine processes: **Shravanam, Kirtanam, Vishnu-smaranam, Pada-sevanam, Archanam, Vandanam, Dasyam, Sakhyam, Atma-nivedanam.** With so many ways to remember, a devotee has to be really in Maya to forget Krishna. Prabhupada gave us the perfect life — from the moment we wake until we take rest, it is all Krishna, as long as we are not distracted by Maya, who is very powerful.
- Story: In the room one time, Prabhupada told one of the speaker's godbrothers, **"The problem with you is you're not afraid of Maya."** Everyone shook. How not to become afraid of Maya — feel the shelter of Krishna. Krishna is far away in Goloka, but he has come in the form of his **holy name**, present in the sound: Hare Krishna.

## Dramas Touch the Heart — The Polish Festival Tour

- Enacting Krishna's pastimes consoled the gopis. Devotees do dramas even for the public — on the Polish festival tour, all the plays are about Krishna-lilas and inspire the people. "Krishna in Vrindavan" comprises ten dramas about Krishna in Vrindavan, and it touches the hearts of people who know very little about Krishna, because what they are seeing is transcendental — it is from the Bhagavatam.
- Story (a favorite moment in 35 years of the festival tour, performed nightly along the beaches for 60 days): They performed **"Krishna in Vrindavan"** where Krishna gets tied up by Mother Yashoda with the rope. Afterward there was a standing ovation, then silence before the next act. One six-year-old girl stood up on her mommy's lap and said at the top of her voice, so everyone could hear, **"Mommy, I want Krishna."** The speaker thought: 35 years of struggle, austerity, collecting and pushing the festival tour forward — all worth it, just for that one little girl's statement.

## Wandering in Search of Krishna — Our Mood on Parikrama

- After the dramas, the gopis' divine madness subsided and they wandered, searching for Krishna — as we will do this month. This is our mood on Parikrama: not to have a good time or see far-out sights. Life is short and then you die; we want to also find Krishna.
- Previous acharyas set the example: **Sanatana Goswami** did Parikrama every day around Govardhan Hill (the outer route), offering **1,008 obeisances** every day. We go out so we can increase our **laulyam** — our greed for Krishna.

## Following Krishna's Footprints (Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.25)

- **Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.25**: The plot thickens — the gopis came across **Krishna's footprints**. They knew they were his because they love Krishna and know everything about him.
- On the footprints in the dust they noticed the marks of a **flag, a lotus, a thunderbolt, an elephant goad, and a barley corn** (this is just one foot; there are other distinctions). Sometimes these footprints are found immortalized in stone — the ground/stone melting in ecstasy, because everything is personal in Vrindavan.
- This ecstasy is called **Tadiya** — what is connected to the Lord and to the pastime, a particular item that causes one to remember Krishna. (Connected to the ongoing lecture series on **stimulation for ecstatic love** — up to number 50 next Friday.) Example: when Radharani sees a **tamal tree** (which is black) she thinks of Krishna; when Krishna sees a golden tree he thinks of Radha. So many stimuli — this is why it is nice to be in Vrindavan: in the material world there are so many ways to forget Krishna, but here so many ways to remember him.

## Radha's Footprints and the Different Groups of Gopis

- Most important: to the left of Krishna's footprints was a set of **smaller footprints** with similar distinctions but arranged in a different way. Because they are great devotees — some very attached to Radharani — they understood that Krishna had taken **Sri Radha** with him. They thought, "He took her, not me" — not in a jealous or envious way. (There is transcendental jealousy in the spiritual world, but no envy.)
- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** comments on how the different groups (**gopi-gana**) — with different moods and different types of love — reacted to seeing Radharani's footprints ("variety is the spice of life"):
  - **Vipaksha gopis** (Chandravali's group, Radha's competitors/rivals): They became disturbed. Chandravali is Radharani's rival — they compete in loving Krishna; sometimes Krishna is with Chandravali, sometimes with Radharani (he prefers Radharani, but Chandravali is also a gopi). Her gopis thought, "Why not our Chandravali?" (It is all transcendental.)
  - **Tatastha gopis** (neutral to Radha): They did not pay much attention to Radha being with Krishna; they are attracted to Krishna, so his footprints were most important to them.
  - **Suhrit-paksha gopis** (including **Lalita sakhi**): They immediately recognized Sri Radha's footprints and were enlivened, wanting to glorify them. Lalita is the main gopi who takes care of Radharani; immediately under her is our beloved **Rupa Goswami (Rupa Manjari)**. But since they were amongst many different kinds of gopis, they pretended not to recognize the footprints — inspired in their hearts, they said nothing, not wanting any controversy.

## Glorifying Radharani's Good Fortune (Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.28)

- Because it was now an established fact, all the gopis together joyfully celebrated by declaring **Radharani's good fortune** in one of the most famous verses in all of Gaudiya literature. (We cherish special verses — **Padyavali** is Rupa Goswami's book of favorite verses; Vishvanatha Chakravarti is a great *shlokavali*, number one in ISKCON.)
- **Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.28**: "Certainly this particular gopi has perfectly worshipped the all-powerful Personality of Godhead Govinda, since he was so pleased with her that he abandoned the rest of us and brought her to the secluded places."
- The word **aradhita** ("she who worships the best") is present — they were glorifying Radharani.
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi-lila 4.87** (Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami): "Her worship (**aradhana**) consists of fulfilling the desires of Lord Krishna; therefore the Puranas call her **Radhika**."
- This is important because some ask, "Where is Radharani mentioned in the Bhagavatam?" She is mentioned indirectly — her name **Radha** comes from the word **aradhita**, meaning she is the best gopi.
- What does "best worship" mean? One may offer thousands and millions of things, but if you offer even one thing with devotion, that is most valuable. Radharani has that purest love for Krishna, so she stands out among all the others — yet the others are not jealous; they like to serve her (servant of the servant of the servant), the mood of the spiritual world.

## Radha Asks to Be Carried (Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.37)

- **Srimad Bhagavatam 10.30.37**: As Radha and Krishna passed through one part of the Vrindavan forest, the special gopi began feeling proud of herself. She told Lord Keshava, "I cannot walk any further; please carry me wherever you want to go." The Bhagavatam uses the word **pride** — she became a little proud.
- But **Ananda-vrindavana-champu** by Kavi Karnapura gives another explanation: not pride, but **compassion for her friends**.
  - "The generous and soft-hearted Radha thought: my friends have been deprived of Krishna's association during my private time with him. The Lord of my life has shown attraction only for me. How can my friends such as **Lalita and Vishakha** maintain their lives in separation from Krishna? Therefore I shall play some tricks, pretending to be proud, so he will move slightly away from me, and then my companions will be able to come and meet me."
  - So good-natured Radha said: "O ocean of unlimited enjoyment and love, I am completely fatigued. I cannot go any further; I cannot walk anymore, and I have no means to move forward. Please carry me wherever you will go." **There is no pride there** — she is thinking about her friends.

## Krishna Disappears Again — Installing Radha as the Topmost Lover

- Krishna said, "All right, climb on my shoulder." She got onto his shoulders, and he disappeared again — Vishvanatha says, "as unexpectedly as if **Rahu had devoured the full moon**" (as happens tonight).
- **Kavi Karnapura** explains why Krishna disappeared and left her alone: he wanted her gopi friends and ultimately the whole world to realize even more that, by the intense feelings of her separation from him, she demonstrated herself as the **topmost lover**. The gopis had already realized it from her footprints, but very soon they would see that she had been abandoned by Krishna and that the intensity of her **vipralambha-bhava** was greater than theirs.

## The Gopis Find Radha Fainting

- Soon after Krishna left, the gopis found her. Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura states that she was so **effulgent** lying on the ground that they saw her in the darkness of the night.
- When they came, she was crying out (naming her from the Rasa-lila): *ha natha ramana prestha kvasi kvasi maha-bhuja / dasyas te kripanaya me sakhe darshaya sannidhim* — "O possessor of my life, O Krishna, O lover, my dearest one, where are you? Where are you, O mighty-armed one? O my friend, I am your most fallen servant — quickly come to me and give me your darshan."
- The gopis saw the intensity of her love: **Mahabhava**. Only three persons have this Mahabhava for Krishna, whom we worship with all our hearts and souls — Vrindavaneshwari Srimati Radharani, and Madhavendra Puri, who introduced the concept of Braja-bhakti to this world before the appearance of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- How did the gopis know she was so special? First, they saw her footprints and thought she must be a special gopi, number one. Second, when they came across her abandoned by Krishna's plan — his plan to install her as number one — they were simply amazed and stood there.

## Rivalry Transformed into Mutual Affection

- Feeling great sympathy, all the gopis tried to revive and comfort her. This is very significant, because there was some **rivalry** between the gopis (in a transcendental way that helps increase everyone's attachment to Krishna) — such as the rivalry between Sri Radha and Chandravali.
- The beauty of spiritual relationships: all of them at the same time tried to comfort her; they all came together with one cause — to revive Radha.
- One acharya wrote beautifully: **"In the mutual pain of separation from Krishna, even competitors' feelings of rivalry transform into mutual affection."**
- Prabhupada paraphrased this: **"Your love for me will be demonstrated by how you cooperate."** We are culturally many (many countries, skin colors, languages) but spiritually one; on the spiritual platform we can all cooperate — the International Society for Krishna Consciousness. To achieve full cooperation, we have to reach the stage of feeling separation from Krishna.
- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura, quoting an unknown author in his Sarartha-darshini (10.30.40)**: *ata eva vishleshane...* — "Separation from a mutual beloved awakens the spontaneous display of heartfelt affection in lovers."

## Fainting, and Awakening by Krishna's Fragrance

- Despite all their efforts they could not revive Radha; her condition put them in such distress that some of them started fainting — Radharani fainting, and the gopis fainting one by one.
- How did they awaken? The **fragrance of Krishna** still lingering on the gopis' bodies (from dancing with him — his body is very fragrant) reached Radharani's nose and caused her to awaken and immediately sit up. Another **stimulus for ecstatic love**: the fragrance of Krishna's body.

## Following the Footprints; Gathering Flowers for a Garland

- At that point they all sat in deep separation, having exhausted every way to find Krishna. This is all to increase their devotion — **separation makes the heart grow fonder** — the purpose of this separation. (One day, as we become attracted to Krishna, we too will feel separated from him.)
- One gopi said, "He might be out of sight, but maybe he's nearby." Taking the hand of a friend, Radharani rose to her feet, and joined by the other gopis they started following the footprints from where she had been abandoned.
- While walking, they were **gathering flowers to make a garland for him** — always remembering Krishna, never forgetting him.
- Two ways to always remember and never forget someone:
  - If you **hate** them — like **Kamsa**, who could not forget Krishna because the son of Devaki was foretold to be his death; he thought of Krishna day and night, but not favorably (per Rupa Goswami's analysis).
  - If you **love** them — like the gopis, who thought of Krishna day and night out of love.

## "Better He Come to Us" — Love Is Reciprocal

- Walking through the very dark forest following the footprints, **Sarartha-darshini** (10.32) records one girl's suggestion: the forest Krishna has retreated into is so dark that even moonlight cannot enter — how can we follow him if we cannot even see his footprints? Better we return to the **Yamuna** and let him come to us.
- **Love is reciprocal.** Never think that because you are practicing devotional service it is all for nothing. Krishna: "All of them, as they surrender to me, I reward them accordingly." As you advance, you keep that eternally — it is not lost at death. Whatever spiritual progress you make, you keep; whatever material assets you have will all be lost. One day your love will attract Krishna, because love is reciprocal: as you love Krishna, Krishna loves you.

## The Gopi-gita

- So these poor girls, on the bank of the Yamuna, out of love began **singing in a unified concert of voices** — singing their hearts out to Krishna. This is the **Gopi-gita**, one of the favorite parts of the Bhagavatam.
- Why do we sing (Hare Krishna)? "O Krishna, O Radharani, please pick me up from this world of death and make me a servant — take me back to Godhead."
- Story: A devotee once asked Prabhupada what the Hare Krishna mantra means. Prabhupada paused for a minute and said, **"It means: my friend, my friend, my friend."** The gopis are the best example of this. (Same with Gita Govinda.) The mantra is more than a mantra — it is a prayer from the heart. We may sing it a little mechanically, but keep doing it; all the obstacles to devotion and love are bound to disappear.
- Encouragement: If Jagai and Madhai can become favorites of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and achieve a very exalted state of pure devotional service, and if even the animals in the Jharikhanda forest could cry tears of devotion, then certainly this is no fairy tale or show — anyone can chant and achieve this state, but we have to try very hard.

## Krishna Returns; Pastimes Continue Eternally

- That Gopi-gita caused Krishna to come, and there is much more, deep discussion of what happened when Krishna came back (a whole other class — only six minutes left). In short, this is a summary of the **Sharadiya Rasa-lila** performed 5,000 years ago.
- But in Vrindavan, Krishna's pastimes are **continuing** — **aprakata-lila** — although he disappeared from our vision. There is Goloka and there is Gokul; this is an eternal dawn where his pastimes are still taking place. **Brahma-samhita 5.38**: *premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilochanena santah sadaiva hridayeshu vilokayanti* — for those who have the eyes to see, the pastimes are still going on.

## Glossary

- **Damodara** — 'bound at the belly by a rope'; a name of Krishna and the worshipable deity of the month of Kartik
- **Saradiya Rasa-lila** — Krishna's autumn full-moon dance with the gopis, celebrated on Kartik's first day
- **Vrata** — a vow of observance or austerity taken especially during Kartik
- **Vipralambha-bhava** — the ecstatic mood of love felt in separation from the beloved
- **Pramada** — the divine madness of the gopis in separation from Krishna
- **Mahabhava** — the topmost ecstasy of love, held only by Radha, Chaitanya and Madhavendra Puri
- **Tadiya** — things connected to the Lord that stimulate remembrance of him
- **Aradhana** — the best, most perfect worship; the root of the name Radha
- **Parikrama** — circumambulating and wandering the holy places in search of Krishna
- **Aprakata-lila** — the Lord's unmanifest pastimes, still ongoing though hidden from ordinary vision
- **Yogamaya** — the Lord's internal potency (as Purnamasi) that arranges the pastimes
- **Sakhi** — a confidante-gopi; the gopis form groups (Vipaksha, Tatastha, Suhrit-paksha)
