# How to enter Vrindavan

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 68 · Foundations in Bhakti · 54:28

## Summary

Opening a month-long Kartika parikrama, Indradyumna Swami frames the pilgrims as students on their first day of transcendental school, quoting a Braj-bhasa saying that in the classroom of Sri Vrindavan Dham love is the teacher, Radha and Krishna are the students, and the attentive gopis are their classmates, all learning to relish Krishna's pastimes. He glorifies Vrindavan as the best place in all creation and Kartika as the best time to learn about the Divine Couple and to serve them with pure devotion (amala-bhakti), showing through Srila Prabhupada's example and purports (such as Srimad-Bhagavatam 4.29.39-40) that the dham is supremely merciful—an expansion of Lord Balarama—and awakens love of God even in the sinful, so much so that Prabhupada invited the whole world, and elderly people especially, to settle there permanently and become brajabasis. The heart of the lecture answers how one truly 'enters' Vrindavan: citing Krishna Book chapter 38, he stresses that one cannot reach Vrindavan merely by buying a plane or train ticket or by being physically present, but must follow the ideal footsteps of Akrura. Retelling how Kamsa, having failed with daily demons because Krishna is Ajita (unconquerable), sent Akrura to lure Krishna and Balarama to Mathura to be killed by the wrestlers Chanura and Mustika, the Swami shows that Akrura was secretly a great devotee (gupta, hidden) who had to conceal his ecstasy and who approached the dham feeling himself a wretched, unqualified materialist—teaching that humility is the first qualification for entering Vraj, and that if we lack it we should at least 'desire to desire' it. He reinforces this with the humility of Prahlada Maharaj before Lord Nrisimhadev, of Krishnadas Kaviraja ('lower than the worm in stool'), and of Mahaprabhu's trinad-api-sunichena verse, which should be strung on the thread of the holy name and worn like a garland around the mind, adding that a devotee's strength is his confidence that the Lord's mercy is greater than his disqualifications—even a fallen soul swept along in the river of time may reach the shore of Goloka. Weaving in Rupa Goswami's prayers (Stava-mala and Padyavali verse 61, the elephant of the mind rescued by the Sudarshana chakra) and the mood of chanting every kirtan as if it were one's last—modeled by Madhava and by a Christian priest who died mid-sermon crying out to his Lord—he closes with Akrura's meditation on descending from his chariot to bow at the lotus feet of Krishna and Balarama and to all the residents of Vraja, longing for the Lord's fragrant lotus hand (scented like the saugandhika lotus of Manasarovara from touching the gopis' faces) to rest upon his head and remove all fear, trusting that Krishna will recognize his devotional heart and not merely a minister of Kamsa.

## Key Points

- Vrindavan is the best place in all of Krishna's creation and Kartika the best time; pilgrims should come as eager students on their 'first day of transcendental school' to learn about Sri Sri Radha-Krishna and serve them with pure devotion (amala-bhakti). Braj-bhasa saying: love is the teacher, Radha and Krishna the students, and the gopis their classmates.
- The dham is supremely merciful (an expansion of Lord Balarama, an original spiritual master): even a sinful person who comes automatically chants the names of Krishna and Radha, so Prahlada recommended even his demon father Hiranyakashipu come, and Prabhupada invited the whole world—especially the elderly—to settle permanently and become brajabasis.
- Prabhupada lived in Vrindavan not as a kshetra-sannyasi (one who stays in a holy place until death) but treated it as a stop on the way to fulfill his guru's mission in the West, ultimately to bring everyone home to Gokula Vrindavan here or Goloka Vrindavan in the spiritual sky.
- Central teaching: one cannot enter Vrindavan simply by purchasing a ticket or being physically present (Krishna Book ch. 38); the ideal process is shown by Akrura, whose footsteps a would-be visitor should follow.
- Akrura, sent by Kamsa to bring Krishna and Balarama to Mathura, was a hidden (gupta) devotee who concealed his ecstasy and approached in deep humility, feeling himself a wretched, fallen materialist—so humility is the first qualification for entering Vraj; if one lacks it, one should 'desire to desire' it.
- Humility is not imitated but arises from realizing the Lord is great and oneself small—modeled by Prahlada before Nrisimhadev, Krishnadas Kaviraja ('lower than the worm in stool'), and Mahaprabhu's trinad-api-sunichena verse (to be worn like a garland around the mind). A devotee's real strength is trust that the Lord's mercy exceeds his disqualifications.
- We are all rupanugas following Prabhupada and Rupa Goswami from shraddha to prema; chant each kirtan as if it were your last (Madhava's mood; the priest who died mid-sermon), longing like Akrura to bow at Krishna and Balarama's lotus feet and all of Vraja, and for the Lord's fragrant lotus hand—scented like the saugandhika lotus of Manasarovara from touching the gopis' faces—to rest on one's head and remove all fear.
