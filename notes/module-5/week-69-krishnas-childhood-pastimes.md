# Krishna’s Childhood Pastimes

## Summary

In part 96 of his mini-series on stimulating ecstatic love for Krishna, Indradyumna Swami continues examining Krishna's first transcendental quality—that He performs wonderful varieties of pastimes—focusing on His childhood lila as relished by the elder gopis of Vrindavan. Drawing extensively on Srila Jiva Goswami's Gopala-campu, he explains how the different phases of Krishna's pastimes (birth and infancy witnessed by the elders of Braja, boyhood victories over demigods seen by His young friends, His mercy to Kaliya and other demons seen by the devotees, and His love pastimes with the gopis) are all glorified in song, and that this Vraja-prema is beyond logic and cannot be dissected by reason. Jiva Goswami further teaches that there is no real difference between Krishna's 'happy' and 'sad' pastimes—they are as inseparable as milk mixed with water—and that all five rasas (santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya) are woven together in them, illustrated by tender exchanges between baby Krishna, Mother Yasoda, and Nanda Maharaja. He adds Parmananda Das's account of the sages Garga, Sandila, Bhrigu, and Agastya coming the next day to bless Yasoda, marveling that the infinite Lord who reclines on the milk ocean stayed in her small womb, and Kaviratna's verse that one who daily sings the glories of Yasoda's son is never troubled by Kali-yuga. Summarizing a week of his own reading, he shares intimate vignettes: baby Krishna and Balarama clothed only by the ten directions with waist-bells and anklets as their sole ornaments; Yasoda repeatedly dressing Krishna as a prince only for Him to strip off every ornament and dhoti, keeping just a peacock feather; His possessiveness over toys and tantrums; His clapping and dancing ('ti-ti, ti-ti') to the gopis' singing with skill mysterious even to Lord Shiva; and His stumbling, then running to hide under His mother's veil to drink her breast milk. He closes with a prayer from Rupa Goswami's Padyavali longing for the vision of infant Krishna decorated with a peacock feather, nursing at His mother's breast, to enter the pathway of one's eyes.

## Jiva Goswami's Gopala-champu — Krishna's Childhood Pastimes

- In the **Gopala-champu**, in the first *puranam* (chapter) of the Purva section (text 142), **Srila Jiva Goswami** shares that Krishna's childhood pastimes are mainly enjoyed by the **elder gopis** of Vrindavan. He then expands upon that point.
- Jiva Goswami writes: **"Lord Krishna's birth and early childhood, which are mostly seen by the elders of Braj; Lord Krishna's boyhood victories over the demigods, which are mostly seen by his boyhood friends; Lord Krishna's great mercy to Kaliya and other demons, which are seen by the devoted people of Braj; and Krishna's pastimes of love with his gopis — are all glorified in song."**
- Further: **"Fully aware of what those activities are, the worshipable people of Braj always perform activities that inflame their thirst for Krishna. As they come and go, busy about their business, their hearts yearn after Krishna. I hope someday I will love Krishna as they do."** Jiva Goswami finishes like that — "and so do we."

## A Warning Against Understanding This Love by Logic

- Jiva Goswami then clarifies that love and warns us not to try to understand it with logic and reasoning:
  - **"Although their love brings great happiness, their only motive is to please Lord Krishna. As the Supreme Lord's creation of the world is beyond human understanding, so the love felt by the people of Braj is also beyond human understanding. That love will not tolerate the logician's attempts to discuss and understand it."**

## The Special Love of the Residents of Braj

- Jiva Goswami further clarifies the special love of the residents of Braj for Krishna:
  - **"Is it Lord Krishna who stands before us, like spiritual love personified, or is it the people of Braj, assembled together? Though they may try again and again, neither Brahma, Shiva, Narada, nor anyone else has the power to love as Krishna and the people of Braj do."**
- He then glorifies the people of Braj:
  - **"The followers, friends and associates of the Gopa King Nanda and his wife Yashoda, who is like his second self, are overcome with love for Krishna, because they are tightly tied to Krishna with ropes of love. The splendor of Krishna's handsomeness has made Nanda and Yashoda radiant like the sun and the moon, and their associates splendid like the stars."**

## No Difference Between Krishna's "Happy" and "Sad" Pastimes

- Jiva Goswami makes an interesting statement — that there is no difference between Krishna's happy pastimes and His sad pastimes:
  - **"Anyone who thinks that some of the pastimes of Lord Krishna are happy pastimes and others are sad pastimes is bewildered. How can anyone speak like that about the pastimes of Lord Krishna — Shanta, Dasya, Sakhya, Vatsalya and Madhurya rasa? Can anyone separate milk and water when they are mixed together?"**
- He then concludes: **"Ah, even a person with a peaceful heart will feel his heart tremble when he reads these next two verses."** These are quoted directly from the Gopala-champu:
  - **First verse — Krishna cries out:** *"Mother! Mother! Give it to me! Please give it to me!"*
  - **Second verse — Yashoda replies:** *"O my child! My calf! My boy who will live a long life! My boy, more dear to me than my own life! For what do you ask?"*
- With that, Jiva Goswami writes: **"When will affectionate words like these make me remember Krishna and Balarama and their mothers in Vraja?"**
- Finally he quotes **Nanda Maharaj:** *"O Queen of my house, Yashoda! You must have performed many pious deeds in past lives! That now your glorious son talks to you, eats in your presence, asks you for things, smiles, laughs, and is so charming!"*
- And Jiva Goswami concludes: **"When, thinking of these affectionate words whispered by Vraja's king Nanda, will my heart be bewildered with love?"**

## The Ocean of Bliss — Krishna's Appearance

- In Vrindavan, Krishna's childhood pastimes are known as **Ananda-sagara** — literally, "an ocean of bliss."
- This ocean of bliss inundated Vrindavan when Krishna took birth and appeared in the home of Nanda Maharaj and Mother Yashoda.

## Surdas' First Poem — Krishna's Appearance in Nanda's Home

- The great poet **Surdas** describes this pastime of Krishna's initial appearance in his very first poem — which he recited in front of his spiritual master, **Vallabhacharya**, at Mathura. It is a very beautiful poem in Braj-bhasha describing Krishna's appearing in the house of Nanda and Yashoda.
- **The poem describes:**
  - The whole of Braj was shaking in bliss; everything unfolded in the most auspicious way, and the brahmanas began chanting Vedic hymns.
  - Hearing the news of Krishna's birth, countless gopis started running toward the doorsteps of Nanda Maharaj. They were heavily decorated, like a parade of Lord Indra happening in Braj.
  - Their breasts were bound by silk cholis (blouses); their hair-parts decorated with vibrant vermilion; all were draped in new saris, their eyes lined with collyrium.
  - Their foreheads were decorated with bright bindis, and their hands were occupied with big plates or dishes containing auspicious items. They left their houses and ran to Nanda's home **like caged birds freed after ages of captivity.**
  - Their lotus faces were charming, like lotus buds blossoming in the morning sun. Running in every direction, they perspired heavily, their braids loosened, and their flower decorations fell on the ground of Braj — as if flowers were raining down from their dark, storm-cloud braids.
  - When they reached Mother Yashoda's chamber, where all-attractive baby Krishna was sleeping in Yashoda's lap, the gopis fell at the lotus feet of Mother Yashoda, appreciating their good fortune, and then gave her overwhelming good wishes.
  - Hearing the news, even the (young) gopis decorated themselves — they put on gunja-malas, ground the minerals of Govardhan Hill, and marked each other's bodies with auspicious signs and patterns.
  - The cows sensed the excitement and ran maddened with ecstasy. Many Brajabasis came bringing pots with yogurt and butter; many played mridanga and jhanjhas (cymbals); and many sang songs of joy.
  - Maharaja Nanda put tilak on the brahmanas and gave them cows whose horns were covered with golden coverings. Mother Rohini gave gold ornaments to all the brides of Braj. Mother Yashoda, with a flood of tears flowing from her eyes, joined her palms to the demigods.
  - The family members of Krishna worshipped the Yamuna by pouring endless streams of milk, thus causing the dark Kalindi to turn into a bright, milky river.
  - The warriors in Maharaja Nanda's army showcased their skills in the courtyard, while the servants accompanied their weapon performance by playing big drums.
  - **"Witnessing the unbeatable beauty of the child, everyone became overwhelmed and started embracing each other."**
- At this point, as Surdas was reciting to his guru, **his voice choked up with ecstasy and he could not complete the poem.** So his guru Vallabhacharya completed it by singing the final stanza (in Braj-bhasha):
  - *"Te din ve log, shukasam pati na tajay, suni shura shaban ke yeha, gati johari chanan vajay."*
  - Vallabhacharya's meaning: **"From that day on, the residents of Braj were never lacking pleasure, luxury, or satisfaction. Hearing this from Surdas, one can develop the love of one who serves the lotus feet of Lord Hari."**

## Parmananda Das — The Next Day, the Sages Come

- The poet **Parmananda Das** describes what happened the next day:
  - **"The next day all the brahmanas, including the sages Garga, Shandilya, Bhrigu and Agastya, came to give their blessings to the mother and son."**
  - They said: **"O Queen Yashoda, your fortune brings the fruit of Dharma. How is it that He, who is the greatest opulence, who sleeps on the waters of the milk ocean, who is endless and infinite, stayed in your small belly?"**
  - **"To whom the demigods pray, to whom Brahma awakened with hymns, upon whom the sages meditate — the same Narayana is now playing on your lap."**
  - **"Parmananda Das says: hearing these words from the sages, Maharaj Nanda danced blissfully in his heart, hearing the glories of his new child."**

## Kaviratna (in Rupa Goswami's Padyavali)

- About these childhood pastimes, the great poet **Shri Kaviratna** says (this is actually in Rupa Goswami's **Padyavali**):
  - **"One who daily sings the glories of Yashoda's son — glorious and cooling as sandalwood and camphor — is never troubled by Kali-yuga. Rather, at every step he finds a torrential flood of the sweetest nectar."**

## Summary of Krishna's Childhood Pastimes (from Six or Seven Days' Reading)

- When Krishna and Balarama were young babies, they were **"clothed only by the ten directions"** (naked); waist bells and anklets were their only ornaments.
- As they grew older, their mothers wanted to dress them like princes:
  - Mother Yashoda would often tie a soft golden dhoti around Krishna's waist, hang costly necklaces on his chest, and place earrings in his ears.
  - But as soon as she finished, Krishna would undo all her hard work. He would forcefully remove his ornaments — sometimes crying when a ring or bangle would not come off — and finally pull off his dhoti, keeping only a peacock feather like a crown on his head.
  - Mother Yashoda was just as persistent, saying, "The son of a king cannot be seen parading around naked forever," and would dress him like a prince again. But again he would take everything off.
  - Gradually, Mother Yashoda's perseverance won out, and Krishna had to submit to her will.
- When the elder gopis would flatter Krishna, praising his handsome looks, **he would strut around like a peacock.**
- Krishna as a child was very possessive of his toys and belongings:
  - If anyone, desiring to tease him, took away even the most insignificant toy, he would fly into a tantrum until it was returned.
  - But whenever he stole one of Balarama's toys, he would laugh and refuse to return it; and if it was taken from him, he would pout for some time and then scheme how to steal it again.
- **The dancing pastime:** Often, to distract his roving eyes from the world around him, the cowherd women would say, *"O son of Nanda, let's perform arati to keep time in our singing — please accompany us by dancing. Now, Lala, dance, dance, dance!"*
  - Krishna would clap his small hands and recite mantras like *"ti-ti, ti-ti, ti-ti-ti,"* while cleverly moving his feet and body in time to the gopis' singing. Pastimes like this left everyone enchanted.
  - One time the gopis wondered: how does this little child know dance steps that are a mystery even to Lord Shiva? Someone answered that he learned by watching them dance during their festivals, and everyone admitted that was the most logical explanation. But they also wondered whether it could really be true, because he danced with such expertise — how could he simply be imitating?
- Just as his audience was thinking like this, Krishna would stumble and fall, then get up and run to the safety of his mother's lap, hide himself under her veil, and drink her breast milk contentedly.
- And as soon as Balarama saw Krishna in the center of attention, he would become angry, jealous and frustrated; he too would run to the shelter of his mother's arms and drink her breast milk. In this way the two brothers would fall asleep.

## Glossary

- **Gopala-campu** — Jiva Goswami's Sanskrit poetic work on Krishna's Vraja pastimes
- **Vraja-prema** — the pure love the people of Vraja feel for Krishna
- **ananda-sagara** — 'ocean of bliss,' the term for Krishna's childhood pastimes
- **rasa** — a devotional mellow or loving relationship with Krishna
- **vatsalya** — parental love, the mood of the elder gopis for baby Krishna
- **madhurya** — the sweet, conjugal loving relationship with Krishna
- **Brajbhasha** — the vernacular language of Vraja in which Surdas sang
- **gunja-mala** — a garland strung from small red-and-black gunja berries
- **Padyavali** — an anthology of devotional verses compiled by Rupa Goswami
