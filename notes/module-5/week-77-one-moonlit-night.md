# One Moonlit Night

**His Holiness Indradyumna Swami** · Week 77 · Festivals and Kirtans · 1:23:12

## Summary

Continuing his series on the glories of Vrindavana Dhama and its great devotees, Indradyumna Swami tells the life and famous pastime of Shyamananda Pandita, one of the three stalwarts (with Narottama dasa Thakura and Srinivasa Acharya) who spread Mahaprabhu's movement after His disappearance. Born in Bengal to pious parents whose earlier children had died, he was named Dukhi ('the sad one') to ward off Yamaraja; drawn since childhood to Gaura-Nitai katha, he took initiation from Sri Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika Kalna and was named Dukhi Krishnadasa. After eight years of pilgrimage he was given the seemingly menial services of watering the deities' flower garden (during which his head became wound-ridden and worm-infested in his ecstatic absorption, healed by his guru's mystic touch) and later, in Vrindavana under Jiva Goswami, of sweeping Sevakunja, which he did while weeping and thinking he touched the very dust that once touched Krishna's and the gopis' feet. The central story ('One Moonlit Night') recounts how, after Radha and Krishna danced all night in Sevakunja, Radharani's sapphire-studded golden ankle bell Manju Ghosha fell unnoticed; Dukhi Krishnadasa found it (his iron spade turning to gold at its touch) and, guided by a voice from the sky, kept it safely. Radharani sent Lalita Sakhi, disguised as a village girl named Radha-dasi, to recover it; recognizing something extraordinary in her, he refused to hand it over until taken to her 'sister,' whereupon Lalita revealed her form, gave him the fifteen-syllable Radha mantra, and had him bathe in Lalita Kunda, from which he emerged as a twelve-year-old gopi and entered the unmanifest eternal Vrindavana. There Radharani accepted the anklet, pressed it to his forehead to form the Sammohana tilaka in the shape of her lotus foot, inscribed the name Shyamananda ('one who gives bliss to Sri Radha') on his chest, and, to console him over the coming separation, manifested from her heart a deity of Shyamasundara, bestowing twelve blessings on all who serve it. Swami weaves in repeated teachings on the rarity of worshiping Radha-Krishna and residing in the Dhama, the value of dham-seva and of the Dhama's dust as a stimulus to devotion, and the principle that only through the mercy of Lalita (hence the servant-of-the-servant mood, and Lalita-Vishakha beside Radha-Krishna in ISKCON temples) can one approach Sri Radha.

## Key Points

- Shyamananda Pandita (originally Dukhi Krishnadasa; eternally the gopi Kanaka Manjari) was one of three great acharyas who carried Mahaprabhu's Sankirtana movement forward after His disappearance, trained in Vrindavana by Srila Jiva Goswami.
- His guru Sri Hridaya Chaitanya recognized his qualification 'as a jeweler spots a diamond among glass,' and his advancement came through humble, seemingly menial service watering flowers and sweeping Sevakunja, done in tears with the mood that the dust once touched Krishna's and the gopis' feet.
- Worshiping Radha-Krishna and living in or even visiting Vrindavana are extremely rare privileges granted only at an advanced stage; conditioned souls approach through Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and worship in the mood of Lakshmi-Narayana, all by Srila Prabhupada's mercy.
- In the 'One Moonlit Night' pastime, Radharani's ankle bell Manju Ghosha fell during the all-night dance; Dukhi Krishnadasa found it (his iron spade turning to gold) and was drawn by Radharani's design into her direct service.
- Radha renamed him Shyamananda, gave him the Sammohana tilaka shaped like her lotus foot, and manifested from her heart the Shyamasundara deity (now in the Radha-Shyamasundara temple, Vrindavana), attaching twelve blessings for those who serve it.
- Radharani deliberately sent Lalita Sakhi rather than a maidservant because only by Lalita's grace can one gain direct contact with Sri Radha, even if sponsored by another gopi, illustrated by Lalita and Vishakha standing beside Radha-Krishna on ISKCON altars.
- Shyamananda and his companions were both deep rasika devotees and bold, compassionate preachers who carried Braja-bhakti to the most fallen, reflecting Mahaprabhu's unmatched mercy as the yuga-avatara; the talk ends on a call to preach now, not later.

- Prema Vilasa by Nityananda dasa — states that in Goloka Vrindavana Shyamananda Pandita is the gopi Kanaka Manjari.
- Bhakti Ratnakara (Narahari Chakravarti) — biographical details: parents Krishna Mandala and Durika Devi, 'exceedingly pious people of impeccable character.'
- Bhagavad-gita 4.34 — 'tad viddhi pranipatena pariprashnena sevaya' (approach the spiritual master by submission, inquiry, and service; realization comes through serving).
- Rupa Goswami, Upadeshamrita (Nectar of Instruction), verse 11 — glorification of Radha-kunda ('of the many objects of favored delight... Srimati Radharani is the most treasured... Radha-kunda is very rarely attained even by great devotees').
- Prabodhananda Saraswati, Vrindavana Mahimamrita — the dust of Vrindavana is made of camphor, saffron, ground musk, and aromatic substances used in religious ceremonies.
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Saranagati — poem beginning 'Radha-kunda-tata-kunja-kutir,' listing stimuli to devotion (Govardhana, the Yamuna, birds, deer, peacocks, the flute, springtime, the moon, the dust of Braja) and concluding 'to abandon them is to abandon life itself.'
- Bhaktivinoda Thakura, song 'Madhava-tithi bhakti-janani jetane palana kori' — the desire to visit and worship the holy places of Krishna's and Chaitanya's pastimes.
- Srila Prabhupada, letter to Jamuna dasi, 16 January 1970 — 'the worship of Radha and Krishna as they appear in Vrindavana is very advanced stage... so we worship them in our temples as Lakshmi-Narayana.'
- Srila Prabhupada, letter of 30 January 1973 — 'For a conditioned soul there is no possibility of worshipping Radha and Krishna, but we worship through Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.'
- Rupa Goswami, Lalita Ashtakam, verses 7 and 9 — Lalita informs Radha of a maiden inclined toward her and secures her acceptance; reciting the Ashtaka brings a devotee into Radharani's group of sakhis.
- Rupa Goswami's prayer 'namo maha-vadanyaya krishna-prema-pradaya te...' (spoken at first seeing Mahaprabhu; Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 19.53) — there is no more merciful incarnation than Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- Padma Purana / Shad-gosvami-astaka style pranama-verse to the spiritual master quoted: 'O beloved spiritual master, you are always in the presence of the cowherd girl Radha, the daughter of King Vrishabhanu... please award me service at your lotus feet' (guru-pranati emphasizing the guru's nearness to Sri Radha).
- Twelve blessings of Srimati Radharani for those who serve the Shyamasundara deity (recited at length from shastra) — e.g., darshana even once grants prema-bhakti and the divine abode; cleaning, garlanding, garmenting, feeding, circumambulating, and building/renovating the temple deliver many generations of one's family.
