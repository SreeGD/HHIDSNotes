## Welcome to Budapest and the Facility for Going Back to Godhead

- Warm welcome to the weekly gathering (formerly called a "love feast") of devotees, congregation members, and friends of Krishna; happiness at seeing such a large group.
- Srila Prabhupada once said, **"Krishna consciousness is nice when it's expanding"** — glossed by the phrase *ananda* (blissful): Krishna consciousness is blissful when it is expanding.
- Under the direction of His Holiness Sivarama Swami and his co-workers, everything is going nicely in Hungary: a beautiful temple in Budapest, many Nama-hatta centers around the country, and the new Braja-dham (New Vraja-dhama) where the *braja-basis* serve the lotus feet of Sri Sri Radha-Syamasundara.
- Conclusion: "You have no excuses for not going back to Godhead" — all facilities are present. The hope is to meet everyone in the spiritual sky soon: "Within the next hundred years we should all be up there, not down here."

## The Sannyasi Who Travels with the Sun; Spring as Krishna

- A sannyasi has the liberty to "travel with the sun" — to preach. As soon as it gets cold in the northern hemisphere, "we follow the birds south." He calls this "an endless summer."
- Places named for winter preaching: Africa (Mombasa, Johannesburg, Durban), Australia — "lots of people who have forgotten Krishna."
- Krishna is now "showing his smiling face in the form of springtime." **Bhagavad-gita 10.35** — "of seasons I am flower-bearing spring" (*ritunam kusumakarah*), Krishna as the beauty of the seasons.
- On this beautiful spring afternoon, with doors open and sunshine present, the class turns to the teachings of Krishna in Bhagavad-gita.

## Bhagavad-gita 18.55 — Understanding Krishna Only by Devotional Service

- Verse chosen: **Bhagavad-gita 18.55** — *bhaktya mam abhijananti yavan yas casmi tattvatah / tato mam tattvato jnatva visate tad-anantaram* — "One can understand Me, as I am, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, only by devotional service. And when one is in full consciousness of Me by such devotion, he can enter into the kingdom of God." (Verse recited and translated line-by-line, Sanskrit and Hungarian.)
- Purport points:
  - Krishna and His plenary portions cannot be understood by mental speculation nor by non-devotees. One must take to pure devotional service under the guidance of a pure devotee.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 7.25** — "*naham prakasah sarvasya*" — the Lord is not revealed to everyone. No one can understand God by erudite scholarship or mental speculation.
  - "University degrees are not helpful." Only one actually engaged in Krishna consciousness and devotional service can understand what Krishna is.
- When one is in full consciousness of Krishna by such devotion, he is immediately transferred to the spiritual world.

## The Long Journey Back — How the Soul Fell

- Such a moment of pure devotion is the culmination of a long process. **Bhagavad-gita 7.19** — *vasudevah sarvam iti sa mahatma su-durlabhah* — "After many, many births, one who is in full consciousness of Me surrenders to Me."
- The journey back is long "because we've fallen so low." Prabhupada: once we were with the Lord in the spiritual world; somehow we became a little jealous.
- Described by Kaviraja Goswami in Chaitanya-charitamrita — **"Krishna bahirmukha hana bhoga-vancha kare"** — as soon as a living entity becomes inimical to Krishna and desires sense gratification, he is immediately struck down by the illusory energy.
  - No arrest, no court, no jury, no lawyer, no six-month or five-year trial — the fall is immediate. "There's no room for envy in the spiritual sky."

## The Lord Pleads as We Fall; We Only Think We Are Independent

- From a Prabhupada lecture: when the living entity falls from the spiritual world, the Lord accompanies him for some distance and pleads, "Please don't leave."
- The Lord's appeal: "Why are you leaving this wonderful abode? You won't find any real happiness in the material creation — that's My inferior creation."
  - The material creation is the Lord's inferior energy: temporary, full of ignorance and suffering. The spiritual world is the opposite — eternal, full of knowledge and bliss.
  - "You are My part and parcel. You are My servant. You can't be the master... Stay here with Me." But we refuse — like an obstinate child running away from home.
- Even when we reject Him, the Lord does not abandon us. We only *think* we are independent; at all times we are dependent on His mercy.
- **Bull-and-post analogy:** Tie a bull by a rope through the ring in his nose to a post; he prances around and thinks he is free, but as soon as he reaches the end of the rope he is checked. So too we only think we are independent from God.
- When we leave the spiritual world, the Lord puts us under the charge of Maya Devi, who "takes care of us."

## Prabhupada, Damodara Das, and the Ropes of the Modes

- Prabhupada's own example: as a young boy he was sometimes "nutty" and wouldn't go to school.
  - Aside on children: little boys especially can be "nutty," including in "the terrible twos" — a difficult stage for parents. Prabhupada said that when little boys are naughty it means they will be very intelligent when older — and Prabhupada was most intelligent later.
  - Aside on the speaker's own boyhood: he spent more time at the beach than in the classroom — but nowadays children are career-oriented and should not miss school ("You can't follow me in that example"). There is no surf in Hungary.
- **Damodara Das story:** When Prabhupada wouldn't go to school, his mother called a man named Damodara Das, whose paid job was to tie up naughty children with rope and drag them to the classroom. Thanks to this, Prabhupada went to school — and later could write his books about Krishna.
  - The name Damodara is a name for Krishna: "He who was bound by the rope (or the love) of Mother Yasoda."
- **Mother Yasoda binding Krishna:** Krishna's naughtiness gives joy to the whole world (unlike a conditioned soul's naughtiness, which causes grief). Yasoda chased Him to tie Him to the grinding mortar. It was not easy to catch God, but out of her love God allowed Himself to be captured and bound.
- Similarly, when we are "a little naughty" and don't want to stay in the spiritual world, Maya Devi ties us with ropes.
  - The three *gunas* — *sattva* (goodness), *rajas* (passion), *tamas* (ignorance) — and *guna* means "rope." We are tied to the material energy by the ropes of mundane goodness, passion, and ignorance.

## Durga — the Prison Warden, and the Certainty of Death

- Maya Devi can be harsh; another of her names is Durga, meaning "the warden in the prison."
- She rides a tiger and holds a three-pronged spear (trident). She is an actual personality — "not some mythology like we read in Greek literature" (Zeus, Hercules). She is "up there somewhere in the sky," a real person currently in charge of the conditioned souls.
  - In Hindu homes one sees the deity of Durga on the tiger, a demon beneath the tiger's feet being bitten, and Durga poking everyone with her trident ("out, out, out").
- **The tiger represents death.** **Bhagavad-gita 2.27** — "For one who has taken birth, death is certain; and for one who has died, birth is certain." This may sound like poetic language, but in time one sees it is very real.
- Most listeners are young and think the end is "further down the road," but the end could come at any moment. Such statements should make us sober and not waste time.
- Prabhupada: "If you've got time, chant Hare Krishna."

## The Melanoma Scare in Durban — Death Made Real

- The speaker's personal story from a routine medical exam in Durban, South Africa:
  - Feeling healthy, planning his Japa walk by the beach and a full preaching tour (Budapest, the farm, England, the temples, Russia, America, the Polish tour).
  - The doctor examining his back exclaimed "Oh my God," then called a nearby dermatologist. They found a black mole and said, "melanoma — a very dangerous type of skin cancer." Asked if he had been in the sun a long time, he admitted, "I used to surf."
  - **Prabhupada's "surfers/sufferers" line:** In Hawaii, Prabhupada watched surfers and was told, "They're surfing the waves." Prabhupada said, "They're not surfers, they're *sufferers*. In their next lifetime they'll be dolphins." The speaker realized: "My surfing is turning into suffering" — "the words of the pure devotee always ring true."
  - A specialist cut out a large piece of skin (four stitches, still present) for a lab test. This was Friday the 13th; results promised for the 16th. The specialist warned it looked like an advanced, most-dangerous stage of melanoma, said they would have to discuss chemotherapy, and told him to cancel all plans and stay in Durban 5–6 months.
  - Reading on the internet, he learned that at that stage one might have six months to live. Unable to sleep, he began composing a farewell letter to his disciples, friends, and well-wishers, planning to go to Vrindavan and just chant.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 2.27** suddenly became "a very real fact of life," not poetry. The "so-called beauty of this world" became pale and unimportant, like colors fading. The one important thing: to become Krishna conscious in the few months remaining.
  - He phoned the doctor for early results. A friend did the lab test; Dr. Mohan called and, after a pause (which the speaker took as bad news), said, "everything's okay, it's negative; it really is bad melanoma — I made a big mistake, sorry."
  - He then tore up the letter — but reflected that someday all of us will get that prognosis, and it will not be favorable.
- Lesson: always keep the consciousness that the beauty of this world is fading and "the only beauty is the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna." Read Prabhupada's books in reality, not in illusion; take this philosophy seriously — especially since we have a wonderful alternative: we need not take birth again and again but can go back to Krishna in one lifetime.

## Durga's Trident — the Threefold Miseries

- The tiger (death) "is going to get you one day." Meanwhile Durga's spear represents the three miseries: *adhyatmika*, *adhibhautika*, *adhidaivika*.
  - **Adhyatmika** — misery caused by one's own mind and body.
  - **Adhibhautika** — misery caused by other living entities: overcrowding so you can't move, mosquitoes, bedbugs.
  - **Adhidaivika** — misery caused by natural occurrences, like bad weather (today being a very nice exception — a garden across the road, church bells ringing, babies smiling; "so many babies now in ISKCON Hungary — they're our future").
- Even sleep gives little relief: the bugs bite in the bed, and there are bad dreams and nightmares.
- **Palika (traveling companion) story:** When traveling with a devotee ("Palika"/Pallad) after a hard day of service, that devotee would say, "Now I'm going into the nectar zone." He never had bad dreams, was always Krishna conscious even in sleep — talking in his sleep ("You students have to surrender to Krishna"), singing Hare Krishna, even "giving a 45-minute class" while asleep. For him sleep was the nectar zone; for most of us it is nightmares.
- Narottama dasa Thakura's mood: "Here I am in the middle of a holocaust, thinking, trying to enjoy myself."

## The Foster Mother Who Makes You Cry

- Durga is our "foster mother," under whose charge Krishna places us; she can be "a little mean sometimes."
- **Prabhupada's mother, Rajani:** Prabhupada's father, Gour Mohan De (family name "De"), had him marry Rajani though Prabhupada had "another girl picked out." Prabhupada said his father was kind, "thinking of my future"; the girl "wasn't so devotional," and later Prabhupada was obliged to leave, take sannyasa, come to America, and preach.
- Durga is beautiful in pictures but "her heart is a little different" — like someone very handsome or beautiful who has a wicked heart.
- As the wife of Shiva (called Rudra), she is called Rudrani.
  - Shiva is *Ashutosh* — very easily pleased (offer him a red flower, that's enough), but also very easily displeased; if displeased, "all hell breaks loose." When Shiva becomes really angry, the whole cosmic manifestation burns up.
  - **Rudrani** in Sanskrit means "she who makes you cry." "That's your foster mother."
- We have left the real mother, Hara. "Hare" (the vocative *Hara*) refers to Radha; her expansion is Durga, "of another nature," who "makes you cry forever."

## Material "Happiness" Is Only Relief

- The "cry forever" is an exaggeration — devotees are not crying all the time.
- **Gallup poll:** A recent American survey concluded that people are miserable 5% of the time, happy 5% of the time, and bored 90% of the time. "What are we? We're happy 98% of the time" (occasionally falling into illusion and feeling sad).
- Prabhupada said that even the 5% "happiness" is "not real happiness, only relief."
- **Geneva kirtan reference:** One cannot imagine what it was like when Prabhupada led kirtan — so ecstatic; unfortunately devotees didn't record in those days (no tape recorders, no computers, no cell phones — one big black phone upstairs in the office).
- The "happiness" people experience in this world is just relief: like someone banging on your head who then stops — "Oh, I'm so happy." People work hard and enjoy little.

## We Are the Servant — the Hand and the Stomach

- Devotees work hard and *enjoy* the hard work, "because we're the master." When the master is pleased, the servant is automatically pleased.
- **Hand-and-stomach analogy:** The hand is only the servant/extremity of the body. Suppose there are big gulab-jamuns and the hand rebels: "I'm tired of giving all this food to that big fat stomach; I'm going to enjoy the gulab myself." Foolish — the hand is happy *being the servant*: it takes the gulab, gives it to the mouth, it goes to the stomach, and all the nutrition and sugar returns to the hand. "Now I feel better. Big smile."
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 20.108)** — *jivera svarupa haya krishnera nitya-dasa* — the living entity's constitutional nature is to be the eternal servant of Krishna. Just as sugar is sweet, water is wet, and salt is salty, the intrinsic nature of the soul is to be the servant of God. Anything done outside the realm of service brings no satisfaction; as soon as one takes up that long-lost service, "I feel revived, enthusiastic, happy in Krishna consciousness."

## The Remedy for Sadness — Be Who You Are

- Sometimes devotees become unhappy and sad, but there is a remedy: "Get up! Stand up! Do some service. Just pick up your mala. Just be who you are." Don't try to be something artificial.
- Critique of artificiality: nowadays men try to become women and women want to be men, trying to pass it off as natural though it is not; newspapers show two boys or two girls kissing at their marriage — "the decadence of modern society."
- "Just be who you are." You are not really Hungarian, American, French, black, white, yellow, or green — and not Martians or aliens. "We're brothers and sisters, part of one spiritual family." Go back home; don't land here.
- But who are you? **Bhagavad-gita 4.34** — *tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya* — approach a guru with submission, inquiry, and service.
  - **Sanatana Goswami to Mahaprabhu:** "*Ke ami* — who am I? People say I am a learned person; I know many things, I'm expert at many things, but actually I am a fool. I don't know who I am. Please tell me who I am." Then you'll be happy.

## Maya's Thankless Task and the Purpose of Creation

- Maya can be cruel, but she is only trying to convince us that we cannot be happy without her Lord. She has a "thankless task": she is a pure devotee, and this harassment is her service to Krishna.
  - **Temple-president analogy:** Imagine Vaisnava temple president Vijaya-Gauranga calling you into his office and assigning you the service of harassing everyone, making life miserable, making everyone cry forever — no arati, no cooking, no book distribution. That is Maya's job, done because eventually the soul turns back to the Lord.
- **Bhagavad-gita 7.16** — four kinds of pious people approach Krishna: the distressed (*arta*), the curious/inquisitive (*jijnasu*), those seeking material facility (*artharthi*), and those seeking knowledge (*jnani*). Prabhupada said most people turn to God when in distress.
- The whole purpose of the creation — "the only reason this world exists" — is to make the soul turn its face back to God.
  - **Shadow-and-light analogy:** When you turn away from the sun you see your shadow and darkness; turn around and you see the sun. Turned away from the effulgent form of the Lord, you see the darkness of material existence; turn your face to God and everything is full of light and bliss.
  - The purpose of material existence is that people become frustrated, and their frustration turns them back to God. "Everybody will be brought to their knees by material existence."

## Even the Atheists Come to Their Knees

- Maya is very powerful. To the women's-independence movement: the CEO in charge of the material energy is "a mataji" — Durga. (Wordplay: Polish "*uwaga*"/French "*attention*" — attention: it is a mataji, Durga.)
- **Khrushchev / cosmonaut / Sputnik story (1960s):** The Russians put the first satellite into space; President Khrushchev spoke by radio to the cosmonaut, broadcast to the whole nation (with the CIA listening in). Khrushchev asked, "Comrade, you're out there in space — do you see God?" The cosmonaut looked out the little window: "No, sir. No God — just meteors going by." Khrushchev declared, "You see, there is no God. Lenin and Marx were right." Everyone clapped: "Yes, no God."
  - Then the satellite ran into trouble (electrical failure), began falling back to earth and burning up, and the cosmonaut's last recorded words were, "Oh my God, oh my God, please help me." Khrushchev said, "Delete that part."
  - "It's foolish to be wise where ignorance is bliss."
- Everyone will be brought to their knees — either by good intelligence or by death.

## Order in Creation Proves an Intelligent God

- Good intelligence shows there must be a God; otherwise how is this whole creation going on so nicely, "from the tiny atom up to the giant universe," with so much order? "Order is a sign of intelligence."
- **Pujari-in-the-back-room analogy:** If everyone is out on Sankirtan and you enter and see no one, but everything is nicely arranged — "a place for everything and everything in its place" — you conclude that intelligent people must live there. It couldn't come about by chance.
- **The atom:** (Aside: at Chowpatty, Mumbai — Radhanath Swami's disciples who preach at universities — the speaker once asked who had a PhD in physics; about 32 brahmacharis raised their hands, 20 saying "just a master's.") A little knowledge of physics shows the atom is "like a little universe" — amazing how the protons are arranged.
  - Who is inside the atom? The Supersoul. **Kshirodakasayi Vishnu** "is in every atom of the universe" and in the heart of this entire universe. So even a little intelligence reveals that everything is going on "by higher arrangement."
- Those who come by intelligence, having heard the perfect philosophy, are fortunate; those who are not so intelligent will also be brought to their knees.
- **Dandavats:** Unlike the church where one bows on the knees, "we go all the way down — *dunda*." Like the sannyasi who touches his danda (stick) to the ground and then prostrates. Everyone will be forced to offer obeisances to God — either willingly by intelligence, or by death ("He's paying dandavats to God — he's not getting up for a long time, saying very sincere prayers").

## Rupa Goswami's Prayer and the Swift Deliverer

- The scientists search for the meaning of creation; "the whole purpose of this creation is to make you frustrated."
- **Rupa Goswami's prayer** (a favorite of the speaker): "O my Lord, I am drowning in the painful, fathomless whirlpool of repeated birth and death. O Lord, O friend of those who have no shelter, O effulgent moon of mercy — please, just this one time, quickly, extend your hand to save me."
  - Gloss: like moonshine spreading light in the darkness of night so one feels safe walking home from the temple to one's house in New Vraja-dhama. This is the devotee's prayer while drowning in the "inauspicious ocean of Kali-yuga."
- The Lord comes riding on His bird-carrier Garuda; when you hold your hand up in the ocean, He leans over from Garuda, picks you up, and takes you back home, back to Godhead.
- **Bhagavad-gita 12.7** — "For My devotee, I am the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death" (*tesham aham samuddharta mrityu-samsara-sagarat*). "It sounds poetic, but it is actually a fact."

## How God Hears You — the Supersoul, the Closest Friend

- The question: How does the Lord hear you crying "O God, please help me" in your closed-up apartment in Eger, Pecs, Budapest, or Debrecen ("See, I know all the words") — doors and windows closed in winter, no one on the street hears you?
- He hears because He is the closest thing to you. You think your nose is the closest thing to you, but the Lord is in the heart, right next to you, "just waiting" — He has been waiting for a long time.
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 19.151)** — *brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva / guru-krishna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija* — wandering the universe, a fortunate soul receives the seed of devotion by the mercy of guru and Krishna.
- Since you left the spiritual world, He put Durga in charge of you — but "He pulled a little trick": He entered your heart and accompanies you wherever you go. The subtle body (mind, intelligence, false ego) carries to the next body, and the Supersoul accompanies you there too.
- Therefore the Supersoul is called "the best friend of every living entity."
  - Proverb: "A friend in need is a friend indeed." You know your real friends when you are down and out.
  - **The burned-house story (the speaker's own life at age 18):** He rented a small house with a large garden in a beautiful field far from the city. Many "friends" came, sat in the garden, listened to the birds, played guitar, ate from his kitchen, used his car. One day the house burned down. Word spread, but no one came. Only a rocking chair remained; he pulled it out and sat in it all day, expecting friends to come, help, and offer him a place to live — but nobody came. He realized what kind of "friends" one has in this world.
  - But Krishna is not that kind of friend: He is with us every moment — when things go well and when they go badly — always there in the heart, waiting for the moment we say, "My Lord, help me. Yes, I'm listening."
- **Bhagavad-gita 15.15** — "I am seated in the heart of every living entity; from Me come knowledge, remembrance, and forgetfulness."

## Giving Up the False Desire to Be the Master

- At the moment you cry out, the Lord takes control — because you have given up the false desire to lord it over material nature and to be the master. "I just want to be the servant again... the humble servant of a glorious master."
- **South African maids analogy:** In South Africa, families still have live-in African maids — "big mamas" from the townships, trained and given a room in the house. It is not slavery; they are treated as family members, get food, go on vacation with the family, carry the baby ("the baby loves the maid, the maid loves the baby, the mother loves the maid"). To be the servant of a good master, "there's nothing wrong with that."
- **France — "*Ni maître, ni Dieu*"** — "no master, no God." ("Just wait, you'll see.") But it is nice to have a master.
- **"My Glorious Master":** Bhurijana Prabhu titled his book about Prabhupada *My Glorious Master* — "that type of master."

## The Guru — External Manifestation of the Supersoul

- When you finally call out to the master, He takes charge: He guides you from within the heart to a bona-fide spiritual institution, to the association of devotees, and ultimately to the lotus feet of a bona-fide spiritual master.
- You may not hear Him clearly in the heart — the false ego is still there after millions of lives of rebellion; the signal is "sometimes on, sometimes off" (even in Krishna consciousness — "living in the temple, but our mind is in Budapest").
- Therefore the spiritual master is considered the external manifestation of the Supersoul, "because you can't disobey the guru" (whereas you can disobey your inner conscience). The guru takes responsibility for you — a serious thing: if you go off, he will drag you back, even taking birth again to reclaim you. "Don't make things difficult for the spiritual master. He wants to go back to Golok."
- **Syamasundara and the cigarette:** When Syamasundara was carving the first Jagannatha deities, he set a lit cigarette on the head of the Jagannatha deity while chipping away. Prabhupada, coming outside to see how the carving was going, saw it and said, "Take that cigarette off the head of Jagannatha — what are you doing? You have this bad habit? Why let such a stupid habit come between you and God? You're willing to sacrifice your loving relationship with God for some stupid habit? Stop all that."
- **Guru means "heavy."** If he is not "heavy" with you, that does not mean he is not guru; he must also inspire and bless, but he knows when to bless and when to use "the rod of chastisement."

## God's Mercy and Doing Your Part — the Life Preserver

- This heaviness is God's mercy. You may not see God yet, but you can see the manifestation of His mercy — just as you cannot see the prime minister, but there are many indications around society that he is at work.
- You may wonder, "How does God know me? How does He know I exist?" — Answer: you know He knows, because you are sitting in this room, and "it's not by chance you came here." **Bhagavad-gita** — "Not a blade of grass moves without the sanction of God." If you are sitting in this temple, it is not by chance, karma, or material destiny — the Lord has intervened, seen some sincerity in your heart, and directed you through the doors. So take comfort: the Lord knows you are there.
- **Life-preserver analogy:** The life preserver can be thrown right in front of a drowning person, but that is not enough — he must grab it and hold on tight so he can be pulled to the boat. Likewise, being directed here and sitting between these four walls is not enough. The association of devotees are "the feet of the Lord"; you must grab that life preserver and hold on tight — "you have to do your work as well."

## Your Part of the Deal — It's All Play

- What is our work/our part of the deal? You have to chant, sing, dance, hear this nectarean philosophy, take prasadam, and go to the holy places — Vrindavan, Mayapur, Jagannath Puri (so many holy places in India), and New Vraja-dhama to see Radha-Syamasundara. "That's your work."
- Proverb: "All work and no play made Jack a dull boy" — only working and never playing made the boy dull and unhappy. But in fact "we don't have any work — it's all play." It is intense service, but very relishable.
- Your part of the deal: "Hold on tight to the holy names. Hold on tight to / embrace the Vaisnavas. Hold on tight to the feet of the guru. Hold on tight to Gaura-Nitai" — don't embrace the deities, but dance for their pleasure. "That's all you have to do. It's all play."
- **Paris 1972 story:** When Prabhupada came to Paris in 1972, the devotees had renovated the old temple building he had originally seen. Returning the next year, Prabhupada said from the Vyasasana, "I want to thank you very much for all the hard work you have done for me" — then paused and added, "And actually, it is not hard work. It's devotional service. It's all ecstasy."

## Mother Yasoda's Rope — Our Small Effort, His Great Mercy

- You have to do your part, just as Mother Yasoda bound baby Krishna: Krishna did His part — the rope was always short by two inches, so Krishna "made up one inch," but Mother Yasoda had to add her one inch of effort as well.
- Our effort is very little compared to the Lord's — "He's doing most of the pulling. Just don't let go" and drown again in the ocean.
- Don't lose this golden opportunity of Krishna consciousness. "It's all fun, but it's serious fun" — be very serious about singing and dancing. By the combined mercy of your effort and the mercy of guru and Krishna, "you can make it. You can leave Budapest forever."
- The question is: "Do you want to?" We want to go back home, back to Godhead — life eternal, full of knowledge and bliss. You get a little taste of that here in the temple, which is why it is important to come; but wherever you are, you can live in the spiritual sky by always chanting Hare Krishna.
