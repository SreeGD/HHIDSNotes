## The Verse and Prabhupada's Purport (SB 7.9.28)

- **The famous verse (SB 7.9.28)** — Prahlada Maharaja prays that the Lord place him in touch with a pure devotee, that he may serve him as a sincere servant.
- **Purport (summarized as read):**
  - By studying Srimad-Bhagavatam, every intelligent man can gain experience like that of Prahlada Maharaja through the historical incidents mentioned in this great literature of spiritual knowledge.
  - Following in the footsteps of Prahlada Maharaja, one should gain through experience that **all material opulence is perishable at every moment.** Even this body, for which we try to acquire so many sensual pleasures, may perish at any time.
  - The soul, however, is eternal. **"na hanyate hanyamane sharire"** (*Bhagavad-gita 2.20* — "the soul is never vanquished even when the body is destroyed"). An intelligent man should therefore care for the happiness of the spirit soul, not of the body.
  - Even if one receives a body with a long duration of life, like those of Lord Brahma and the other great demigods, it too will be destroyed; the intelligent man should be concerned with the imperishable spirit soul.
  - To save oneself, one must take shelter of a pure devotee. **Narottama dasa Thakura** therefore says one must become Krishna conscious and try to fully understand Krishna if one wishes to be saved from the onslaughts of material nature that arise because of the material body.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 4.9** — one should understand Krishna in truth, and this one can do only by serving a pure devotee. Thus Prahlada prays that **Lord Nrsimhadeva** place him in touch with a pure devotee and servant, instead of awarding him material opulence.
  - Every intelligent man must follow Prahlada Maharaja: **"mahajano yena gatah sa panthah"** (follow the path of the great souls). Prahlada did not want to enjoy the estate left by his father; rather, he wanted to become a servant of the servant of the Lord. The illusory human civilization that perpetually endeavors for material advancement is rejected by Prahlada and those who strictly follow him.

### Three Types of Material Opulence: Bhukti, Mukti, Siddhi

- **Bhukti** — being situated in a very good position, like a post among the demigods in the higher planetary systems, where one can enjoy material sense gratification to the greatest extent.
- **Mukti** — being disgusted with material advancement and thus desiring to become one with the Supreme.
- **Siddhi** — executing severe meditation like that of the yogis to attain the eight kinds of mystic perfection (*anima, laghima, mahima*, etc.).
- All those who desire material advancement through bhukti, mukti, or siddhi are punishable in due course of time and must return to material activities. **Prahlada rejected them all.** He simply wanted to engage as an apprentice under the guidance of a pure devotee.

## The Theme: Appreciating Devotees

- Today's assigned subject is **appreciating devotees.** Sometimes a lecturer, even an experienced one, gets a hard verse and must think about what to say. But a subject like this is not at all difficult — it comes easily for Vaishnavas, because we are taught to see the good qualities in others.
- We may also see the bad qualities, but we see them with the desire to help the other person.
- **Prabhupada's teaching:** "In Krishna consciousness we have to have a good eye and a bad eye." You must be able to see the good qualities in others; and if you are a leader, a parent, or a spiritual master, you must be able to recognize the weaknesses and bad qualities — but with the desire to help that person overcome them. Generally, in our association, we focus on appreciating the good qualities of other devotees, and they are many.

### The Lord Decorates His Devotees with Good Qualities

- **"sad-guna" / satva-bhusana** — the Lord decorates His devotees with all good qualities so that the mass of people know whom to follow. When one is possessed of divine, spiritual qualities — Krishna consciousness, love of Krishna — it is attractive even to non-devotees, even to demons.
- **Sad-Gosvamy-astaka** — it is stated that the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan were popular both with the gentle and with the ruffians. "Ruffians" is a polite way of describing someone who may even be asuric. Somehow or other, even they are attracted to the divine qualities of a saint, a *sadhu* or a *sadhvi*.
- Krishna wants His devotees in the limelight. Devotees themselves do not want the limelight — they are shy, they do not want personal honor, they want to give all honor to Guru and Krishna. But the Lord puts them in that position, **because "whatever a great man does, the common man will follow; whatever exemplary acts he pursues, the whole world will follow"** (*Bhagavad-gita 3.21*).
- It is far better to have Krishna conscious leaders leading us out of ignorance to truth, out of darkness to light, out of the material world into the spiritual world, than to have leaders with no knowledge of anything spiritual — what to speak of any spiritual qualifications. There is a great need for spiritual leadership in human society. Therefore Krishna decorates His surrendered, accomplished devotee with all spiritual qualities, so the public can see: "Here is My devotee — follow in his footsteps."

### Story: The Decorated War Veterans

- We do not see it so much anymore, because the older generation — the speaker's parents' generation — has mostly departed. But after World War II, those who were heroes in battle were recognized by the government, awarded medals, and actually asked to display those medals on their chests.
- As a young boy going into San Francisco to shop with his mom or dad, the speaker would often see men — and sometimes women — walking down the street with medals of distinction from World War II on their coats, and the people would appreciate them.
- Even as a small child, he once saw a decorated war veteran approaching the door to a shopping center; some children stepped forward, recognized the medals, and opened the door for him. "Those days are gone," where the youth respect the qualities of the elders.
- In the same way, the Lord wants His devotee to be recognized and to be in charge.

### The Lord Places His Devotees in Responsible Positions (Yudhisthira as Emperor)

- One reason the entire Kurukshetra war was fought — there were many reasons — but one of the principal reasons of the greatest battle of all time, in which millions upon millions of men were killed for upholding *dharma*, was to install **Maharaja Yudhisthira** as emperor of the world, the king of kings.
- "Emperor" means the king of all the kings. There are many minor, subordinate kings, but the emperor is over all. Five thousand years ago, we hear from Srimad-Bhagavatam, the emperor was in charge of the whole world; the capital of the whole world was **Hastinapura**, very near modern-day New Delhi.
- Vedic culture was all over the world, and someone had to manage it. The emperor managed the affairs of the entire world so that it would be in a good state — a state for becoming Krishna conscious. Krishna wanted Yudhisthira to be the emperor.
- So the Lord places His devotees in responsible positions so the general mass of people can follow them, and we can appreciate them for what they are.

## The Qualities of a Devotee (SB 3.25.21)

- **"titiksavah karunikah suhrdah sarva-dehinam / ajata-satravah santah sadhavah sadhu-bhusanah"** (*Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.25.21* — the symptoms of a sadhu: tolerant, merciful, a well-wishing friend to all living beings, having no enemies, peaceful, adorned with all good qualities).

### Titiksavah — Tolerant

- The devotee is very tolerant because he is fixed on the goal. He is neither elated when there is an opportunity for sense gratification, nor discouraged when there is some difficulty in his life.
- **"Kuru-nandana"** — he is fixed on Krishna, the Absolute Truth, on his service to the Lord. Even in the midst of the greatest calamity, the devotee is unmoved, equipoised, situated in the Absolute Truth (*shanta*).

### Karunikah — Merciful

- Although he may be very advanced, very fortunate, having received many blessings from Krishna, he is not a miser. He wants to share his good fortune with others.
- **Compassion is the crest jewel of all Vaishnava qualities** — a devotee's compassion for others.
- **Prabhupada's teaching:** "The duty of a first-class man is not to subjugate others. The duty of a first-class man is to make a fifth-class man also a first-class man." Devotees want to elevate others.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** — devotees are restless until they see the whole fourteen worlds chanting Hare Krishna.
- Any opportunity to share Krishna consciousness with others, they will take:
  - It may be an **organized effort** — the December book marathon, a collection drive, or the "carnival spiritual."
  - It may be **unorganized** — the devotee riding on the bus to work always carries a little *Perfection of Yoga* in his pocket, or a *Reservoir of Pleasure*. People start up conversations, inquire about you, and may see a little glimmer in your eye they do not see in others.
- **Prabhupada:** since my disciples have given up sense gratification and taken to the path of devotional service, they are all bright-eyed and smiling and look like they have come from Vaikuntha. So someone may ask, "Why are you smiling on this cloudy day?" — and the devotee takes out the *Reservoir of Pleasure* or the *Perfection of Yoga*, because Prabhupada said: "What will your ten-minute lecture do? But if they get one of my books, they can read it and later become perfect."
- We always want to plant the seed. We are transcendental gardeners. **Chaitanya-caritamrita** — Lord Chaitanya begs: "I am a gardener with these fruits of love of God. Alone, I cannot distribute them everywhere. Who will come forward and help me?" And the devotees call out, "Me!"

### Suhrdah Sarva-dehinam — Friend to Every Living Entity

- How is it possible to be everyone's friend? Look to **Lord Jesus Christ** — whose holiday (25th of December) is coming up, celebrating the birth of the Son of God. When He was on the cross, after all they had done to Him, He looked to the heavens and said, "Father, forgive them for what they do. They don't understand what they are doing."
- This is a devotee. Even if he meets a person who is inimical, he knows that is not that person's original nature. **"jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya-dasa"** (*Chaitanya-caritamrita* — every living entity is by nature the eternal servant of Krishna). Everyone is a loving soul; only due to past sinful activity and ignorance do they become covered and possessed by lust, anger, and greed.
- Knowing this, the devotee — even if someone yells an insult, chides him, or physically attacks him — remains understanding; he is a friend to every living entity.

### Ajata-satravah — He Has No Enemies

- It is said that **Maharaja Yudhisthira's enemy was never born.** This is a devotee — a devotee has no enemies.

## Do Not Judge a Devotee by His Past

- If we look at devotees who have become cleansed, we do not think of what they were like before Krishna consciousness. We do not consider or judge a devotee by what they did before Krishna consciousness — that is not important.
- We look at a devotee on the basis of their devotional service since they joined. And it is a very potent process, so the transcendental qualities come forth very quickly.
- We can appreciate a devotee even after **one week** in the temple. How, after only seven days, when he has been in the material world for millions upon millions of births? Because he has made a **quantum leap** — deciding to give up the false pursuit of sense gratification and his selfish endeavors, and making the attempt to become a devotee, to give pleasure to Guru and the Vaishnavas. We honor that.
- So when we speak of devotees, we mean everyone from the **Uttama-adhikari** down to the **Kanistha-adhikari**. Even a devotee who may be fallen, who may even have left the movement — for the service they have done for this movement, which Krishna never forgets, we honor that devotee, and we try to make friends and bring them back into the fold of Krishna consciousness.

## Two Essential Ingredients: Association of Devotees and the Yuga Dharma

- This festival is about **appreciating and associating with devotees** — an essential aspect. We should have more festivals like this.
- **According to Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya**, associating with devotees and the Yuga Dharma of chanting Hare Krishna are the two essential ingredients of becoming Krishna conscious in this age.
- Sometimes people (e.g., a newspaper reporter) ask us to summarize Krishna consciousness in a few words. One reply: "singing, dancing, a little philosophy, feasting." Another reply: "It's chanting Hare Krishna and the association of devotees." Essentially that is what it is — these two ingredients.
- A verse states that in this age of quarrel, the remedy for those fallen souls who have been bitten by the serpent of *samsara* in the age of Kali is **the name of the Supreme Lord and menial service to exalted Vaishnavas.**

### Story: "Packed Up Tight"

Prabhupada said, "This is very nice — we are packed up tight. This is how we want to be: safe in the association of devotees."

## We Take On the Quality of Our Association

- Advancement is very quick for one who associates on a regular basis with devotees, because, as Prabhupada explains, **we take on the quality of that with which we associate.**
- **Businessman:** if someone associates with a businessman long enough, he starts dressing, talking, and acting like a businessman.
- **Athlete:** if someone associates with an athlete, he starts lifting weights, taking protein powder, and buying a shirt two sizes too small.
- **Rock star:** if you associate with a rock star, you may be a wannabe, but you start dressing like one and trying to croak in the shower like one — you take on the qualities.
- So if we associate with devotees — by chance, by good fortune, or consciously — we begin to take on their qualities.

### We Are the Tatastha-Shakti

- It is mysterious: we are the **tatastha-shakti** (marginal energy) of the Lord. We are in a special category — the minute living entity. God is great and we are very small. We are spiritual, but being infinitesimal parts and parcels of the Lord, we are apt to become influenced either by matter or by spirit, depending on which association the living entity falls into.

### Analogy: The Chameleon Lizard

- The speaker recalls buying chameleon lizards as a kid — they had to send away for them, and they would arrive in the post in boxes (from Indonesia or somewhere; "pretty cruel," he admits). He could play for hours:
  - Put one on a green leaf → he turns green (nature's defense, so predators can't see him).
  - Put him on a red surface → he turns red. "Wow, Mom, look!" — "I've looked a thousand times, son." — "No, look..."
  - Put him on brown → he turns brown; on yellow → kind of orangey.
- We are like that — the tatastha-shakti, very tiny. It depends on with what and who we associate in our lifetime. We have to be very careful to choose our friends.
- **"A person is known by the friendship they keep"** — not only because others know you by your associates, but because, due to that association (*sangam*), you begin to manifest those qualities.

## Living and Working Among Materialists: Give Association, Don't Take It

- 98% of the community has to work, so we are always in association with materialistic people. But the secret is: **we are not taking their association — essentially, we are giving our association.**
- How do you live "out there" and not get affected? Because you are not taking their association; you are not intimidated by them.
- **Prabhupada's instruction (giving danda to one sannyasi in Mayapur, 1975):** "Never look back and think you've left anything important behind, and never envy the position of the materialistic sense enjoyers." So we are not out there feeling unlucky and forlorn — we are there to give association, not to get it.
- At the coffee break or lunch, you open up your lunch and there is no meat, fish, or eggs. People think it is radical. "What's that?" — "Well, I don't eat dead bodies; you won't find any corpses in my lunch bag." — "What do you mean by that?" — "So then I'll tell you." Then you share your lunch (made by Kurma): "Try this."

### Subtle Association: Media as "The One-Eyed Guru"

- We must be careful, because we are the tatastha-shakti. Associating with materialistic people does not just mean being physically in their presence. Even more, it may mean **hearing their philosophy, hearing them pour out their hearts in their music** ("I love you, you love me...").
- Or the **television** — this is the most sinister way that Kali pervades in Kali-yuga. We call it **the one-eyed guru.** The karmis are pouring out their hearts in so many ways — soap operas, news broadcasts, movies — revealing their hearts. It appears innocent, but this is Maya's trick.
- By watching television, hearing the radio, and following your favorite bands, it has a subtle influence; it collects after a while and becomes a material desire, and you wonder, "Where did that come from?"
- So association does not just mean rubbing elbows on the bus on the way to work. It also means hearing mundane discussions. What do people discuss? What they do for pleasure — they go to the pub, stand outside with the beer, and talk for hours; they go to the beach or the pub.
- With all due respect — because ultimately they are our brothers and sisters, and we speak in a mode of compassion, not disrespect — they have lost their way.

### Asat-sanga-tyaga: Give Up Bad Association

- That type of association is called **"asat-sanga-tyaga,—ei vaisnava-acara"** (*Chaitanya-caritamrita* — to give up the association of non-devotees is the conduct of a Vaishnava), from the words of Mahaprabhu through the line of the Goswamis. A Vaishnava should not associate intimately with ordinary people. Even a little association can get you off the path.

### Story: The Fall of Ajamila (from the Bhagavatam)

- **Ajamila** was a *pukka* brahmana boy, born in a brahmana family — which is very rare. To be born in a brahmana family means that in previous lifetimes you acted in a very pious way; more than pious, you were learned, and you got the opportunity to take birth in a righteous family. They performed all the *samskaras*, and he wore the sacred thread.
- One day Ajamila was walking through the forest to the ashram of his guru, and he happened to see a *sudra* couple hiding, kissing and embracing in the forest. That is not much compared to what a five-year-old can see on TV today, but in those days it was enough to cause his mind to become agitated.
- In a very short time he gave up his pure lifestyle; as he grew up he married a prostitute, they had a family, and he maintained that family by being a thief.
- How did a pukka brahmana boy become a thief? **Just one moment of inattention** was enough to cause him to fall away.

### Analogy: Swine Flu (H1N1)

- What is swine flu? You don't expect a huge monster with "H1N1" on his chest to walk up and say, "I'm going to gobble you up, I'm swine flu." Swine flu is a **virus** — so difficult to detect that sometimes even the doctors can't detect it; they take your blood, put it under the microscope, and analyze it; it is so small they might miss it. But when they catch it, you have H1N1.
- It is very little, but it can bring down the biggest, most influential, most important person. A big Hulk of an athlete, a pole vaulter ready for the meet — and the next day, "Achoo... oh, my tummy, my head, I can't go to the meet." "You're a big strong..." — "H1N1."
- It doesn't take much to bring down even a transcendentalist. We have to be very careful. There are so many examples in scripture of great souls who, due to a little inattentiveness, some past conditioning, or some desire they hadn't washed from the heart — or just by chance, in a weak moment when they weren't chanting and were thinking of something else — then, boom.

### Story: "You're Not Afraid of Maya"

- Prabhupada once told one of the speaker's Godbrothers: "Your problem is you're not afraid of Maya. You're so confident, you're so advanced now, you don't have to be afraid of Maya." We must be **on guard.** The race is not over till it's over.

### Analogy: The Tortoise and the Hare

- The hare (rabbit) was way ahead, so he took a nap (Prabhupada said "snap"). The turtle kept going. Just before the turtle crossed the finish line, the rabbit woke up and tried to run to catch up, but he couldn't, and the turtle won.
- The race is not over. The rabbit was very confident: "I'm so fast, I've made so much progress, I'm practically there, I can take a nap, I can take it easy." But he became proud and complacent.
- The turtle was very determined — he was a **sadhaka-bhakta:** "I'm chanting my rounds, following the principles, reading the books, going to the temple, and I'm done." And he made it. So we have to be careful — Maya can attack at any moment.

## The Same Principle Works for Spiritual Association

- We do not aspire to bad association, and we will try to avoid it. Fortunately, the **same principle** that makes brief moments of bad association (like watching TV) so influential also holds true for spiritual association and becoming Krishna conscious. (Joke: "I'm sure nobody's going to go home and watch TV tonight — you'll be too scared. Maybe tomorrow morning. But at least this lecture will carry you till midnight.")
- We are the tatastha-shakti — small; and if we place ourselves under the care of **Hari, Guru, and the Vaishnavas**, even just a little of their association can give us perfection.

### A Moment's Association Cannot Be Compared to Any Material Benediction (SB 1.18.13)

- **Srimad-Bhagavatam (Suta Goswami)** — the value of a moment's association with a devotee of the Lord cannot be compared even to the attempt to attain the heavenly planets or liberation from matter, what to speak of worldly benedictions in the form of material prosperity, which are meant for those who are meant for death.
- You cannot compare a moment's association with the devotee to any material benediction, mystic perfection, liberation, or merging into the Brahman — **because a moment's association with the devotee gives you the opportunity for pure devotional service, the supreme occupation for all mankind.**

### The Supreme Occupation Must Be Unmotivated and Uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6)

- **"sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhoksaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati"** (*Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6* — the supreme occupation for all humanity is that by which one can attain to the loving devotional service of the transcendent Lord; such service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self).
- What we should be doing and trying to attain is pure devotional service. That service must be **unmotivated and uninterrupted.** And what happens if you get that type of bhakti, that love for Krishna? You are completely happy — nothing else can give that.

### How Do You "Catch" Bhakti?

- How do you get bhakti? You get it from another devotee — like catching a cold. Somebody sneezes, and three days later you come down with it.
- When the pure devotee is speaking on the Bhagavata, because it is such a nectarean vibration, it collects like dew drops in the air and falls down on the heads of the audience, and they experience ecstatic symptoms.
- It is a **good disease.** You get the good disease of Krishna consciousness by staying in the association of devotees — even if you can't understand a word they are saying.

### Story: "Why Are You Sleeping When I'm Speaking?" (Hyderabad)

- The speaker was traveling in South India with a group of devotees, with Srila Prabhupada. They went down to Hyderabad, where Prabhupada was to install the Radha-Krishna deities in a beautiful new temple. Afterward they went out to the farm.
- Prabhupada sat down to give a lecture to some guests. (Aside — the result of eating a feast before your lecture: the speaker couldn't even stand.) Prabhupada spoke to the guests in Hindi, going on and on, and the speaker — who doesn't know any Hindi — fell into a deep slumber.
- Prabhupada stopped the darshan. In his sleep the speaker heard the words: "Why are you sleeping when I'm speaking to the guests?" He thought he was dreaming, then woke up. "But Prabhupada, I don't speak Hindi." Prabhupada said: **"It does not matter that you don't speak Hindi. It is transcendental subject matter — it will act. Stay awake."** After that the speaker didn't even blink for one hour.
- Even if we don't understand, just being there in that environment, in that atmosphere — that is the effect. Therefore we must come into physical association with as many devotees as possible; a sustained period of time has such an effect.

### The Verdict of All Scriptures (Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya 22.54)

- What did Mahaprabhu say? **"sadhu-sanga, sadhu-sanga—sarva-sastre kaya / lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya"** (*Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya 22.54* — the verdict of all revealed scriptures is that by even a moment's association with a pure devotee, one can attain all success). What to speak, then, if you are constantly in the association of devotees.

## How to Get Association Without Living in the Temple

- It doesn't mean you have to live in the temple — of course that's not possible; this temple isn't big enough for the whole congregation. But it means you have to come here as often as possible and participate in the various devotional activities:
  - See the deities.
  - Take **prasada** — the maha-prasada of **Radha Govinda.** Nothing in the three worlds can compare with that maha-prasada.
  - Hear the discourses.
  - Go on the **harinams**, start the **Nama Hatta**, visit devotees' houses.
  - Go to **Mayapur**, go to **Vrindavan.**
- It's not easy, because we're all "busy as bees." But **"there's always room for jello."**

### Analogy: "There's Always Room for Jello"

- There was a marketing campaign in the speaker's country many years ago for jello — a sweet at the end of the meal. Usually by the end of a meal you think, "I'm full." But their slogan was: "There's always room for jello."
- Someone brings the sweet rice around and says, "Oh, you look too full." — "No, no, no, it's okay, I'll take some sweet rice." You should see this: this bhajan of chanting Hare Krishna and the association of devotees is the **sweetest of the sweet.**

### The Sweetness of Krishna (Vallabha's Prayer)

- There is a beautiful prayer in the **Vallabha Sampradaya**, where Vallabha, the originator of the sampradaya, sings a song about the sweetness of Krishna's feet, the sweetness of Krishna's body, the sweetness of Krishna's flute — everything sweet, sweet, sweet (*Madhurashtakam*).
- If you realize that, if you become a little purified — if your senses have become purified by chanting Hare Krishna — then you'll hanker for that sweet, you'll hanker for that association. It's not that someone will have to tell you to come to the temple; you'll already be there. "Where are they? We have to find them and bring them." "Probably they've been there all day, doing devotional service."

### Analogy: The Magnet

- The same principle that takes us away from Krishna consciousness can, in a moment's association, bring us into good association — *sadhu-sanga.*
- Just like a magnet: if you put the negative poles of two magnets near each other, they repulse and go away from each other; but if you turn them around and put the positive poles together, they attract. It's just how you use it.
- So if you use your life to associate with non-devotees, you'll be pulled away from Krishna consciousness; but the same principle of association, dovetailed in Krishna consciousness, pulls you toward Krishna.
- **Prabhupada said he created the International Society for Krishna Consciousness for one reason only:** so that we could come and associate with devotees.

## Different Levels of Devotees in the Society

- Naturally, in the society of devotees you're going to get devotees on different levels:
  - Some are advanced due to their number of years in Krishna consciousness.
  - Some, because of their sincere practice of Krishna consciousness for many births, had just a little touch of subtle desire left to cleanse, so they took another birth — and for them it just appears so easy.
- Many of us struggle through the years, but then you meet devotees for whom it looks so natural — from day one it was natural to take to the path of Krishna consciousness and to chant and dance. They remain aloof from politics, backbiting, criticizing, and fault-finding; they are not prone to laziness; they can control their senses and their minds; and they do find (make) the time to associate with devotees.
- So just being here in the movement means you're going to have their association — the association of advanced souls.

### Friendship at Your Own Level

- Association with devotees at your **same level** is also good. You need a strong bond of friendship in Krishna consciousness — someone at the same level with whom you can talk about it: the struggles, the trials, the joys, the victories.
- You need a friend you can just talk to, who understands, who can cry when you cry and laugh when you laugh. If you don't have that here, you'll have to go "out there" to get it. We need friendship.

### Compassion for Those Less Advanced — Spiritual Mathematics

- You'll always find devotees who are less advanced than you. That shouldn't make you feel good ("Yeah, look at you"). No — a sense of compassion should rise up in your heart, and you should go to them and give them whatever you have.
- "But if I give them whatever I have, I'll have nothing left." No — that's **mundane mathematics.** **Spiritual mathematics means one minus one is a million.** If you give, you get back more, by Krishna's mercy. Krishna is very happy when we take care of our devotees, and He blesses you.

## The Five Most Essential Items of Devotional Service (CC Madhya 22.128)

- Association with devotees is so important that it is listed as one of the **64 items** of devotional service. But there are **five items** to summarize it — because life is short and we want to get in the fast lane and get home quickly. We want to take the elevator, not the steps; the steps are too risky — we may get deviated, we may lose interest.

### "There's No Place Like Home"

- We just want to get home safe. "There's no place like home," the speaker's mother used to say — every time they returned from a vacation, the first thing she said pulling into the garage was, "There's no place like home." His father would say, "That's what ladies feel like — the shelter, the home, the kids, the family."
- But where is our **real** home? That (the spiritual world) is our real home.

### The Express Route — The Five Items (CC Madhya 22.128)

- **"sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-sravana / mathura-vasa, sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana"** (*Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya 22.128* — one should associate with devotees, chant the holy name, hear Srimad-Bhagavatam, reside in Mathura, and worship the deity with faith and veneration). "If you learn any verse, learn that one."
  1. Associate with devotees.
  2. Chant the holy names.
  3. Hear the Bhagavatam.
  4. Reside in Mathura.
  5. Worship the deity with faith and veneration.

### Story: "If I Told You All of Them, You'd Faint"

- "What do I have to do to become Krishna conscious? It's so complicated, there are so many things to do, I can't get all the rules and regulations." One time a devotee asked, "Prabhupada, are there any more rules we have to follow besides chanting Hare Krishna?" — and Prabhupada hadn't even introduced the four regulative principles yet. Prabhupada said: **"Yes. If I told you all of them, you'd faint."**
- So it is with the five: chant Hare Krishna, hear the Bhagavatam, reside in Mathura — or Sydney — come to the temple, and worship Radha Gopinath. They are all important.

### Of the Five, Association Is the Essential One (The Salt)

- You could say that, of course, the **Yuga Dharma** — chanting Hare Krishna — is the means for achieving liberation and becoming Krishna conscious. But you could also say that the **essential of those five is associating with devotees**, because the success of the other four is dependent on the association of devotees. We really need devotees to do those other things.
- It's like **salt.** Generally, to appreciate the taste of food, you have to add salt.
  - Aside: The speaker recalls two times since he's been here when a sweet had no sugar — one day a **laddu** with no sugar (he was so hungry, said the prayer really fast, bit into it, and didn't want to say anything because it was offered to Govinda — "there wasn't one grain of sugar"); another day a **sandesh** with no sugar. So too with salt.
- We salt not for ourselves but for Radha Govinda. Imagine food without salt — salt brings out the flavor of any kind of food. **Association with devotees brings out the essence, brings out the results, of any other kind of devotional activity.**

## Chanting — And Why We Still Need a Devotee

- There are many ways to chant. Even if you just mimic the devotees ("oh, the Hare Krishna..."), still you're saying Krishna's name.
- **Prabhupada:** if someone chants Krishna's name even one time, the Lord notes it, marks it down in a little black book, and follows that person birth after birth until He can make him a devotee.
- So if you have someone you don't really like, just get them to say "Hare Krishna." Even if you just say part of the syllables — "haram, haram" —

### The Meaning of "Haram" and the Wild Boar

- "Haram" is an Arabic word meaning "how horrible." Prabhupada gave the analogy: in the old days, warriors would go to the forest to hunt. Muslim warriors would sometimes be attacked by a wild animal — a wild boar. For our Muslim brothers and sisters, the most horrific thing is to be killed by a pig (they avoid eating pigs). So if you got stabbed by a boar, you'd cry, "Haram, haram — how horrible!" — and yet you'd chanted a syllable of the holy name.
- Some benefit is there: you get to take birth again, but this time in a devotee family — and you might be a little rascal when young because of your past conditioning, but "haram" — you chanted just a syllable of the holy name.
- **There is no difference between the name, fame, form, pastimes, and paraphernalia of God and God Himself.** The holy names are identical to Krishna.

### But for Prema, You Need Initiation and Training

- To get the **prema** (the ultimate benefit of chanting Hare Krishna), you have to receive the mantras of the **guru-parampara** — you have to take initiation from a bona fide spiritual master in Krishna consciousness. Then you become blessed to get the full benefit.
- In essence, there is someone there to train you. Krishna consciousness is a **science** — there's a hypothesis, an experiment, and a result — but you need someone to train you in the finer aspects of chanting.
- **"trnad api sunicena taror api sahisnuna / amanina manadena kirtaniyah sada harih"** (*Siksastaka* verse 3 — one who is more humble than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, devoid of false prestige, and ready to offer all respect to others, can chant the holy name of the Lord constantly). If you're going to chant, chant like that — in a humble state of mind, thinking yourself lower than the street, more tolerant than the tree, devoid of all sense of false prestige, ready to offer all respects unto others. It's not just that the lips are moving; there's a science, and you need someone to train you.
- To get initiated means that someone has taken the responsibility to educate you and enlighten you about the process and the glories of chanting Hare Krishna. To get the full benefit of chanting, you have to take initiation in the **Brahma-Gaudiya Vaishnava Sampradaya** — then only can you get prema. And devotees are very happy to spread the chanting.

### Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's Prophecy About ISKCON

- Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya wrote a beautiful prayer: **"From the moment that the holy names of Hari appeared on the earth, Vaishnavas became manifest everywhere. Adorned with faultless tilaka and neckbeads and equipped with the maha-mantra, in the midst of the age of Kali they purified the atmosphere, chanting 'Hari! Hari!'"**
- This is essentially a prophecy, made 500 years ago, about ISKCON — Vaishnavas became manifest everywhere: Australia, America, China. "So indeed, it came to pass."

## Bhagavata-Sravana — Hearing the Bhagavatam (SB 1.2.18)

- **"nasta-prayesv abhadresu nityam bhagavata-sevaya / bhagavaty uttama-sloke bhaktir bhavati naisthiki"** (*Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.18* — by regularly attending the Bhagavatam class and rendering service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is praised with transcendental songs, is established as an irrevocable fact).
- You have to hear the Bhagavatam. If you can't hear it here, you have to hear it at home. That's why Krishna gave the intelligence to the scientists (Mr. Steve Jobs) to make iPods.
- **Bhagavad-gita 10.10** — "tesam satata-yuktanam bhajatam priti-purvakam / dadami buddhi-yogam tam yena mam upayanti te" (to those who serve Me with love, I give the understanding by which they can come to Me). So the Lord gave the intelligence to Steve Jobs: "Do it like this." — "What do I call it?" — "Call it iPod." You may not be at the temple, but you're home cooking and hearing.

### Hear From Your Own Spiritual Master

- If you have chosen your spiritual master and you're following him — being initiated by him — you must hear from him regularly. You cannot advance in Krishna consciousness without hearing the Lord's representative, your chosen representative.
- Of course we hear from all gurus, but in particular we must hear from our own spiritual master, because he is the one meant to inspire us, the one who has taken responsibility to take us back to Godhead, the one whose footsteps we want to follow.
- The essential relationship between guru and disciple is one of **hearing.** **Bhagavad-gita 4.34** — "tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya" (approach such a person, inquire from him submissively, and render service).

### Hear From a Devotee, Not for Money

- The Bhagavatam does not mean you just sit and read (of course we read Prabhupada), but you have to hear from a devotee. **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu:** "One should hear and study the Bhagavatam only from the person Bhagavatam" (the self-realized devotee).
- The speaker must be, as far as possible, a **transparent via-medium**, because it is the Lord speaking through him — not that the Lord is up top speaking through him, but that he is repeating the message of Godhead. He must be a transparent via-medium in order to instill Krishna consciousness into the hearts of his audience.
- If there is some selfish motivation — as with a **Bhagavata-saptaha** done as a business (Bhagavata-saptaha means someone takes the Bhagavatam at a certain time of year and speaks for seven days, reading all 18,000 verses; afterward he goes around asking for a donation, reading not to enlighten but as a profession to make money) — hearing from such a person will not give the same effect. Hear instead from a devotee who does it out of mercy, simply sharing his realizations.

### Story: "You've Only Seen Stone" (Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati)

- Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati was giving a lecture in a hall in India; there was a deity room nearby. In the middle of the lecture the arati bell and the conch began. A few of his students stood up, walked out to see the arati, and came back to the lecture half an hour later.
- Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati stopped and asked, "Where were you?" They said, "Guru Maharaja, we went to see Krishna." He replied: **"You've only seen stone. If you want to hear Krishna, you sit and you listen to your spiritual master. Then Krishna will be revealed."**

## Mathura-Vasa — Residing in a Holy Place

- Many people have a misconception about a holy place — that you just go there, and simply by visiting you get purified.
- Nowadays there's a new four-lane highway from Delhi to Agra. They didn't make it for Vrindavan; they made it for the tourists. (Fortunately, "the Muslims built that place out there," not knowing how it would facilitate the Vaishnavas hundreds of years later.) You can now speed out to Vrindavan in about two hours.
- On weekends Vrindavan is chock-a-block, full of people — Delhi-wallas. They come out and try to drive through the small streets; the big fat man, the lady, they don't want to get out of the car and walk. So they drive these big Tata Sumos (jeeps) through streets where there's hardly room to walk, getting angry: "Hey, out of our way!" Then they get out, see the deity, buy some sweets, and go back to Delhi: "I have been to Braj. I have seen Krishna. Have you seen Krishna, Swami?"
- Still, there is so much benefit from going there. But **Prabhupada said the real benefit in going to a holy place is to associate with the holy people who are living there.** Those holy people are there for their own benefit, and someone happens to come. Those sadhus are always speaking — that is how we become realized.

### Example: How Lord Brahma Became Realized

- The example is there in Lord Brahma. How did Brahma become realized? His guru was Krishna — he had a special guru, the *adi-guru*, Krishna. How did Krishna enlighten him? He played His flute (*venu*). From His *vamsi* came the **Gayatri Mantra**, out of Krishna's flute; it entered into the right ear of Brahmaji, into his heart, and he became Krishna conscious.
- So when you go to Vrindavan, make sure you spend a lot of time hearing the Bhagavatam classes and going on parikrama with **Dina Bandhu** — "he's my Vrindavan parikrama guru."

## Sri-Murtira Sevana — Serving the Deity

- Serving the deity is very potent. But how do we know anything about the deity if it wasn't for Prabhupada? **Prabhupada introduced deity worship to the Western world.** Westerners thought that anything with even a scent of deity worship was idol worship.
- But now it is flourishing in the West — thousands of temples. So many Westerners have become Vaishnavas and Vaishnavis, and with great faith they approach the Lord with folded hands.

### The Faith of the Deity Worshiper (Sakshi Gopal)

- They repeat the prayer from **Chaitanya-caritamrita** — the young brahmana who went back to ask the deity to stand witness (**Sakshi Gopal**): "My dear Lord, you are not a statue. You are directly the son of Maharaja Nanda. Please bear witness. Come back to my village in Orissa and stand witness for me, please."
- The deity said, "Well, how will I go? I am a deity. How can I walk?" The boy said: **"Lord, if you can talk, you can walk."**
- We have this faith — the deity is not just stone. And Krishna is there, by His mercy, because of one pure devotee, Srila Prabhupada.

### We Are Assisting the Spiritual Master

- **"sri-vigraharadhana-nitya-nana-srngara-tan-mandira-marjanadau..."** (*Sri Gurvastaka* verse 3 — the spiritual master is always engaged in the temple worship of Sri Sri Radha and Krishna; he also engages his disciples in such worship — they dress the deities in beautiful clothes and ornaments, clean the temple, and perform similar worship).
- When we worship the deity — even "my deities" — we should think: "Who am I to approach Krishna? I'm still an aspiring devotee, still a sadhaka, still a Kanistha-adhikari. I'm assisting my spiritual master in the worship of this deity. This is not my deity. I don't own Krishna — Krishna owns me."
- By his causeless mercy, Krishna has appeared. So before his puja, the speaker worships his Guru Maharaja and takes his blessings: "Guru Maharaja, please let me assist you in the worship of this shalagram; let me assist you in the worship of your beloved Radha and Krishna." We are assisting the spiritual master.
- That is the real meaning, because everything is meant to train us to be the servant of the servant of the servant. When we go back to the spiritual world, we don't have a direct relationship with Krishna — we're not like the *sakhis* and special *manjaris.* We will assist the liberated souls; we'll assist our spiritual master in his service to his spiritual master, to Krishna. **We are forever the servant.**
- Don't get discouraged — it's very nice to be the servant of a glorious master. If you have a very glorious, benevolent, realized master, you're safe and secure; he'll provide everything you need to get out of this world and into that world — that's the spiritual master's responsibility. You just serve faithfully. **Prabhupada says in one purport: "For the sincere disciple, the spiritual master takes full responsibility for his deliverance."**
- We'll always be the servant. Even as masters, we're servants — because all of us who are doing the service of the spiritual masters sit at the feet of Prabhupada. **"Prabhupada" means "he at whose feet all the masters sit."** He is the Founder-Acharya of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness. Anyone acting as a spiritual master can do so only on his authority, as a proper representative, spreading the message exactly as Prabhupada did — then they can be effective.

### Prabhupada Brought Krishna to You: "Here Is Krishna, Take Him"

- What do we know of Krishna? "I know He's blue. I know He plays a flute." We know little. Gradually all will be revealed by mercy.
- Even our **Radha Gopinath** — how did we get them? Prabhupada brought them here. You should always remember that. That is not the same everywhere in the world; you have some special grace. You have **no excuse** for not becoming pure devotees in your lifetime — Prabhupada personally, with his own arms, brought Krishna to you.
- One time he said: **"Here is Krishna. Take Him."** That is to say — you cannot achieve Krishna through any type of material activity, through millions of years of practicing mystic powers or merging in the Brahman; but you meet one pure Vaishnava like Prabhupada, and "Here He is. Take Him." That is the shortcut — to get the *sanga*, the association, of a pure devotee and his representatives.

## Summary: Association Is the Salt

- Altogether, these five most essential items of devotional service are like the salt. You put the salt in the preparation, and the flavor, the benefit, comes out. You salt all the other activities of Krishna consciousness with the association of devotees, and you become Krishna conscious.
- **Prabhupada:** anyone who thinks he can be Krishna conscious outside of the association of devotees (whenever we can get it) is living in a hallucination.
- "The medicinal herb to purify the minds of fools absorbed in sense pleasure is faithful service to Sri Guru; the diet is remnants of prasada taken by Vaishnavas." (Aside: "Are there any remnants left over here today? Does anyone know where Kurma Prabhu's plate is? We can take a little remnants.")

## Srila Prabhupada's Association Is Always Available

- We must take advantage of this priceless treasure of associating with devotees while we can. This verse mentions in particular **pure devotees** — and the association of Srila Prabhupada is always available.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** — "He reasons ill who says that Vaishnavas die, when thou art living still in sound. The Vaishnava dies to live, and while living tries to spread the holy name around."
- Prabhupada is as present now as he was when physically present — for a sincere practitioner. Why do you think he spent from one o'clock in the morning till four o'clock every day (once he was older) writing the purports to his books? Just for the few of us who were his direct disciples for the few years he was with us? No — he said, "One day my books will become gospel." That's how you get his association.
- So we should also hear the taped lectures of Srila Prabhupada as our Founder-Acharya, our Param-guru; we should read his books very carefully; and we should associate with senior members of the movement.

### There Are Advanced Devotees — Which Proves Prabhupada's Potency

- Although we respect all devotees, there are senior members who have grasped and caught the message and are practicing it. If that were not the case, it would not attest to the spiritual powers of Srila Prabhupada to elevate his disciples to an exalted level.
- Some people are kind of hoping that there won't be any advanced members of ISKCON attaining prema-bhakti — but that would mean Prabhupada was not the pure devotee he was, able to elevate even the most fallen soul to the topmost stage of devotional service.
- So you will find such devotees: **Radhanatha Swami, Sivarama Swami, B.B. Govinda Maharaja, B.B. Krishna Goswami, Prabhavishnu Swami, Kurma Prabhu**, and Matajis as well; on the GBC, **Malati**, and so many. "Not by gender — it's a question of the heart." We should recognize that these great souls are there, and while they are here, we should take their association, serve them, and assist them.

### Every Generation Is a Wave

- They will not be here forever; another generation will take over. In that generation you'll find Uttama, Madhyama, and Kanistha too, but there is something special about every generation.
- **There was something special about Prabhupada's generation.** The speaker remembers going with Prabhupada to programs in Bombay and India — to the houses of very, very wealthy, yet extremely Krishna conscious families. How they received Srila Prabhupada; the culture that was there; how they were dressed; how the house was sparkling clean; how every preparation was so perfect; how opulent were the deities in the house. Those older Indian people, how they talked and interacted — "it's gone now." He feels so fortunate to have seen that generation, so rich with Chaitanya-ism and Vaishnavism (especially the Bengali culture). As a young boy of 21 or 22, walking in — "wow." The food; the way they served it — remarkable. And it's gone; India is losing it so quickly.
- Then this next generation of Vaishnavas — so many wonderful devotees in ISKCON — something unique; grasp it while it's there. Then your generation, as you grow up, will have something special about the **Gurukulis** — the Gurukuli generation (maybe they didn't go to the Gurukul, but who took up after "we left") — with your own unique contribution, keeping the flow going.

When he came on the Polish tour a couple of years ago doing cooking demonstrations, to this day at every festival somebody comes and asks, "Where's the man from Australia with the Australian accent who cooked that nice food?" ## Honoring Everyone — Especially the Silent Soldiers

- We should also recognize and honor those who are up and coming. "You're all up and coming." Every generation has its devotees who lead the congregation, and amongst the congregation there are many **silent soldiers.**
- It's not that Kurma cut all the vegetables himself — he had a team who worked for many hours; he started cooking two days ago. The silent soldiers do all the cut-ups, clean the temple, work in the administration, are out with the buckets (collecting), and are at home raising their children.

### Turning Your Back on Maya — "Just Say No"

- Everyone is contributing; everyone is a special soul who is part of this movement; everyone should be recognized and honored, because they have taken the bold step of turning their back on Maya.
- There was a campaign in America a few years ago to get kids to stay away from drugs, with the slogan **"Just say no."** The drug pusher comes to your high school and offers you marijuana, crack, or speed at a reduced price — "Just say no."
- If you can turn your back on the material energy, the propensity to enjoy in that way, and just say no — and turn around to Krishna and say, "I do" — we honor you.

### Rama Takes Charge of the Surrendered Soul

- **Ramayana** — Rama says: anyone who comes to me and says, "My dear Lord, for so long I have forgotten you, but now I am your surrendered servant," I take charge of that person, I take care of that person.
- From a lecture by Radhanatha Swami: in the untold stories of the Ramayana, Rama said, "Even if Ravana were to come to me and say, 'Ram, for so long I have forgotten you; now I am yours,' I would take charge, I would take care of him."
- So turn your back on the elusive energy of Maya; by turning your back on the darkness, you'll face the light — you'll face Krishna consciousness, a whole new experience. Someone who does that, we honor them.
