## The Auspiciousness of Initiation

- Welcome to this very auspicious function where devotees are taking initiation:
  - Some are taking **first initiation** (one in particular is taking first initiation).
  - Others are taking **second initiation**.
  - Some have already received second initiation but are sitting in for the fire *yajña*.
  - Gopi Gita was first initiated by Goswami Maharaj, but as a service to him, "I'm giving her another second initiation." This is extra mercy — "she has him on one side and me on the other; we'll lift her back to Goloka." Others as well.
- Generally these functions are performed on especially auspicious days like **Janmastami** or **Radhastami**, but initiation can be performed on **any day of the year**, because the initiation itself is all-auspicious:
  - The disciple is taking a firm commitment to surrender to Krishna's representative, and thus comes closer to Krishna.
- The definition of auspiciousness (as said the other day): **coming closer to God**. Inauspiciousness is anything we do that takes us further away from God.
- So this is a big step forward for the initiates — "like we say in physics, this is a quantum leap." (Aside: "I'm not a physicist, so I don't know exactly what it means, but I know it's a big step forward.")

## The Vows and the Reciprocal Responsibility of the Spiritual Master

- The disciple is taking shelter of the spiritual master. If the disciple remains loyal by upholding the vows taken in initiation:
  - To **chant 16 rounds a day**.
  - To **follow the four regulative principles**.
  - To **associate with devotees**.
  - To **render some service to the Sankirtan movement**.
- In reciprocation, the spiritual master takes on the responsibility to deliver us.
- Contrast of paths:
  - On the path of *yoga* or *jñāna*, a devotee's success depends largely on his own effort.
  - But on the path of Krishna consciousness — *bhakti-yoga* — we have many well-wishers who take responsibility to carry us to Krishna's lotus feet.

## Prabhupada as "Ever Well-Wisher"

- When Srila Prabhupada wrote letters to his disciples engaged in service, he always signed **"your ever well-wisher."**
  - "I don't know if previous acharyas did this — traditionally I don't think there was such a form — but Prabhupada always signed 'your ever well-wisher.'"
- So for an eager, obedient disciple who wishes to serve, the spiritual master is merciful: he recommends us to Krishna and guides us on the path of devotional service back to the lotus feet of Lord Krishna.
- In fact, in the later cantos of **Srimad Bhagavatam** there is a verse to the effect that if a disciple very enthusiastically serves the Sankirtan (preaching) mission, the spiritual master takes responsibility to bring him back to Godhead.

## Tamala Krishna Goswami and the Krishna-Balaram Mandir

- **The difficult service:** Tamala Krishna Goswami was placed in charge of building the Krishna-Balaram Mandir in Vrindavan. This was no easy service:
  - In India there is much corruption; people try to make money any way they can, so the building materials were of poor quality — the iron/cement was "fifth-rate, sixth-rate."
  - The workers were lazy and always trying to steal.
  - The public officials always wanted to take bribes.
  - The weather was hot and there were many mosquitoes — a very difficult service.
- **His qualification:** But of course Goswami Maharaj was a first-class servant of Srila Prabhupada. Prabhupada often took him as his personal secretary and sent him to complicated, difficult places to do pioneer preaching. So he did this managerial service.
- **His complaint:** One day, frustrated, he came before Prabhupada and said:
  - "I live in Vrindavan, this is Krishna's land. Here it is easy to always remember Krishna and never forget Him, because everyone here — the sadhus, men and women — are saintly. There are 5,000 temples, 5,000 deities, the **Yamuna** river, and **Govardhana Hill**. Every step is a dance, every word is a song, and every day is a festival. If you sat in Vrindavan and *wanted* to fall into Maya, you'd have to work very hard, because even the land is Krishna conscious."
  - "Yet, though I live in Krishna's land of Vrindavan, I find it very hard to remember Krishna, because all day I am thinking about the quality of the cement, how lazy the workers are, how to improve the quality — and so on."
- **Prabhupada's answer:** He gave a very interesting reply: "You just continue your service to your spiritual master, and at the time you leave this world, **Lord Chaitanya** Himself will appear in your mind and take you back to Godhead."
- **The lesson:** Because Prabhupada was pleased by his service — even though the service was very difficult, and *because* he did so much of it — Prabhupada granted him Krishna consciousness.

## We Are Servants by Nature (Siksastakam)

- What do we get when we surrender to a Guru — and to Krishna?
- **Siksastakam, verse 4** — *"na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ kavitāṁ vā jagad-īśa kāmaye / mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi"*: Mahaprabhu said, "I have no desire to accumulate wealth, nor do I want the undue, improper association with the opposite sex, nor any number of followers, name, fame, or prestige. All I want in my life is a little devotional service to Your lotus feet."
- **We are servants by nature.** When we serve in the right capacity, we are happy.
  - Everyone is a servant. Either we serve the material energy or we serve Krishna. Either we serve the dictates of our senses and mind, or we serve the instructions of the spiritual master and Krishna. But we are *always* servants.
  - When we are servants of the material energy — servants of our own selfish desires — we are never happy.
  - Mysteriously, when we serve our Guru and Krishna's instructions, because that is our constitutional position, we become happy.
- And for that little service — which really is not so significant — the spiritual master takes responsibility for our deliverance. This is his causeless mercy.
- So even a new devotee is happy. Even a **kanistha-adhikari** (neophyte) is happy in Krishna consciousness, as is the **madhyama-adhikari** and **uttama-adhikari** — because we are properly situated, and we always have our eternal well-wisher.

## Prabhupada's Beach Example: The Two Groups of Dogs

- Once Srila Prabhupada was walking with his disciples on the beach (Juhu). Prabhupada had many interesting ways of helping us understand philosophy — examples, analogies, stories — because people in this age do not have strong philosophical minds; our minds have been ruined by sense enjoyment, so we cannot listen to philosophy for long.
- On the beach he gave an interesting example of the result of accepting a bona fide spiritual master, using the many dogs in India. (Aside: "I haven't seen a dog in Hong Kong — maybe they keep them indoors, I don't know the law. In China surely there are dogs. But in India there are many dogs.")
- **Digression on cleanliness:** Indians generally do not own a dog, let alone keep it in the house. Not that in our culture we dislike animals, but every living being has its proper place. Pigs, dogs, cows — we don't keep them in the house because they are not clean, and **cleanliness is next to godliness.**
  - Even Prabhupada said "cleanliness is godliness." Once, giving second initiation, he said cleanliness is godliness — "if you are not clean you cannot receive second initiation."
  - Unless we are internally clean, we cannot think of Krishna, because Krishna is **suddha-sattva** — completely transcendental and pure.
- In India there are many dogs, but they live on the street. Over the past 40–50 years, many Indians began learning from Westerners, buying pet dogs (especially Western breeds) and keeping them at home.
- **Prabhupada's challenge to the West:** When Prabhupada first came to America, he was surprised to see so many people with so many dogs. He said: "My challenge to Western civilization is that you make friends with dogs but you are killing your own mother" — killing the **cow**, our mother (one of our seven mothers).
- **The scene on the beach:** Many dogs were running around, very dirty, with skin diseases, fighting each other over food, very fierce and angry.
  - Then Prabhupada noticed some other dogs — "some white fluffy dogs, very clean and fat, with big smiles on their faces, at the end of a leash." One "gentleman" was walking his two white pedigree dogs.
- **Prabhupada's question:** Having the spiritual eye, he turned to his disciples: "What is the difference between these two groups of dogs?"
  - The devotees answered: "Well, some are white, some are brown... some are dirty and scraggly, others are clean and fluffy and happy."
  - But asked what the *real* difference was, the devotees couldn't come up with the right answer.

## Why We Need a Spiritual Master / The First Canto

- "That's why we need a spiritual master — so we always know the right answer, the complete answer."
- We always have lots of questions, so in spiritual life we must ask our questions to the spiritual master. And there is an answer for every question in Krishna consciousness.
- In fact, all the answers to all our questions can be found just in the **First Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam.**
  - Prabhupada said he translated the First Canto and brought it to America because the complete science of Krishna consciousness is found there. "If that's all you had, shipwrecked on a lonely island, you could still become fully Krishna conscious just by reading the First Canto of the Bhagavatam."
  - "The only problem," Prabhupada said, "is that my disciples don't read my books."
- So we don't want to be mere sentimentalists — we want a firm philosophical foundation. We should read steadily and constantly.
  - Prabhupada said we should be like a lawyer who knows the law books — clear about our own shastra. This is one of the qualifications to receive **brahmana** initiation, so we should work very hard.
- **Prabhupada's kindness:** The devotees couldn't answer, so they came before the spiritual master to ask, and Prabhupada both posed the question *and* supplied the answer.
  - We disciples are so fallen that we cannot even frame a few proper questions, let alone answer them — yet Prabhupada gave both the question and the answer.
  - (Aside on Umapati Maharaj: When he lectures and asks for questions, no one asks. He says students *should* ask questions; if we have no questions... as he concludes his class, "either we understand everything or we understand nothing.")
- A person who wants to benefit from the spiritual master's knowledge should consider himself a fool before the spiritual master — and indeed we *are* fools, even after many years in Krishna consciousness. "If you don't know what question to ask, then ask this question."

## The Answer: One Group Has a Master

- The question was: what is the difference between the happy, clean dogs and the dirty, suffering dogs?
- **Prabhupada's answer:** One group of dogs **has a master**; the other group has **no master.**
  - The masterless group is independent, on its own.
  - The dogs with a master render some service (staying near him, etc.), and in return the master takes care of them.
- Krishna consciousness is the same:
  - Some people live aimlessly, with no spiritual master to guide them, subject to their own fate.
  - But if others sincerely just *serve*, the spiritual master takes care of them.
- We don't even need to serve with the desire to go back to Godhead — we can serve simply to please our spiritual master, because we are unable to repay him.

## Raja-vidya: The King of Knowledge

- **Bhagavad Gita 9.2** — *"rāja-vidyā rāja-guhyam"*: What the spiritual master gives us is the **king of knowledge**, the king of all education.
- He gives us the means to extricate ourselves from the material energy and become servants of the Lord.
- Nothing in this world can compare with that gift of divine knowledge; this highest knowledge cannot be obtained anywhere else.

## Chanakya Pandit on the Value of Knowledge

- "There's a good verse — when you get older, you forget some verses." **Chanakya Pandit** gave a beautiful verse: knowledge from the Guru is more valuable than money, more valuable than a beautiful house in the countryside, more valuable even than liberation.
  - "The ornament of the night is the moon; the ornament of a woman is a good husband; the ornament of the earth is the king; and **knowledge is the ornament of everything.**"
- Even mundane knowledge is important, because before we accept Krishna consciousness it teaches us how to survive in this material world. That is the value of Chanakya Pandit's writings.
  - Chanakya Pandit was not a devotee — he was a very learned brahmana. His knowledge teaches us how to live well in the material world so that we can properly chant the **Hare Krishna Mahamantra.**
- So "I encourage my disciples to accept education — high school, college, even higher — this helps us get by better in the material world."
- **But do not make this mistake:** worldly education ends at death. It is valuable but temporary, because at death we take another body and go elsewhere, and that knowledge becomes worthless.
- Krishna knowledge, however, is valuable both in this life and the next, because it takes us back home, back to Godhead.
- The most valuable commodity is knowledge. Our relationship with the spiritual master is to receive this knowledge and serve it: we serve the spiritual master, he gives us knowledge, we apply it to our lives, and thereby he takes us back to Godhead.

## The Essence of the Guru–Disciple Relationship (BG 4.34)

- This is a very powerful statement — the essence of the guru-disciple relationship. Because we are now Krishna's representatives, whatever we say should be supported by scripture.
- **Bhagavad Gita 4.34** — *"tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā"*: approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively, and render service unto him; the disciple should humbly and submissively serve the spiritual master and receive knowledge from him.
- Everyone should keep this verse on the tip of the tongue and carry it everywhere.
  - Everyone in society carries something: businessmen carry briefcases, athletes carry a football, musicians carry instruments. We carry our **japa-mala** (beads), our dhoti, our sari, and — most importantly — these verses, ready on the tip of the tongue at any moment.
- Prabhupada would say: "It is not I who say it — it is Krishna who says it; He is the ultimate authority." This is one of the most important verses of the Bhagavad Gita: we approach a bona fide spiritual master and receive the ultimate truth.
- **The disciple's most important duty:** before, during, and after initiation — to inquire, learn, study, and then apply the knowledge to our lives, while rendering service to the spiritual master.
- As Vaishnavas we know this benefits both ourselves and the whole world — especially because this is a **missionary movement.** Under the order of Guru and Gauranga we should learn this knowledge and pass it on to others.
- We should try to repay the debt we owe our Guru. From the time of initiation, we are **eternally indebted** to our Guru.

## Becoming Guru's Happy, Clean Dog — The Joy of Service

- Wearing this **tilak**, we become our Guru's happy, white, clean dog — "our Guru's dog."
  - So we tell devotees with a little knowledge, "We've become a dog," and they say, "You've become a dog" — a very happy, clean, good dog. We serve him; we have become servants.
- Whatever service the spiritual master gives us — cleaning the temple floor, the deity room, the office, or the bathroom — we say, "Wow, Guru, thank you for giving me such nice service."
  - For 10,000 years we have been avoiding this service; now the Guru has given it, so we should be very happy to clean the floor.

## Prabhupada Cleaning the Floor / Service in the Spiritual World

- **Prabhupada cleaned the floor himself.** One time **Yamuna Dasi** was cleaning the floor but didn't know how to do it properly. Prabhupada got down on his hands and knees, took the same bucket and the same rag, and started cleaning the floor.
- **In the spiritual world:** we hear how the **Lakshmis** (goddesses of fortune) — which can also mean the **gopis**, since they too are goddesses of fortune, expansions of the original goddess of fortune, **Srimati Radharani** — clean the pillars in the palaces of **Vaikuntha**, even though there is no dust there (it is the spiritual world). But they love to serve so much that they clean the pillars anyway, and they are so happy.
- What to speak, then, of when our spiritual master — though we are not advanced, we are very lowly, and we are here only by his mercy — chooses to engage us in the highest service: going out to preach the divine command. How grateful we should be.
  - To go out and share this most sacred, highest knowledge with the conditioned souls is the highest and greatest service.
- **Even Mahaprabhu did this service.** The Lord Himself went out daily — the incarnation who chanted the holy name, surrounded by His devotees chanting the holy name.
  - After some time they took it out of **Srivasa-angan**, all over **Navadvipa**, all over India, and eventually empowered **Srila Prabhupada** to take it all over the world.
  - "If it's good enough for God in His purest form, it should be good enough for us."

## Servants Forevermore — Becoming a Vaishnava

- We are servants forevermore. It is not that by serving we ever become the masters — we always will be, and now are, the servants. That is the highest we can be.
- It is a great goal to become a **Vaishnava.**
- **Prabhupada and the Vaishnava:** One time a devotee said, "Prabhupada, you are the greatest servant of the Lord, the greatest Vaishnava." Prabhupada replied: "A Vaishnava is a very special thing. I am just trying to become a Vaishnava."
- We are all servants, but we want to offer *quality* service. We want to become pure and advanced — not for our own sake, but so that we can be a valuable instrument in the hands of our spiritual master.

## Everything Needed Will Be Provided (Yajña; BG 3.10, 9.22)

- As for whatever we need, we should rest assured it will be given, both materially and spiritually — because by performing this **yajña**, the material facilities required to perform it are also given by God.
- **Bhagavad Gita 3.10** — Krishna says: "In the beginning, at creation, I sent forth generations of men and demigods, and blessed them that by the performance of *yajña* (sacrifice), whatever they require would be provided."
  - So both material and spiritual prosperity come by the grace of God. For His servant, the master provides everything required. What we need — materially and spiritually — will come if we are fully surrendered.
- We should just be **eka** — one-pointed: single-minded, wholehearted service to **Guru and Gauranga.** Whatever happens will be auspicious and will be by the grace of God, and ultimately He will take us back to Godhead.

## The Social Security Story: No Anxiety for the Future

- "I was laughing the other day." In America there is a system called **Social Security**: as you work throughout your life, the government takes some money and puts it aside for your old age. At first people are unhappy (paying in), but later they are happy (receiving it).
- In California, "I received a letter from the government — because I'm getting older, 60, approaching retirement age (I think 65) — letting me know how much they'd put aside for me at retirement." (Aside: "Actually I retired at 29 when I took sannyasa.")
- Some old schoolfriends of the same age had also received such letters months earlier, showing thousands and tens of thousands of dollars — they had worked their whole lives for big companies. They asked, "So, Swami, how much is the government going to give *you*?"
  - Expecting thousands, they opened the envelope: it was only **$16.43** — because "I joined Krishna consciousness at 18, having only worked a little while in a shoe store, nothing much."
- **The point:** Materialists seeing such a figure would be horrified — "My God, what will I do?" But "I am not anxious at all."
  - "My whole life I have steadfastly, with firm faith, served the message/messenger of the spiritual master, and my whole life I have been maintained — provided far more than I could have hoped for."
  - "I am not anxious about my old age. I am fully confident He will take care of me in the years to come, just the same."
  - Even if in the material world everything is eventually taken away, "I still have the Mahamantra" and the opportunity to render service to Prabhupada and Krishna. The most valuable thing cannot be taken away.
- **The book-distribution reflection:** Sometimes "I think that even if a great calamity strikes and I have to go back to France, I would take my old book bag — which is hanging in someone's room at **New Mayapur** — put some books in it, and, just as I used to do, wander through the countryside distributing books door to door, sleeping in the Sankirtan van at night. No problem. I'd be happy doing that."
  - So the nice gifts my disciples give me — clothes and so on — I use well, because they are used to serve Srila Prabhupada. But if instead I had to go door to door distributing books, I would also be happy, because I would be distributing this transcendental knowledge.

## Krishna Repays With More Service

- What happens for the good servant? **Bhagavad Gita 9.22** — *"yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham"*: Krishna says, "For My devotee, I protect whatever they have and I provide whatever they need" — I supply what My servant lacks and preserve what he has.
- **Good-quality service** means: in the process of serving, we ask for no reward. If we really want anything, we want *more service*.
- **The gopis:** So at midnight, when Krishna plays His flute, the so-called married **gopis** abandon everything and go to Him. Krishna says, "I owe you so much; how can I ever repay you for such nice service?" — because the master likes to reciprocate his devotee's service.
  - Krishna thought: "What shall I give these gopis? I know — I will give the most valuable thing: I will give you **more service.**" And He did.

## The Contract of Initiation / Result: An ISKCON in the Spiritual World

- When we begin this ceremony, we establish this service relationship, so this **yajña** is very important.
- It is like a **contract** — a two-way contract in which both sides take responsibility:
  - The disciple makes his vows.
  - The spiritual master reciprocally vows in his heart: "I will take responsibility for this candidate; I will deliver him to the lotus feet of **Srila Prabhupada.**"
- Prabhupada is the **acharya**, our founder-acharya; his position means that all spiritual masters sit at his feet, and we are all here by his mercy.
- Our success lies in absorbing his example and his teachings into our hearts.
- **The result** (as Prabhupada said): one day, in the spiritual world, we will also have our own **ISKCON.** *(Applause.)*
