## Festivals Are Essential to the Gaudiya Vaishnava Tradition

### The Vaishnava Calendar and the Variety of Festivals

- Festivals are an essential and intricate part of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition.
- The Vaishnava calendar is filled with festivals throughout the year — across the months, weeks, and days. Types include:
  - Appearance days of the Lord.
  - Appearance days of the Lord's expansions.
  - Appearance days of His pure devotees.
  - Festivals celebrating the various pastimes of the Lord.
- Even **Ekadashi** can be considered a festival for a devotee, because it means more time to chant Hare Krishna and hear Hari-katha — which is really the essence of a festival program.

### Not Observing Festivals Is an Offense (Nectar of Devotion)

- So important are such events that **The Nectar of Devotion** states clearly that it is an offense (*aparadha*) to ignore or fail to observe any particular festival on the Vaishnava calendar.
- We naturally give greater importance to certain festivals over others, but in truth they are all important.
- Festivals help nurture the seed of *bhakti* within the heart.
- Devotees have no difficulty observing festivals — they look forward to them with great anticipation.
  - The speaker admits he anticipated this festival eagerly, though it is a little embarrassing for him since on at least one day he becomes the center of attraction. Still, it is wonderful to come together with all the Vaishnavas, chant Hare Krishna, hear the glories of the Lord, and take Bhagavat-prasadam.
  - Many have come from a great distance, at great inconvenience and expense — and that is all right.

### Devotees Anticipate Festivals — Prabhupada and the Mayapur Festival

- **Story (heard from a GBC member):** At the annual Mayapur Festival in Sri Dhama Mayapur, Srila Prabhupada told the GBC that it is so nice that all the devotees should come every year.
  - A GBC member objected: "But Prabhupada, if all the devotees come to the Mayapur Festival every year, it will be very expensive and different services will be interrupted."
  - Prabhupada replied: "It does not matter. It is so important that we all come together like this and chant Hare Krishna and hear the glories of Lord Chaitanya." — that was his instruction.

### Demigods Travel the Universe to Attend a Yajna

- The *shastra* describes how *devatas* (great devotees / demigods) will travel across the universe to attend a festival, a *yajna*.
- **"Yajna vai Vishnuh"** (Vedic dictum): sacrifice/festival is Vishnu — festival programs are meant to glorify Vishnu.
- Naturally, when a demigod hears of such an event, his heart goes out to it.
- **Analogy:** Just as a materialistic person may travel hundreds or thousands of miles for a football, soccer, or cricket game, we can imagine that a demigod — whose heart is in devotional service and in glorifying the Lord — will traverse the entire universe to attend.
- There is a **Vedic injunction** (the speaker says he has forgotten the exact Sanskrit) that **people love festivals.** But to be truly fulfilling, festivals must be Krishna conscious — God-centered.

## Festivals Come from the Spiritual World

### Goloka Vrindavan: A Festival Every Day (Brahma-samhita)

- We have a wonderful example of God-centered festivals in the supreme abode (*Paravyoma / Param Gati*), **Goloka Vrindavan.**
- **Brahma-samhita** enlightens us about the beauty of the spiritual world: there in Vaikuntha, there in Goloka, **all walking is dancing, all talking is singing, and there is a festival every day.**

### Why We Have Festivals: Practicing to Return Home

- Now we understand why there are so many festivals: because we are **practicing to go back to the spiritual world.**
- *Sadhana-bhakti* / *vaidhi-bhakti* means devotional service in practice — we are doing the same thing the liberated souls do in the spiritual world.
- The only thing missing here is that pure consciousness — but that will come in time.
- Therefore we hold festivals to practice for our return to the spiritual sky.

### Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura on Goloka's Daily Festival

- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** elaborately describes the festive mood of Goloka Vrindavan:
  - In the morning, after Krishna has awakened, bathed, dressed, and been fed, the cowherd boys come.
  - They all gather and go to fetch the cows at Nandagrama — specifically at **Ter Kadamba** — and then go out to the forest for most of the day.
- This scene is **heartbreaking** for the townspeople of Vrindavan — the elderly gopis, the elderly gopas, the little children, and those residents whose pastimes with Krishna take place in the village itself — because it means Krishna is gone for the whole day.
- They love Krishna more than anyone has ever been loved before.

### Separation and Reunion — The Daily Festival of Vrindavan

- To convey the depth of this love, the speaker cites Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's mood of separation — **Shikshashtaka, verse 7** (*yugayitam nimeshena…*): "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain. I am feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence."
- This is the mood of the residents of Vrindavan **every single day.**
- Though the pastime happens again and again, by the nature of the spiritual world — by the potency of **Yogamaya** — it always seems like the first time. It is ever-fresh.
- **Principle of separation:** You feel separation from someone in proportion to your attachment to them — as much attachment as you have, that same intensity of separation you will feel.
- When Krishna and the cowherd boys go to the forest, the whole village follows them to the frontier, to the boundary. Then Krishna, the boys, and the cows disappear over the horizon.
  - It is stated that the residents of Vrindavan stand there in deep separation **like wooden dolls**, tears flowing from their eyes.
- **The reunion / the daily festival:** Conversely, the greatest celebration comes when Krishna returns home in the evening with all the boys.
  - The boys pour out their stories to their parents — how Krishna killed this demon and that demon, how they played and frolicked in the forest, what they talked about, and how Krishna is their very best friend. They go on and on.
  - As they re-enter Vrindavan, **that** is the festival being described — a festival every day.
  - Every day Krishna comes; every day is the mood of meeting Krishna again after a long time. Though it is only one day, it seems like millions of years. That is the meaning of "a festival every day" in the spiritual world.

## The Descent of the Festive Mood to This World

### Krishna Brought It 5,000 Years Ago

- Krishna very kindly brought that mood of festivity to this world **5,000 years ago.** It is so attractive that it melts the heart.
- He brought it to this planet — to **Bharatavarsha**, to **Vrindavan**. By His mercy, **Goloka became Gokula**, so everyone could see it and become inspired.

### Lord Chaitanya's Greater Mercy: Conditioned Souls Can Participate

- Then, **4,500 years later** (a little over 500 years ago), **Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** brought the same mood again — but with one crucial difference: **the conditioned souls could participate.**
- This is the beauty of Lord Chaitanya's pastimes:
  - Krishna's pastimes we get to watch, witness, and hear about — becoming inspired to take up devotional service and one day qualify ourselves to enter.
  - Lord Chaitanya is even **more merciful than Krishna Himself**, because when He brings the festive mood of Vaikuntha to this world, He invites the conditioned souls to participate — through the medium of chanting and dancing the **Hare Krishna maha-mantra**:
    - *Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.*
- The speaker recites **Rupa Goswami's prayer** (Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 19.53): *"Namo maha-vadanyaya krishna-prema-pradaya te / krishnaya krishna-chaitanya-namne gaura-tvishe namah."*
- **Rupa Goswami at Ramakeli:** At their first meeting, the very first time he saw Mahaprabhu, Rupa Goswami understood the deep meaning of Mahaprabhu's appearance — "You are more merciful than any other incarnation, because You freely bestow this love of Godhead even upon fallen conditioned souls."

### Vaikuntha — A Place Without Anxiety, Tasted Through Kirtan

- So we do not have to wait very long to experience the mood of Vaikuntha.
- **Word meaning:** *Kuntha* means a place of anxiety — the material world. *Vaikuntha* means a place where there is **no** anxiety.
- Wherever there are great festivals of chanting the holy names of Krishna, we can experience that mood immediately.
  - Example: When **Badahari Prabhu** was leading kirtan, it did not take long — close your eyes, and in a few seconds this whole hall is turned into Vaikuntha.

### Srila Prabhupada's Unique Contribution: The Sunday Love Feast

- Just as Krishna introduced the festive mood 5,000 years ago and Lord Chaitanya introduced it a little over 500 years ago, their most beloved representative, **Srila Prabhupada**, also introduced the same festive mood — in his own unique way.
- Prabhupada introduced the **Sunday Love Feast.**
  - We hear of festivities in the Vedic scriptures, and of **Ratha Yatra** — but there is no *shloka* in the Chaitanya-charitamrita about the Sunday Love Feast. That was Prabhupada's unique contribution.
- **This is the right of the acharya:** the *acharya* is the one who founds/establishes the movement according to time, place, and circumstance.
  - Seeing the nature of Western culture and Western people, Prabhupada **adapted without compromising** by introducing this festival.
  - On Sunday — the day God set aside for rest — people go to church, mosque, temple, or park; they relax and enjoy. What greater enjoyment is there than to chant Hare Krishna, hear some Hari-katha, perhaps see a theater performance, and take some Bhagavat-prasadam?
- It has been so successful that to this very day, in all ISKCON centers, the Sunday Love Feast remains very popular — Prabhupada's special contribution to the lineage of the Gaudiya Vaishnava sampradaya.

## Why Festivals Are So Important

- Restating the question: People love festivals — but *why* are they stressed? Why did Krishna, Mahaprabhu, and Prabhupada all stress festivals, so much that not to observe them is an offense?

### A Big Dose of Krishna Consciousness

- Festivals are so important because at such events **we get a big dose of Krishna consciousness.**
  - The speaker says the assembly has already received a big dose — after that 25-minute kirtan he already feels *"yayatma suprasidati,"* fully satisfied — **Srimad Bhagavatam 1.2.6** (*"…ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati"*): the process by which the self becomes completely satisfied.
- **Why we need it:** Normal life unfortunately does not leave enough time for Krishna — not enough time to chant Hare Krishna, nor to prepare, offer, and honor prasadam to our heart's content.
  - If a Vaishnava had the opportunity, he would chant all day, hear Hari-katha all day, and serve the deity all day.
  - But we live in the world of mortals; we have to work, and nowadays we must work very hard "to keep up with the Joneses." So there is little time.
  - We only get in the minimum "to keep our head above water" — we are treading water, not swimming across the ocean of material existence toward the shore of Vaikuntha.
- Therefore, practically every year, month, and week there is a festival where we go and receive a big dose of Krishna consciousness.
  - **Analogy (the flu injection):** It is like coming down with the flu — you go to the doctor, he gives you a big injection, you grow strong and overcome the illness. So too the festivals are essential; we cannot ignore them.
- No one serious about advancing can ignore or fail to participate in these *yajnas* / festivities in their variety of forms.

### Festivals as Spring Cleaning: Cleansing the Mirror of the Heart

- Festivals are, in effect, like **spring cleaning.**
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu put it poetically — **Shikshashtaka, verse 1**: *"Ceto-darpana-marjanam"* — by chanting Hare Krishna we cleanse the mirror of the heart.

### Cleansing the Subtle Body — The Dirty Mirror

- The mirror gets dirty and must be cleaned repeatedly.
- **Analogy (the driver and the mirror):** When the speaker went to driver-education school at age 15 (before getting his license at 16), he was taught something he still does whenever he drives: before getting in, he sprays and cleans all the windows, gets in, and cleans the mirror.
  - He notes a devotee host (at whose house he stays about a month each year) can attest to seeing him spray and wipe the windows and clean the mirror day after day.
  - Why? To be a good driver — even a little dirt on the mirror could distort the reflection, cause a wrong turn, and lead to an accident. Because this world is a dirty place, the mirror gets dirty; even once cleaned, it must be cleaned twice, thrice, and so on.
- Just as we clean our bodies every day — you cannot say, "Oh, I took a bath last week."
  - **Anecdote (the unwashed boy):** The speaker once met a boy who was filthy — clothes stinking, hair matted. Asked why he did not bathe, the boy replied, "No, I like to keep life's experiences with me." (The point: we must actually cleanse the body.)
- We must cleanse not only the gross body but also the **subtle body** — mind, intelligence, and false ego — which is synonymous with the heart.
  - Vedic culture means two, perhaps three, showers a day.
  - The subtle body becomes contaminated through association with the modes of nature — *tamas* (ignorance), *rajas* (passion), and even mundane goodness to some degree.
  - Therefore we must cleanse the mirror of the heart every day by chanting Hare Krishna.

### The Spring-Cleaning Analogy Expanded

- Coming to a festival is like spring cleaning.
  - You clean your house every day and every week, but come spring — after a winter with all doors and windows shut against the cold — a certain musty uncleanliness accumulates.
  - Then spring arrives: the sky is clear, the yellow daffodils and flowers come up, the birds sing. You open the windows and doors, take the bucket, and clean the house — throwing away things you did not need, putting away the coats and big blankets. That is spring cleaning.
- Likewise, in the **winter of material existence, things pile up.**
  - We are *chetodak* (chanting) every day, but often not the quality of kirtan we should be doing — sixteen good, attentive rounds, chanting and dancing and twirling and chanting at the top of our lungs at every Mangala-arati in our little house. Honestly, we don't.
  - So we need a spring cleaning once in a while — the purpose of these festivals: to cleanse the heart of all the cobwebs.

### Incremental Advancement vs. the Quantum Leap

- Routine devotional service is essential, and through it we do make advancement.
  - The speaker cites **Brahma-samhita** for the idea that we advance in small increments — "little steps." Looking back over five years, you see you have taken that many steps.
- But when you come to festivals and **give your heart** to them — listening with a desire to advance and to apply the speaker's knowledge in your life when you leave, chanting from the heart, chanting *japa* in the association of devotees (remembering the japa retreats of past years), and in kirtan not merely sitting in your chair but setting the chair aside, standing up, and dancing — then, instead of just an increment, you go away having made a **quantum leap** in devotional service.
  - **On dancing at kirtan:** If you are an old man, do the "Swami step"; if middle-aged, "rock back"; if young — "hey, you let go." Chant and dance.

## How to Participate Fully

### The Speaker's Recent Festivals in Russia and Ukraine

- The speaker had just attended major festivals in **Ukraine, Moscow, and Yekaterinburg** (near Siberia).
- At each of these festivals there were no fewer than **1,000 devotees**, and every night they had kirtan for three to four hours.
- Some kirtans were led in a soft-and-building style (like Badahari Prabhu's) — soft, then big — and the devotees kept wanting more; even at 12 o'clock at night they had to say, "Stop."

### Kirtana-Rasa Is the Safest Situation (Prabhupada's Conclusion)

- Festivals are so important because they are based on the essential truth and process of Krishna consciousness — **chanting.**
- Prabhupada's summary of the festival: *"Elegance is truth spoken concisely"* — **chanting, dancing, some Hari-katha, and some feasting.**
- **Prabhupada's written conclusion** — from the introduction to the songbook *Songs of the Vaishnava Acharyas* (quoted):
  - *"We should always remember the danger of Maya's influence and endeavor to save ourselves from her great power. We must therefore always merge in the transcendental mellow of kirtana-rasa, for kirtana-rasa is the safest situation within this material world."*

### Take Everything With Gusto — Prasadam as Mercy

- **Humble request:** Over these next three days, do not hold back.
- **Story (Prabhupada in America):** Visiting America, Prabhupada once saw a devotee not eating. The spiritual master takes great pleasure in seeing his disciples take Bhagavat-prasadam, knowing the purification that comes from properly respecting it. Prabhupada said, "No, no — take with gusto." He cited a **Hindi proverb**: "Don't be shy when doing business or taking prasadam."
- **What "prasad" means:** *Prasad* means **mercy** — not merely the foodstuff. It includes the *katha*, the *sanga* (association), the chanting, the hearing — everything connected. So do not let the days simply go by; open your hearts and give yourselves fully.

### Give Your Whole Life to Krishna (Vishnujana Maharaj)

- **Story (Prabhupada chastising Vishnujana Maharaj):** One time Prabhupada chastised **Vishnujana Maharaj** for his lack of surrender — because the duty of the spiritual master is to push us.
  - Prabhupada told him: "You have lived so many lives; you have done everything. You have been a demigod on the higher planets, an earthling here, a snake on the lower planets — you have done everything. The only thing you have not done is give your life fully to Krishna. Because if you had, you would not be here. So why not, in this one lifetime, give your whole energy to Krishna consciousness?"
- These festivals give us the opportunity to learn what it means to give everything to Krishna.
  - You cannot do it at the office — you cannot start dancing like a madman, cooking prasadam, and listening to Hari-katha there. But here at the festival is the opportunity.
  - So please, do not be shy — give yourselves to the process.

- Certainly when we leave, we will feel the purification of *ceto-darpana-marjanam* — the heart will be cleansed.
- And the tender creeper of devotional service (*bhakti-lata*) will have grown that much more, and borne the divine fruit of **love of Godhead** (*krishna-prema*).
