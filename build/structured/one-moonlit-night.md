## Introduction: A Great Devotee Who Served the Dham

- Part of a continuing series on the glories of Vrindavan Dham and the great devotees who served that Dham.
- Invocation: *nama om vishnu-padaya krishna-preshthaya bhu-tale / srimate bhaktivedanta-svamin iti namine* — the pranati to Srila Prabhupada — *namas te sarasvate deve gaura-vani-pracharine / nirvishesha-shunyavadi-pashchatya-desha-tarine*.
- Today's subject: a great devotee who, along with **Srila Narottama dasa Thakura** and **Srinivasa Acharya**, carried on the Sankirtan movement after the disappearance of Lord Chaitanya — the illustrious **Shyamananda Pandit**, "one of my favorite devotees."

## Glorification from the Shyamananda-shataka

- From the **Shyamananda-shataka** (the hundred verses glorifying him) by his disciple **Rasikananda Murari-dasa**, verses 3–4:
  - "I offer my respectful obeisances to the great devotee who, because he delights the three worlds with the bluish nectar of Lord Krishna (**Shyam**), the poets call him **Sri Shyamananda**. He is the king of the devotees of Lord Krishna. He reveals the secret truths of the beautiful gopis, dear to Braja's handsome prince. He is the son of **Sri Durika-devi** and the merciful spiritual master of all the worlds."
  - "O people, if your hearts yearn to taste the rare sweet nectar of love for Lord Krishna; if you wish, without the trouble of great searching, to find the **chintamani** jewel of ecstatic love for Krishna; and if you are attracted to the great treasure of spontaneous love for Lord Krishna — then, with all your heart, please always worship the cup-of-victory tree that bears the name **Sri Shyamananda**."
- Our acharyas often say Shyamananda Pandit was one of the greatest stalwart Vaishnavas ever to grace our **Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya Vaishnava Sampradaya**.
- **Prema-vilasa** by Nityananda Dasa: in Goloka Vrindavan, Shyamananda Pandit is **Kanaka Manjari**.

## Birth and the Name "Dukhi"

- He appeared in **1535**, in the month of **Chaitra**, in the village **Dharenda Bahadurpur**, West Bengal.
- **Bhakti-ratnakara**: his father was **Krishna Mandal**, his mother **Durika**; his parents were "exceedingly pious people of impeccable character."
- All of their previous children had died at birth or shortly thereafter, so when this child was born they gave him the unusual name **Dukhi** ("sad one" / one who is sad) — hoping that **Yamaraja**, the god of death, assuming the boy was so unhappy, would not bother to take him.

## Childhood and the Desire for Diksha

- As Dukhi grew up and attended school, his teachers were astonished by his ability to learn difficult subjects — Sanskrit grammar, poetry, and rhetoric. From childhood he was very attracted to hearing the pastimes of **Lord Chaitanya and Lord Nityananda**.
- He quickly realized the importance of **diksha** — taking shelter of a spiritual master. Hearing about the extraordinary devotion of a saintly person named **Sri Hridaya Chaitanya**, who lived in **Ambika Kalna**, he asked his parents' permission to journey there and take shelter of Hridaya Chaitanya as his spiritual master.
- His parents were impressed by such realization in one so young, but nervous because the trip to Ambika Kalna went through dangerous jungle. Finally they agreed, and he left.

## Meeting Hridaya Chaitanya

- On arriving in Ambika Kalna, Dukhi found Hridaya Chaitanya's ashram. When he walked in, Hridaya Chaitanya was absorbed in meditation on **Krishna-Balaram** — a deeply realized devotee in **sakhya-rasa** (the mood of friendship with Krishna, like the cowherd boys in Braja).
- As soon as Hridaya Chaitanya saw young Dukhi, his meditation broke and he stared in wonder at the boy. "As a virtuous man is able to easily recognize other virtuous men, and as expert jewelers are able to identify diamonds among many pieces of glass," Hridaya Chaitanya understood that the newcomer was a highly qualified devotee — **"qualified to worship Lord Krishna."**
- In the months that followed, Dukhi won his Gurudeva's heart by his eagerness to hear Krishna-katha (especially **Gauranga-katha** and **Nityananda-katha**) and by his **Guru-nishtha**.
- Story: The speaker once asked his godbrother **Tamal Krishna Goswami** what the most important quality a disciple can have in service to his guru is. He immediately replied: **"Guru-nishtha."**
- On **Phalguni Purnima in 1553**, Hridaya Chaitanya initiated him and gave him the name **Dukhi Krishnadasa**.

## Eight Years of Pilgrimage

- After serving his spiritual master in various ways, Dukhi Krishnadasa one day requested permission to travel and visit various holy places. Pleased with his service and progress, Hridaya Chaitanya immediately gave permission, and Dukhi Krishnadasa spent the next **eight years on pilgrimage**, visiting many holy places around India.
- We see this in the lives of all the great devotees — taking time to visit holy places for inspiration.
- **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura** sings of this desire in his famous song **"Shri Bhakata-charana-renu"** (*madhava-tithi bhakti-janani, jetane palana kori... gaura amara, je-saba sthane, koralo bhramana range / se-saba sthana, heribo ami, pranayi-bhakata-sange*): "The holy days, like **Ekadashi** and **Janmashtami**, are the mothers of devotion for those devotees who respect them. Let the holy places of Krishna's pastimes be my places of worship and bless me. May I always visit all the holy places associated with the lila of Lord Chaitanya and his devotees."

## Return, and the Service of Watering the Garden

- When Dukhi Krishnadasa arrived back in Ambika Kalna eight years later, his Guru Maharaj was waiting for him.
- Reflection: The spiritual master is always eager to check on the progress of his disciples, because he considers that his **main service**. Even while preaching around the world, he considers his disciples his most important service, because they have dedicated their lives to him and trust him to deliver them back to Godhead. It is a transcendental investment — the disciples go on to continue spreading the Krishna consciousness movement.
- Amazed at his advancement, Hridaya Chaitanya entrusted Dukhi with the service of **daily watering a flower garden** that produced flowers exclusively for the deity worship of the ashram's deities. (In India, especially previously, kings would help establish a temple and give many acres of land for the deity's service — growing fruits, vegetables, etc.) In this ashram they were worshipping **Lord Chaitanya and Lord Nityananda** (some time after Mahaprabhu had left), so watering flowers for **Gaura-Nitai** was a very special service.
- Dukhi performed this seva religiously: every day he filled pitchers of water from the **Ganges**, placed them on his head, carried them some distance to the garden, watered the flowers, and repeated the process many times a day.
- The mere thought that he was watering the garden of Gaura-Nitai would make his eyes fill with **tears of love**. In his ecstasy he was even unaware of how many pitchers of water he brought each day.

## The Wounds and Worms; the Guru's Mystic Power

- Gradually, due to the friction of the pots on top of his head, wounds appeared, became infected, and unfortunately became infested with worms. Being so absorbed in his service, he noticed neither the wounds nor the worms — transcendental to the body.
- One day, as he came to the garden with a full pitcher on his head, his Guru Maharaj was present. As Hridaya Chaitanya watched him place the pitcher onto the ground, a worm fell from Dukhi's head. Observing more closely, Hridaya Chaitanya saw the head full of infected wounds.
- Appreciating his disciple's determined service, Hridaya Chaitanya's heart melted, and he embraced Dukhi Krishnadasa. Then he "placed his hand endowed with mystic power on his disciple's head and made the wounds and the worms immediately disappear."
- Reflection: A pure devotee has all mystic powers but generally does not display them — not wanting the cheap adoration of the public; they want the public to follow them for the **katha**, the **divya-jnana** (transcendental knowledge), which is infinitely more important than mystic powers. But sometimes, when necessary, those powers are shown.

## Sent to Vrindavan to Worship Radha and Krishna

- A few days later, Hridaya Chaitanya called for Dukhi Krishnadasa and said: "My dear child, now I can see you are **qualified to go to Vrindavan and worship Radha and Krishna**. There, all your desires will be fulfilled by the great devotee **Srila Jiva Goswami**; take shelter of him, and he will continue to instruct you in Krishna consciousness."
- Note: At this point Dukhi Krishnadasa had reached a very elevated state of Krishna consciousness. Only then did his spiritual master feel he was ready to worship Radha and Krishna and go to Vrindavan — a very elevated place.
- **Prabhupada's letter to Jamuna Dasi, January 16, 1970**: "The worship of Radha and Krishna as they appear in Vrindavan is a very advanced stage. So we worship them in our temples as **Lakshmi-Narayan**."
- **Prabhupada's letter, January 30, 1973**: "For a conditioned soul there is no possibility of worshipping Radha and Krishna. But we worship through **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu**. Therefore we keep Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu along with Radha and Krishna."
- Conclusion: We are very fortunate to be worshipping Radha and Krishna under Srila Prabhupada's guidance — just as it was only at a certain stage of Dukhi's devotional service that his Guru Maharaj entrusted him to worship Radha-Krishna and go to Braja.

## The Rarity of Vrindavan and Radha-kund

- Visiting Vrindavan is also a very rare privilege, sometimes taken for granted.
- **Rupa Goswami, Upadeshamrita**: "Of the many objects of favored delight, and of all the lovable damsels of Brajabhumi, Srimati Radharani is certainly the most treasured object of Krishna's love. And in every respect her divine **kund** (Radha-kund) is described by great sages as similarly dear to him. Undoubtedly, Radha-kund is very rarely attained, even by the great devotees; therefore it is even more difficult for ordinary devotees to attain. If one simply bathes once within those holy waters, one's pure love of Krishna is fully aroused."
- These are rare things — to worship Radha and Krishna, and to go to Vrindavan — an unlimited good fortune, all because of the mercy of Srila Prabhupada.

## Never Take Mercy for Granted

- Story: The speaker was recently speaking with his son about **Gaura-shakti**, and his son said, "Pita, these days I never take anything for granted, materially and spiritually." A good attitude — "familiarity breeds contempt," so we must be careful.
- Story: Prabhupada's father told young Prabhupada (then known as **Abhay**): "The **Purusha**, the great Lord, has unlimited arms and unlimited hands. If he wants to give you mercy with all of those hands, how much can your two little hands hold?" We have received so much mercy — the honor of worshipping Radha and Krishna, worshipping them as **Lakshmi-Narayan** (in the mood of awe and reverence, understanding him as the supreme master and ourselves as humble servants, before moving on to a deeper understanding of the Lord and his Vrindavan pastimes).
- Prayer to Prabhupada: "O beloved spiritual master, you are always in the presence of the cowherd girl **Radha**, the daughter of King **Vrishabhanu**. Please award me service at your lotus-like feet, which are the proprietors of devotional service. Please place me in the ocean of joy by bestowing upon me happiness in the mellows of service at the feet of Sri Radha in the groves of Vrajadham."

## Under Srila Jiva Goswami

- Upon reaching Vrindavan, Dukhi Krishnadasa followed his spiritual master's instructions, sought out **Jiva Goswami**, and surrendered at his lotus feet. Under his guidance he went deeper in understanding, and through his **seva** came his realizations.
- **Bhagavad-gita 4.34**: *tad viddhi pranipatena pariprashnena sevaya* — first one inquires from the spiritual master about Krishna consciousness, and then, out of gratitude, one puts that knowledge into service; by serving, the realizations come. Knowledge (**jnana**) becomes **vijnana** (realized knowledge) when we use it in service to guru and Gauranga. It is not enough to study — we must put knowledge into action under the guidance of the spiritual master, and then the blessings come.
- Under Jiva Goswami, Dukhi Krishnadasa learned very deep subject matters and continued to progress, at one point spending most of his time in the service of Radha and Krishna inside an **underground cave**, meditating on Radha-Krishna with a fixed mind while constantly chanting Hare Krishna on his beads.

## The Service of Sweeping Seva-kunj

- Recognizing his progress (due to his sincerity and determination), Jiva Goswami entrusted him with another "menial" service — though nothing is truly menial when done for the spiritual master (previously he had joyfully considered watering flowers extremely special). Jiva Goswami gave him the service of **sweeping the sacred Seva-kunj** every day.
- **Seva-kunj** is where many of the deepest pastimes of Radha-Krishna took place 5,000 years ago — and on the unmanifest platform are taking place even today. That is why there is a huge wall around Seva-kunj (a garden/forested area not far from the **Radha-Ramana** and **Radha-Gopinath** temples), and no one is allowed there in the evening or night, because Krishna is having his pastimes there with Radha and the gopis. A very deep, esoteric place — and Jiva Goswami entrusted Dukhi with sweeping it in the morning.

## Dham-seva: Serving the Holy Dham

- The practice of serving the Dham itself in such a menial way is called **Dham-seva**, an important part of living in or visiting the Dham. It can take the form of weeding gardens, cleaning ponds or lakes, sweeping the ground, securing trees to their supports, planting and caring for flowers, or cleaning paved stones and pavilions.
- Usually we come for Parikrama (chanting here, visiting there), but we might also consider service to the Dham. The Dham is (technically) an expansion of **Lord Balaram** — the playground of God, so sacred that all the opulence of **Vaikuntha** does not compare to one grain of dust in Sri Vrindavan Dham.
- We should have the consciousness: "I am here as a servant." We want to learn to serve our Guru Maharaj, the previous acharyas, the Vaishnavas, Radha-Krishna, Mahaprabhu and Lord Nityananda (**Pancha-tattva**) — and also do service to the Dham (e.g., cleaning up garbage). But everything in Krishna consciousness must be done in the proper consciousness.
- As Dukhi Krishnadasa swept Seva-kunj each morning he thought, **"This is the same dust that once touched the feet of Krishna and his gopis,"** and thinking like this he muddied the earth with a steady stream of tears. We can therefore consider him a **Dhamvasi** (resident of the Dham) — but we too must elevate our consciousness and qualify. Prabhupada said, **"Work now, samadhi later."**

## The Pastime "One Moonlit Night": The Lost Anklet

- Dukhi Krishnadasa's service to the dust of the Dham so endeared him to the queen of Vrindavan, Srimati Radharani, that she decided to bestow her causeless mercy upon him. This amazing pastime, the acharya says, "shall ever be glorified in the pages of Gaudiya history" — well-known, but with some details mined from the treasure-chest of the shastras.
- **One night** Lord Krishna and the gopis were absorbed in singing and dancing under the **full moon** in beautiful **Seva-kunj**. At one point the pace of the dancing increased to a very intense state, and a golden ankle bracelet named **Manju-ghosha**, studded with beautiful sapphires, slipped off the **left foot** of Srimati Radharani and fell into the dancing arena, unnoticed by anyone. They danced all night (sometimes said to be a night of Brahma).

## Dukhi Krishnadasa Finds the Anklet

- Early the next morning, Radha, Krishna and all the gopis headed for their respective residences. Just after they left, Dukhi Krishnadasa arrived for his sweeping of the kunj.
- On reaching the kunj he noticed jasmine creepers and other flowers scattered everywhere (not normally so many), and the parrots, cuckoos, and black bees singing their morning songs with more enthusiasm than usual.
- Out of gratitude for having this seva, he started **rolling in the dust of Seva-kunj**, crying tears of joy before he even began.

### Digression: The Dust of Vrindavan as a Stimulus for Devotion

- We do this on Parikrama — roll in the dust — and should not hesitate to roll in the dust of Vrindavan.
- **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's poem "Radha-kund tata-kunja-kutir"** (in **Saranagati**) mentions the dust of Vrindavan as an impetus in spiritual life, amongst many other stimuli:
  - "The cottage and the grove on the banks of **Radha-kund**, **Govardhan Hill**, the banks of the **Yamuna**, **Kusuma-sarovara**, **Manasa-ganga**, the daughter of Kalinda (the Yamuna) with her many waves, **Vamshivat**, **Gokul**, **Dvadashaditya-tila**, the trees and creepers and reeds of Vrindavan, the different varieties of colorful birds, the deer, the cooling breeze from the Malaya mountains, the peacocks, the bumblebees, the pastimes of the flute, the flute itself, the buffalo-horn bugle, the footprints of cows in the dust of Braja, the rows of blackish rain clouds, springtime, the moon, the conch shell and the kartals — all these I know to be very conducive for the pastimes of Radha and Krishna. I recognize in them a transcendental stimulus for making the Lord's charming pastimes more intense."
  - He concludes: "I refuse to go anywhere if a stimulus to devotional service is not there, for to abandon them is to abandon life itself."
- Point: the dust is included among these stimuli. When we come to Braja and walk around, we should notice these things — the banks of the Yamuna, the waves, birds, deer, peacocks, bumblebees, rain clouds, springtime, the moon, conch shell, kartals — and remember they are a stimulus to devotion.

### Even Outside Vrindavan: Preaching as a Stimulus

- One may object: "But I'm leaving Vrindavan — going back to Moscow, Almaty, Christchurch, Sao Paulo. Where is the stimulus there?" But when we do book distribution, hold festivals, have a preaching center or restaurant, or even just say "Hare Krishna" to someone, that mood of preaching is itself a huge stimulus.
- Story: On the streets of Delhi, amid heavy car fumes, someone complained about "those horrible car fumes." The speaker admitted he does not really mind them, because they **remind him of street sankirtan** — years of doing harinama on the streets of the world's cities and distributing books right in the center of Kali Yuga. Car fumes, billboards, and the like just remind him of sankirtan.
- So we need not be afraid to leave Vrindavan. (Spoken during the pandemic:) Hopefully, when the pandemic is over, we can get back to the real business of preaching — though even now preaching is being done through the internet, and in many ways it has increased. Godbrother **Vaisheshika Prabhu** (a great general of the sankirtan movement) reported that his temple, distributing books online, is doing more books than ever before — their recent Christmas marathon goal was **two million Bhagavad-gitas**. Anything used in Krishna's service takes on a spiritual quality.

### The Varieties of Dust in Vrindavan

- Usually we think of dust, earth, or mud as the lowest thing, but in Krishna consciousness it is one of the highest, because it has touched the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna.
- **Vrindavana-mahimamrita** by **Srila Prabodhananda Sarasvati** describes how the dust of Vrindavan is made of different substances (not merely the **chintamani** earth): "In some places the ground of Braja is made of camphor dust; in some places the dust of Vrindavan is made of saffron; in some places it is made of ground-up musk perfume; and in some places the dust is made of the aromatic substances used in religious ceremonies." Through these acharyas we see things as they are.

## The Anklet Turns to Gold; the Voice from the Sky

- After rolling in the dust and crying in ecstasy, Dukhi got up and began cleaning and sweeping the kunj, when suddenly he saw a glittering object under a **pomegranate tree**, lighting up the whole grove with a golden radiance. Coming closer, he saw, to his surprise, a divine golden anklet studded with sapphires, emanating an otherworldly fragrance — mostly covered by dust, just sticking up a little.
- With an **iron spade** (which gardeners use), he very gently unearthed the anklet, and as soon as the iron spade touched it, the spade turned into **24-karat gold**.
- As Dukhi held the ornament, he felt currents of **divine ecstasy** surge through his body and mind. (Analogy: the speaker once accidentally put his finger into a wall outlet and felt the current go through his body.) He was holding **Tadiya** — the paraphernalia of Vrindavaneshwari Srimati Radharani, the anklet from her left foot.
- Suddenly he heard an **akashvani** (a voice from the sky) say: **"Keep this anklet very carefully."** Understanding it was no ordinary anklet and must belong to some exalted personality, he took a part of his cloth and tied it in there to keep it safe.

## Lalita Sakhi Comes Disguised

- Back home, Radharani realized she had lost one of her favorite ankle bells and asked **Lalita sakhi** (the guru of the gopis) to go back to Seva-kunj to retrieve it. Generally such a service would go to one of her **manjaris** (maidservants), but Radharani had special reasons for sending Lalita — revealed later.
- Disguised as an ordinary village girl, Lalita sakhi approached Dukhi Krishnadasa and introduced herself: "My name is **Radha-dasi**. I am a **Kanyakubja brahmini** by caste and a resident of the village **Yavat**." She said that the previous evening her sister had come to Seva-kunj to pick flowers but became frightened on hearing the roar of a tiger and ran away, not noticing that the golden ankle bells on her left foot had fallen off somewhere in the grove.
- She asked Dukhi (who was sweeping) if he had found it. He said, **"Yes, I have found it"** — but his spiritual intuition told him this so-called village girl was anything but ordinary, and that she was hiding her true identity. So he said, "No, I'm not going to give you the anklet. I think I've found what you're looking for, but how could you and your sister — simple villagers — afford such a costly item?"
- Lalita sakhi (always defending Radharani, with a strong nature) replied, "Well, we may be villagers, sir, but we're not poor. Now please give me the anklet." But Dukhi persisted: "Dear girl, I want to see for myself if your sister is as wealthy as you insist. Take me to her, and if what you say is true, I will happily hand over the anklet."

## The Bath at Lalita-kund and the Revelation

- Getting impatient (but recognizing his spiritual depth and not wanting to offend him), Lalita agreed to take him to her "sister." She led Dukhi to nearby **Lalita-kund** and told him to bathe. Confused ("if this girl's sister is a simple village girl, why do I have to take a bath to see her?"), he asked, "Young lady, can you please tell me why I have to bathe?"
- Now really frustrated (things were going too slowly, and Radharani was waiting), and in order to facilitate the pastime and give mercy to this great devotee, Lalita **suddenly revealed her original form as Lalita sakhi**. Upon beholding "the unrivaled beauty of Radha's foremost friend," Dukhi Krishnadasa **fainted**.
- Lalita revived him with the touch of her lotus hand upon his head. To further give him mercy and the qualification for meeting Srimati Radharani, she gave him the famous **Srimati Radharani mantra**, which consists of **fifteen syllables** and is full of the **six divine opulences** — she initiated him into that mantra.

## Transformation into Kanaka Manjari

- She again requested him to bathe, and this time he did so gladly. He entered Lalita-kund fully dressed and completely submerged himself — and upon resurfacing was stunned to find he had taken on the form of a most beautiful **twelve-year-old Braja-gopi**, complete with dress and ornaments.
- By the unfathomable potency of **Yogamaya** (who ultimately orchestrates all these pastimes), Dukhi Krishnadasa (as a gopi) and Lalita sakhi "entered the unmanifest eternal feature of Vrindavan."
- Reflection: There are the **manifest** pastimes (as 5,000 years ago), but even when Krishna winds everything up and returns to the spiritual world, he simultaneously remains in Vrindavan on earth, and the pastimes continue — this is **aprakata** (unmanifest) lila. We should think like this while in Vrindavan (riding a rickshaw, shopping): these pastimes are going on, so how careful, respectful, and Krishna conscious we must be within the Dham.

## Meeting Srimati Radharani

- The two entered the unmanifest eternal Vrindavan, where Lalita escorted the new gopi to a **kunja** (grove) where Vrindavaneshwari Srimati Radharani sat on a throne surrounded by her friends.
- Standing next to Radharani was **Vishakha sakhi**. When Vishakha saw how Dukhi Krishnadasa had assumed the form of a Manjari, appearing "resplendent as gold," she addressed him (in his gopi form): **"O Kanaka Manjari, welcome home!"**
- But in the ecstasy of seeing his worshipable deity, Dukhi Krishnadasa (as Kanaka Manjari), upon seeing Srimati Radharani, could not bear it — "to the amusement and curiosity of the other gopis, he fainted," this time at the feet of Srimati Radharani on the throne.
- When he came to, Lalita sakhi said, "Here is my sister. You wanted to know whether she could be the rightful owner of that anklet — so what do you think?"
- In his gopi body as Kanaka Manjari, Dukhi Krishnadasa gazed at Sri Radha, the gopis, and the kunja, and his senses and mind became "overwhelmed by the inconceivable grandeur of the sweet rural atmosphere surrounding him."

## Radha Accepts the Anklet and Blesses Him

- Unable to speak, he simply reached out to give Radha the magical anklet. As he did so, Sri Radha pulled him toward her with her **right hand** and gratefully accepted the ornament with her **left hand**.
- Sri Radha said (we like to hear the words of Krishna, but we love to hear the words of Sri Radha): "I am very pleased with your devotion and with the service you render by sweeping this kunj. I want to bless you through the anklet you have brought back to me."
- She pressed the anklet into Kanaka Manjari's (Dukhi Krishnadasa's) **forehead**, leaving a mark in the shape of **Sri Radha's lotus foot**, the size of our tilak.
- Then (as the Shastra says) Srimati Radharani mixed sandalwood paste, camphor, and honey along with saffron, rubbed the mixture onto a **moonstone**, and, using part of the anklet, affectionately made a resplendent **dot** in the middle of the new tilak on the forehead of that doe-eyed Manjari.
- Lalita sakhi then gave a name to that tilak with its resplendent dot: **Sammohana**.

## The Name "Shyamananda"

- Srimati Radharani said: "From today on, your name will be **Shyamananda** — meaning one who gives bliss to Sri Radha." As she said this, his new name **Shyamananda** appeared inscribed on his chest — written there, not something that could be wiped away.
- She continued: "Continue in your service, and one day you will be able to live with me forever."

## The Manifested Deity of Shyamasundara

- Radharani then requested our Shyamananda — Dukhi Krishnadasa / Kanaka Manjari, now the great Shyamananda Pandit — to return to the mortal world and share his good fortune with others.
- The prospect of impending separation from Sri Radha caused Shyamananda to start crying uncontrollably. With tears he begged: "Please don't send me back to the mortal world; don't deprive me of service to your lotus feet."
- His appeal melted her heart with pity. To relieve his distress, she **manifested from her heart a beautiful deity of Shyamasundara Krishna** — the **tribhanga-sundara** (threefold-bending) form — gave it to Lalita sakhi, and asked her to give it to Shyamananda.
- She told him: "My dear Shyamananda, as long as you live in the mortal world, keep yourself engaged in the loving service and worship of this deity. In this way you will not suffer due to separation from me. After that, you will come back to my eternal service in the eternal abode."

## Radharani's Twelve Blessings

- She then gave **twelve blessings** to whoever renders service to that deity in this material world — meaning we too can share in the good fortune of Shyamananda Pandit. (These devotees live to share their good fortune with us; we should open our hearts and accept it without conditions.)
- The deity of Shyamasundara is in Vrindavan at the **Radha-Shyamasundara temple** (one of the seven main temples pilgrims visit morning and evening). It is a small deity, off to the side (on the left) of the big deities. The **big** deities of Radha-Shyamasundara were worshipped by **Baladeva Vidyabhushana** (discussed earlier — how he came to Jaipur to defend the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition), but the **small** deity of Shyamasundara is the one manifested from the heart of Radharani.
- We may not be allowed on the altar to render direct service, but we can give donations, or simply go and offer service to Krishna — chant his holy name, do kirtan before the deity, give a donation, take some sweets. The nine processes: **Shravanam, Kirtanam, Vishnu-smaranam, Pada-sevanam, Archanam, Vandanam, Dasyam, Sakhyam, Atma-nivedanam** — all count as glorification/service to Krishna.
- The twelve blessings Radharani gives eternally to anyone who serves that deity:
  1. Whoever has **darshana** even once with devotion shall obtain **prema-bhakti**, go to the rarely attained divine abode of Sri Krishna, and never be born again; misfortune, poverty, and heinous sins shall not enter his home.
  2. Whoever has **regular darshana** will go to Krishna's abode accompanied by **ten past and ten future generations**; with sons, grandsons, and relatives they shall enjoy life, happiness, wealth, and opulence desired even by the demigods, moving fearless like a lion, freed from the blazing trap of the threefold misery (caused by one's own mind, by other living entities, and by natural forces).
  3. Whoever regularly **cleans** the temple of Sri Shyamasundara shall go to the heavenly planets, and after enjoying all pleasures there, attain Krishna's abode as one of his attendants; and if one regularly smears the temple with cow dung after cleansing it, the relatives of one's mother, father, and spouse will never go to hell.
  4. Any pious person who places a **flag** atop the temple and decorates it with banners will immediately become free from all sins committed since birth.
  5. A fortunate soul who regularly **worships** the deity shall attain the power to deliver the whole world, and all his desires will be fulfilled. (Reminder: one of the offenses to the holy name is to consider the glories of chanting and devotional service as exaggeration — this is the full truth.)
  6. A fortunate person who gives **new garments** to the deity shall reside on the moon planet and then attain bliss in the abode of Lord Hari; they will become wealthy, fortunate, disease-free, and attractive, obtain the fruits of the **Ashwamedha** and **Rajasuya** yajnas, and live eternally in the abode of Lord Hari. (Note: the real blessing in almost every verse is going back home to Godhead; the rest describes what happens on the way.)
  7. A rich person who offers **precious ornaments** studded with pearls and gems to Lord Shyamasundara shall become emperor of the whole earth, destroyer of enemies, free from all sins, dear to the ladies, and supremely fortunate; and after obtaining the fruits of the Ashwamedha and Rajasuya yajnas, reside eternally in Krishna's abode. If, full of devotion, one has the fortune of even once seeing Sri Shyamasundara adorned with golden ornaments, all his relatives going back seven generations will be delivered.
  8. A pious person who offers the deity **flower ornaments** — an umbrella of flowers, a pavilion of flowers, a bed of flowers, etc. — shall obtain the best objects of enjoyment, amusement, and pleasure, and at the end go to the eternal abode of Lord Shyamasundara.
  9. A fortunate person who offers **first-class foodstuffs and beverages** shall have all wishes fulfilled and become long-lived, rich, powerful, handsome, attractive, and free from worries and diseases; obtain the fruits of many types of yajnas; and at the end go back home, back to Godhead.
  10. Whoever **circumambulates** the temple four times with a heart full of devotion shall obtain the fruits of circumambulating the whole universe, visiting all holy places, and bathing in all of them; become free from all sins; attain the fruits of ten Ashwamedha yajnas; and at the end be carried by a **swan** to Krishna's own abode, never to be born again, with all good wishes fulfilled.
  11. A fortunate person who has darshan of Sri Shyamasundara during **Kartik** shall obtain the fruits of all worship, mantras, and rituals previously performed (even if given up); millions of sins — even the killing of a brahmana or illicit intercourse — shall be destroyed; and at the end they shall attain the supreme abode of Lord Krishna.
  12. If a fortunate person **constructs, renovates, and cleanses** the temple of Shyamasundara, then ten thousands of past and future generations of his family will go to Krishna's abode; all their sins shall be burnt to ashes, and they shall very soon attain the bliss of serving Lord Krishna.
- So this deity, which came from the heart of Srimati Radharani, is in Vrindavan at the Radha-Shyamasundara temple, at the feet of Shyama. Put it on your "transcendental bucket list": go straight to that temple, and know that Radharani's blessings never go in vain.

## Return to the Material World

- While still in the eternal realm with Radharani, the newly named Shyamananda bowed down to Srimati Radharani — and when he looked up, everything had changed. Radha, Lalita, Vishakha, all the Brajagopis, and the eternal realm of Vrindavan had all vanished, and once again he was in the garden where he had found the anklet, back as a **brahmachari** in a brahmachari's clothes.
- But by the touch of the **Manju-ghosha** anklet from Radharani's left foot, his iron spade had become golden. Looking at himself in the reflection of a puddle of water, he saw that his own complexion had changed — he now had a **golden aura**. On his forehead was the **Sammohana tilak** in the form of Radha's foot with the special dot in the middle, and on his chest was written his name, **Shyamananda**.
- With the divine deity (manifested from Radharani's heart) in his hand, he placed it on his head and, shedding tears with his heart overwhelmed with love, started running to **Jiva Goswami's Bhajan Kutir** to tell him everything that had happened.
- Everything that happened after this point — just as amazing — will be shared in the next lecture, **"Shyamananda Pandit, Part Two."** (There is a lot of nectar there.) This hearing of Vraja-katha and Gaura-katha is the best way to spend our time in the human form of life, rather than letting our minds wander to something temporary and full of stress.

## Why Radharani Sent Lalita (Answered)

- Answering the earlier question — why Radharani sent **Lalita** to the kunj to find the ankle bell, when generally that service could have gone to a manjari:
  - **Reason 1:** to find the anklet.
  - **Reason 2:** to bring Dukhi Krishnadasa to her, so she could bless him, because she was so pleased with his service.
- The deeper meaning: **without the grace of Lalita sakhi, one cannot come into direct contact with Sri Radha** — even if one has the sponsorship of some other gopi. This may be one reason many ISKCON temples have Radha and Krishna with **Lalita and Vishakha** on either side. We consider ourselves servant of the servant of the servant, and it is through all these servants and their combined mercy that we qualify to approach the divine couple.
- **Rupa Goswami's Lalita-ashtakam, verse 7**: "I offer my respectful obeisances unto the supremely charming Sri Lalita Devi, the treasure house of all good qualities. Upon seeing any young maid anywhere in Braja and discerning that she is inclined towards her **priya-sakhi** Srimati Radharani, Lalita immediately tells Radha that she must accept this person into her own party. Radha obeys Lalita, who thus fulfills that maiden's desires." (These are secrets of devotional service we must learn, and one day, by Prabhupada's grace, employ to attain perfection.)
- **Lalita-ashtakam, verse 9**: "If a devotee with a cheerful and pure heart recites this Ashtakam (eight verses in praise of Lalita Devi), then such a devotee will be affectionately brought into Srimati Radharani's own group of sakhis. Lalita Devi is supremely ornamented with beauty, grace, and charm, and along with Srimati Radharani is the effulgent wish-fulfilling creeper (**Kalpavali**) of Vrishabhanu Maharaja's family that winds around the **Kalpavriksha** of Krishna."
- So Radharani sent Lalita to fetch Dukhi Krishnadasa because only by Lalita's grace could he attain the grace of Srimati Radharani — and in the meantime she got back her favorite anklet.

## Shyamananda Pandit the Preacher

- Concluding with verses glorifying Shyamananda Pandit — we began with "Dukhi" (the sad one) and end with the beautiful name Shyamananda Pandit.
- Besides being a very deep **rasika** devotee, Shyamananda Pandit was a **powerful preacher**. Many find that **Narottama dasa Thakura**, **Srinivasa Acharya**, and Shyamananda Pandit were all trained up by Srila Jiva Goswami in Vrindavan into the deepest depths of Krishna consciousness. They were practical, innovative, courageous, determined, energetic preachers — not just to the brahmanas and royalty, but to the down-and-outs, the most unfortunate — imbibing the mood of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu as a **Yuga-avatar** to bring the message of **Braja-bhakti** to the most fallen people of Kali Yuga. The topmost message to the most unqualified.
- When Rupa Goswami first set eyes upon the beautiful form of Gauranga (the combined form of Radha and Krishna, this Yuga-avatar), he said: *namo maha-vadanyaya krishna-prema-pradaya te / krishnaya krishna-chaitanya-namne gaura-tvishe namah* — there has never been a more merciful incarnation of Krishna than Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, because he is giving this highest message to the most fallen people of Kali Yuga. Therefore the demigods are lining up in the heavenly planets to take birth in this movement — and **we are in this movement**. This is our time — now, not later: to chant from the heart, study the Shastras, worship guru, Gauranga, and Srila Prabhupada, and spread the message all over the world. This is our work, our heartbeat. (We still have practical affairs — maintaining the body, family, our roles in society — but this is our heartbeat.)
- Shyamananda Pandit, Srinivasa, and Narottama are among the speaker's heroes (all our acharyas are heroes). As the second generation, they took up where Mahaprabhu left off — he could have inundated the whole world, but the devotees are always begging for service: "Give me seva, give me seva." "Okay, here's some seva — take this book, get onto the internet, and preach." A movement means it is always moving, generation after generation.

## Final Verses of Glorification (Shyamananda-shataka)

- His disciple Rasikananda writes:
  - "I offer my respectful obeisances to Krishna-Nanda's exalted son, Sri Shyamananda. He is great mercy personified. He is the captain of the boat desiring to rescue the people drowning in the horrible, impassable ocean of repeated birth and death. He came to this world bringing with him the wonderful boat of pure love for Lord Krishna — a boat that at once takes one to the further shore."
  - "Let me offer my respectful obeisances to Sri Shyamananda, who is an ocean of mercy, his kindness and spiritual love personified. His face is always cheerful with spiritual love. He is a soldier who defeats the horrible sufferings of life in the material world. By his merciful embrace, they who are tortured by the flames of Kali Yuga's torments find themselves plunged in the nectar of Lord Krishna's pastimes — Lord Krishna, who is the master of the life of the Braja girls."
  - "Glory to a saintly, wonderful, powerful person — a person who is the master of masters; a person who, with his saintly virtues, delights the pious; a person who respectfully leads even the wicked to the path of devotional service." (We do not think of the fallen as demons, just as unfortunate; we do not think ourselves better than them, but more fortunate, so we respectfully take the fallen people of this world back home, back to Godhead.) "A person who, with his great learning, pleases the learned; a person whose glorious form, filled with the nectar of love for Lord Krishna, delights the whole world."
