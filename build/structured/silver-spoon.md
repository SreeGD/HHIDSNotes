## Introduction: Meeting Srila Prabhupada Through His Disciples

- Devotees had asked about **the famous "silver spoon" story**, which comes from the book **Radha-Damodara Vilasa** — the pastimes of the Radha-Damodara traveling sankirtan party overseen within the pastimes of Srila Prabhupada's movement. The speaker decides to go back a little before that story and share his own coming to Krishna consciousness — "a treasure of all of our lives."
- Like many devotees, he first met Srila Prabhupada **through his disciples**. In 1969–1970 Prabhupada was traveling but could not go everywhere, so many were blessed to meet him through the medium of his disciples — just as the spiritual master represents Krishna, the disciple always represents his spiritual master.
- He heard Srila Prabhupada say once: **"We can judge the quality of the guru by the quality of the disciple."**
- He describes himself as so steeped in ignorance, so degraded and fallen, that **it took four sannyasis to pull him out of the quagmire of material life.**

## Before Krishna Consciousness — The Museum Show on Krishna

- Before coming to Krishna consciousness he was in the "grihamedhi ashram" — really "the grihamedhi *well* of existence, a deep well." Somehow Krishna inspired within his heart a desire for spiritual life, so he and his former wife visited various yoga clubs, institutes, and institutions.
- They heard of a **museum show on Krishna**. ("Krishna? Who's Krishna?") At the museum they wandered among mostly **Pichhwai / Rajasthani paintings from Jaipur** — very captivating and fascinating.
- One especially beautiful painting was of the **Rasa-lila**. He was attracted — "this cute little blue boy" appearing one, two, three, four times, all the same figures in the circle — and couldn't figure it out.
- He woke the sleeping museum guard (an African-American man) and asked him to explain: "Who is this little boy, and who's Krishna? K-R-I-S..." The guard read the caption closely and said: **"It says here that boy, this is the good Lord. He's up there in heaven enjoying with the girls. He's having a real fine time up there."** Asked for more, the guard could only repeat that "the good Lord" was "having a fine time with those girls."
- The closing bell rang; the guard told them to come back tomorrow. The speaker walked out scratching his head — "Krishna, heaven, girls, dancing... Who's Krishna?" His former wife didn't know either.

## The Four Sannyasis at Ann Arbor

- Suddenly he heard the sound of **karatalas** ("ch-ch-ch..."). Looking across the lawn at **Ann Arbor (University of Michigan)**, he saw **four angelic boys in saffron robes, with shaved heads and sikhas (ponytails) in the back.**
- Knowing something of Buddhists, he wondered aloud: "These don't look like people from India — are they Buddhists with ponytails? They look like American boys." They went closer.
- One tall devotee played the drum nicely and was "singing about that little blue boy with the girls in heaven."
- Ann Arbor was a very intellectual school, and these were **Vietnam War days** — full of challenging, contesting, and debating. When the kirtan stopped and the tall man began to speak, students objected ("How do you know? How can you prove this? There's no such thing as God"). He did his best, but finally all 40–50 students walked away.
- Only the speaker and his former wife remained. He was moved: "That's really nice what he was saying."

### Vishnujana Maharaja — the First Sannyasi

- Seeing they were the only two who stayed, the tall boy came over: **"Sit down, young man. I will tell you about my spiritual master, His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Srila Prabhupada."**
- He preached for about two hours; the speaker just nodded his head the whole time. When the devotee paused for a drink, the former wife asked, "What do you think about him?" The speaker answered: **"I want to be just like him."** Her fateful words: "That would be very nice."
- Within moments of hearing about Prabhupada and Krishna, the speaker "felt like I was Srila Prabhupada's disciple."

### Krishna Consciousness Is Contagious

- Prabhupada said Krishna consciousness is **very contagious, in a good way.** Just as a sneeze spreads germs about ten meters so another catches the disease, Prabhupada writes in the **Krishna Book** that when a devotee speaks *Krishna-katha* about the Lord, **little drops of nectar collect in the sky and fall on the heads of the audience, turning them into pure devotees.** This is the process.

### Introducing the Four Sannyasis

- The tall devotee introduced himself as **Vishnujana Maharaja.**
- **Brahmananda** — "one big one, very big."
- **Gargamuni** — "very far out," with sikha and a little mustache.
- **Subala Maharaja** — "a short little, kind of quiet one."

### Subala and the Shoes — "Krishna Will Provide"

- Even the speaker became challenging. When Subala preached to him, he pointed at Subala's bare feet: "If Krishna's providing everything for you, where's your shoes, Maharaja?" Subala said he needed shoes, but **"Krishna will provide. Have faith."** ("What, Krishna's going to drop you some from the clouds?")
- A few minutes later a group of hippies passed; one boy was hobbling in a new pair of tennis shoes, complaining they were too small ("I wear a size 10, and somehow that guy gave me a size 9"). He held them up: "Anybody need a pair of size 9 tennis shoes?" Subala smiled — "I'm size 9" — and said he would gladly take them as **Krishna's mercy.** The boy gave them, and Subala said, "Hare Krishna."

## Inviting the Sannyasis to Stay

- As it grew dark and they prepared to leave, the speaker begged these "amazing personalities" to spend a couple of days with them. They were living in an old, soon-to-be-demolished apartment building — mostly vacated, with only their own apartment and one next door habitable.
- Vishnujana asked the Maharajas' opinion. **Brahmananda** folded his arms and asked, **"Will there be some good prasadam?"** ("What's prasadam?" "Prasadam is Krishna's mercy." "I don't have any Krishna's mercy." "No — foodstuff.") The speaker said they were vegetarian, eating applesauce, bananas, and a "little macrobiotic diet" — at which Brahmananda groaned, "Oh, no."

### The Shopping Trip

- Vishnujana asked if he had a little money. Having just received his monthly paycheck (he worked as a gardener, putting his former wife through school to be a teacher), the speaker offered to "spare something."
- He imagined buying "a loaf of bread, some cheese, some cream, a couple of Coca-Colas." Instead, Brahmananda, Subala, and Vishnujana each took a shopping cart and filled all three in ten minutes — "like one of those contests where you get to go through the store for 10 minutes and put in everything you want." The speaker was left with **one dollar** after shopping.

### The First Taste of Prasadam

- Back at the apartment next door they cooked. Vishnujana Maharaja was a very good kirtan man, a very good preacher, and an excellent cook — "that was my first taste of prasadam." They cooked for about three hours.
- The speaker protested ("just put it in the oven, five minutes"; "let me try that") and was told he could not taste it — "so many rules and regulations." He argued that with his meditation and the mantra Maharishi's yoga had given him, he was becoming God conscious and didn't need all this. Vishnujana replied: **"This is bhakti, this is love. Attention to detail is a sign of love."** Every time the devotee spoke, the speaker thought, "Wow, that's true" — falling in love with the devotees through everything they did.
- They cooked a fantastic feast of 10–15 preparations. About six or seven bites into his plate, **Brahmananda asked for seconds** (having finished a huge plate in about 20 seconds), then thirds — up to **six plates** by the time the speaker finished his one.

## Classes Morning, Noon, and Night

- They asked for local friends to invite, but most weren't interested — so the two of them received classes **morning, noon, and night**: **Srimad-Bhagavatam** in the morning, **Sri Isopanishad** in the afternoon, and **Bhagavad-gita** in the evening — four sannyasis lecturing to just the two of them. "This is what it took. It dragged me out of material existence." In the afternoons the sannyasis also preached at the university.

## The Silver Spoon — The Lesson of Spiritual Attachment

- After two or three days the speaker sensed they would leave — perhaps from a lecture point that **sadhus can only stay three days and then must move on.**
- On the last evening he challenged Vishnujana Maharaja, who was speaking about detachment: "But you're not renounced from that **silver spoon**. You're a monk, yet I see you always carry it everywhere — you're very attached to it."
- Vishnujana explained: the silver spoon had belonged to **a small cowherd-boy Krishna Deity** (standing in a transcendental pose with His flute; Whisper renders the Deity's name as "Kartamashi"), one of the first Deities in ISKCON. Srila Prabhupada used to take this Deity with Him to different preaching programs, and one day, **out of affection, Prabhupada gave the Deity's spoon** (with which the Deity took His prasad) to Vishnujana Maharaja. Prabhupada told him: **"A gift from a Vaishnava is a very, very, very special thing."**
- The speaker couldn't understand: "But still it's silver, and you're attached to it." Vishnujana: **"It reminds me of Krishna. These type of attachments are good. We want to become detached from the material world and attached to Krishna — not totally detached from everything."** The speaker insisted attachment was all bad; the exchange became a simple "No." "Yes." "No." "Yes." "No." — and ended for the evening.

### The Departure and the Verse

- The next morning the speaker woke at three o'clock — not because of the usual sweet knock on the door (which had trained him to rise at 3 a.m. for showers and morning prayers, though he had formerly risen at eight for meditation), but **because there was no knock.** He felt an emptiness, ran to the sannyasis' door, and banged on it — "Maharaja! Wake up, you're late! It's time for the prayers to Prabhupada!"
- Their shoes were gone. He banged so hard the door opened — the room was empty. On the little table that had been their altar was **a note: "Dear Brian"** (his name). It said, in effect, that they were going on with their preaching mission, and quoted a verse from the **Eleventh Canto of Srimad-Bhagavatam** (**SB 11.23.53**, paraphrased): **"Just as sticks, twigs, and leaves come together in a river by the force of its waves and are again separated by that same force, so all of us come together by the force of time and our past pious and impious activities, and by that same force we are again separated."**
- He fell to the ground and cried — "a nineteen-year-old boy, tough American boy, never cried" — grieving that his best friends were gone. Then his former wife found, **under the paper, the silver spoon** that Vishnujana had left for him. He grabbed it, took it to his heart ("Maharaja! Come back! Oh, Krishna!"), and became more attached to that silver spoon than anything else in the world. **That is how Vishnujana Maharaja taught him the lesson of spiritual attachment.**
- The sannyasis had also left a little picture of Krishna and a little picture of Prabhupada, which the two received very reverentially. Through the association of devotees they gained a little understanding of what devotion is.
- **Sadhu-sanga verse** (Chaitanya-caritamrita, Madhya 22.54, paraphrased): *"sadhu-sanga, sadhu-sanga — sarva-shastre koy / lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi hoy"* — even a moment's (*lava-matra*) association with a devotee of the Lord can give the higher taste for Krishna consciousness, just as a moment's association with an intense materialist can deviate one from the path.
- That altar became as dear to them as the temple Deities are to the audience. The silver spoon remained on his altar for many years; when he took the sannyasa order of life, he gave it to his former wife as her shelter.

## Searching for the Devotees

- The sannyasis had never even mentioned there were other temples (from **Radha-Damodara Vilasa** one learns there had been some arguments and disagreements, and philosophically they were a bit off at that point — but one would never have known it from the love they showed for Prabhupada).
- He rode his bicycle all around the city at four in the morning, checking every exit, and came back weary — thinking **there were only four people on the planet who knew anything about Krishna, Prabhupada, the holy name, and Lord Chaitanya.** A devastating time.
- Weeks later, someone said he had seen "orange-clad people" in **Detroit**, an hour's drive away. The speaker drove every street of the town for eight hours — couldn't find them. The next week, following a lead to a house, they smelled incense and saw a **bead bag hanging from the windowsill and karatalas** — but the devotees had just moved to **8311 East Jefferson Street.**

### The Detroit Temple — Storming Out

- Arriving on a Sunday just as the feast was served, the speaker met a devotee, said "Hare Krishna," and mentioned his hero, "my best friend in this whole world, Vishnujana Maharaja." But this devotee had had a disagreement with the Maharaja and said, "Oh, I don't like that guy." Enraged that anyone would speak so about his friend who introduced him to Krishna, the speaker **stormed out and slammed the door and drove off.**
- The temple president and about **20 devotees ran after him**, saying it was just a misunderstanding — and they chastised that devotee for the offense.

## Coming to the Temple — Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura's Mercy

- About a month later, lured back by "really good food," he kept coming for about six months. It was difficult to give up his last attachments; doubts came about having to **shave his head and beard** and losing his freedom.
- Devotees told him: **if you pray to Prabhupada, Prabhupada will give you mercy and you can give up your attachments.** He prayed, but "it wasn't working."
- Then came the **appearance day of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**, Prabhupada's spiritual master. The devotees told him Prabhupada's famous line: **"Sometimes the grandfather is more merciful than the father himself"** — so he should pray to the grandfather-guru, who would be very kind. For about ten days he planned his speech, his prayer, his approach, and the flowers — anticipating "like a darshan" with Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura.

### Praying in the Basement — and Getting Fired

- On the day, he had to work as a gardener. One chore was to go into the basement of the **university president's house** on the property to clean up after the dog — a little **dachshund** that did its business there and that used to bite him despite his service.
- Having cleaned up after the dog, he set out his little candles, flowers, a special rug bought for the occasion, and his written prayer, turned off the lights, and offered **full dandavats** for the first time. He prayed: **"Dear Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, can't you see how fallen I am, down here cleaning up after these low living entities? I can't give up my attachments, but I want to become a devotee and serve your dearest son Srila Prabhupada — please do something, help me get out of this job, help me get out of this material world."**
- Suddenly the door opened — his **boss**, a very conservative man, had been listening. Seeing a young American boy stretched out on the ground before a large picture of an Indian sadhu with candles and incense burning, he demanded, "What in the [expletive] are you doing?" The speaker looked up: **"I'm praying to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura... that I can become a devotee of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, whom Lord Brahma says is Krishna."** The boss: **"You're fired."**
- Still on the ground, the speaker looked at the picture and said, **"Thank you, Guru Maharaja,"** got up, and walked out. "Never come back." "No problem" — and he handed over the doggy dish.
- He told his former wife: "Pack the whole house — we're moving to the temple." "How is it possible?" **"Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura Maharaja's mercy."** They packed everything and moved in.

## Srila Prabhupada Comes to Detroit

- A few months later, "the big day of the year" came: **Prabhupada would come to the Detroit temple** (he came about once a year). The speaker had moved in only a couple of months before. This arrival (either 1970 or 1971) is mentioned in the **Prabhupada-lilamrita.**
- He asked the temple president for **initiation** — devotees were being initiated easily then, sometimes after days or weeks — but their strict president said, **"One year, then you can take initiation."**

### The Arrival — "You Are Not These Bodies"

- Prabhupada arrived at the Detroit airport before many senior devotees. What stood out was that **everyone was crying** — some 300 devotees from around the Midwest and East Coast. "Prabhupada just drew out this bhakti, this love for his lotus feet," even at that very beginning stage.
- As Prabhupada came down the gangway into view, the speaker looked through his camera (there were very few pictures of Srila Prabhupada in those days): "Wow, he looks just like he does in the pictures." *Click.* Everyone fell down crying; Prabhupada patted devotees on the head, embraced senior devotees, and sat down.
- **The lecture line the speaker never forgot:** Prabhupada leaned over to all the young boys and girls and said, **"Please, please believe me when I say you are not these bodies."** Somehow it penetrated the speaker's heart — "I started looking at my body for the first time... and *not* this body."
  - When you hear such realizations **from the lips of a pure devotee**, it has a tremendously powerful effect. That is why Prabhupada said the chanting of Hare Krishna especially should be **heard from the lips of a pure devotee** — it has a transcendentally powerful effect. Prabhupada then went on chanting *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare.*
  - The speaker notes his lifelong confidence in leadership when he hears **Prabhupada's bhajans** being played, because that sound vibration is so potent to destroy the sinful reactions in the heart and uproot material desires.
  - This memory is confirmed by others present at that same lecture — **Ravindra Svarupa Prabhu** (with whom he discussed it recently at Govardhan Hill during Kartik) and his godbrother **Bhakti Bringa Govinda Maharaja** — who remember the very same line.

### The Children Are Special Devotees

- Prabhupada went upstairs for a quick change and came back down while the kirtan raged. Beside Prabhupada's Vyasasana stood a little boy about two years old — **Vaishnava Das** — old enough for mothers to "just start to hold the karatalas," playing along. Prabhupada patted the boy on the head; his mother exclaimed, "My little ray of Vishnu!"
- Prabhupada sat down and, instead of singing, spoke about devotees:
  - **A devotee who leaves Krishna consciousness** because of material desires — who gives up the process and goes back to material life to fulfill his desires — upon leaving his body **goes to the heavenly planets**, where he can fulfill those desires while getting purified, and from there transfer himself back to Godhead. But that can take a very long time, since the demigods live many thousands of years.
  - **But a strict devotee who does not finish the course** of Krishna consciousness within his lifetime — who dies not yet having attained the exalted stage of **prema-bhakti** — **takes birth immediately in the family of devotees.** Therefore Prabhupada said **these children are all very, very special; treat them properly and raise them to be nice Vaishnavas.**
- Ever since, the speaker has been inclined toward the children in Krishna consciousness, seeing them as devotees who in a previous lifetime did not deviate from the process but died "untimely," not yet finished — now with a good birth in Krishna consciousness and every chance of going back home, back to Godhead.

### The Ten-Dollar Offering

- Prabhupada stayed a few days. As he was leaving for his car, the speaker — having gotten neither initiation nor a personal darshan — wanted to show his appreciation. He quickly scribbled a note: **"Dear Srila Prabhupada, please accept my most humble obeisances. Thank you so much for coming to Detroit. I'm going to miss you so much and feel so much separation. Please accept this ten dollars for your newly created book fund. Your servant, Bhakta Brian."** He put the ten dollars in the envelope.
- As Prabhupada was getting into his car, the speaker called out, **"Srila Prabhupada, wait!"** Everybody froze. Prabhupada turned with "an amazing look on his face." The speaker walked up very close, said softly, "Srila Prabhupada, this is for you," and looked him in the eyes. **Prabhupada said, "Hare Krishna, thank you."**
- "That was one of the best days of my whole life." A spiritual master's appreciation of your service makes life perfect. That is all devotees want: no matter what you achieve — prime minister, president, the richest man in the world — you don't get the same satisfaction as when your spiritual master says "thank you very much for that devotional service," because **if you please your spiritual master, then Krishna is pleased with you, and if Krishna is pleased with you, your life is perfect.** Just a short exchange — "Hare Krishna, thank you" — but it meant everything in the world.

### Prabhupada's Letter

- About ten days later, a letter arrived from Prabhupada. In those days a letter from Prabhupada was not the private property of the one addressed — so they **blew a conch** in the middle of the day. Whatever they were doing, "just like the gopis who gave up everything and ran to see Krishna in the forest," everyone dropped it and ran to the temple living room to sit and hear the letter read.
- Like "transcendental roll call in the military," the reader announced: **"Prabhupada is writing Bhakta Brian."** It didn't register at first, and everyone looked at him — "Me? Prabhupada wrote *me* a letter?" His first thought: **"Prabhupada knows I exist."** His hands shook, fearing he might be chastised in front of everyone.
- **The letter (paraphrased):** "My dear Bhakta Brian, please accept my blessings. I want to thank you so much for the letter which you gave me as I was leaving the Detroit temple. I also want to thank you very much for the ten dollars which you have very graciously given for my newly created book fund. I have information from the authorities in the temple that you are doing very well in your first months of Krishna consciousness, so please continue. And my instruction to you, as always: **follow in the footsteps of advanced devotees**, and surely in a very short time you will become Krishna conscious, and I will be very happy to accept you as my duly initiated disciple. Your ever well-wisher, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami — Srila Prabhupada."
- This even more intimate exchange shows **how the process of Krishna consciousness actually works** — through these loving dealings and exchanges between devotees, between disciple and spiritual master, and between devotees and the Lord.
  - Are we meeting the Lord? Yes — the Lord kindly gives His darshan every day and encourages us with His transcendental form, His prasadam, and His holy name. Through these, what would otherwise take lifetimes of penance, austerity, study, and knowledge becomes very easy — so easy that it becomes easy to give up our attachments to this world. (Spoken at the "Chowpatty temple," he tells the audience they have a very good chance of going back home in this lifetime because of the sweet loving exchanges there.)

## The Frontier Mission — Traveling to Europe with Prabhupada

- A couple of months later, Prabhupada requested their temple president, **Bhagavan Das**, to go on a frontier mission to **Europe** and start a temple in **Paris, France**. Bhagavan needed someone who knew Europe. Having taken a two-week European vacation once, the speaker volunteered ("Yes, I know everything about it, I travel all over the place") — and his wife would go too.
- He was in **duality**: whether to go to Europe or instead join the newly created "transcendental road show" at **New Vrindavan** (with **Kirtanananda Swami** and **Vishnujana**). He asked **His Holiness Satsvarupa Maharaja**, offering, "I'll do whatever you say, Maharaja." Satsvarupa thought a moment: **"Well, go to Europe."** "Okay."

### Getting on Prabhupada's Flight

- Bhagavan said he had heard **Srila Prabhupada would also be going to London around the same time** — "Why don't you see if you can get on the same plane and travel to Europe with Prabhupada?"
- The speaker phoned the New York temple to learn Prabhupada's flight, then called the airline. "Sorry, sir, completely overbooked; this flight closed weeks ago." Then, after some talk in the background: "Actually, two seats have just been cancelled. Although I'm not supposed to do this (we're supposed to take people from the wedding), I'll give you those two seats." They drove to New York.

### In Prabhupada's Room — "Preach Boldly and Have Faith in the Holy Names"

- Prabhupada, having heard they would come to Europe with him on the mission to start Krishna consciousness in France, **called them into his room.** He preached about spreading Lord Chaitanya's sankirtan movement, translating books, starting temples, and doing Harinam-sankirtan; the two sat saying "yes, yes, yes."
- Prabhupada told his servant to get something from his closet — some **socks** and a **dhoti**. He looked the speaker in the eyes and gave the instruction: **"Preach boldly and have faith in the holy names."** Then he gave his dhoti as **maha-prasad.**
- "If I could open up my heart like Hanuman, you would see those words etched there: **preach boldly and have faith in the holy names.**" He took it as a personal instruction — to be taken as one's very life and soul.
- **Parallel with Prabhupada's own life:** Srila Prabhupada received instructions from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura — "if you ever get money, print books" — and, at their very first meeting on a Calcutta rooftop, before Prabhupada had even finished offering his obeisances, Bhaktisiddhanta said: **"You are a nice, educated Indian young gentleman — why don't you spread Lord Chaitanya's teachings in the English-speaking language?"** Prabhupada kept those instructions within his heart, and later, reading **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's** famous purport in **Bhagavad-gita** that one should take the instruction of the spiritual master as one's life and soul, he lived by it — an example for all of us.

### The Airport — "Transcendental Chaos"

- The next morning the airport scene was "completely transcendentally chaotic." Some **400 devotees** overtook the check-in area in a roaring kirtan, so the other passengers couldn't check in. Only the speaker and Prabhupada got squeezed to the front (with a few others coming along — Bhagavan, **Pradyumna**, and perhaps **Shyamasundara**).
- The customs officer — a grouchy, big, burly man — barked at Prabhupada, **"Open up that bag!"** — Prabhupada's little **white medicine bag** which he carried everywhere (containing his medicines, passport, pen, tilaka container, "all these transcendental items"). Prabhupada put it on the counter but couldn't get the latch open. The man repeated harshly, "I told you to open up that bag."
- The speaker leaned over: **"If you speak like that one more time to my spiritual master, I'm going to break your nose."** The officer: "I thought you guys were peaceniks, monks or something." "You're going to see what I am if you speak like that — I'm going to break your face." The man backed off: "Okay, *you* open it."
- As a result of "speaking up like that," the speaker got the special mercy of looking inside Prabhupada's bag, which he had always wondered about. Opening it slowly "like a little kid at Christmas," he found a pen — and Prabhupada said, "I'm supposed to look at it, not you." The officer, puzzled by the contents ("What are those medicines?"), was told it was **Ayurvedic medicine, no drugs** — "Get out of here, this is enough."

### On the Plane with Prabhupada — the Charlie Chaplin Movie

- In those days one walked to the plane; there were no other passengers yet (devotees still blocking check-in), and the speaker sat next to Prabhupada.
- About an hour into the flight the **movie** came on. The speaker resolved to show Prabhupada "how fixed up I am" ("movies are maya"), while still wondering what the movie was — and tried to read his Bhagavad-gita in the dark (reciting **Bhagavad-gita 18.66**, *sarva-dharman parityajya mam ekam sharanam vraja...*). But **Prabhupada was watching the movie and laughing** — it was a **Charlie Chaplin** film. The speaker, Prabhupada, and Pradyumna ended up laughing more than anyone on the plane.
- Afterward doubts arose: "I thought we weren't supposed to watch movies — Prabhupada is supposed to clear our doubts." Too nervous to ask directly, the speaker asked **Pradyumna** to ask Prabhupada. Pradyumna returned with a big smile and Prabhupada's answer: **"Charlie Chaplin's humor is so original, and Krishna is the source of all original things"** — so Prabhupada was seeing **Krishna** in Charlie Chaplin's humor. (The speaker reflected that he had only been seeing Charlie Chaplin, not Krishna.)

### Landing in London — Left Behind with the Bag

- They landed in London — "the best day of my life." The speaker anticipated going with Prabhupada to the **London temple (7 Bury Place)** for a lecture, kirtan, and delicious prasadam — maybe even meeting **George Harrison** and **John Lennon**, the Beatles.
- But **Shyamasundara** discovered one of Prabhupada's bags was missing — the one with all his books for translation — and someone had to stay back. Trying to glorify the speaker, Shyamasundara said, "We can leave this nice boy." ("You're a nice boy, nicer than me; I did many bad things.") **"We'll leave Indradyumna behind."** — Goodbye feast and George Harrison.
- Given the baggage tag, the speaker was left alone, having been in London only once before (passing through by train): he couldn't understand the accents, the money was "all funny," and he didn't know how to take a taxi. Two hours later a man asked, "Are you waiting for a bag? It's very heavy — you'll have to pay extra." With only a little money, he paid; he was angry, hungry, tired, and miserable, and "just about to curse that bag but didn't." A long drive in pouring rain got him and the bag, all wet, to the temple.

### Arriving at the London Temple

- Opening the door, he saw all the **brahmacharis** sitting against the banisters and walls, the paper plates showing "finger streaks" where they had scraped up the last of the sweet rice or sweet-and-sour sabji. "That was the best feast I ever ate!" "The best lecture I ever heard!" "The kirtan — Prabhupada was singing so nicely!" The speaker — "the nicest boy in the world" — asked if they'd saved him any prasadam. **Nobody had.** There would be an offering at nine o'clock, three hours away.

### Carrying the Bag Upstairs — The Slap of Mercy

- He asked one of the men to carry Prabhupada's heavy bag upstairs to Prabhupada's room on the third floor. Nobody would help ("too much sweet rice, too much" — "Sorry, Prabhu"). So he carried it himself up the steep English stairs, taking about ten minutes.
- At the top he saw the brass plate: **"His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami."** "The fool that I am," he didn't even think to knock — he just grabbed the suitcase and **went in backwards** because it was so heavy. (Proper etiquette: knock, get permission, then immediately fall on your face — but he backed in.)
- Suddenly a voice: **"Watch out — you're going to bump into Srila Prabhupada!"** He turned; Prabhupada's face was inches from his own. Sweating, exhausted from the bag, and hungry, he apologized — "Srila Prabhupada, I'm so sorry" — and Prabhupada just looked at him with compassion. The speaker fell to the floor right next to Prabhupada's "soft lotus feet."
- Then — a **slap on his back** ("right here — you can see it's like a transcendental impression on my back; the doctor saw it today and asked, 'What is that handprint on your back?'"). Prabhupada said something, then turned, went into his other chamber, and closed the door softly.
- Getting up in transcendental bliss, he found Prabhupada's servant **Nandakumara** — normally a conservative young man — amazed: **"Prabhupada slapped you on the back! I never saw him do that before. Prabhu, you got a lot of mercy!"** ("He hit me really hard, too — that's what it takes to get someone like me out of the material world.")
- Nandakumara relayed what Prabhupada had said: **"So much endeavor in this material world — but when I take you back to Godhead, everything will be easy and sublime."**
