## Officially Opening the Kartik Parikrama (Vrindavan, 2014)

- The speaker formally opens the Kartik Parikrama in the holy place of Vrindavan for the year 2014.
- Many devotees have looked forward to this opportunity for months — "perhaps from the very moment that we finished last year's Vrindavan Parikrama." Some have lived the whole year in anticipation, the feeling arising immediately after last year's parikrama ended.
- We are fortunate this year to have a number of senior devotees — Prabhupada disciples — among us. Their presence forms "the entire spirit of Parikrama."

## Welcoming the Senior Devotees

- **Chaturatma Prabhu** — dear godbrother.
- **Bada Hari Prabhu** — and this year his good wife **Kesarupa** will also be present.
- **Kalasambhara Prabhu** — an *avadhuta*, "out there wandering someplace in Kokilavan looking for Radha and Krishna."
- **Madhava Prabhu** — who will lead a lot of the kirtans.
- **Mother Sitala** — a little late because she is now part of the Mayapur Management Committee, "stuck there," but coming the day after tomorrow.
- Other illustrious personalities will probably join during the month "because this is just pure nectar."
- All these devotees will actively participate this year — all of them will be asked to speak and give lectures on the acharyas and the places being visited.

## Thanking the Organizer, Rasika Siromani

- Everything has been so nicely organized that it will go very smoothly — due to the efforts of one person, **Rasika Siromani**.
- Usually one thanks such a person after the parikrama is over, once everything has gone well. But there is so much confidence in her that — "despite it being India, where anything can go wrong and go topsy-turvy" — things will run smoothly by her expert management.

## Thanking Srila Prabhupada for Welcoming Us in Vrindavan

- Most appropriately, we should thank **Srila Prabhupada** for welcoming us here in Sri Vrindavan Dham.
- It was Prabhupada's very strong desire that his followers — not only his immediate disciples, but future generations of devotees — would regularly come to Sri Vrindavan Dham, "both for purification and for inspiration."
- This is one reason, if not the foremost, that Prabhupada put so much effort into constructing the **Krishna Balaram Mandir**.
- Of the many great contributions Prabhupada made to the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition worldwide, one of the foremost was the construction and development of the Krishna Balaram Mandir.
- Prabhupada understood that Westerners — especially preachers — face constant chance of contamination in Kali-yuga, and need to be revitalized by coming to holy dhams like Vrindavan, Mayapur, or Jagannath Puri.

## Prabhupada's Instruction: Come Every Year (1974/75 Mayapur Lecture)

- The speaker recalls a lecture Prabhupada gave in Mayapur, around 1974 or 1975, which his godbrothers and godsisters will remember.
- In that lecture Prabhupada said all the devotees from the Western countries — "which meant the whole world" — should come every year to the Vrindavan–Mayapur festival.
- "We couldn't believe our ears." The devotees looked at their GBC and Temple Presidents: "You hear what Prabhupada said?"
- After the lecture, one of the GBC men, **Rameshwara Prabhu**, went to Prabhupada: "With all due respect, this may not be so practical, that all the devotees come from the West to Vrindavan and Mayapur."
  - Prabhupada asked, "Why not?"
  - Rameshwara: "It's expensive — it's like 800 dollars for each devotee to come to India." He added something significant: "It means the book distribution will stop for some time; the book sales will go down."
- As Rameshwara tells the story, Prabhupada simply did not take that into consideration: "It's so important that my disciples come here for their purification and their inspiration every year."

## The Scriptural Basis: Rupa Goswami's Five Essential Practices

- What Prabhupada was saying was grounded in our philosophy and scriptures. **Sri Rupa Goswami** outlines the process of Krishna consciousness in his **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu**.
- Of the 64 items important for those practicing Krishna consciousness, five are most important — Rupa Goswami calls them **essential**.
- Chaturatma Prabhu was asked to quote the verse; it "would just flow off his tongue like the wind." The verse of the five essentials:
  - **"sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-shravana, mathura-vasa, sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana"** — the five most potent limbs of devotional service.
- The five, explained in order:
  1. **Sadhu-sanga** — one should associate with devotees; "the devotee must communicate with other devotees."
  2. **Nama-kirtana** — one should chant/sing the holy names of the Lord.
  3. **Bhagavata-shravana** — one should regularly hear the Srimad-Bhagavatam.
  4. **Mathura-vasa** — one should reside in or visit a place like Mathura or Vrindavan.
  5. **Sri-murti-seva** — one should worship the deity of the Lord with faith and veneration.
- Thanks to Prabhupada's expert organization, all of us are doing these five things regularly. Prabhupada founded and organized ISKCON so that every devotee is practically doing all five essential items almost every day, if serious.
  - Whether we live in the temple or outside, we regularly associate with devotees.
  - "I hope you're chanting your rounds every day."
  - If you're not hearing Bhagavatam, you should be reading Bhagavatam.
  - Nowadays everyone in ISKCON has a deity — "at least a picture of Panchatattva."
  - And we all have the open invitation of Srila Prabhupada to regularly come to Vrindavan or Mayapur and go on parikrama.

## "This Is What We Do"

- Once, giving a newspaper interview, Prabhupada summarized these simple activities of Krishna consciousness and told the reporter: **"This is what we do."**
- The speaker believes this was the theme of a kirtan/Sadhu-sanga retreat T-shirt: "This is what we do."

## Prabhupada as the Perfect Example (Acharya Teaches by Example)

- Prabhupada is the perfect example of a devotee who practices these five essential activities, because he is the Founder-Acharya of ISKCON — and *acharya* means "one who teaches by example."
- **On residing in / visiting a holy place:** As a young child Prabhupada always dreamed of going to Vrindavan, Mayapur, and Jagannath Puri, having heard of these places from his father. He visited them briefly while doing his business to maintain his family.
- As soon as Prabhupada was freed from family responsibilities — when he became a sannyasi, "a citizen of the world" — he came here to Vrindavan, "you could say, to retire as a sannyasi." But his retirement meant having more time to prepare for his mission of going to the West.
- Mainly in Vrindavan, staying in one, two, or three different places, Prabhupada was writing his **Bhaktivedanta purports to the First Canto of Srimad-Bhagavatam.**
- The first volume of the **Srila Prabhupada-Lilamrita** (by Satsvarupa Das Goswami) is titled **"A Lifetime in Preparation"** — Prabhupada's whole life was a preparation for the ten years he spent preaching Krishna consciousness in Western countries.

## How Prabhupada Lived Like a Vrajabasi

- When in Vrindavan, Prabhupada lived very much as the Vrajabasis do:
  - Lived very simply at the **Radha Damodar temple.**
  - Washed his own clothes, prepared his own meals, chanted his japa every morning.
  - Like Brajabasis do, once in the morning and once in the evening he would visit all the **seven main temples** of Vrindavan — a practice that takes a few hours every day.
- We don't do that today because we have our Krishna Balaram Mandir — "the heart of our movement," where Prabhupada's samadhi is — but that is what the Brajabasis do and what Prabhupada did, "all the while preparing his mission to go to the West."

## Following the Six Goswamis

- Prabhupada was following in the footsteps of the **Six Goswamis**, who had received an order from **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (the Yuga-avatar) to go to Vrindavan for three particular reasons:
  1. To **rediscover the lost places** of Krishna's pastimes.
  2. To **establish temples and deity worship** in them.
  3. Most important — to go through all the Vedic literature and extract the important verses that **prove Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead**, distinct from all the other worshipable deities in Vedic culture.
- Prabhupada was in the same mood in Vrindavan. Specifically, his commentaries on the Srimad-Bhagavatam establish Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

## Gauridas's Story: "I Have Appeared for Two Reasons"

- The speaker recalls a godbrother, **Gauridas**, who was in Vrindavan when Prabhupada had established the Krishna Balaram Mandir and was organizing things around the world from his headquarters there.
- Just outside Prabhupada's living quarters in the Vrindavan temple was a little garden, where Prabhupada liked to sit and chant his japa.
- One day Gauridas was fanning Prabhupada when another godbrother, **Bhaktisvarupa Damodara Goswami**, came to report on the scientific preaching.
- Prabhupada was a little sick at that time, not moving much. But hearing how Bhaktisvarupa Damodara was challenging — and in some instances defeating — the scientists, Prabhupada sat up very straight, enlivened.
- Especially when Bhaktisvarupa Damodara said, "Srila Prabhupada, we are establishing in the scientific community that God is a person," Prabhupada sat up, raised his hand with his finger in the air, and said something that stayed always in the minds of Gauridas and Bhaktisvarupa Damodara:
  - **"Actually, I have appeared in this world for two reasons. One: to establish Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. And number two: to defeat these rascal scientists."**
- By writing his purports to the Srimad-Bhagavatam in Vrindavan, Prabhupada was establishing exactly this. **Brahma-samhita 5.1** — *"ishvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam"* — Krishna is the Supreme Ishvara, "above all the devatas," the cause of all causes.

## The Unique Contribution to the West: Revealing the Personality of God

- For Western society Prabhupada made a very unique contribution: he **revealed the personality of God.**
- In Western countries people believe in God — "there's a church on every street corner." But if you ask, with due respect, "Who is God?" — "they go mum" (a British saying). They have no idea who or what God is.
- Our humble challenge, "with a blade of grass between our teeth," is: **"How can you love someone if you know nothing about them?"**

## Analogy: Why We Love Chaturatma (God Is a Person)

- Why do we love Chaturatma? Because of his unique personal characteristics:
  - He's very Krishna conscious; a very good, stylish dresser.
  - At 64 he still has blonde hair; always jolly, always laughing and smiling. "When he smiles you can see all 36 of his teeth" (a joke — he's actually missing some).
  - He wears earrings — "because Madhava does it." ("If you had told him at 9 years old that at 61 he'd be wearing earrings, he probably would have fainted.")
- The point: we love him because of his personal characteristics. **Variety is the spice of life** — we like people the way they are.
- Similarly, Krishna is a person, and it is the personal characteristics of God, ultimately as **Bhagavan**, that endear us to Him. This is what makes Him dear/"expensive" to us — and it was Prabhupada's ability to convince us of that.
- Again: Prabhupada "appeared in this world for two reasons — to reveal Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and to defeat the rascal scientists." As his perfect representative, he carried on that tradition in modern times, and all over the world people are accepting Krishna as God.

## Prabhupada Saw Krishna Face to Face

- Prabhupada could do this because he could see Krishna face to face. It was not merely establishing something on a philosophical basis (*jnana*); Prabhupada had the **vijnana** (realization), and what's more the **rasa** — the intimate relationship with Krishna — so that when he spoke, "you were convinced."
- A newspaper reporter once asked, "How do you know all these things?" **Rameshwara**, sitting nearby, answered, "Because he reads the authorized scriptures." But Prabhupada said, **"No, Krishna tells me directly."**
- Prabhupada was a **Vrajabasi in Gokula Vrindavan.** He once told the devotees, "I do not know what is sinful activity," and "There was never a moment in my life that I forgot the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna."
- It was only natural, then, that at a certain stage Prabhupada came home to Vrindavan — "this is a replica of the spiritual world; this is what we're hankering for."

## Prabhupada's Attachment to Vrindavan (Vrindavan Days)

- **Hayagriva Das**, a godbrother who spent much time with Prabhupada personally, wrote a book called **Vrindavan Days**, recounting what Prabhupada said in the early days of the movement.
- Prabhupada once told Hayagriva: *"My dear Hayagriva, I lived very simply in Vrindavan, very content to be in Srila Rupa Goswami's presence. Now my movement, ISKCON, has grown; I have hundreds of temples I can go and stay in. But I like my quarters in Vrindavan best of all."* — This reveals where Prabhupada's heart was.
- Even while preaching in the West he was remembering Vrindavan. The most famous quote: *"Here I am now sitting in New York, the world's greatest city, but my heart is always hankering after that Vrindavan."*

## Why He Loved Vrindavan So Much (The Purport)

- Prabhupada writes in a purport (Fourth Canto, 29th chapter of Srimad-Bhagavatam — **SB 4.29, purport**), addressing the whole world: there is a place called Vrindavan, and if one goes there and gets the chance to hear from the pure devotees who live there, "then the cultivation of Krishna consciousness becomes very easy."
- We are all trying to become Krishna conscious, but most of us struggle to greater or lesser degrees, afflicted by the threefold miseries — **adhyatmika, adhibhautika, adhidaivika.**
- It's hard to remember Krishna when living in Chicago, New York, London, or Sydney — "Kali-yuga is very advanced, and Kali-yuga is most advanced in the cities."
- But even the most neophyte or fallen devotee will admit: when you come to Vrindavan, it's really easy to remember Krishna.
  - There are 7,000 temples, most significantly the Krishna Balaram Mandir that Prabhupada established.
  - **Gaura-Nitai, Krishna-Balaram, Radha-Shyamasundar** — "from his own heart they manifested."

## "Just Stay in the Krishna Balaram Mandir and Chant"

- Prabhupada once said there's no need to go anywhere else — "just stay in the Krishna Balaram Mandir compound." He was afraid devotees might go off to the babajis at places like Radha Kund and get bewildered. So one time he said: "No parikramas. Everyone just stay in the temple and chant Hare Krishna."
- Yet ultimately Prabhupada did want us to go on parikrama — he led the first ISKCON Vrindavan parikramas himself, taking the devotees to all these holy places in the early years.
- **Personal memory:** The speaker once went on parikrama with Prabhupada near Madhuvan; Prabhupada was bathing in the Yamuna. Watching Prabhupada's secretary and servant bathe him, the speaker wished he could pour some water and do abhishek to Prabhupada there in the Yamuna.
- Prabhupada wanted us to come here because it is a **stimulus**: "It's hard to remember Krishna in the West; it's hard to forget Krishna when you're in Vrindavan."

## Vrindavan Is for Devotees — and Even Welcomes Sinners

- Vrindavan is a place for devotees of the Lord.
  - The yogis go to **Rishikesh** (now very popular, because people are into ashtanga yoga).
  - The impersonalists go to the **Himalayas** to merge into the Brahman.
  - But going to Vrindavan means you naturally get the association of the Vaishnavas and Vaishnavis.
- Vrindavan is so merciful that she even welcomes sinners with open arms — and if they come, they get purified.
- **Srimad-Bhagavatam, Third Canto (purport)** — Prabhupada writes that any person who goes to Vrindavan, even if very sinful, "will at once contact the spiritual atmosphere of Vrindavan and automatically find himself chanting Hare Krishna."
- Sinners are actually encouraged to go to Vrindavan, because just by being there all the sinful reactions in the heart are washed away, material desires are defeated, and **prema** (love of God) can manifest in the heart, even of a sinner.

## Prahlada Maharaj's Instruction to Hiranyakashipu

- Millions of years ago, **Prahlada Maharaj** recommended to his father **Hiranyakashipu** — "who can be a bigger demon or asura than Hiranyakashipu?" — that he go to Vrindavan.
- Prahlada was very intelligent: he first glorified his father to gain his confidence, then instructed him. He addressed him as **"O best of the Asuras."**
  - Joke: If the speaker said to Bada Hari, "My dear Bada Hari, you are the best of the Asuras," he'd become very upset — because we are trying to become devotees. But Hiranyakashipu, hearing this title, "his chest swelled with pride — yes, I am the best of the Asuras."
- Then Prahlada instructed him (from **Srimad-Bhagavatam, Seventh Canto**): any person who has accepted a temporary body and temporary household life is certainly embarrassed by anxiety, having fallen into "a dark well where there is no water, but only suffering." As a **Mahajan**, Prahlada recommends to such suffering persons: **go to Vrindavan, where only Krishna consciousness is prevalent, and there take shelter of Krishna, the Supreme Personality of Godhead.**
- Did Hiranyakashipu do it? No — because of his false ego he couldn't accept the instruction, "especially if it's coming from a family member."
- But we can accept the instruction, because we are followers of the **12 Mahajanas** — and specifically we are here on the invitation of the Founder-Acharya of ISKCON, Srila Prabhupada.

## Prabhupada's Invitation to Come and Stay

- In another purport in the Bhagavatam, Prabhupada writes: *"In our Krishna conscious movement we therefore invite all the elderly persons of the world to come to Vrindavan and stay there in retired life,"* making advancement in Krishna consciousness.
- To his disciples and direct followers, Prabhupada actually invites us to come and stay **permanently**: "Don't go home — don't go back to Moscow, Kiev, New York, Sydney; don't go back to Alachua. Move with your family to Vrindavan. Krishna will take care of everything."
- **Prabhupada's letter of November 9, 1975:** *"Anyone of my disciples who can stay permanently in Vrindavan is welcome. I want that as many of my disciples stay permanently here and become Vrajabasis. That would be very inspiring."*
- A humorous caveat: this doesn't mean our parikrama party of 350 will keep doing parikrama for the next 25 or 30 years. Prabhupada also said: for inspiration, then go back and preach. But realistically, at some stage in life you retire — it becomes difficult to bend down, your eyesight goes — and at that point you can come and retire and spend the last days of your life here.

## Prabhupada Is the All and End-All of Our Lives

- The spiritual master is described in the scriptures as the **revealer of the dawn.** For all of us that is Srila Prabhupada — whether as our direct spiritual master or our *shiksha-guru.*
- It is by Prabhupada's mercy that we will understand anything in this Vrindavan Dham. On his invitation we joined the movement; by his mercy we are maintained in it; and by his mercy, under the direction of our various spiritual masters, we will be delivered back to Goloka Vrindavan in the spiritual world. Prabhupada is "the all and end-all of our lives."
- Therefore, for members of ISKCON, the **most holy place in Vrindavan is Prabhupada's samadhi**, his resting place, within his beloved Krishna Balaram Mandir.
- Personally, one of the speaker's first places of interest on coming to Vrindavan is to go to Prabhupada's samadhi and thank him for the opportunity to come here — "where one grain of dust is more valuable than all the opulence of Vaikuntha," where the heart's contamination is easily washed away, and where one can develop *prema* for the Supreme Personality of Godhead. We should remember Srila Prabhupada "every step of the way throughout our parikramas."

## Why Are We at the Temple of Saturn (Shani Dev)?

- "A couple more interesting points." For the managers there is always a competition of mind about where to begin the Vrindavan Parikrama — Seva Kunj, Govardhan, Radha Kund? Vrindavan is full of wonderful holy places.
- Yet we find ourselves here at the **temple of Saturn, Shani Dev** — who gives you your **sadesati**, "seven and a half years of bad luck," two or three times during your life. "What are we doing here?"
- Description of the Shani temple: if you walk in to the altar, you see **Shani** — black, oily, holding bows and arrows and two knives, and sitting on a **crow.**
- People come here on Saturdays to appease Saturn, considered one of the inauspicious planets in astrology.

## Who Is Shani? (Family and Function)

- Shani is infamous as the **older brother of Yamaraj.** Their father is **Surya** (the sun).
- The two brothers: **Yamaraj**, the lord of death, and **Shani.** And they have a little sister, **Yamuna.**
- Shani is in Vrindavan because the brother is supposed to protect the sister — he is here to protect Yamuna.
- Whereas Yamaraj gives the results of your sinful activities in your next life, Shani doesn't wait — he gives the results "the next moment." This is called **instant karma.**

## Seeing the Demigods as Servitors of the Lord

- We understand these personalities as servitors of the Lord. Sometimes they have the thankless task of doing such services on the Lord's behalf. "Somebody's got to do it" — the Lord employs His devotees to administrate the creation rather than put demons in charge who would take over. So He puts the **devatas** in charge; sometimes it's a tough, unattractive job.
- Just like some of these senior men have been **temple presidents** — "the least popular person in ISKCON is a temple president, generally."
  - Joke: Chaturatma was temple president of Alachua for 11 days, then quit — "and as a result he has gray hair, and that's why he dyes it blonde."
- Like **Maya Devi**, who has the thankless task of keeping the conditioned souls in this material world until she sees we are actually sincere about Krishna consciousness; then she lets go, and we're free to serve Krishna to our heart's desire.

## The Nature of Maya (Yogamaya / Mahamaya)

- Prabhupada once told a godbrother who was becoming loose in Krishna consciousness: **"Your problem is that you are not very afraid of Maya."**
- Maya is a powerful personality — but who is she? She is an expansion of **Yogamaya** in the spiritual world, who as **Purnamasi** arranges all the settings for Radha and Krishna's blissful pastimes in the groves of Vrindavan.
- Yogamaya accepts the thankless task of being **Mahamaya** in this world — creating a world of delusion to keep us bewildered — until the moment we say, "Enough! Krishna, help!" Then she sends the devotees, whom we meet "somewhere on the street of St. Petersburg," and we start chanting Hare Krishna.
- The analogy of **Lord Shiva**: an amazing personality — ashes all over his body from the crematorium, snakes and scorpions, smoking ganja without being affected — but ultimately he is **the greatest of all devotees.** *"vaishnavanam yatha shambhuh"* — Shambhu (Shiva) is the greatest of Vaishnavas.
- One of Maya's expansions is **Katyayani**; the gopis go to Katyayani and pray to her to somehow arrange that Krishna may become their husband.

## Shani's Ishtadeva Is Nrisimhadev

- So what relationship does this Saturn — who rides a crow, black and oily — have with Krishna? Very interesting. **Who is Saturn's ishtadeva?**
- Shani never claims to be supreme to his followers. If you read the books and see whom he worships, you understand his personality. His worshipable Lord is **Nrisimhadev.**
- Shani is the author of a famous prayer — the **Nrisimha-stuti** — a number of prayers to Lord Nrisimhadev. And Krishna Himself declares that anyone who chants these prayers to Him as Nrisimhadev "shall never fall under the effect of Saturn."
- **Shani's prayer to Nrisimhadev** (paraphrased): *"My dear Lord Nrisimhadev, You protected Your own devotee, Prahlada, from a raging fire, from being drowned in the deep ocean, from falling from a tall mountain peak, from poison, from a mad elephant, and from the fangs of a poisonous serpent. My dear Lord, You are omnipresent and very generous. Please bestow upon me Your merciful, sidelong glance."*
- This recalls the beautiful prayer the Brajabasis — especially the young girls — chant every day to Radharani when going to fetch water and begin their chores, each stanza concluding: *"My dear Radha, please bestow upon me Your merciful, sidelong glance."*
- So here is Shani saying to Nrisimha: "Just give me a little look, some of Your mercy, to protect me in this world of birth and death."

## Sadesati and Taking Shelter of Krishna

- **Sadesati** is a natural cycle that comes roughly every 30 years — the period of "seven and a half years of bad luck." It doesn't mean everything goes wrong; it's "like a sunny day, but there's a haze" — you don't get the full effect of the sun; things just don't go right, and even in a peaceful situation you feel overwhelmed with concerns.
- **Rasika Siromani** is just coming out of one of her sadesatis; despite doing a wonderful job managing the tour, she's had a few mishaps and bad luck, and will be relieved when it's over.
- **Personal story:** In his second sadesati, as a young boy, the speaker got spinal meningitis, got scarlet fever, and their house burned down — "but I made it. That's Saturn."
- A mundane astrologer who is not a devotee will tell you to wear a *kavacha* to appease Saturn, or wear certain jewels to diminish the planetary effects. "But we know better than that — we just take shelter of Krishna, and we chant Hare Krishna."
- **Bhagavad-gita 18.66** — *"sarva-dharman parityajya mam ekam sharanam vraja"* — Krishna practically says: "Abandon all these nonsense, surrender to Me — I will protect you from Shani, from the immediate person who gives you your bad luck. Just become My devotee" — which in Kali-yuga means becoming a devotee of Lord Chaitanya and chanting Hare Krishna.
- You can also chant prayers to Lord Nrisimhadev — Prabhupada introduced the **Nrisimha prayers** into ISKCON specifically for the protection of this movement, our spiritual master, and the devotees. "That's authorized."

## The Real Reason We Came: This Is a Forest of Vrindavan

- Perhaps the most important reason we have come here today is that this is **one of the forests of Vrindavan.**
- You might think we left Vrindavan and traveled through villages, industry, and garbage — "No." Vrindavan is 80 square miles; its **64 square miles** encompass the entire area — and we are situated right in the heart of Vrindavan.
- Taking some dust from this field is **identical**, on a certain level, to taking dust from Govardhan Hill or Seva Kunj: "It's all absolute, it's all Vrindavan." Although we say some places are holier than others, "that's just the variety of *bhava*." A pinch of dust from that tree — that's Vrindavan.

## "Van" Means Forest — and the Meaning of Vrindavan

- **"Van" means forest.** Originally Vrindavan was just forest — different forests (7, 12, whatever the number), all different places of Krishna's pastimes. For the purpose of agriculture and industry we don't see the forests anymore; it's farmland, factories, or apartment buildings.
- **"Vrindavan" means the forest of Vrinda Devi** — an intimate associate of Radharani. She gave her forest to Radha; so Radharani is **Vrindavaneshwari**, the controller of Vrinda's forest — the perfect place for Krishna to have His pastimes with His cowherd boyfriends and His secret meetings with the gopis.

## Kokilavan and the Cuckoo Birds

- This is **Kokilavan** — named after a particular cuckoo bird, the **kokila.** It's a black bird with brown wings; its favorite tree is the mango tree, and it makes a certain call that "goes higher and higher and higher."
- Nowadays we hardly see any birds here, but 5,000 years ago — or even 100 years ago — there would be lots of mango trees and lots of these kokila birds chirping in their unique way.

## The Pastime of Kokilavan: Krishna and the Cuckoo Birds

- Out of the millions of pastimes that took place here in Kokilavan, one interesting pastime (told now "because we're all getting hungry").
- Not far from here is **Yavat** — the place where **Radharani lives with her husband, Abhimanyu.**
  - In Vedic culture the girls took care of the home and the man took care of the agricultural field; ladies generally didn't leave home except for a little shopping, and then they would go in a palanquin.
  - Because Radharani is "married," quote-unquote, to Krishna's shadow — personified as **Abhimanyu** — she cannot leave Yavat. Although she gave her heart to Krishna (the first thing she saw when she opened her eyes in this world was Krishna, and she fell in love with Him), she cannot leave Yavat to see Him.
  - So Radharani is always thinking how to get out of this prison, and Krishna is always thinking how to get into the prison to see her. There are so many pastimes of getting in and out (to be told at Yavat).
- **Krishna's brilliant idea:** One day He came here to Kokilavan. The acharyas say Krishna resembles a **kokila bird** — a black bird who sings very nicely. He came to enlist the help of all the cuckoo birds to get Radharani to come here.
  - Every living entity in Vrindavan Dham is a self-realized soul, a **parikara** (associate) of Krishna. Even the water buffaloes are part of Krishna's Goloka and have their service. These cuckoo birds are all great enlightened sages who developed the Vrindavan *bhava* and live here to assist Krishna in His pastimes with Radharani.
  - Krishna took His flute and made noises like the cuckoo birds — His flute-playing being the perfection of the cuckoo birds' sounds. Millions of cuckoo birds came, fascinated.
  - Between playing the flute, Krishna would sometimes **sing the glories of Radharani.** The cuckoo birds went mad (Kokilavan being close to Yavat). **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakur** says (as described in the accounts of Krishna's dancing) that the cuckoo birds "totally lost it" — they fell off the branches onto the ground unconscious when Krishna started dancing. All part of Krishna's plan to get Radharani out of the prison of Yavat.

## The Gopis Come to Kokilavan

- As the pastime unfolds, **Jatila** (always nervous about Krishna coming to her home and seeing Radha) heard this beautiful sound and came out onto the rooftop, wondering at the wonderful sound of all the cuckoo birds singing so sweetly.
- The two sakhis, **Vishakha** and **Lalita** — very close associates of Radharani, always trying to facilitate her meeting Krishna — were there. Vishakha thought to herself, "This must be some trick of that rascal boy who steals the hearts."
  - Vishakha said to Jatila: "Something must be going on — some great occasion, some festival, everyone's rejoicing. Maybe I should go see." Jatila: "Yes, go see what's happening."
  - Vishakha: "But I'm a young gopi girl; it's not proper for a young girl to go alone anywhere — she must be accompanied by her father, brothers, or husband. But a group of young girls can go together. Let me take Radha, Lalita, and all the other gopis." Jatila: "Very good — for your protection, take my daughter-in-law and go to see what is happening."
  - Radharani just smiled, opened the door — "and she's out of the prison."
- They came toward Kokilavan accompanied by the sound of Krishna's flute, which melts the hearts of the gopis; immersed in divine love, they fainted at every step, so it took them a long time.
- Finally they came to a place where a little grove was completely surrounded by cuckoo birds singing very nicely. They broke through the branches, and there was Krishna underneath the tree playing His flute, "something like a kokila bird."
- When all the cuckoo birds saw Radharani, they knew their service had come to an end — they had facilitated Radha and Krishna's meeting — so they immediately stopped, waiting to see what would happen next.
- As Radha and Krishna came together in that grove for their transcendental pastimes, out of shyness all the cuckoo birds flew away, and even the **sakhis** could not stay (they are not allowed to see the most intimate loving pastimes) — only the **manjaris** stayed, facilitating the young gopi-manjaris.
- Radha and Krishna had Their wonderful pastimes in one of the **kunjas** (groves) of Vrindavan. Since that time this place has been called **Kokilavan.**

## "This Is What We Do" — Our Parikrama Formula

- By Prabhupada's invitation, here we are. **"This is what we do."** We go on parikrama; we drive out here; we have bhajan with Madhava; after breakfast, bhajan with Bada Hari Prabhu; we have katha, take a little prasad. "This is what we're going to do for the next month."
- It's a little different from the old days, when devotees would run around trying to see all the holy places of Vrindavan in one month before returning to their sankirtan duties in the West.
  - The old idea was to see as many holy places as possible in 30 days — "even if you didn't know what the place was called, if you just saw it." ("How was your trip to India?" "Very nice." "What did you see?" "I don't remember.")
- The way it has developed now: we pick a few places (about **30 places** over the month). We go there, have kirtan, get into the mood, have hari-katha and prasad, more kirtan, and are back home by the afternoon. "It's not too difficult. This is our mood."

## Seeing Through the Ears — The Vrindavan Experience

- **"The saintly person sees through his ears, not through his eyes."** The glories of Sri Vrindavan Dham may not necessarily be seen through your eyes.
- But if you enter into the mood of the kirtan nicely, chant from the heart, and remain in a very submissive mood — trying to relish the *katha* so that your *prema* for Radha and Krishna may one day be awakened — then you'll truly get the Vrindavan experience we're all hankering for.
- "We have organized this Parikrama to serve your Vrindavan *bhava.* So stick with us. This is your Parikrama."
- According to **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakur**, there are **60 billion holy places** in Vrindavan. You can't see them all anyway; you'll get the most out of your trip if you stick with us.

## Resist the Enticements — "Stick With Us"

- There will be lots of enticements: "Did you hear? There's this special temple you haven't heard about — Indradyumna Swami's parikrama isn't going there, but you should come see it." **Don't go.** Come to see Shani Dev in Kokilavan with all of us. "This is what we do."
- "There's this sadhu who lives over here — you could come see him." **No, stick with us.** "This is what we do."
- **Shopping** should be last on your list of activities, not first. **Bhagavad-gita 9.22** — *"yoga-kshemam vahamy aham"* — Krishna says whatever you lack, "I will supply it Myself."
- This is India — going on parikrama is austere — but the austerities are minimal compared to the very rich, deep, devotional experience you'll carry back to your home countries by visiting the sacred places in our group.

## Practical Instructions for Surviving in India

- **Don't miss the bus.** Rasika's management means being punctual — the bus pulls away at six o'clock. If you're left in the dust, "okay, it's Brajarenu," but you'll miss all these nectarean kirtans and katha.
- **Cold weather:** In about 15 days it will start to get cold. Put on your jackets and sweaters. You don't want to catch sick in India, because a cold here means a flu, and a flu means your immune system goes down and you can get a serious disease.
- **Cuts and bites:** If you get even the tiniest cut, or scratch a mosquito bite and it gets infected, take care of it immediately — antibiotic cream (neosporin), a band-aid. "In India these things expand very quickly."
- **The sun:** Be very careful of the sun (**Surya Bhagavan**). Keep your heads covered; the sun can be very fierce here.
- **If you get sick / diarrhea:** Don't freak out — it happens. Come see Rasika; she can take on unlimited responsibilities and will direct you to a proper doctor — allopathic, ayurvedic, or mystic, whatever you want — to get you back on parikrama as quickly as possible.
- **Animals — to be seen, not touched:** ("Children are to be seen, not heard" — so in Vrindavan animals are to be seen but not touched.)
  - **Cows** may look pretty but can be in an angry mood; you can be gored. "Prabhupada was gored by a cow in Vrindavan."
  - **Dogs** look cute but can bite, have moods, or have rabies — don't pet them.
  - **Monkeys** are just rascals: "Don't trust a person who can't control his senses — that's a monkey." Don't pet or talk to them; keep any fruit hidden or they'll grab it.
  - Monkeys love glasses — they put them on and look very intelligent. "I have lost at least 44 pairs of glasses in the last 44 years," especially in downtown Vrindavan. They used to give the glasses back for a banana; nowadays "they take the banana, crush the glasses, and laugh."
- **Water and food:** Drink only bottled water; don't drink from the taps or the shower. Drink a lot of water (dehydration makes you sick), but don't overeat (digestion isn't strong here, especially the first few times) — better to under-eat than over-eat; try not to eat late at night. ("These are things we have learned by experience, not from Shastra.") Don't eat any food outside what we provide, or what the temple provides, or the MVT — samosas or sweets from elsewhere can make you sick for weeks.
- **Safety of the ladies:** Girls should not go anywhere alone — go three or four together. This is the land of Krishna, gopis, cows, and the Yamuna, "but there are criminals here" (drawn by money and power).
  - There have been recent instances where young Western women have disappeared — the sex trade, kidnapping and selling young girls; this is happening all over the world, and Vrindavan is not an exception.
  - A 20-year-old Australian girl visiting the Banke-Bihari temple with her family disappeared about three weeks ago.
  - Not to make anyone scared or paranoid — this is an isolated instance — "but this is not a first-world country; it's still like a third-world country," and not everybody has good intentions. The ladies should stick together in small groups; the safest place is on the parikrama party. **"Our safety is in our numbers, and our spiritual deliverance is also based on the mass congregational chanting of Hare Krishna."**

## Get to Bed on Time — Don't Sleep in Class

- Try to get to bed on time, because we leave early every morning. If you come tired, you fall asleep — "I'd say 30% of the audience was sleeping during class this morning."
- **Personal story:** Prabhupada once chastised the speaker for sleeping "right in front of where Madhava now sits." He had been working very hard and fell asleep; Prabhupada yelled, **"Why are you sleeping when I'm speaking?"** The speaker woke, speechless. Prabhupada said, **"Never again will you sleep when I'm speaking."** "I said, 'Yes, Prabhupada.' And I never slept again in class."

## Our Purpose: Awaken Love for the Divine Couple

- We are here for a purpose — to awaken our love for the divine couple, Radha and Krishna — and that will gradually happen as a result of kirtan and hari-katha.
- Evening time is when people socialize, "but we're here for an express purpose," and we have a wonderful medium: our parikrama. Anything you do should be connected to it. So go to bed a little early, get up in time, and be alert to take advantage of all this nectar over the next month.

## This Is What We Do — Sitting for Kirtan and Katha

- Basically, for the next month, your main activity will be **sitting** — many hours a day, while we have kirtan and katha. "This is what we do in Vrindavan."
- If the Goswamis could sit — **Raghunath Das Goswami** could sit for 22 hours a day and chant Hare Krishna — we can sit four, five, or six hours every day for kirtan and hari-katha without becoming restless.
- Just by sitting with such a great soul in spiritual practice, **Bhagavad-gita 2.59** — *"param drishtva nivartate"* — by tasting the higher taste (the *param drishtva*) we give up the lower. When we leave this place we'll be different people: some of our material attachments will weaken, and to a greater or lesser degree our *bhava* / love for Radha and Krishna will strengthen. So we need to make this sitting posture for *shravanam* and *kirtanam* — "because this is a process."
