## Clarifying His Absence: Chastisement Is a Service

- "It's not that I didn't want to come here." Something came out that may have seemed like displeasure, but "I wasn't displeased with anyone."
  - He had chastised a disciple (Gurungi), "and she took it."
- **The duty of the spiritual master is to chastise:** "If he just tells us how nicely we're doing, we'll never make the attempt to correct ourselves and get out of here."
  - Prabhupada told us several times that **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** was more chastising.
  - But you can only chastise senior disciples "whose faith is not broken." They cry a lot, it's hard, "but then they rise to the challenge — which you did. And I will continue to chastise them."
- "It's not that I don't want to be here with you." He *wants* to be with his disciples and godbrothers and godsisters, because this is one of the first *yatras*, with many senior devotees present — "a very successful yatra."

## More Responsibilities With Time — Chanting as Prayer for Service

- "As time goes on, I have more responsibilities. I can only attribute that to chanting and begging for service for 43 years."
- When we chant **Hare Krishna**, it is *not* merely to finish our quota of rounds and leave the rest up to Krishna.
- More than a mantra (a sound vibration that purifies the heart), it is **like a prayer.**
  - **Prayer (vandanam) is one of the nine authorized processes of devotional service:** *śravaṇaṁ, kīrtanaṁ, viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṁ, pāda-sevanaṁ, arcanaṁ, vandanaṁ, dāsyaṁ, sakhyaṁ, ātma-nivedanam* **(Srimad Bhagavatam 7.5.23)**.
- It is a prayer from the heart, asking the Lord for service — service we have been bereft of "since time immemorial," meaning ever since we fell from grace, from the spiritual world.
- **Madhava Prabhu's famous quote,** expressed emphatically in each kirtan — "Everybody now, a little louder, from the heart" — captures the essence of Krishna consciousness: **a heartfelt prayer.** (Aside: "Now I can say it, because I'm in England.")
- A heartfelt prayer that we are *desperate for service*, because for so long we have been **go-dasa, not gosvami:**
  - *Go-dasa* means **servant of the senses.** Our senses and minds — our material desires — have "dragged us mercilessly over the coals of material existence, from time immemorial. And we're tired of it."
  - "We surrender. Like **Draupadi**, finally." (When Krishna's help was needed, she put up two words / surrendered to Krishna.)

## Overcoming the Fear of "Service" — The Merciful Master

- We are not hesitant to accept service. But initially, when we first contact Krishna consciousness and hear the word "service to God," it may sound a little repugnant — "only because our masters in this world have been merciless."
  - They have abused us, mistreated us, dealt with us unkindly.
  - **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** said material life means: "You put your head on my lap to take shelter — and I cut your throat." Merciless masters we have had.
  - So many authorities in whom we placed our faith were mainly interested in their own selfish desires (though of course, even in material life, we do meet some kind people).
- But when we contact our **merciful master** — who reciprocates our insignificant service, done with even "a half drop of devotion," so benevolently — then we know what service really is. His reciprocation comes as:
  - the **holy name**, the **shastras**, the **holy dhamas**, **good association**, **prasada**, and a **bright future.**
- When we learn to serve **Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava**, we know the real joy of service.
  - Prabhupada's statement: **"Faith means to put your trust in something or someone sublime."** And then we surrender more.

## Chanting in Knowledge — "Knock, Knock on Heaven's Door"

- "After so many years, since 1970, this aspiring devotee..." *(chants Hare Krishna)* — consciously and unconsciously, still: "if someone knocks on your door long enough, I'm going to answer, 'Who's there?'"
  - There's a famous song, "Knock, Knock, Knock on Heaven's Door" — "I never heard it, but I heard about it." That's the **Maha Mantra.**
- **We must be in knowledge of *why* we are chanting** — that's why there are so many *japa* seminars now: so we don't chant mechanically, but from the heart.
- We are not afraid to ask for service, because we know intellectually it is our **constitutional position.**
- And we know that by taking shelter of **Hari** (He who removes all inauspiciousness), **Guru,** and **Vaishnava**, it is a *real* shelter.

## The Beach at Juhu: The Dog With a Master

- Prabhupada's walk on the beach at **Juhu**: he saw dogs, not playing but **fighting** over some scraps on the ground — not enough to go around. They were skinny, with mange and disease.
- Then there was another dog **on a leash** (common in India today): a big, fluffy, white dog with a collar — "it had a master. He was fat and content."
- Prabhupada asked: "What is the difference between those dogs and that white fluffy one?" The devotees didn't catch it at first.
  - Prabhupada: "The difference between those scruffy dogs and the happy dog is that **the happy dog has a master.**"
- We learn this in Krishna consciousness — how fortunate we become; our spiritual master, the Vaishnavas, and the Lord are **the source of all auspiciousness** for us.

## The Process Works — Malati and "Do You Think I Would Cheat You?"

- "After some time, the process must work."
- **Malati's question to Prabhupada:** "Prabhupada, is it really true that there is a planet of cows — the pasturing grounds of the cows — where God, the almighty God, is a nine-year-old boy with long, cascading black hair, eyes like lotus petals reaching to His ears, lips red like the **bimba** fruit, teeth set like rows of pearls, who plays the flute and charms little girls? Is that really true?"
  - Prabhupada bowed his head, thought for a moment, and said: **"Do you think that I would cheat you?"**
  - Having faith — not so much knowing Krishna, but knowing her Guru Maharaj — she replied about how kind he was and how much mercy she had received. That was enough.
- So it is true: after some time the process works — even if you are resistant, it still works in the course of time.
- **The medicine analogy:** medicine received from a bona fide physician works regardless of who you are — king or pauper, intelligent or fool, man or woman, "black, white, yellow, green." The medicine works.
- So **Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement** — the chanting of the holy name of Krishna — is for each and everyone. We advance in different stages: some quickly, some less quickly. But if we adhere to the process, in due course of time it will work.

## A Sign of Advancement: More Responsibility (Agarwal & Sons)

- One sign that the process is working: **we get more responsibility in Krishna consciousness.** There are many ways to gauge advancement; this is one.
- **The business analogy:** a sign of advancement is that your father entrusts the business to you. In India you walk by and it says **"Agarwal and Sons"** — "Patel and Sons," "Agarwal and Sons." The father grew old and, with faith, entrusted the work to his sons.
  - Just like "these two boys in particular — good boys, good devotees. I meet a lot of youth, but these two are serious about Krishna consciousness." One plays excellent **mridanga**, sings nicely, speaks nicely — "and they're so young."
- So as we grow older, our **spiritual father** may, out of his kindness, entrust us with more responsibility. That is no small thing.
- **The highest responsibility is the spiritual welfare work of Krishna consciousness.**
  - We are sometimes impressed with **material welfare** — rightfully so; anyone with a soft heart can appreciate how the downtrodden are helped in various ways.
  - But Prabhupada was very firm: **the real welfare work for society is spiritual welfare**, because ultimately it gives people the opportunity for Krishna consciousness, whereby all problems are solved by going back home, back to Godhead.
  - **Bhagavad Gita 8.21** — *"yad gatvā na nivartante tad dhāma paramaṁ mama"*: "Having once come home, you never come back."

## Preaching Is the Lord's Own Work — Entrusted to Intimate Servants

- To be entrusted with the work of **spreading the movement** — "this is a missionary movement," as I always say. That's what I observed in my Guru Maharaj, **Srila Prabhupada.**
  - Whoever he is in the spiritual world — a very intimate associate of the Lord — as I know him here, and as I meditate on him until I join him by his grace, his work in this world was **continuing the missionary activities** of the previous acharyas: **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura, Gaurakisora dasa Babaji, Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura, Narottama dasa, Srinivasa, Syamananda,** and **Gauranga Mahaprabhu** Himself.
- That is the work of the Lord when He comes to this world in this age: **preaching activities.**
- It is so important that the Lord only entrusts it to His **most intimate servants.**
- **The example of Jesus (the "anointed one"):** In Christianity we refer to Jesus as the *chosen one.* **"Christ" means "the anointed one"** — not just Jesus, but Jesus *the Christ.* God has many sons (a father like God does not have only one son), but this son was special.
  - God gave Jesus the service of coming to the Middle East and, against all opposition, beginning the spiritual revolution required at that time in history. He paid the heavy price with his own life.
  - "The Lord gave His only begotten son" — not that he is the *only* begotten son, but the son dear enough to the Lord that He could entrust him with the mission.
- So if we get some responsibility in Krishna consciousness, it means the Lord has some confidence in us — "maybe we're doing something right." You see this with all older devotees: they have serious responsibilities.

## Trying to Slow Down in Vrindavan — But Always Preaching

- "It wasn't that I didn't want to come" — but "I'm getting older now, everyone's always telling me to slow down, take rest. So I tried."
- **Vrindavan:** "I went to Vrindavan, I tried to be a Babaji" — but even there, "I was out on Parikrama every day."
  - "Where do you think all those photographs came from? Sitting under a banyan tree chanting all day? No — I was running around 'kicking,' as they say in India."
  - (Aside on the camera: devotees say, "How many *clicks* have you taken today, Swami? I like your snaps." "It's like an old Kodak camera — *schnapps.* It's not a Canon 5D Mark II; it doesn't take 'clicks'; it's a digital camera, it costs a lot.")
- The photography doesn't come from sitting, but from **the desire to share Vraja with the world.** Like the expensive camera, the expensive land, so much time invested — "it's just another *anga*, another arm of sharing."
  - "I never thought of becoming a photographer. I still don't know how it works. I just aim, shoot, point, shoot. You add a little something after it. It inspires the audience."
- So even in Vrindavan, this mood is for **preaching purposes.** Vrindavan is for **inspiration.**

## The Idea to Preach — and Sharing What Is Special

- "We had the idea to preach in Italy. Because when you have something nice, and a lot of it, you want to share it. **It's all about sharing.**"
- "Actually we were getting accustomed to living that simple life there. It was so nice."
- Prabhupada was so kind — he took us to **Bharat**: to **Vrindavan, Mayapur,** and **Jagannath Puri.** There is something special about these places; it grows on you after a while, and you become very grateful.

## Ujjain: The School of Sandipani Muni and the Real India

- On the invitation of **Bhakti Charu Maharaj**, "I went down to **Ujjain**" for a wedding, then started exploring, because it is very historic for **Gaudiya Vaishnavas:**
  - It is where the **school of Sandipani Muni** was — where **Krishna and Balaram** went to school.
  - "After the wedding I thought, I want to see the school of Sandipani Muni, where Krishna and Balaram learned in the **gurukula.** I read the chapter in the **Krishna book.**"
  - "It was such an amazing experience — history just came alive. It's so well preserved, everything's still there. I was sitting in the classroom, looking at Sandipani Muni's deities, remembering the pastimes."
- **The great Shiva temple / Jyotirlinga:** There are, "I think, **12 Jyotirlingas** — self-manifested Shiva lingams — in India, and that's one of the main places."
  - The **Shiva Bhaktas** were so kind: they took me inside, put my head on the **lingam,** and said, "Say some prayer to your Krishna."
  - Remembering **"vaiṣṇavānāṁ yathā śambhuḥ"** — that **Shiva is the greatest devotee.** He is one of the **five protectors of the Dham**, one of the five personalities who grants entrance into the real mood of the Dham and the mood of the **Vrajavasis.**
- **The riverbank — the real India:** Walking down to the riverbank, "it was like the 1800s. It hadn't changed — old temples, cows, sadhus, the river clean and flowing, parrots chirping. I thought, *this is India.* You get out of Bombay, Delhi, Calcutta — this is the real India, still there, still available to us by Prabhupada's mercy."
  - "I was thinking I could just pick up the loincloth on the bank, take a stick, and walk around this country for the rest of my life, doing **Madhukari.**"
  - "I even tried — I went to one door in Nogda and said, 'Ma, hungry, roti.' 'Oh, come, come, eat 10 rotis!' I can't do that every day, I'll get too fat."
- **The elephant analogy:** This is the beauty of **Vedic culture** — the remnants are still there. "The elephant, living or dead, is still valuable: even when he's dead, his skin is valuable, his tusks are valuable. So even though Vedic culture is practically dead, it's still appreciable, still there."

## Praying to Lord Shiva: "Do Both Here" — The Festival Tour to India

- Sitting on the bank next to a small Shiva temple shrine, "I was praying to Lord Shiva," torn between two desires:
  1. To follow his **spiritual master's instruction** to preach boldly and have faith in the holy names until death ("I can't say I have fulfilled the instruction yet").
  2. His **heart's desire to be in Bharat**, where he feels so natural, with so much inspiration to read, to chant, and to go deeper.
- "I said, praying to Lord Shiva, why did I do this? I either stay here or I go there." All of a sudden the thought came: **"No — not here or there. Why don't you just do both here?"**
  - "I thought, 'Thank you, Lord Shiva' — not that Shiva said something inspired; it was just so coincidental. I'm thinking: **bring the festival tour to India.**"
- **Was that what Prabhupada wanted? Yes.** In **1972**, Prabhupada wrote that **every temple should give three good men or ladies to his preaching in India.** He wanted **Western-body devotees to preach in India.**
- **Why — preserving Vedic culture:** Prabhupada wanted to preserve Vedic culture "in case the rest of the world went to hell."
  - "Knock on wood," suppose the movement at some point in **Kali Yuga** were no longer successful — because sometimes things appear and disappear like the **Saraswati** river; sometimes the preaching is manifest, sometimes not.
  - If Krishna consciousness became extinguished, but was *also* extinguished in India, "that would be the greatest calamity."
  - Prabhupada wanted **the pilot light to always be burning in India**, so that, just in case, it could always be reignited in the **land of Dharma.**
  - His idea was to revive Vedic culture so the rest of the world could eventually benefit. Just as the culture — once ruling the whole world, Krishna conscious — was lost for thousands of years and disappeared, but because it was still present in Bharat, it could be revived again.
  - It is quickly disappearing in India now, so Prabhupada wanted Westerners — whom he called **"dancing white elephants."**

## Respect for the Sadhu — Three Hours to Walk 20 Minutes

- "I stood up and walked into town, and all the people were paying obeisances to me — not to me, but to a **sannyasi.** Just see: a white man, dressed as a sadhu, and people have respect."
  - They came asking: "Sir, where are you from? Are you Vaishnava?" "Yes." "Who is your guru? What is his teaching?"
  - "It took me three hours to get back to the temple, 20 minutes away. So I think: yes, this is **Mahaprabhu's desire.**"

## One Thing Led to Another: Gujarat, and Then China

- **Gujarat:** "I had Nandini and Jayatam fly to India. We chose **Gujarat.**" (Aside: "How many of you are Gujaratis?")
  - They made a plan for the entire months of **January and February** — Rajkot, Allahabad, Baroda, and more.
  - "I started learning Gujarati — *Kem cho?* 'How are you?' — to prepare."
  - And Krishna / Mahaprabhu arranges it so "it doesn't cost me one pound," whereas usually you'd have to spend the whole year traveling and collecting for those two months.
  - His godbrother **Yashomatinandana Prabhu** and **Basu Ghosh** called all the temple presidents. One stood up and said: "Indradyumna Maharaj wants to bring his [festival]; we will facilitate him by providing all the finance and all the organization. All he has to do is walk in with his team and do the show. And then — die."
  - "So I came to rest, but I got *more* service."
- **China / Hong Kong:** "Then I went to China, to Hong Kong. I'm always resisting going into the mainland, but this time the devotees who came were so sweet, begging me with tears in their eyes, 'Please, Guru Maharaj, please come.' And one time I just gave in — and it escalated into another festival program."
  - "Not the same way — we have to be a little careful, but things are changing a little." So they'll take a cultural program with 10 or 15 devotees; "Dina Dayal will show them what is real, have dancing, and the lecture will be toned down, if I can do it."

## Why He Came Back: One Service Not at the Expense of Another

- "All of a sudden I'm thinking that I came to rest, and my service *multiplied.*"
- So, having so much service, he thought he might cut back on something — "not because I didn't want to come, but because I have so many responsibilities."
- **But then the loving letters came:** "I started getting all these wonderful letters from my disciples, and I was reminded that one of my services should not be at the expense of another service."
  - **"When the tide rises, all the boats are elevated to the same level."**
  - A spiritual master has many responsibilities, all in this regard of preaching. But "after reading your letters, I could not put all the energy into these programs at the expense of continuing to inspire my disciples here in England. So I came back."
- "And I am very glad I did, because I could see this afternoon how much progress you are making."

## A Vaishnava Is Understood When He or She Speaks

- "I always say this at the **Vyasa Puja** ceremonies: a Vaishnava can be understood when he or she speaks."
  - Not that one must be an eloquent or trained speaker, or take classes in public speaking — but when devotees speak, **they speak from the heart.**
- **Because Krishna consciousness is all about the heart:**
  - developing spiritual relationships with the devotees;
  - finding real friendship in Krishna consciousness;
  - a relationship with one's spiritual master;
  - going deeper into the science of Krishna consciousness;
  - and ultimately, developing a very deep and loving relationship with God.
- "So at moments like this, we really do speak from the heart. And I was very much impressed with the offerings today — very mature, seasoned, and realized."

## Life Is Not Easy — But We Face It in a Krishna Conscious Way

- "Life is not easy. Even though we are devotees, we still live in this world." We are not free from **birth, disease, old age, and death**; we also go through these miseries — "but we do so in a Krishna conscious way."
- We have the **shelter of the teachings of Guru and Gauranga**, which helps us deal with those things and, if nothing else, gives us the faith that there is Krishna.
- After this life, if our faith in the Lord has developed and matured, we can go back home, back to Godhead.
- We can tolerate the miseries of material existence, knowing the reality of Krishna consciousness. "Everything here is like the summer and winter seasons — it comes and goes. But **Krishna consciousness is the constant reality**," always there for us in the form of **Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava.**
- We may deviate, get captured by Maya again, and get burnt. Then we remember Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava, and come back to that shelter.

## The Spiritual Master Doesn't Forget — The Lighthouse

- Sometimes disciples go and may forget. **But the spiritual master doesn't forget.**
  - Like a child who leaves home and forgets the parents — the parents don't forget, because they are older and more mature. "The nature of children is to be a little naughty and forget; parents are mature."
- When I see disciples who — not that they've gone, but who may have struggled in Krishna consciousness, "cried an ocean of tears," and maybe made some mistakes — offer such beautiful offerings of love and appreciation for the **steadfast spiritual master who doesn't deviate**, who is always there to set the example and show how nice it is to be a devotee...
- **The lighthouse / Rock of Gibraltar:** the spiritual master is like a **beacon**, a **lighthouse** in stormy weather — firm, its light always shining. He must be like the **Rock of Gibraltar** in the midst of the storm.
  - It's not that, being the spiritual master, he does not experience birth, disease, old age, and death — he does. But because of his faith, he remains firm, and even in the midst of the greatest calamity he is still happy in Krishna consciousness.
  - **Bhagavad Gita 6.22** — there is a verse like that, where even in the midst of the greatest calamity the *yogi* remains firm, because he sees that the storm will pass but the lighthouse will always stay.
  - "We have one place we do our program in **Poland** where there is a lighthouse — nowadays we do our program right by it. The storms have come and gone, people have come and gone, war has come and gone — the lighthouse is still there."

## Where the Strength Comes From — Weathered Faith

- "If one wants to accept the service — because being a spiritual master is a **service to one's guru** — where does one get that strength? It's no mystery." *(chants Hare Krishna)*
- "When I hear the offerings like today, I see all the disciples who have weathered the storm — been through thick and thin. The wrinkles are there now, the gray hair is coming. We're not young anymore, weathered by life — but through all that weathering, we have this **precious jewel of faith** in Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava."
- "I'm very pleased to see that — just that you're still here says a lot."
- **His pledge:** "And I'll be here for you. Always. That's my duty to my Guru Maharaj. I took this service, so I will be here for you — like the lighthouse — and to smile even in the midst of the greatest calamity, knowing that the future is very bright in Krishna consciousness."
- If we just continue as best we can, following the process, especially chanting Hare Krishna.

## The Worried Parent — His Gift of Steadfastness

- When a child struggles through school or social life, the parents worry and become more attached. "So it's like that in Krishna consciousness — when I see disciples struggling, I worry more. But what can I do? I just have to be a good example and continue preaching the process."
- "I think **Kurma** was saying that it's not that the spiritual master necessarily has to deal with each and every problem, be a psychologist, or deal with married problems. We do some things, we don't do others."
- "Right now I'm too busy spearheading the movement in **India and China** with the festival. So perhaps you'll see me less — but you always know that I'm there and I do care."
- **His gift to the disciples:** "that steadfastness, and that example — that if you follow in the footsteps, then you'll also be happy in Krishna consciousness."

## Closing: The Vyasa Puja Season and Loving Exchanges

- "I thank you for the offerings — they were very beautiful. Actually, every disciple should write, if not speak, on the **Vyasa Puja.**"
  - "This is a little early — my Vyasa Puja is late May. Someone called this the 'warm-up Vyasa Puja.' No, it's a *real* Vyasa Puja, but it's like a warm-up, because you have six of them" — even one in June, after his birthday.
  - "I hope all the rest of them are like this. This was very sweet, and you endeared me to you by all the offerings."
- **His promise:** "I look forward to coming back again next year. I promise you I'll come every year. Vyasa Puja! And then if we have to do something in Alaska, or the Arctic — an Eskimo Vaishnava too — we'll fly from London to the Antarctic."
- "It's been a very beautiful day. I'm looking forward to tomorrow — we also have nice programs."
- **On being with disciples:** "I like being with anybody and everybody... but there's some special nectar in being with the disciples. We're like a **family**, working together through all the problems — our problems and the world's problems — and working in that way makes us very close."
- "Each and every one of you is very dear to me. I love you very much. And I look forward to getting back home, back to Godhead, and enjoying these loving exchanges with **Sri Prabhupada** and **Radha and Krishna.** Hare Krishna."
