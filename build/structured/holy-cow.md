- Whenever Maharaja sees these deities (**Radha-Damodara**), he remembers **Vishnujana Maharaja.**
- Story: On the way to Europe (London, then Paris), the devotees were fortunate to fly with Srila Prabhupada from New York to London and on to Paris. They stopped at the **Henry Street temple** in New York, where Prabhupada was visiting.
  - Vishnujana Maharaja took these very deities out on **Harinam Sankirtan** in the streets of New York — on a big palanquin carried by four big muscular boys, right downtown, the deities in full glory and beautiful outfits, incense billowing, with chamara fans and peacock fans.
  - Maharaja led an amazing kirtan; people were stunned, stopping to look, throwing money, bowing their heads. "Radha and Krishna were just penetrating into the darkness of ignorance of Kali Yuga."
  - They stopped and had **arati for Radha-Damodara right in the middle of downtown New York.** Now the deities reside here very beautifully and regally.

## The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 4.11

- Reading from **Bhagavad-gita As It Is**, translation and commentary by His Divine Grace Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada — **Chapter 4, "Transcendental Knowledge," text 11.**
- **Bhagavad-gita 4.11** — **"ye yatha mam prapadyante tams tathaiva bhajamy aham / mama vartmanuvartante manusyah partha sarvasah"** — "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly. Everyone follows My path in all respects, O son of Pritha."
- **Purport (read):** Everyone is searching for Krishna in the different aspects of His manifestations. Krishna is partially realized in His impersonal **brahmajyoti** effulgence and as the all-pervading **Supersoul** dwelling within everything, including the atoms; but He is fully realized only by His pure devotees. Thus Krishna is the object of everyone's realization, and everyone is satisfied according to one's desire to have Him. In the transcendental world Krishna reciprocates with His pure devotees just as the devotee wants Him — one wants Him as supreme master, another as personal friend, another as son, another as lover — and He rewards them according to their intensities of love. The same reciprocations occur in the material world with different types of worshippers. Pure devotees, both here and in the transcendental abode, associate with Him in person and render personal service, deriving transcendental bliss.

## Reciprocation Is Proportional to Surrender

- The famous point of this verse: **the Lord reciprocates with His devotees.**
- Srila Prabhupada once said the Lord is **more eager that we return to the spiritual world than we are** — and He is always ready to give Himself fully. Yet His loving reciprocation is **in proportion to our surrender:**
  - If a devotee is only 25% surrendered, the Lord gives 25% of His love. There are different stages of Krishna consciousness in which the Lord reciprocates proportionately.

## The Path of Devotional Service (Sraddha to Prema)

- The path is nicely outlined in the shastras:
  - **First stage:** by chance or good fortune, we come into the **association of devotees** and develop some **sraddha** (faith) — the wish to give up material life and embrace Krishna consciousness.
  - We then practice **bhajana-kriya** (the rules and regulations) strictly under the guidance of the spiritual master.
  - **"Ceto-darpana-marjanam"** (Shikshashtaka, verse 1) — the heart becomes clean.
  - In that clean heart, freed from nasty desires like lust, anger, and greed, the pure seed of devotion — the **bhakti-lata-bija** — grows nicely.
- **Analogy — Pennsylvania soil:** Driving through the countryside, the soil is very dark and rich; Pennsylvania is famous for its farms. Spring has sprung; devotees will plant, and in summer and autumn harvest the crops, vegetables, and fruits. Good soil for planting the seed and reaping results. Likewise, when the heart is clean of the **anarthas** (unwanted things), the pure seed of bhakti — eternally present in the heart of every living entity — begins to grow.
- As we advance from **sraddha to prema** — from a little faith and curiosity to the stage of feeling Krishna's separation — Krishna reciprocates accordingly at each stage.
  - **Shikshashtaka, verse 7** (paraphrased): "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I consider a moment to be like twelve years or more; tears flow from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I feel all vacant in the world in Your absence."

## Seeing Krishna: From Archa-Vigraha to Direct Vision

- We cannot see Him at the beginning, though by His mercy we can see Him in His **archa-vigraha** (deity) form — He is so kind that He appears in so-called matter, still being Krishna.
- When the senses are purified and we awaken the senses of our spiritual body (**siddha-deha**), our original form, then — **"premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilochanena"** (Brahma-samhita 5.38) — we see Krishna directly, just as you now see Maharaja sitting here.
- **Description of Krishna** whom you will see: His **tri-bhanga-sundara** (threefold-bending) form, long black flowing hair, lotus eyes, lips red like the **bimba fruit**, teeth set like rows of pearls, three lines in His neck, a little roll on His tummy, His deep navel, His **vaijayanti garland** of five kinds of flowers extending down to His knees.
- When the heart is completely pure and you are **100% surrendered**, Krishna gives Himself 100% to you. "If we really want 100% of Krishna's love, we have to work on giving 100% of our love to the Lord."

## Examples of the Lord's Intimate Reciprocation

We often hear of devotees in the Bhagavatam and Chaitanya-charitamrita whom the Lord reciprocates with in such an intimate way, and we think, "I'd like to be like that." Several examples follow.

### Hanuman's Embrace

- After the battle of Lanka between Ravana and Ram, Ram won — killing the great asura with an arrow through his chest, sending him to the abode of Yamaraja.
- The Lord assembled His monkey warriors, bear warriors, servants, and devotees. He was especially happy with **Hanumanji**, such a surrendered servant, who practically single-handedly rescued Sita — burning down the capital city of Lanka, fighting the soldiers, and chastising Ravana (telling him Ram would come and rescue His consort).
- Ram called Hanuman forward: **"Hanuman, what can I give you in reciprocation for all the service you've done?"**
- Hanuman has the **dasya** (servitorship) relationship, because **Ayodhya is in Vaikuntha**. Vaikuntha is a little different from Goloka (the topmost planet, Vrindavan) — it is **aishvarya-bhava**, a mood of reverence.
- So Hanuman said, **"Lord, could You give me a hug?"** That is all he wanted — a hug.
  - A Vaishnava considers that embrace of the Lord more valuable than all the opulence of the three worlds combined. Put all the opulence of **Bhur, Bhuvah, and Svah** on one side of the scale, and a single embrace of the Lord on the other — the embrace far outweighs it.
- In the West they think God is all-great, all-powerful, there for judgment day. But in Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding He reciprocates in an **intimate, loving way** — in dasya, the master puts his hand on the servant's head, rewards him, even embraces him.
- To be like Hanuman, you must be surrendered like Hanuman. **"It's nothing sentimental. This is real devotion, and you have to work on it."** The **Brahma-samhita** says we make advancement in **small increments** — little steps forward; it is a long process.

### The Fruit-Vendor Lady

- Krishna wanted some fruits from the lady with the fruit basket in Vrindavan. She said, "You're so cute, such a darling, so enchanting — take whatever you want."
- To reciprocate with her offering, Krishna turned all her fruits into **jewels.**
- A fruit-seller on a street corner in an Indian village makes only a few paise — but when she looked down after her offering, her whole fruit basket was full of jewels. "Oh, I'd like to be that type of devotee Krishna would receive." *(Cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Tenth Canto — the fruit vendor.)*

### The Cowherd Boys and Aghasura

- The cowherd boys were rescued from the belly of **Aghasura.** His belly was very smelly; when he breathed, his breath smelled like old fish (he chewed no gum to freshen it) — a breath that would knock you over and leave you unconscious.
- The cowherd boys went into his belly, playing in his tummy, and he was about to gobble them up — finished. But Krishna jumped into the belly and **expanded Himself like a balloon**, until Aghasura got so big he popped. No more Aghasura. "Oh, I'd like Krishna to rescue me."

### Full Surrender and Its Guarantee

- If you are fully surrendered — **atma-nivedanam**, the culmination of the **nine processes of devotional service** — then: if Krishna wants to save you, no one can kill you; and if Krishna wants to kill you, no one can save you. This is a level of very full surrender.
- To receive that kind of attention, we must have the same love as Hanuman, the lady with the fruits, the cowherd boy, and the gopis. When fully surrendered, we can have that relationship with Krishna — not once, but **every day, for eternity.**

## Rasa: Different Moods in Different Places

- In Vrindavan every devotee has an eternal, intimate, loving relationship with Krishna, day in and day out, now through eternity. That is **rasa** — loving exchanges.
- Krishna reciprocates in different moods in different places; **He doesn't mix it all up.**
  - **In the village of Vrindavan** — He associates with His parents, **Nanda and Yashoda**, and the elderly gopis. This is **vatsalya-rasa** (parental love): wanting Krishna as one's child. We cannot become Mother Yashoda or Nanda Baba, but the elderly gopis share that mood — wanting to nurse or babysit Krishna, an affection for children.
  - **Out in the country (the pastures)** — the **cowherd boys** have a **friendship** with Krishna. "Boys will be boys — frogs and snails and puppy-dog tails" (versus girls, "sugar and spice and everything nice"). Boys go off into the forest to play; they don't stay home (nowadays they play artificial video games). That is where Krishna associates with the cowherd boys.
  - **The cowherd girls (gopis)** have special meeting places arranged by the elderly cowherd folk, who are clued into these things:
    - Krishna meets Radharani at **Terakadamba** — the goshala/pasture of Nanda Maharaja — their first meeting place. After she comes back from cooking for Krishna at Nandagram, going back to **Yavat**, she meets Him there on the side of the forest.
    - At noontime, Krishna sneaks away from the forest with the cowherd boys and meets Radharani at **Radha-kunda.**
    - In the evening they meet at **Vrinda-kunda.**
  - It is not all mixed up — because the moods are different, the places are not mixed.

### Union and Separation; the Daily Festival of Vraj

- When Krishna goes to the forest with the cowherd boys, all the residents of the village — elderly cowherd men and women — feel so much separation: **one moment away from Krishna is like twelve years.** Because the love is so intense, the separation is equal. (If you are very attached to something and lose it, your feeling of separation equals your attachment.)
- The residents of Vrindavan love Krishna more than anyone has ever been loved. If they lose His association even for a moment — He is just going out to herd the cows — they fall apart and cry all day.
- When Krishna returns from the forest in the evening, **that is the festival of Vraj.**
  - **Brahma-samhita** — in Vrindavan every step is a dance, every word is a song, and there is a festival every day. The festival is Krishna coming back from herding the cows: all the residents go to the edge of the village to receive Him, the whole town stopping whatever they are doing. ("Just like you greeted me when I came, we all came into the temple room. Every day they greet Krishna.")
- These are the two moods: **sambhoga** (union) and **vipralambha-bhava** (separation) — "diving and surfing in the nectar of the ocean of devotion." Because the moods differ, the places differ; you cannot mix the moods too much.

### Story: The Paris Altar — Balaram and Radharani

- When Prabhupada came to Paris one time, the altar had **Jagannath, Subhadra, and Balaram**, and then **Radha and Krishna** — so Balaram was placed near Radha.
- Prabhupada came in, saw the arrangement, and became very angry: **"Why is there no separation between Radharani and Balaram?"** Radharani would be embarrassed associating with the younger brother (Krishna) while the older brother (Balaram) is present — feeling very uncomfortable.
- The new devotees were bewildered, but Prabhupada was speaking of the deeper rasa — intricacies we hope to study one day. The Goswamis gave these teachings, and Prabhupada gave them in the **Nectar of Devotion.**
- He said you cannot have Balaram and Radharani so close; it is not proper. The next morning they put up a sliding bamboo division, and Prabhupada said, "That's very good." **To this day there is still a barrier between Balaram and Radharani.**

### Story: Jagadananda Pandit Wanting to Go to Vrindavan

- From Chaitanya-charitamrita: there are very nice pastimes between **Jagadananda Pandit** and Lord Chaitanya. Jagadananda Pandit argues with the Lord, has a little spat, pouts and goes away, and Lord Chaitanya says "I'm sorry" and asks him to cook again.
- In Krishna-lila, Jagadananda Pandit is **Satyabhama**, one of the chief queens in Dwaraka. That mood — husband and wife, "henpecked husband," little spats — carries over into Gaura-lila.
- One time, so frustrated, Jagadananda Pandit wanted to get away from Puri and go to Vrindavan. Mahaprabhu refused: "You will not understand the mood of the Vrajabasis, because the mood of Dwaraka (where Satyabhama is) and the mood of Vrindavan (where the gopis are) are very much different."
  - The queens have a kind of formal relationship with the husband; in Vrindavan the gopis have a very intimate, loving, paramour relationship — a whole different thing.
- Lord Chaitanya said, "I don't think you should go; you won't fit in." But Jagadananda insisted a long time, so finally Chaitanya said, "You can go — but don't associate too intimately with the Vrajabasis, because you'll misunderstand them and may make offenses. Just associate with **Sanatana Goswami**; he'll take care of you."
- Jagadananda Pandit went (Vrindavan is cooler) and spent the whole time with Sanatana Goswami, who did not let him associate with all the devotees. **"A place for everything, everything in its place."**

## Govardhan Lila: All the Moods Under One Roof

- Sometimes everyone gets together under one roof — a special moment.
- During **Govardhan Lila**, Krishna lifted **Govardhan Hill** to protect the inhabitants of Vrindavan and to diminish the pride of Indra. ("By doing one thing, the Lord does many things.")
- For the Brajabasis — and for the followers of the residents of Vrindavan, which we are meant to be as followers of Lord Chaitanya (that is His mood) — this pastime gave the opportunity for everyone, regardless of their mood or place of association (village, pasture, or kunda), to come together under one roof for **seven days**, "all packed up tight." (Like the brahmachari ashram in our temples, which Prabhupada would call "packed up tight.")
- A special quality of Govardhan Lila: everybody is all together and all the moods are present — "like just a pot of just boiling the nectar in all these various moods for seven days."

### Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura on the Gopis and Purva-raga

- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** comments that the gopis were especially pleased, because it was the first time these young girls were able to be so close to Krishna.
- Previous to the lifting of Govardhan Hill, their mood was what (Rupa) Goswami describes as **purva-raga** — the pastimes building up to the meeting.
  - Krishna is a boy and there are girls, neither yet married. In Vedic culture boys and girls did not associate (even today, at least in South India, girls walk to school on one side and boys on the other; Bombay is different because of Bollywood — very unfortunate). There was no mixing.
  - The little girls are all about 12, 13, 14 at maximum. With deep loving sentiments between Krishna and the gopis, the pastimes involved side-long glances: Krishna coming back from the forest toward Nanda's house would see the gopis, they would look at him, there would be smiles, and sometimes letters passed between the two — by the gopis or even the elderly cowherd folk. **That is purva-raga.**
- When Govardhan Hill was held up, they were all very close, packed up tight, and Krishna was gazing at his beloved gopis.
  - It is mentioned in the **Krishna Book** that Krishna was looking at the breasts of the gopis, and **Balaram saw Krishna glancing.**
  - Actually, Balaram was laughing that the elderly cowherd men thought they were holding up the hill with their sticks — "Krishna's the Supreme Personality of God!" But Krishna thought Balaram was laughing at him for staring at the gopis, and **he blushed.** (This is right out of the Krishna Book — a little confidential, but Prabhupada wrote it.)
- These things can be heard in small doses, because we are not so pure yet that we can go very deep into the subject matter — but we should know the **siddhanta** and some of the pastimes.
- Prabhupada says that hearing these things in a **regulated way under the guidance of the spiritual master** — even hearing these loving pastimes, such as the rasa dance as explained by Prabhupada — **decreases material lust in the heart**; it does not have the opposite effect.

### How Did Everyone Fit Under the Hill?

- Someone once asked: with so many devotees in Vrindavan, how did they all fit under the hill?
- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti** answered: Govardhan Hill felt so much ecstasy being held up by Krishna, as well as serving the Lord's devotees, that **he expanded in size.**

## The Five Rasas — None Inferior

- Krishna reciprocates with us however we want, within the **five rasas** of devotional service: **neutrality (shanta), servitorship (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parenthood (vatsalya), and madhurya-rasa (conjugal love).**
- The gopis' love is the highest, but we should never think any devotee's love for Krishna is inferior. All the rasas make Krishna very happy.
  - Analogy: you may have a very lovable wife, but what would life be without your loving parents, children, and friends? It is a complete picture.
  - The cowherd boys, elderly gopis, cows, and trees are no less dear to Krishna, and the devotees would never change their relationship for anything — "those cowherd boys would never become girls." It is all perfect and complete. **Variety is the spice of life.**
- Prabhupada said one time that anyone who thinks the love of **Mother Yashoda** is inferior to the love of Radharani is an **aparadhi** (offender).

### Brihad-Bhagavatamrita and Mother Yashoda's Love

- **Brihad-bhagavatamrita** is a very important book that all devotees should study carefully. Prabhupada mentions it in the Bhagavatam, and the BBT published it a couple of years ago — an in-depth study of the science and loving mellows of Krishna consciousness, in three volumes; "we should know it backwards and forwards."
- It was the first book written by the Goswamis — **Sanatana Goswami's** favorite book, on which he wrote his own **tika** (commentary): he wrote the verses, then wrote a purport.
- Sanatana Goswami's attachment to the Bhagavatam:
  - As a boy of about seven, he dreamed that a **brahmana** came to the door and gave him a Srimad-Bhagavatam. He awoke, heard a knock, went to the door, and there was a brahmana with a Bhagavatam — his dream came true. ("When you wish upon a star..." — the real star of good fortune is Krishna consciousness.)
  - Even as prime minister in the government of **Hussain Shah**, when he quit and went home, he studied Bhagavatam with a council of brahmanas.
- On Mother Yashoda's love, Brihad-bhagavatamrita cites **Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.9.20** — **"nemam virinco na bhavo nasrir apy anga-samsraya..."** — "Neither Lord Brahma, nor Lord Shiva, nor even the goddess of fortune, who is always the better half of the Supreme Lord, can obtain from the Supreme Personality of Godhead — the deliverer from this material world — such mercy as was received by Mother Yashoda." Just an exalted devotee.

## Our Loving Mellow Is Already Within

- However we surrender to Krishna, He reciprocates accordingly. But it is not that you simply decide, "I think I'd like to be a cow" or "a cowherd boy."
- The loving mellow (rasa) is **eternally there within our hearts** — not something new obtained from outside. Our loving sentiments for Krishna will be naturally aroused at a later stage; it is already decided.
- At a very advanced stage, you will be attracted to a particular resident of Vrindavan and want to follow in their footsteps under the guidance of your spiritual master, and the Lord will naturally reciprocate.

### Even the Abodes Reciprocate — the Dham as Balaram

- This is true even of His abodes. As you surrender to His abode — **Vrindavan, Mayapur, or Puri** — that abode (which is not different from Him) will reciprocate with you accordingly.
- The abode of the Lord is an **expansion of Balaram**. Balaram serves the Lord in many ways — expanding as His bed, His shoes, His slippers, His sitting place, and **the dham** — to facilitate His little brother's pastimes. So the dham is non-different from God — esoteric, but so it is — and it reciprocates with your desires. (This too is in Brihad-bhagavatamrita.)

### Brihad-Bhagavatamrita: Mathura Gives Whatever You Want

- A beautiful verse (paraphrased from Brihad-bhagavatamrita):
  - To those who have material desires, **Mathura** gives the three aims of human work — **religiosity, economic development, and sense gratification.**
  - To those who want liberation, Mathura grants liberation.
  - And to those rare souls who want pure devotion, Mathura gives pure devotion.
  - "What intelligent person, therefore, would not take shelter of Mathura?"
- Whatever you want, Mathura will give. Even if you are foolish enough to want material sense gratification, Mathura delivers it — and you get tired of it and ask for something better, like **Dhruva.** If you want liberation, it gives liberation. And to those rare souls who know the priceless jewel of **bhakti** — of giving everything to Krishna — **Krishna gives His very self.**
- Prabhupada said one time in class: "This movement is so nice — we're giving Krishna. Here's Krishna. Take Him." He who cannot be achieved for lifetimes of penance and austerities, by mystic powers, or by knowledge, is given by His devotee through **bhakti**: "Here He is; take Him." Bhakti is so wonderful.

## Krishna Can Reciprocate with Everyone — This Is God

- Krishna is able to reciprocate with everyone's love — **this is God.**
- In the material world a man can reciprocate with the love of his immediate family, or the people of his village, or (as a humanitarian) people all over the world — but he cannot help people all over the universe, in both the material and spiritual worlds.
- Krishna can reciprocate with the loving sentiments of every single living entity to their full satisfaction — **santushti** (fully satisfied).
- **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6** — **"sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhokshaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati"** — through bhakti (unmotivated, uninterrupted service), **"yayatma suprasidati"**: you have no anxiety, no hankering, no lamenting. "Sir, I am satisfied; I have nothing more to desire." So when death comes — "all right, let's go; no problem. I have no desires left in this world. I'm ready to go."

### You Don't Even Know What You Want

- In due course, all your desires are fulfilled — but we are so fallen we don't even know what we want. We must ask the spiritual master: "What do I really want?" "You want Krishna." "I do?" "Yes."
- In the material world, one's desires may or may not be fulfilled — risky business; some yes, most not. But if you cultivate Krishna consciousness, every desire you have in relation to Krishna is fulfilled.
- **Desire trees** are all over the place: from a desire tree you can ask anything to serve Krishna — it gives fruits, vegetables, wood for sacrifice, even a turban. "Tree, I want a golden spoon... a nice turban — now go and serve Krishna." Whatever you need, you don't have to waste time working for it.
- It doesn't matter if you are a gopi, a cowherd boy, a tree, or a cow — you are happy.

## Holy Cows: How Krishna Loves Them

- Consider how happy the cows are. (The cows here look like really old cows — "like our generation, the Prabhupada disciples; we're like old cows and bulls now, they're going to put us out to pasture pretty soon.")
- Consider that there are cows in the spiritual world — how much Krishna loves those cows.
- **"namo brahmanya-devaya go-brahmana-hitaya ca / jagad-dhitaya krishnaya govindaya namo namah"** — He loves cows and brahmanas: brahmanas because they preach the Absolute Truth, and cows because they give milk (from which come yogurt, ghee, butter, and ice cream). When it is time to go to the forest, Krishna doesn't go with the brahmins — He goes with the cows, spending most of the day with them.
- **These cows here, Prabhupada said, are "cousins" — kissing cousins, quite close** — and these cows are more dear to Krishna than His very life.
  - If you take care of these cows, you please Krishna. "Love me, love my dog." If all you do is make sure the cows get enough to eat, are well brushed, happy, warm in winter and cool in summer — that is all you have to do, because He loves them so much. We should not think of them in a mundane way.

### The Two Categories of Cows

- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura** says the cows in Krishna's Goloka pastimes come from two categories:
  - **Nitya-siddha** associates — who never left the spiritual world.
  - **Sadhana-siddha** — those who achieve final perfection through **sadhana-bhakti**, like we are doing every day (following rules and regulations, chanting), and then are perfected. (Same with devotees.)

### Vishvanatha Chakravarti's Poetic Descriptions

- He speaks so nicely of the cows:
  - They are like **chintamani jewels** that fulfill any desire.
  - Their mooing is the most beautiful poetry.
  - Their happy calves are like pleasant summers.
  - They always lift their ears to hear Krishna's flute.
  - As they wander the earth, they resemble the bubbles of the milk ocean, the chuckles of Lord Shiva, and the infant children of moonlight.

### Nanda Maharaja's 900,000 Cows

- **Nanda Maharaja (Nanda Baba)** has **900,000 cows**, divided into **108 groups.** There are four main colors — white, red, black, and yellow — and mixing the colors yields very interesting cows.
- Krishna knows the name of each and every one of those 900,000 cows at **Terakadamba** (the goshala of Nanda Baba), and He can produce their names from His flute — when He plays, the name of the cow sounds.
  - At the end of the day He climbs the **Kadamba tree**, sits and plays the flute, and holds a string of pearls; as each cow comes in, He pulls down a pearl — so with 900,000 cows, "He finishes rounds."
- Their ornaments: horns decorated with gold, hooves with sapphires, tails with peacock feathers (or bunches of pearls), and they wear necklaces of **Navaratna** (the nine gemstones used in astrology) or crowned jewels between their horns.
- The cows that accompany Krishna to the forest are personally picked by Him — "you, you, and you." Aware of their good fortune, when they go with Krishna every morning they walk with their heads up and tails erect, smiling in all directions, casting happy glances at everyone.
- They are **fully enlightened saints** — demonstrated by the fact that when Krishna takes an afternoon nap with His friends, everything becomes very quiet as the cows guard their beloved Krishna until He awakes. They form a circle around Him, very quiet — no munching grass, no mooing — just Krishna sleeping. When Krishna wakes up: "Moo!" — a symphony of moos, as beautiful as any orchestra you have ever heard.

## The Ever-Increasing Bliss of Devotional Service

- Krishna loves these cows; they are an intricate part of His pastimes. His love for them is always increasing, and their love for Him is always increasing — which we cannot understand. It is called **ananda-(am)budhi-vardhanam** (Shikshashtaka, verse 1), the ever-increasing ocean of bliss of devotional service.
- In the **Nectar of Devotion**, Prabhupada says that is spiritual life, Krishna consciousness: "loving propensity expands like a beam of light."
- **Story — the father's lesson about light:** When Maharaja was a boy, his father took him out one night and said, "Son, shine this light into the dark sky with the stars. That light is going to go on forever. What will stop it? It may hit an object and bounce off in a different direction — but it doesn't fade away." That is a material analogy: love of the Lord, in a true principle, is always going and always expanding.
- Prabhupada said it is impossible for us to understand the infinite happiness relished in bhakti, because it is beyond the realm of anything we can experience in our finite existence.

## Material "Love" Has a Short Life Expectancy

- In the material world, love has a very short life expectancy.
- Palmistry aside (heart line, head line — right hand for boys, left for girls — and life line): "See my life line? It's getting shorter — about 71 or 72, maybe." **"padam padam yad vipadam na tesham"** (Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.14.58).
  - If you see someone's lines broken off, you don't say anything. Once, seeing children with a line broken at about 15 years, Maharaja said nothing — "very nice, very smart"; and when asked "What about that line?" he said, "Oh, your hand's dirty, I can't see it." "Astrology doesn't tell like that."
- Love in this world, as dear as it is, has a short life expectancy — as evidenced by three out of four marriages ending in divorce in this country. The love is superficial: skin love, body love; they don't know the soul.
- In Krishna consciousness you can really love your partner — a deep friendship — but in the end love here is finished by death. **There is no perfect love in this world.**
- But in the love of the residents of Vrindavan — because there is no birth, disease, old age, or death (conspicuous by their absence in the spiritual world) — the loving relationship goes on and on, **ad infinitum.** That is the love we are looking for.

## Love Is the Essence — Only Krishna Fully Satisfies

- Love is the essence of everything. If you have everything but no love, you are sad; if you have love (someone to love) and are poor, you can still be relatively happy. What to speak of having Krishna's love, when He can fully satisfy your desire to love Him — just as He fully satisfies everyone else.
- If we don't have Krishna, we try to replace that love with something else — a person or an object. But it never really satisfies, because once having experienced the real thing, you can never be happy with the imitation.
  - Analogy — **Pierre Cardin:** if you have the genuine French clothing and then buy a cheap imitation somewhere on vacation, it is nothing compared to your Pierre Cardin.
- Once you have had that love with Krishna (which we once had), and you come to this material world, no matter how beautiful she is or how handsome he is, it is all temporary. As **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** said, "the whole show is only temporary": the beauty of the flower wilts by evening, and the beauty of our youth goes away — you look in the mirror at a sad, old face.
- If you are Krishna conscious, though: "Good — now I don't have to worry about this body anymore; no one's going to look at me, and I don't have to look at anybody else — just Krishna, Krishna, Krishna." **Old age is such a nice thing in Krishna consciousness.**

### Story: The Monkey and the Puppy

- We need love, so without Krishna we adopt some substitute.
- In Vrindavan, Maharaja once saw a monkey who evidently could not have baby monkeys of her own. There were some newborn puppies (six or seven), and she stole one from its mother and took it up into a tree.
- For three days she tried to nurse the puppy — it worked a little — but she was ostracized by the other monkeys because it was too weird. Eventually, having tried her best to love it as a **surrogate mother**, but artificially, she dropped the puppy back on the ground. The puppy scampered back to its mother, but the mother rejected it.
- Like that, we try to have a surrogate lover in this world, thinking it is all perfect and will work out — but what we are really looking for is Krishna. Krishna is the supreme lover, waiting for us to love Him in any of those ways we want, even as the cows.

## This Nectar Is Waiting — the Krishna Book

- That will come one day, following in Prabhupada's footsteps — "you'll never be disappointed."
- **Story — "Do you think I would cheat you?":** One of Maharaja's god-sisters asked, "Is it really true, Prabhupada, that Krishna's pastimes in Vrindavan — that God is a cowherd boy, that He dances with the cowherd girls, kills big snake demons, His brother throws asses into the trees, and He frolics all day — is it really true?" Prabhupada became very moved and quiet, and said: **"Do you think that I would cheat you?"**
- All this nectar is waiting for us and is being revealed. That is why Prabhupada took the time to translate the **Krishna Book**, the summary of the Tenth Canto — "everything's there." He said one day this Krishna Book would give solace to his devotees who are feeling **vipralambha-bhava** (separation).
- It is all waiting for the sincere followers of Srila Prabhupada and Lord Chaitanya.

## Closing: Radha-Damodara and This Community

- Hope is expressed for this beautiful pastoral ground — like Vrindavan (though "your peacocks are making so much noise I can't concentrate on my talk") — with **Radha-Damodara**, the deities of **Jiva Goswami.**
- The original Damodara deity is only this big — **hand-carved by Rupa Goswami** as a gift to his nephew **Jiva** — a little deity now residing in Jaipur; at the Radha-Damodara temple in Jaipur one can see this hand-carved deity.
- That was the deity to whom Jiva Goswami prayed — **"he radha-damodara... Jiva Goswami pranadhana he"** — meaning that deity is the **life and soul (pranadhana)** of Jiva Goswami. So you also make Radha-Damodara **pranadhana** — the lord and substance of your life — serving Him with His beloved consort Srimati Radharani, and easily you can go back to Godhead.
- You do not need to live in these Kali-yuga cities. Take care of Krishna's cows, take care of Krishna — bathe Him just like Mother Yashoda, wake Him up in the morning, bathe Him, dress Him, feed Him (a little boy), go out and herd His cows, grow the fruits and grains for Him — do all these things just as they do in the spiritual world.
- This is a good place to practice **sadhana-bhakti** — devotional service in preparation — preparing yourself to go back home, back to Godhead, and you have everything necessary for doing it.
- Encouragement: there are not a lot of devotees living here, and there have been some rough times, but those problems are small compared to the great opportunities of living in the personal association of God and His eternal consort — deities whom Srila Prabhupada saw, loved, and worshipped, so much a part of the history of this movement.
- Praise for the temple room — one of the most beautiful in ISKCON: simple but very elegant, perfectly appropriate for a country ashram, lots of wood. Not the big silver thrones (**simhasanas**) seen elsewhere, but like Vrindavan, where everything is simple and the most important element is **bhakti.**
- "So learn to love these deities and these devotees, these Brajabasis who live here, with your heart and soul, and you have every chance to go back home, back to Godhead in this lifetime."
