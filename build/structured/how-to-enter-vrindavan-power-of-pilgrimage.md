## The Verse for Today — SB 10.13.60

- **SB 10.13.60** — recited from the 10th Canto, chapter 13, text 60.
  - **Translation:** *"Vrindavan is the transcendental abode of the Lord, where there is no hunger, anger, or thirst. Though naturally inimical, both human beings and fierce animals live there together in transcendental friendship."*
  - **Purport (Prabhupada):** The word *vana* means "forest." We are afraid of the forest and do not wish to go there, but in Vrindavan the forest animals are as good as demigods, for they have no envy. Even in this material world, in the forest the animals live together, and when they go to drink water they do not attack anyone. **Envy develops because of sense gratification, but in Braj there is no sense gratification — the only aim is Krishna's satisfaction.** Even in this material world, the animals in Vrindavan are not envious of the sadhus who live there. The sadhus keep cows and supply milk to the tigers, saying, "Come here and take a little milk." Thus envy and malice are unknown in Vrindavan. That is the difference between Vrindavan and the ordinary world. We are horrified to hear the name of *vana*, the forest, but in Vrindavan there is no such horror.

## Cows and Brahmanas — Dear to Krishna

- Cows are Krishna's favorite animal (**Surabhi**). **"Namo brahmanya-devaya go-brahmana-hitaya cha / jagad-dhitaya krishnaya govindaya namo namah"** glorifies what is dear to Krishna: **the cows and the brahmanas.**
- The **brahmanas** are dear because, in the absence of the Lord, they speak transcendental knowledge to the general mass of people — Krishna comes to reestablish dharma, and the brahmanas are like the mouth of God, carrying on where He left off.
- But the speaker thinks the **cows** are even more dear, because in Goloka Krishna spends most of His time with the cows and the boys.
- **Krishna's daily schedule:** He gets up in the morning; Mother Yashoda wakes Him, bathes Him, dresses Him; along with Balaram and the other cowherd boys there's a big breakfast celebration; then they go to the edge of town. The whole village comes, because to be separated from Krishna for even a moment is like 12 years or more — very painful. "As much as you love someone, to that degree you feel separation from them when they leave." The residents of Vrindavan love Krishna more than anyone's ever been loved, and Krishna loves the Brajabasis more than anyone's ever been loved — so there's a lot of crying when Krishna leaves every day to go to the pasturing ground. He spends almost the whole day there and comes back in the evening as the sun sets — with the cows.
- Therefore we have the name **Govinda**, the protector of the cows. **Nanda Maharaj has 900,000 cows** in the *gocharan* (pasture) at Nanda-gaon.

## Krishna Comes Every Day (in the Life of Brahma)

- How is He coming every day? In scholarly circles, time is calculated by the time of the **devatas** (demigods), because they are more important than us in the universal scheme. (Who cares about the time of ants? Our time is more important; and more important than us are the demigods.)
- Once a day, every day in the life of Brahma, Krishna comes — **once in a day of Brahma.** He came just 5,000 years ago, and He brought Goloka with Him to Gokula/Vrindavan.
- Therefore the acharyas say that because He brings that spiritual kingdom with Him, **there's no difference between Goloka in the spiritual world and Gokula** — which is only a bus ride, a train ride, or an airline ticket away for all of you who live here in Mumbai.
- You're so blessed: out of this entire universe of millions upon billions of planets, **Krishna chose the planet Earth** to come to, and that has been replicated not far from here. Whenever you have time you should go to Braj. As Prabhupada said, "If you've got time, chant Hare Krishna." If you live in India you're so close to Vrindavan — go there on your vacation instead of London, Paris, Florida, or L.A. to go to Disneyland. Go to Braj, the playground of God.

## Scriptural Proof That Gokula = Goloka

- How do we know that Gokula (just a stone's throw away) is non-different from Goloka? In learned circles we speak about real transcendental knowledge — **Raja-vidya, Raja-guhyam** — and whatever you say, you back it up with *shastra*; that's authority. "We have to become *shloka-walas*" like **Vaisheshika Prabhu**.
- Where does it say Gokula is an exact replica of Goloka? In his commentary on **Brahma-samhita, verse 2**, **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura** explains it in very eloquent, old-style English.
- **How Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura learned English:** When he was 7 years old, he took a big British dictionary from the library of his father, **Bhaktivinoda Thakura**, locked himself in a room for three days without speaking a word of English, and walked out three days later speaking fluent English — because he had read the dictionary. He had a photographic memory.
  - When a devotee asked Prabhupada to say something about his spiritual master, Prabhupada paused and said, "What can I say? He was a Vaikuntha man."
- We take the words of a pure devotee as good as *shastra*, because a pure devotee is fully realized, **tattva-darshi**, and can see things as they are. Bhaktisiddhanta writes: **"There is practically no difference between Gokula and Goloka, save that what exists in the shape of Goloka in the upper region is the same as Gokula on the mundane plane, where Krishna showed His various activities."** For us, that's proof.

## How to Actually See Vrindavan — the Speck in the Eye

- In the early years Prabhupada was the original **parikrama guru** — he sometimes took devotees on parikrama around Vrindavan. (The speaker's own parikrama gurus include Dina Bandhu, Bhakti Chaitanya Maharaj, and Radhanath Swami.)
  - The speaker had the good fortune to go on one: they went to **Brahmanda Ghat**, where Prabhupada took bath in the Yamuna, invited everyone in, and they bathed and frolicked while Prabhupada splashed water with them. "It was so sweet."
- On one early parikrama, Prabhupada was sharing his understanding of Vrindavan (summarizing the Krishna Book): *"Vrindavan is such a nice place. Flowers are always blooming, and there are even various kinds of decorated deer. Birds are chirping, peacocks are crowing and dancing, and bees are humming. The cuckoos there sing nicely in five kinds of tunes. Krishna, the reservoir of pleasure, blowing His venu (vamsi, flute), accompanied by His elder brother Balaram (Dauji) and the other cowherd boys and the cows, entered the beautiful forest of Vrindavan to enjoy the atmosphere."*
- A disciple raised his hand: *"Srila Prabhupada, you are giving this beautiful, pristine, pure understanding of Vrindavan, but when I look around I see the latrine I just went to — it was filthy dirty — and the mosquitoes, the rickshaw-wallas begging for money, the pigs, and the open sewers. I don't see Vrindavan like you're seeing it. Why?"*
- Prabhupada smiled: **"That's because you have a speck in your eye, so you're not seeing it correctly."** The devotee (who wore glasses) took them off and said, "Oh, it looks the same." Prabhupada said, "No. The speck in your eye is **the desire for material enjoyment.** When you remove that desire and practice Krishna consciousness, what will come?" — **"Premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilochanena"** — *"when your eyes are anointed with the salve of love, you can see Vrindavan as it is."*
- Vrindavan is an abode of love; love reigns supreme there. It's not about me — it's all about you, Krishna. Here in this world it's "I, me, and mine," but **amala-bhakti** (real bhakti) means it's all about You, not about me: because I'm merely a part and parcel, if You are pleased, I will naturally be pleased. "Get me out of here, take me home to that transcendental abode."

## The Pastime of Akrura — Background

- 5,000 years ago in Mathura, the evil asura **Kamsa** (the speaker doesn't even like to say his name) was sending a demon every day to kill Krishna in Gokula. He was unsuccessful — Krishna is **Ajita**, unconquerable.
- So Kamsa changed his plan: instead of sending demons to Gokula, "let's bring Krishna to Mathura and kill Him." They would pretend to hold a wrestling match with **Chanura and Mushtika** — big, powerful men — to kill this little boy.
- As his emissary Kamsa chose one of his ministers, **Akrura:** "You go to Braj and make this invitation to both Krishna and that other one — but that other one, Balaram, sometimes kills my friends when I send them there — bring them both back."
- But we should understand that although Akrura was part of Kamsa's team (like an implanted spy), he was in fact a great devotee of Krishna, and he very happily accepted the service.
- We read in the Bhagavatam that as he traveled to Vrindavan he was meditating on his good fortune at being able to enter Vrindavan, the abode of his beloved Lord.

## Akrura's Humility — a Prerequisite for Entering Braj

- Akrura demonstrated the topmost quality of a devotee — **humility.** He was thinking: *"What auspicious acts have I done? What severe austerities have I undergone? What charities have I performed to be able to enter Vrindavan?"* He realized it was a special place. *"I'm just a wretched materialistic person, absorbed in sense enjoyment."* This is genuine humility — not just saying it — and it is a prerequisite for anyone desiring to enter into Braj.
- **Prahlada Maharaj** (childhood hero), asked by Lord Brahma to approach Lord Nrsimhadeva, showed the same humility — the famous verse: *"How is it possible for me, who have been born in a family of asuras, to offer suitable prayers to satisfy the Supreme Personality of Godhead? Even until now, all the demigods headed by Lord Brahma and all the saintly persons could not satisfy the Lord by streams of excellent words, although such persons are very qualified, being in the mode of goodness — then what is to be said of me? I'm not at all qualified."* This is the crest jewel of all devotees' qualities: humility.

## Humility Comes From Realizing God's Greatness

- Therefore Mahaprabhu stresses humility in the little he left in writing, the **Shikshashtakam**: it's very difficult to make progress in spiritual life unless you're meek and humble. (The Bible says only the meek shall enter the kingdom of heaven.)
- All our acharyas show that humility because they know God. **Analogy of the mountain:** you set out saying, "I'm going to climb that mountain," but as you get closer you realize how great the mountain is and how little you are, and you become humbled. On Mount Everest, you reach base camp and are humbled by the greatness before you. So the sign that one is actually a self-realized devotee — who knows God, has seen God, has seen how great God is — is that he is humble. "They're not faking it."
- **Chaitanya Charitamrita, Adi-lila, Chapter 5, verse 205** — *"Jagai Madhai haite muni se papishtha, purishera kita haite muni se laghishtha"* — *"I am more sinful than Jagai and Madhai, and even lower than the worms in the stool."* We can hear from Kaviraja Goswami because we know he realized how great God is and how tiny he is.

## Mahaprabhu's Verse on Humility — Wear It Around Your Neck

- Mahaprabhu's verse (audience recites along): **"One should chant the holy name of the Lord in a humble state of mind, thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street; one should be more tolerant than the tree, devoid of all sense of false prestige, and should be ready to offer all respect to others. In such a state of mind one can chant the holy name of the Lord constantly."** (Shikshashtakam, *"trinad api sunichena"*)
- Mahaprabhu spoke our philosophy through the **six Goswamis of Vrindavan**, whom he empowered to represent him and told to write books on the science of Krishna consciousness. He is quoted in the Chaitanya Charitamrita proclaiming how important that one verse is:
  - **"Udvahu kariya kahon, shuno sarva-loka, nama-sutre gaanthi paro kanthe ei shloka"** — **"Raising my hands I declare, everyone please hear me: string this verse on the thread of the holy name and wear it on your neck for continuous remembrance."**
- What's around your neck? **Tulasi** neck-beads. **Story of Kishori:** one of the speaker's godsisters, in 1972, took Prabhupada's instruction so literally that she got a big **tattoo** of the *trinad api sunichena* verse around her neck — she was the first to have a tattoo. It's faded now, but "it's in her heart." Very humble and very meek. (The Bible: only the meek shall enter the gates of heaven.)

## How Can the Weak Advance? — The Lord's Mercy Is Greater

- Question: how can a person who admits he's weak be strong enough to advance in Krishna consciousness? **Answer:** a devotee knows the Lord's mercy is greater than his or her own disqualifications, and the Lord happily bestows mercy upon devotees whose hearts He knows want to become Krishna conscious: "Please, Krishna, help me. I want to become Krishna conscious."
- **Padyavali (Rupa Goswami):** *"Lord, I'm drowning in the fathomless whirlpool of material existence. O Lord, O friend of those who have no shelter, O effulgent moon of mercy, please just this one time extend your hand to save me. I'm fallen here — my only hope is you."*
- **Padyavali (another verse):** *"The elephant of my mind is drowning in the waters of material illusion, strongly held by the ferocious crocodile of the threefold miseries; it anxiously cries with fear in its heart. O Lord Hari, please rescue it with your glowing Sudarshan chakra, which cuts the sufferings of the demigods to pieces."*
- We owe so much to Rupa Goswami — his verses and purports bring both the philosophy and the emotion of Krishna consciousness to a very clear picture.
  - **Analogy:** As a boy, the speaker's mother bought little pads with nothing on them, but rubbing the flat part of a pencil over them made a picture appear. That's Rupa Goswami's commentary — "now I see."
  - **Prayer to Rupa Goswami:** *"For the purification of my physical body, I bathe regularly using water; however, for the complete purification of myself and my existence, I bathe in the nectarian words of Sri Rupa Goswami."*

## Akrura's Hope Against Hope — the River of Time

- Back to Akrura on the chariot. He thought, "I'm so fallen," but his next thought was: *"But enough of such thoughts. After all, even a fallen soul like me can have the chance to behold the infallible Supreme Lord, for one of the conditioned souls being swept along in the river of time may sometimes reach the shore."*
- **Analogy of the river:** all the sticks, leaves, and flowers rush along, but sometimes one reaches the shore. "I'm in the river of time going through this world, but somehow by the infallible mercy of the Lord I may find myself on the shore of Vaikuntha."
- **"Brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krishna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija"** — we wander from one universe, species, and body to another, but the spiritual master, on behalf of the Lord, stops us on the street.
  - Preaching scene: "Hey, are you from here or out of town? Here, take this book — we're giving them out today just to cover the cost of printing. Interested in yoga? Here's an invitation to our temple — tomorrow's a big celebration for the new temple construction, an Ayodhya program; come and hear about Lord Ram." (*"Raghupati Raghava Raja Ram, Patita Pavana Sita Ram."*) That's how it happens: they intervene on behalf of the Lord.
- So Akrura is saying: somehow, in this rush of time, by the mercy of Krishna, I may one day find myself on the shores of Vaikuntha. This is how we should go to Vrindavan — not just the ticket, but the **mood.**

## Imagining Being With Krishna — "I'm Gonna Make It"

- As Akrura proceeded, he started imagining what it would be like to be with Krishna. We should think like that too: "I'm gonna make it, by the mercy of my guru, by the mercy of Mahaprabhu, who's even more merciful than Krishna. I'll never give up. Not me." (Peter Pan: "I'll never give up, not me.")
- Then we can imagine: what's it going to be like when we're home? "There's no place like home." Such a relief — to get out of the world of birth, disease, old age, and death, and be back in the pristine beauty, the abode of love.
- Prabhupada said one time to one of the speaker's godbrothers: when we get back to the spiritual world, the spiritual master is waiting for us; he takes our hand, leads us into the **kunja** (grove) where Krishna is waiting, puts our hand in Krishna's hand, and leaves us alone for some time.
- Prabhupada told his godbrother **Dhananjaya** (who did a lot of service) in a moment of intimacy: **"Dhananjaya, when we get back to the spiritual world, we will all recognize each other."** (Playful aside: recognizing "that sannyasi who sat on the big chair in Chowpatty and gave a lecture — what was your name?… Now I have my gopi skirt on… I'm not even qualified to know — I'm not the body yet.")

## Daydreaming — Illusion Versus Reality

- In English we call this daydreaming/meditating. In this world, daydreaming means making plans for future happiness — "I'll work like this, get this much money, invest that percentage, buy a house." But our daydreams are just dreams, and dreams are not real — dreams are illusion.
- Prabhupada said: if you dream of the spiritual master or Krishna, that's very nice, but everything else is nonsense. The mind: you see a piece of gold, you see a mountain, and at night you dream of a gold mountain.
- This kind of daydream is called **go-dadimba** — "the egg of a horse." Do horses produce eggs? No. So daydreaming is a waste of time.
- Another word: **kha-pushpa** — "a flower growing in the sky." There's no such thing. Similarly, why daydream about happiness in this world? It's here today, gone tomorrow, if not two seconds later — like water off a duck's back.
- It's a world of **duality** — up and down, black and white, boy and girl, happiness and distress. **Analogy:** you eat an ice cream cone, it falls in the sand, you pick it up — same ice cream, but crunchy. There's happiness here, but it's always gritting in your teeth.
- But Akrura's dreaming of meeting Krishna is **absolute reality**, because he's a pure devotee. So we can also "daydream" in that context: "I just want to be with Krishna."

## Looking Into the Heart of a Pure Devotee — SB 10.38.17

- What exactly was Akrura daydreaming? We can look into the heart of the devotee — they share their hearts with us; they're not shy; that's what they're here for. **SB 10.38.17** — a rare opportunity not afforded to most of the 7 billion people on this planet. We're very privileged to have access to the thoughts, words, and instructions of a pure devotee.
  - **Story of Prabhupada's father, Gour Mohan De:** He said to young Prabhupada that the universal form, the Supreme Lord, has many arms and many hands; if He wants to give you mercy, how much can your two little hands hold? We have so much mercy we don't know what to do with it — including the mercy of looking into the heart of a completely pure person.
- **SB 10.38.17** — Akrura's meditation: **"When I have fallen at Krishna's feet, the Almighty Lord will place His lotus hand upon my head. For those who seek shelter in Him, because they are greatly disturbed by the powerful serpent of time, that hand removes all fear."**

## The Fragrance of Krishna's Lotus Hand — the Saugandhika Lotus

- The Puranas call the lotus flower found in the **Manasarovara** lake **saugandhika** — literally the white lotus, very fragrant.
- The speaker did the **kora (parikrama) around Mount Kailash** with a group of devotees some years ago — very mystical, "you come very close to Shiva, the greatest devotee of Krishna." The sadhus told them that as you pass Manasarovara lake, the white lotuses growing there can be smelled a kilometer away.
- The acharyas, commenting on this part of Akrura's journey (his reference to Krishna's lotus hand), say that **Krishna's lotus hand acquired the fragrance of this saugandhika lotus by coming in contact with the beautiful faces of the gopis.** That's how Krishna's hand smells — because he touched the gopis. This incident (Krishna acquiring the perfume of the gopis' bodies) is described in the **33rd chapter of the 10th Canto.** Knowing this, Akrura is hankering for the Lord to put that hand on his head.

## What Do We Want? — Guru-nishtha

- What does Akrura want? What do we want? (**Christmas story:** "All I want for Christmas is my two front teeth" — as a boy the speaker gave his dad a three-page list of wants; his dad said, "We don't have that kind of money," and quoted the song.)
- As Gaudiyas, we learn what we want from the spiritual master. We think we want this and that, but we ask, "Dear Guruji, what do I want?" and he tells us: **pure devotional service to Krishna.** Because you have faith in him, that becomes your motivation. You may still hanker for material things, but because you have faith in the words of your spiritual master, you kick those things away one by one out of your heart until there's only pure devotional service. That's **guru-nishtha.**
- **Story of Tamal Krishna Goswami:** About to start initiating, the speaker asked TKG (a great devotee, "a Prabhupada man"), "What is the most important quality of a disciple? What should I look for in a student?" TKG closed his eyes and replied, **"Guru-nishtha — faith in the spiritual master."** All success comes from faith in the spiritual master: what he says, you take to heart; having taken it to heart, you act on it; and that action gives you realized knowledge. (Echoing the Bhagavad-gita: approach a spiritual teacher, inquire from him, and the knowledge becomes realized by serving that instruction. Krishna said, "I can be understood as I am only by devotional service.")
- So we know what pure devotees want, and we want the same thing: "I don't want my two teeth for Christmas — I want **amala-bhakti.** I'm simply running behind you, Guru Maharaj, shouting Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, because that's what he wants." "I don't have it, but you've got it — please give it to me. I'm trying to serve you. Life after life I'll stay here as long as it takes, because by serving you here I qualify myself to serve you there."

## What to Do on Arrival — Take Shelter First

## Krishna Knows the Heart

- The Bhagavatam says Akrura was confident that when he arrived, Krishna would know his heart (and thankfully the guru also knows our heart). Akrura said: **"The infallible Lord will not consider me an enemy, even though Kamsa has sent me; after all, the omniscient Lord perfectly knows the hearts of everyone."**

## Reaching Vrindavan — Krishna's Footprints

- Akrura reached Vrindavan at sunset, and the first thing he saw was **Krishna's footprints.**
- If you have the vision, they're everywhere — because when the gopis sweep the groves and streets and come across one of Krishna's footprints, they go into ecstasy and leave it undisturbed, so there are footprints of Krishna everywhere.
- **SB 10.21.10** — *"O friend, Vrindavan is spreading the glory of the earth, having obtained the treasure of the lotus feet of Krishna, the son of Devaki. The peacocks dance madly when they hear Govinda's flute, and when other creatures see Him from the hilltops, they become stunned."* Having obtained the treasure of Krishna's lotus feet — that's what makes Vrindavan special.
- **Sanatana Goswami** has written that because in Vrindavan Krishna always goes barefoot, the land is covered by His footprints, which make **Vrindavan more glorious than Vaikuntha, where Vishnu wears slippers.**
- **Why doesn't Krishna wear slippers?** One day Mother Yashoda said, "Lala, you're going to the forest every day with your cows, and there are prickly thorns, sharp rocks, and holes — you'll hurt your feet. Please wear some shoes." Krishna said, "Maiya, how can I wear shoes if my beloved cows don't wear shoes? If you want to give all my cows shoes, I'll start wearing shoes." But there are 900,000 cows, so she never got them.

## Rolling in the Dust of Braj

- Seeing Krishna's footprints, Akrura set an example: he exclaimed, **"Ah, this is the dust from my master's feet!"** and got down and rolled and rolled and rolled in the dust.
- **Braj-raj** (Braj-renu, the dust of Braj) is one of the **five personalities who grant entrance and residence in Vrindavan** (along with Govardhan Hill, the Yamuna river, Gopishwara Mahadev, etc.). The dust is made from **Chintamani stone;** it gives bhakti.
- **Story of Raghava Pandit:** During Mahaprabhu's pastimes, he lived in a cave at Govardhan Hill. He was a *paka* (fully clean) brahmana — Vaishnava brahmanas are characterized by cleanliness (Prabhupada told the Detroit devotees a brahmana is so clean that after drinking water he washes his hands). Yet Raghava Pandit would get up, take his bath, and *then* roll in the dust — normally you'd roll in the dust first and then bathe, but he considered the dust of Vrindavan a **bodily ornament**, because Krishna's lotus feet and the cowherd boys have walked that dust. So we take that dust upon our heads.
- Now Vrindavan is becoming more asphalt (the old dusty Parikrama Marg of the 1970s, where a beautiful dust-cloud would rise, is now the paved Ring Road), but there's still plenty of dust — Vrindavan has "60 million holy places."
- Prabhupada's instruction — **Krishna Book, Chapter 38:** *"As soon as one reaches the boundary of Vrindavan, he should immediately smear the dust of Vrindavan all over his body, without thinking of his material position and prestige."*
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** sang in a celebrated song: **"Vishaya chhariya kabe shuddha ha'be mana"** — *"When my mind will be purified, after leaving the contamination of material sense enjoyment, I will be able to visit Vrindavan."* (Speaker: "Raise your hand if next time you go to Vrindavan you're going to roll in the dust.")

## How to Approach, Live in, and Die in Vrindavan — Prabhupada's Mercy

- It is all by the mercy of Srila Prabhupada, the founder-acharya: how to approach Vrindavan, how to live in Vrindavan, how to die in Vrindavan.
- It's said that if one is **born in Vrindavan, lives in Vrindavan, or dies in Vrindavan,** he goes back to Godhead.
- We can also add (based on much research): you can go back home even by living in Ethiopia, Mongolia, or the Arctic Circle — if you're serving Vrindavan. You can make your own home into Vrindavan by following the **nine processes of devotional service** (*shravanam, kirtanam, vishnu-smaranam, pada-sevanam, archanam, vandanam, dasyam, sakhyam, atma-nivedanam*) — that also creates the Vrindavan atmosphere.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** once wrote: *"I woke up this morning and saw that my house, my home, has been transformed into Goloka Vrindavan."* This is Prabhupada's mercy — the way of life he's given us. Living in Vrindavan is a matter of **consciousness**, which is most important. Prabhupada carried Vrindavan to the world because he was a Brajabasi; that's why he was so attractive everywhere he went — he carried that Vrindavan mood.
- So: practice Krishna consciousness carefully and diligently in the privacy of your own homes, but do go to Vrindavan from time to time and become fortunate. For those in India — in Chowpatty and Mumbai — it's a special nectar and privilege. "Count your blessings."
