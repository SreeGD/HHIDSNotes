## Introduction: A Brief Word Before the Initiation Yagya

- Indradyumna Swami says he will speak briefly about this initiation ceremony, and requests the translators to sit in their proper places so they can translate for the devotees.
- After this short lecture, Sri Prahlad (Sri Prahlada) will take over and carry on with the initiation yagya.

## An Auspicious Day: The Candidates Take Their Vows

- As mentioned that morning, this is a very auspicious day.
- The candidates for initiation are making a big step forward in their Krishna consciousness by making their vows before their spiritual master:
  - To chant 16 rounds a day.
  - To follow the four regulative principles.
  - To engage in the devotional service of Guru and Gauranga.
- "It has been a long time since we have been in the spiritual world," but we begin to return — the process is initiated at this initiation ceremony.

## What "Initiation" Means

- **Srila Prabhupada** described initiation as "the beginning of one's spiritual life."
- The word *initiation* comes from *to initiate*, which means to begin something.
- Both candidates have certainly been practicing Krishna consciousness for some time — otherwise they would not have been recommended for this ceremony.

## Initiation as a Formal, Legally-Binding Contract

- This is the formal contract that binds both the disciple to the guru and the guru to the disciple, and ensures that in the very near future one will be successful, achieve love of God, and go back home, back to Godhead.
- **Analogy — a business contract:** In the material world, when a formal contract is made between a company and its employee, or a company and its client, there is a formal signing to ensure both parties are serious. Then it takes on legal binding.
- Similarly, we initially accept someone as our spiritual master, and the spiritual master accepts us as an aspiring disciple. Then the spiritual master studies the character of the disciple and his or her progress in Krishna consciousness. When he feels confident that this student is really determined and serious about going back home, back to Godhead, and is willing to render service to the sankirtana mission of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, then he agrees to accept that disciple as a duly initiated devotee. "That is like a legal contract" — something very serious, taken seriously.
- **The disciple's side of the contract:** to strictly follow the rules and regulations of devotional service.
  - **"ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu-saṅgo 'tha bhajana-kriyā"** — the sequential stages of devotion; *bhajana-kriya* means the regulative process, the rules and regulations we follow in Krishna consciousness, which help us become fixed up and to chant 16 rounds a day.
- **The spiritual master's side of the contract:** he takes very seriously the responsibility of accepting this boy, girl, man, or woman as a disciple — to train them up, fix them up, and help them awaken their love for Krishna.
  - Prabhupada mentions in **Chaitanya-caritamrita** that the spiritual master takes this legal binding so seriously that, if necessary, he is prepared to come back (take birth again) to deliver his disciple.
  - But Prabhupada adds that the disciple should act in such a way as not to cause the poor spiritual master to have to take birth again. In other words, we should fully embrace the process of devotional service, renounce all material desires, and have greed for developing love for Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava. This makes the work of the spiritual master very easy. "This is the type of disciples that we want to be."

## Story: Saving the Drowning Girl in Mexico

- Told to illustrate making it "easy" for the spiritual master to deliver us.
- The speaker has seen people literally drowning on several occasions, and once saved a drowning person himself.
  - He was a young boy, 16 or 17, swimming at a beach in Mexico.
  - A young Mexican girl went into the wave; she couldn't swim very well, and after a short time she started to drown and was screaming.
  - The Mexican people there generally were not good swimmers; it was the young American boys surfing on the coast who could swim.
  - He ran out and swam to her. It was very difficult because she was panicking and very afraid (understandably), and her lungs started to fill with water.
  - When he reached her, she started to beat him out of fear and confusion — flailing her arms, screaming, biting, hitting, kicking.
  - Being stronger, he turned her around and put his arm around her neck (under her arm), and though she kept kicking, he swam back to shore.
  - Her lungs were full of water, so he pushed on her belly and all the water came out. They saved her — but it wasn't easy; he got a couple of cuts and a black eye.
- **The lesson:** Had she known that someone capable was coming to rescue her, she could have been calm, making it very easy. Because she panicked, it was much harder. Likewise, we should make it easy for our spiritual master to deliver us. We should not enter the initiation contract and then maintain our past material conditioning. This is the stage at which we have to give up the past.

## Changing the Name: Cutting the False Identity

- That is why, traditionally, at the initiation ceremony the spiritual master changes the name of the disciple.
- **Analogy — marriage in Vedic culture:**
  - When a young girl gets married, she leaves the house of her father and mother and goes to live in the house of her husband's mother and father. She even changes her name — taking on the last name of her husband, giving up her father's/mother's family name. (This is true also in Western culture.) She has a new identity.
  - The speaker has attended weddings in India: one of the final events is that the girl goes back to her father and mother's house and says goodbye to her relatives. It is a very touching, emotional moment — mother, father, sisters, and brothers break down crying, because for all practical purposes she will no longer live there.
  - The father bestows her upon the young man: "For so long you have been under my protection; now I am giving you over to this boy — he is your protector."
  - **On the protection of women in Vedic culture:** A girl is always protected — by her father when she is young; by her qualified husband when she reaches puberty and is married (he protects her throughout life); and, in the event of the untimely death of her husband, by her eldest son. A woman is always protected.
  - She is no longer considered part of her original family but is welcomed into the new family — new mother, new father, new brothers and sisters — becoming literally part of that family. Part of that procedure is cutting the identity by changing the name.
- If that is true in the material sense, how much more true it is in Krishna consciousness. We want to give up all these false identities.

## Our Only Real Identity: The Eternal Servant of Krishna

- **"jīvera 'svarūpa' haya—kṛṣṇera 'nitya-dāsa'"** — Who am I? "I am the eternal servant of Krishna."
- All the identities we take on in the material world are due to contact with the material body, due to identifying with the body, and ultimately due to the *ahankara* (false ego) — this is all false, all illusory.
  - Because we have the body and identify strongly with it, we think we are the body: men, women, Polish, American, Japanese, German; tall, short, fat, skinny — none of these things. This is all temporary, because the body is here today and gone tomorrow, but the soul is eternal.
  - The soul has an eternal identity as a servant of Krishna, a lover of Krishna. That is our only identity. **We are the servants of Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava.**

## Story: Srila Prabhupada's Passport Application

- When **Srila Prabhupada** was applying for his passport to come to the West for the very first time, to board the *Jaladuta* and bring the mercy of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu to the Western countries, the form asked his occupation.
- He wrote that his occupation was servant of *Om Vishnupada Paramahamsa Parivrajakacharya Ashtottara-shata Sri Srimad Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Goswami [Thakura].*
- This is a symptom of someone who has no material identification. He realizes that he is the servant of the Lord (Hari), of the Lord's representative (Guru), and the humble servant of the Vaishnavas. "This is our only identity, this is our real identity." All other identities are constantly changing from one birth to the next, keeping us bewildered by the material illusion.

## Formally Severing Material Attachments — Yet Not Judging a Vaishnava

- When we come to Krishna consciousness, we attempt to give up all that false identification. The initiation ceremony is the point where we formally sever our attachments to the material world.
- It is understood that some of those attachments still remain in the heart, but as long as we focus on the new, real attachments awakening in our heart — to serve Krishna, to serve our Guru, to serve the Vaishnavas — that is what matters.
- Although those material attachments are still somewhat there — we still carry some of our American nature, some of our Polish habits, some of our Canadian conditioning, etc. — a Vaishnava should not be judged by that.
  - Because of the body or the subtle body (the mind), material identification may sometimes appear, but one should not judge a Vaishnava by his material identification.
  - That Vaishnava has formally renounced those things at the moment of initiation. We should respect the fact that the Vaishnava is identifying himself as an eternal servant of Krishna and is focusing on those [devotional] desires. Old desires may pop up sometimes, but we should not judge a Vaishnava.

## Analogy: The Moon and Its Spots

- The moon is in the sky; the sky is very dark, but by the mercy of the moon there is light — we can see even in the nighttime, especially at the full moon.
- The moonshine also gives taste to the vegetables. Otherwise all vegetables would taste the same; they have their unique tastes by the mercy of the moonshine. So the moon is doing so many wonderful things.
- Yet someone may look at the moon and say, "Look — it has some little pock marks on it; it's got a rabbit in the moon." (The speaker is always trying to point out the rabbit in the moon to Sri Prahlad, who to this day hasn't seen it — but there are marks, craters, some darkness there.)
- Similarly, although a Vaishnava comes from a particular land, has a particular body, or has some habits and conditioning from the past, we should not criticize a devotee because of that. We should consider that this devotee has surrendered his life to Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava, and respect him for that. **This is Vaishnava culture.**

## "Let the Past Sleep Forever"

- We are not concerned about our past; we are concerned about the future.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** says, "Let the past sleep forever." Now we look to the future — becoming Krishna conscious.
- The initiation ceremony is the point where we cut those bonds of material existence, and part of that process is changing the name.

## The Rarity and Value of the Spiritual Father

- We give up the name given to us by our material father and mother, whom we respect — but everyone gets a material mother and father in this world. What is really rare is to get a **spiritual father.**
- Every living being has strong attachments to the maternal mother and father, brothers and sisters. How much more emphasis, then, should be placed on the spiritual master — the spiritual father — who gives the **bhakti-lata-bija** (the seed of devotional service), which, if cultivated properly, ensures that we get out of this **"duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam"** (the temporary abode full of miseries) and go back home, back to Godhead, for a life of eternity, knowledge, and bliss.
- We are indebted to our maternal mother and father — they have given us the opportunity of the human form of life. But the spiritual master is the one who infuses within us that sense of devotion to God, which is an eternal reality.
- So this is a very important day — the disciple takes it very seriously, and the guru takes it very seriously as well.

## Why This Day Is Especially Auspicious: The Panihati Festival

- This is a very auspicious day to take initiation because it is the day on which **Raghunatha Dasa Goswami received the mercy of Lord Nityananda.**

## Lord Nityananda as the Original Spiritual Master

- Lord Nityananda is considered the original spiritual master. He is Lord Balarama, and Balarama is often referred to as the original spiritual master.
- Sometimes **Srimati Radhika** is seen as the spiritual master (the spiritual master being the representative of Radhika), because without Radhika's mercy we cannot enter into Vraja-lila, the service of Radha and Krishna. She recommends the candidate for service to Krishna — and Lord Nityananda also performs the same service.
- Without the mercy of Lord Nityananda we cannot get the mercy of Lord Chaitanya; and according to **Narottama Dasa Thakura**, without the mercy of Lord Chaitanya we cannot begin to get the mercy of Radha and Krishna.
- **Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami** — a disciple of Lord Nityananda — even goes so far as to say (in Chaitanya-caritamrita) that those who are not devotees of Lord Chaitanya but claim to be devotees of Krishna have a demoniac mentality. Because only by the mercy of the *yuga-avatara* can we understand the Lord. Through each *yuga-avatara*, by following him and the process he gives, we become Krishna conscious — a particular process for that age.

## Raghunatha Dasa Goswami: An Eternally Liberated Associate We Can Learn From

- Raghunatha Dasa Goswami is a *manjari*, a very intimate associate of Radha and Krishna in the spiritual world. When the Lord comes to the material world, He brings His intimate associates to perform His lila, and through their practical lives we can understand how to be Krishna conscious.
- We can identify with Raghunatha in the sense of learning from his practical life. But we cannot say, "We are equal to Raghunatha Dasa Goswami" — no.
  - He is an eternally liberated soul, *nitya-mukta*. We are *nitya-baddha* — we have been here since time immemorial.
  - We can never even come close to his devotion — the love he has for his beloved Radha and Krishna. But we can identify with him in the sense that it is by his mercy [that we advance].

## The Story of Raghunatha Dasa: A Boy with the Renunciation of a Sadhu

- Even as a young boy, Raghunatha displayed a strong sense of renunciation and became more and more determined. His parents grew worried (similar to the pastime of Narottama Dasa Thakura): "Our young Raghunatha is not an ordinary boy — this boy is like a sadhu. We don't want him to be a sadhu, because if he becomes a sadhu, the family lineage is no longer continued, since he doesn't marry."
- This is common in India: although families appreciate sadhus, if their own child shows a tendency toward becoming one, they become anxious — "If he leaves home and becomes a sadhu, who will take over the family business, who will produce children and grandchildren, who will continue our lineage and dynasty?"

## The Illusory Energy's Ultimate Trick: A Beautiful Marriage

- His father was very wealthy and employed guards to keep Raghunatha at home, so he would not go join the Sankirtana movement of Lord Chaitanya and Lord Nityananda.
- What is more, they tried "the ultimate trick of the illusory energy": they married him to a very beautiful young girl, qualified in so many ways — very beautiful, very shy, very chaste, well-trained, knowing the proper art of being a wife.
  - On the absolute platform, this attraction between male and female is the basic principle of material existence, which ties together the hearts of the man and the woman.
  - But Raghunatha Dasa Goswami had no aspirations for anything material — he wanted to give his heart to Lord Chaitanya and Lord Nityananda. Though obliged by his parents and married to this beautiful girl, he was not in the least attracted, and was always thinking, "How am I going to be delivered from this ditch of materialistic life?"

## Being in "the Ditch" Without Discouragement

- Sometimes the Shastra speaks very strongly, but we shouldn't become discouraged because we are "in the ditch" — we are all in the ditch.
- It is not meant to demean men, women, or even our attachments to this world, because (as said earlier) the real attachment of a devotee, in his heart, is to Hari, Guru, and Vaishnava.

## Everything Material Turns Sour

- Raghunatha was not interested in the gradual process of Krishna consciousness. He fully realized how entangled he was in material life and how, as a result of that entanglement, he was suffering.
- Because whatever you become attached to in the material world ultimately turns sour:
  - The beauty of a rose fades.
  - A fruit, although very nice, turns rotten if left for some time.
- Whatever is beautiful or attractive in this world is only temporary — only a reflection of the real, eternal beauty of the spiritual world.
- Raghunatha realized this so deeply that he was determined to leave home, but he couldn't figure out how, because his father had married him and there were big, powerful guards. Still, he was very determined in his heart, praying to Lord Chaitanya: "Please deliver me from this ditch of material existence, so that I can be free to serve your lotus feet."

## The Lord Reciprocates with Our Sincere Desire

- This is the process: we express our desire to the Lord, and the Lord reciprocates. He knows the true desire of every living being.
- If we are really praying to become detached, really praying to become attached to Krishna, He knows the quality of that prayer and reacts accordingly.

## Story: Prabhupada Teaches How to Pray (Vrindavan, 1976)

- In 1976, Srila Prabhupada was on a morning walk in Vrindavan with his disciples, who were coming forward with so many problems — in management and in their personal lives.
- Prabhupada said, "You have to pray to Krishna to help you." And he taught how to pray to Gaura-Nitai:
  - "You fold your hands, you bow your head, and you say: 'Dear Sirs, this is the problem.'"
  - You come before Gaura-Nitai, say "Dear Sirs, this is the problem," and explain the problem — and Gaura-Nitai will take care of the rest. Prabhupada said this on the morning walk.

## Lord Nityananda Comes Near — the Village of Panihati

- Raghunatha Dasa revealed his heart to the Lord, and within a very short time, Lord Nityananda — the bearer of Krishna's mercy — happened to be performing His pastimes with His very intimate associates in a village very near where Raghunatha was. That village is called **Panihati.**
- On the role of the spiritual master: The spiritual master is the one who brings Krishna's mercy to us; he is the one who delivers us to Krishna. Krishna sends the spiritual master.
  - **"yasya prasādād bhagavat-prasādo…"** — the Lord sends the guru, and the guru takes us home to Krishna.

## Lord Nityananda's Order: Feed the Vaishnavas

- Raghunatha had to ask permission to do anything — his parents were controlling him so much, afraid he might leave. He got special permission to go see the saintly person, Lord Nityananda.
- He went, and fell at the feet of Lord Nityananda. Lord Nityananda dragged him close, put His lotus feet on the head of Raghunatha Dasa, and said: "I have this very important service that I want you [to do]. You must do it."
  - "What is that, sir?"
  - "I want you to feed all these Vaishnavas. Get some chipped rice, some yogurt, some sweetmeats, some delicious foodstuffs, and feed all of these Vaishnavas. That will please me very much."

## Money as Lakshmi, to Be Used in the Lord's Service

- Coming from a wealthy family, Raghunatha took that money and employed it in the service of Lord Nityananda and the Vaishnavas. This is what money is actually meant to be used for.
- Money represents **Lakshmi Devi.** We should not take Lakshmi and use her separately from the Lord — just as Ravana tried to separate Sita from Rama. Sita is an expansion of the goddess of fortune, meant to be used in the glorification of the Lord.

## The Panihati Festival: Blessed by Chaitanya, Nityananda, and the Vaishnavas

- Raghunatha took a lot of his father's gold coins and arranged this wonderful festival.
- Lord Chaitanya was so pleased that Raghunatha was serving Lord Nityananda and feeding all the Vaishnavas delicious prasad, that Lord Chaitanya personally came to the festival.
  - He enjoyed many eating pastimes with Lord Nityananda — they were putting prasad into each other's mouths — while Raghunatha fed all the devotees.
  - All the Vaishnavas were very pleased and offered their blessings.
- So Lord Chaitanya blessed him, Lord Nityananda blessed him, and the Vaishnavas blessed him. **If you have the blessings of God, the spiritual master, and the devotees, your spiritual life — your perfection in spiritual life — is assured.**

## "Surely Your Desires Will Be Fulfilled"

- After the ceremony, the next day, Raghunatha (not yet a Goswami) came and again bowed down before Lord Nityananda, who again placed His lotus feet upon his head.
- Raghunatha expressed his desire: "I want to be delivered from material existence. I need the mercy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. I want to join the sankirtana movement."
- Lord Nityananda said: "Surely your desires will be fulfilled."
- Reflections: "Elegance is truth spoken concisely." "Man proposes and God disposes" — we just have to know what to propose to Krishna. We don't want to further entangle ourselves in the material world by approaching God for some material blessing.

## The Mood of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu: Wanting Nothing but Devotion

- In the mood of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and His associates:
  - **"na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ kavitāṁ vā jagad-īśa kāmaye / mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi"** — "What do I want from you, my Lord? I don't want money, I don't want the association of the opposite sex, I don't want a following, I don't want name or fame."
  - The Lord, surprised: "This is very unusual — everyone else approaches me for all these things. What is it that you want? You don't even want liberation?"
  - "No — *mama janmani janmani*, birth after birth; I'm not even asking you for liberation. *Bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi* — I only want pure devotional service to your lotus feet."
- This was the prayer of Raghunatha Dasa Goswami.

## Raghunatha's Escape: Yadunandana Acharya and the Deity Service

- By the mercy of the Lord, in a very short time Raghunatha was delivered from his household situation.
- It so happened that his spiritual master at home, **Yadunandana Acharya** — the family priest, by whom Raghunatha had been initiated — came to the compound where Raghunatha Dasa's family was living.
  - Yadunandana Acharya was in charge of a temple where a deity was being worshiped, but the brahmana pujari had irresponsibly left that service, so he was looking for some qualified person to worship the deity.
  - He saw his own disciple Raghunatha: "Can you do that service?" Raghunatha said, "Your wish is my command."

## The Guru's Wish Is the Life and Soul of the Disciple

- Whatever the guru wishes becomes the life and soul of the disciple. Whether a small desire or a big desire, whatever responsibility the guru entrusts to the disciple becomes his sole mission, his sole purpose of existence — the order of his guru.
- **Srila Prabhupada** demonstrated this perfectly. He received a few words of instruction: "You preach this Krishna conscious movement in the English-speaking language; if you ever get money, you print books." Based on those few instructions, Prabhupada's whole life evolved — his whole life was just an effort to fulfill the desires of his guru, which were the desires of Krishna.

## Slipping Away to Chaitanya Mahaprabhu

- Raghunatha said yes, and they walked off out of the compound (which he generally could not leave). Yadunandana Acharya told the guards (most of whom were sleeping) that Raghunatha was just going to do a little pujari work and would be right back.
- As soon as they got into a somewhat isolated area, Raghunatha said, "Prabhu-ji, everything is okay — I'll do this puja." So Yadunandana Acharya left.
- Immediately, Raghunatha took to the woods, to the jungle, and walked and walked and walked until he reached the place where Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was present.
- He fell down at the lotus feet of Lord Chaitanya and begged His mercy to accept him as His eternal servant — which Chaitanya Mahaprabhu did.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu placed him into the care of **Svarupa Damodara Goswami** to train him, and Raghunatha Dasa Goswami received so many nice instructions.

## Conclusion: A Perfect Day for Initiation

- This is a very special day — the day that Raghunatha Dasa Goswami got the feet of Lord Nityananda upon his head, delivering him from material existence. He expressed his desire, but it was by the mercy of Lord Nityananda, the devotees, and Lord Chaitanya that he got delivered.
- So this is a perfect day for taking initiation, because it embodies the whole spirit of initiation:
  - With full humility and folded hands, we approach the representative of the guru-parampara, fall at his feet, and say: "Please accept me as your servant. I promise to follow all these rules and regulations, to chant the Harinam maha-mantra, and to give my life, as best I can, to your service — in the service of the guru-parampara, Lord Nityananda, Lord Chaitanya, and ultimately Sri Sri Gandharvika-Giridhari."
- This day is full of so much transcendental potency, and the speaker is sure it will work to deliver both candidates for initiation from the mire of material existence, ultimately to the beautiful abode of the Lord, Sri Vrindavan Dham, in the spiritual sky.

## Closing Invocations and Handover

- Sri Panihati Festival ki jai; Raghunatha Dasa Goswami ki jai; Lord Nityananda Prabhu ki jai; Sri Gauranga Mahaprabhu ki jai; Sri Guru-parampara ki jai; Hare Krishna maha-mantra ki jai; Gaura Premanande, Hari Haribol.
- Now Sri Prahlad is asked to conduct the procedures of the initiation ceremony — the mantras, the neck beads, and all the intricacies in which he is so expert. Sri Prahlad Prabhu ki jai.
