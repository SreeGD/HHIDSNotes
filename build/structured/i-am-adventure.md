## The Verse: Bhagavad-gita 10.36

- **"dyutam chalayatam asmi tejas tejasvinam aham / jayo 'smi vyavasayo 'smi sattvam sattvavatam aham"** (*Bhagavad-gita 10.36*).
- **Translation** (by His Divine Grace Srila A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada): "I am also the gambling of cheats, and of the splendid I am the splendor. I am victory, I am adventure, and I am the strength of the strong."

### Prabhupada's Purport (as read)

- There are many kinds of cheaters all over the universe. Of all cheating processes, **gambling stands supreme**, and therefore it represents Krishna.
- As the Supreme, Krishna can be more deceitful than any mere man. If Krishna chooses to deceive a person, no one can surpass Him in His deceit. His greatness is not one-sided — it is all-sided.
- Among the victorious, He is victory; He is the splendor of the splendid; among the enterprising and industrious, He is the most enterprising and industrious; among adventurers, He is the most adventurous; and among the strong, He is the strongest.
- When Krishna was present on this earth, no one could surpass Him in strength — even in His childhood He lifted the **Govardhan Hill.** No one can surpass Him in cheating, in splendor, in victory, in enterprise, or in strength.
- (After re-reading the verse, the speaker jokes: "Any questions?")

## When an Important Person Speaks, People Listen

- This is a very significant verse — though of course all 700 verses of the Bhagavad-gita are significant, because they come from the lotus mouth of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- Even in this world, if an important person speaks, everyone listens — even the opposition, to hear what they have to say.
  - **Example:** the Delhi elections — "the police lady" and the other man; the *Times of India* is full of them, every page. But they are important only for now; like glowworms in the night, when the sun rises their importance fades.
- When an important person speaks, people listen, because we all want to improve our lives. We are never satisfied, whatever state we are in, until we reach perfection — which, in our philosophy, is when we become pure devotees of the Lord, eternal servants of Krishna. The speaker is always interested in hearing from significant personalities how to improve life.

### The Most Important Person Is God

- Of all important persons, the most important is God. You could go up the chain — there is always somebody more important. We go through the *devatas* (33 crore of devatas), but ultimately you arrive at the Supreme Ishvara, Krishna.
- Out of all these eternal beings, there is one — *Eka* — who is fulfilling the desires of everyone else. Even Brahma's desires are fulfilled by Krishna; even Shiva's desires are fulfilled by Krishna. (Echoes the *Katha/Svetasvatara Upanishad* — **"nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman"** — the one eternal being among many who supplies the needs of all.) He is the Supreme Ishvara.
- When He speaks, everyone should listen, because He speaks only for our benefit. Many people speak to cheat us, to manipulate us, to get our money; but when Krishna, the all-good Personality of Godhead, speaks, it is ultimately for the welfare of every living entity.

### The Gita Gives Concise Spiritual Knowledge

- He gives concise spiritual knowledge in His Gita. The Vedas are there — the Lord's arrangement, for the intelligent man to gradually read, practice, and become enlightened. But in these days we have hardly any time to read; if we read, we read the newspaper. How valuable is the newspaper?

### Story: The Four Newspapers (Delhi to Mumbai)

- Flying from Delhi to Mumbai, as soon as the plane started to taxi down the runway, it was as if automatic — all the people put a newspaper in front of their faces, like a machine, "you push a button."
- What surprised the speaker even more: after a few minutes they put the newspaper down and picked another out of the pocket in front of them. He thinks they each had four newspapers — the same news. *"Punah, punah, punah"* (again, again, again). This is called **"chewing the chewed."**
  - It reminded him of chewing gum as a kid: he'd take it out when tired and stick it under the desk, then chew it again a few hours later. ("I didn't go to Gurukul. I had one piece of gum I chewed for three weeks.")
- What do they do with the newspaper when finished? They throw it away. **How valuable is something you throw away?** You are very careful about what you choose to throw in the waste bin; even if something has only a little sentimental value, you keep it — people stuff items in the attic or cellar for years. If you throw something away, it has no significance.
- Every day, garbage dumps are full of newspapers everywhere. But **who throws away the Gita?** Only a crazy person would throw away the Gita — because inherently we know there is something important there. It is supreme knowledge.

### Raja-Vidya: The King of Knowledge (BG 9.2)

- **"raja-vidya raja-guhyam"** (*Bhagavad-gita 9.2* — the king of education, the king of confidential knowledge). We need to be educated, because we are born into this world totally ignorant, materially and spiritually. Of all types of education, the most important is Bhagavad-gita.
- Interestingly, the Bhagavad-gita teaches us the best way to live in this world while at the same time getting out of this world. That is Vedic knowledge — the best way to live within this world so that you can meet your material needs as easily as possible and have time for the **Yuga Dharma** (chanting Hare Krishna).

### The Gita Like a Crystal

- We could have opened the book and spoken from any verse — it is all relevant, and we could dissect it from different angles, because the same verse can be understood in a Krishna conscious way from various angles, **like a crystal.** You put a crystal before a light and it is very beautiful: a green color comes out, a yellow, a blue, a purple — from one stone, so much beauty emanates.
- From the Gita's 700 verses, they have been analyzed, studied, dissected, elaborated upon, and illuminated by great devotees since time immemorial. The speaker was going to say 5,000 years, but of course this Gita was originally spoken to the sun god (**Bhagavad-gita 4.1** — "imam vivasvate yogam..."). How far back in time does that go? So it is very valuable knowledge.

## Choosing This Verse: Krishna Is Adventure

- The speaker chose this verse because he has not spoken on it before. It is easy to come to India and speak on **"yada yada hi dharmasya glanir bhavati bharata"** (*Bhagavad-gita 4.7*) — "I could speak in my sleep about that verse." But here is a very interesting group of philosophical points that Krishna makes in this chapter (the *Opulence of the Absolute*), showing Arjuna how He can be seen and appreciated even within this material nature.
- One particular statement is that **He is adventure.** This makes our ears perk up. We are all interested in adventure — to experience our own adventures in this world, or to hear about others' adventurous deeds. We love to hear about adventure in the form of theater, novels, movies. Why? Because adventure creates a little excitement in our boring lives. It defies the very principle of material existence.

### The Principle of Material Existence: Boredom

- **Survey:** the speaker read of a survey done in America that concluded people are happy only 5% of the time, unhappy or miserable 5% of the time, and **bored 90% of the time.** "That sounds like a shloka — or at least a niti-shastra from Chanakya Pandit."
- That survey may not reflect your lives, but it is a fact that we get caught up in the routine of life:
  - As students — by the time you graduate, it feels as if you were a student for 20 years; the same thing every day.
  - Or working, with a career — every day the same shop, factory, or whatever.
  - Or family life — the same people.
- Life can get into a rut, into a routine, and we hanker for some excitement, some adventure. At least when the weekend comes we want to get away, do something a little different. This spirit of adventure is there within all of us.

### But This World Is No Place for a Vacation (BG 8.16)

- Krishna makes it clear elsewhere in the Bhagavad-gita that in this material world there is no ideal place for a vacation or an adventure. What is the nature of this world? The Lord is speaking, so we should take it to heart — there is no discussion or debate when the Lord speaks; He is the supreme authority.
- **"abrahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna / mam upetya tu kaunteya punar janma na vidyate"** (*Bhagavad-gita 8.16* — from the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery wherein repeated birth and death take place; but one who attains My abode, O son of Kunti, never takes birth again).
  - "I will go and have a great adventure in Tahiti. I will go to Australia. I will go to the Himalayas." — "Oh, wait a minute," Krishna says: from the highest planet down to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death take place. (Even Mumbai is covered — "it's not mentioned, but indirectly you can take it.") But one who attains My abode never takes birth again.
- So how can you have a real vacation, a real adventure, in this world? It is like saying, "I'm going to go to the prison and have an adventure."

### Guru Maharaja's Advice: "Take Sannyasa"

- A sannyasi's business is to move around and preach Krishna consciousness — "a rolling stone gathers no moss." Sannyasis have to keep on the move, and there is a need for us to be reminded about the importance of spiritual life, so we go to hear the sadhus.
- The speaker's Guru Maharaja, **Srila Prabhupada**, left India at the ripe old age of around 70 and went to America. But he didn't just stop in America — he **circled the globe 12 times in 10 years.** This is *Parivrajakacharya* — a saintly, wandering person, a *Paramahamsa*, a swan-like devotee of the Lord. He went everywhere — most of the first- and second-world countries; places that would make a person in Mumbai drool: Switzerland (the Eiffel Tower), London (Big Ben), America (the Rocky Mountains).
- Prabhupada came back and said to the devotees: "I've been everywhere. I've seen everything." So one devotee asked, "Guruji, what is your advice?" He said: **"Take sannyasa."**

### Bengali Proverb: Wet Stool, Dry Stool

- We think, "Oh, here or there" (one place is good, one place is not so good). But in Bengal they have a proverb: **"wet stool, dry stool."** If I step in wet stool, that's very bad; if I step in dry stool, it's not so bad. So we think this place is good and that place is not so good.
- But because Krishna said it is a place of repeated birth and death (*abrahma-bhuvanal lokah*), He also says elsewhere: **"duhkhalayam asasvatam"** (*Bhagavad-gita 8.15* — [this world is] temporary and full of misery). You may have your opinion, but if you are realized — if you see according to Guru, Shastra, and Sadhu, with clear, spiritual vision — it is *duhkhalayam asasvatam.*

### Pleasure Is Merely the Absence of Pain

- The speaker's spiritual master once said: "Pleasure in this world — what is it? **The absence of pain.**" (The speaker, 19 years old, was listening: "Yeah, what is it? Maybe I made a mistake — I can go back out.")
- Just like when you have a migraine headache — boom, boom, boom — and it stops, and you think, "Ah, ecstasy." What is that? That's foolishness. What kind of happiness is the mere cessation of pain?
- We are not naive or fanatic — we know there is some pleasure in this world; this is the world of duality. There is springtime, the moonrise, the beauty of the sun, the family, the bonus you get, the promotion — some bright moments. But the idea is that **it is not enough to satisfy the soul.**

### We Are the Soul, Not the Body (BG 2.20)

- We are not the body; we are the soul within the body. **"na jayate mriyate va kadacin..."** (*Bhagavad-gita 2.20* — for the soul there is never birth nor death; having once been, he never ceases to be; he is unborn, undying, primeval; he is not slain when the body is slain). So we are the soul, and the soul has to be satisfied.

### Analogy: The One Drop of Water in the Desert

- If you try to satisfy the soul by fulfilling your material desires, you won't be satisfied. **Prabhupada's analogy:** it will be like a man dying of thirst in the desert. It's a horrible way to die — dehydrated. (The body is actually 72% liquid — "if any of you are studying medicine..." — so dehydration is the most excruciating death.)
- A man is dying of thirst; someone comes along with a camel and finds him: "What do you need?" — "Water! Water!" The man has a big barrel of fresh, clear rainwater. He brings it over, dips his finger in, takes one drop, and says, "Stick your tongue out. There." Then he gets back on the camel. — "Wait a minute! Water! I gave you water. One drop. No, I need gallons, liters of water! — Sorry, bye-bye."
- That is material life. We have to be honest with ourselves. We may have our dreams and hopes, but what really happens when we try to fulfill our desires is that they don't get fulfilled — because it is only the **senses** that are getting fulfilled, not the soul.

### Return to Our Natural State (Analogy: Water)

- We will be satisfied only when we come back to our original nature — the nature of servants of God. We have to be natural.
- Just as water's natural state is liquid: extreme heat turns it to vapor, extreme cold freezes it — but those are the extremes; the natural state of water is liquid. When it's frozen (as in winter in the West), the natural temperature returns in spring and the ice becomes liquid again; and if it's extremely hot, when it cools the water becomes liquid.
- Same with us: we are in so many extreme conditions of life, bodily concepts — "I am this body, I'm a man, I'm a woman, I'm young, I'm old, I'm American, I'm Indian, I'm French, I'm German." That is the wrong conception. The original conception is **"jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya-dasa"** (*Chaitanya-caritamrita* — the living entity's constitutional nature is to be the eternal servant of Krishna). This is our natural state — to be eternal servants of God.

### Satisfaction Through Devotional Service (SB 1.2.6)

- When you come to that natural state and serve God, then there is *santushti* (satisfaction). **"sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhoksaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati"** (*Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6* — the supreme occupation for all mankind is that by which they can attain to loving service to the transcendent Lord; such service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self).
- Any selfish desire — to fulfill the demands of my senses — will not make us happy, no matter how much we try. But make a little effort to serve God, which is our natural position.
- **"patram puspam phalam toyam..."** (*Bhagavad-gita 9.26* — offer Me with love a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water). It doesn't take much to come back to your natural state. Offer that to Krishna, and you'll be perfectly happy.
- Which means you can do anything in this world and be happy if you do it for God: take care of cows for the Lord; cook and offer it to the Lord; do business, make lots of money, and give 50% to the temple. This is devotional service to God.

### Story: The Businessman's Check and the Sweeper's Rose

- One time here in Mumbai, Guru Maharaja Prabhupada was staying in his unfinished quarters at **Juhu Beach.** A big Marwari businessman, very wealthy, came in. He wrote Prabhupada a check for 1,000 US dollars, put it on the table, and was a little offended when Prabhupada just looked at it — "I've given you $1,000." Prabhupada said: **"God is not looking at what you're putting on the table. He's looking at what you're keeping in your pocket."**
- Later that evening, a sweeper lady came — a very humble person, sweeping the halls. She had found a beautiful rose (given or picked), and she brought it in with all humility at her command, out of devotion for Guruji and appreciation for Prabhupada's position as a great saintly devotee. She came in shaking, with a one- or two-paisa rose, put it on the table in front of Prabhupada, and paid her obeisances. Prabhupada patted her head, chanted "Hare Krishna," and quoted Krishna: **"patram puspam phalam toyam"** — one can offer Me with love a fruit, a flower, a leaf; these things I accept.

### Spiritualize All Activities (BG 9.27)

- The point: if we change our conception of life and see ourselves as servants of God, using our body to serve God, then the soul is satisfied.
- **"yat karosi yad asnasi..."** (*Bhagavad-gita 9.27* — O son of Kunti, all that you do, all that you eat, all that you offer and give away, as well as all austerities you perform, should be done as an offering unto Me). In other words, we can spiritualize all activities in the service of God. We don't have to be yogis who go to the Himalayas; we can spiritualize whatever we do — **including adventure.** We can be adventurous in the service of the Lord.

## Dovetailing the Desire for Adventure

- How do we fulfill this desire for adventure? Not by being "footloose and fancy-free," just wandering around the world looking for sense gratification. Not that type of adventure — but we can be adventurous in the service of Krishna, dovetailing our desire for adventure in the Lord's service.

### The Speaker's Adventurous Youth

- When the speaker was young in America, he had an adventurous spirit, because that is how Americans are brought up — to be your own man, a self-made man, to get out and conquer the world.
- By the age of 14 he was traveling outside his country — to the forests of Canada, and south to Mexico. He was a surfer, surfing all the desolate beaches in Mexico.
- Eventually he joined the **Marine Corps** ("America's finest fighting force — better than the sailors, the army, and the air force; the Marines are the first ones on the beaches"), to serve his country and see the world. But he soon became very disappointed with the war in Vietnam — it didn't make sense; it was all political maneuvering. He saw through it and became disillusioned.
- He decided to go to Europe — that too was a disappointment. So he thought, "I have to go to India." In America they say, "Go west, young man," for material progress. But he already knew (in the 1960s there was a little association with India through various sannyasis, yogis, and books coming) — "No, **go east, young man**, for spiritual enlightenment."
- But he got robbed and lost all his money in Europe and came back to America. His good fortune, however, was that later he met his spiritual master, **Srila Prabhupada**, who — seeing his restless nature and desire to be adventurous — engaged him in various adventurous activities in spreading Krishna consciousness, because that is the adventure of the Lord.

### The Lord's Adventure (BG 4.8)

- What is the Lord's adventure in this world? He says He is adventure. **"paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya ca duskrtam / dharma-samsthapanarthaya sambhavami yuge yuge"** (*Bhagavad-gita 4.8* — to deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants, and reestablish the principles of religion, I appear millennium after millennium).
- He is adventuresome — He leaves Vaikuntha to come to this world to deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants, and reestablish the principles of religion. Prabhupada said He is very adventuresome — He travels a long way to do daring things: kill Kamsa, kill Ravana, kill Hiranyakashipu, dance with other men's wives in the *rasa-lila.*
- You can be sure you are taking the right adventuresome path if you take up the same mission as the Lord. You become adventurous not for your own personal self, but for the pleasure of the Lord — helping the Lord to deliver the pious, to annihilate the miscreants, and to reestablish *dharma* in the land of dharma, **Bharat.**
- This is the land of dharma. But dharma is quickly disappearing from this country, because material progress is becoming rampant and we are losing our culture. If India loses its spiritual culture, there is really no hope for the world — because India has the most advanced, highest, most sophisticated spiritual culture that mankind has ever known, and if it disappears it will be a great loss for mankind.
- All of you can become adventuresome — by perfecting yourself in Krishna consciousness and then going out and reviving dharma in the land of dharma. The real spirit of adventure is taking up the adventures of the Lord, helping Him spread dharma in the age of Kali.

### Story: Raising His Hand to Go to France (Detroit, 1971)

- Prabhupada saw that. In 1971 Prabhupada came to our temple in **Detroit** and said he wanted a couple of good men to go to Europe to open a center in France. The speaker raised his hand — then afterward thought, "Wait a minute, what did I do? I don't speak a word of French, I'm only 19 years old, I don't know anything, and I'm going to go to France and open a temple?"
- But that evening a devotee gave a class and said: **"Along with the instructions of the spiritual master comes the ability to execute it."** This is the mystery of Krishna consciousness — we are never acting in our own strength; we are acting on the strength of the mercy of the Guru and the mercy of Krishna. So he went, and began a new phase of Krishna consciousness in his life.

### There Is Always Opposition

- To do as the Lord does, there is always some opposition. When Krishna came to this world 5,000 years ago, it wasn't easy — He didn't just sit under a tree and start preaching Bhagavad-gita. There was much opposition from the very beginning.
  - As a baby, opposition came in the form of **Putana, Trinavarta, Dhenukasura, Aghasura** — all sent by the evil tyrant **Kamsa.**
  - Even after Kamsa was killed, there was **Jarasandha, Shishupala, Dantavakra.**
  - When the Lord came as Ramachandra, there was **Ravana and Kumbhakarna.**
  - When the Lord came as Nrsimhadeva, there was **Hiranyakashipu.**
- There is always opposition, because this is the world of forgetfulness — the place to which those souls who want to reject God are sent. We are a very infinitesimal part of God's creation. All living entities emanate from the Lord and are very happy serving Him in the spiritual world; only a small fraction are so foolish as to turn their face from the Lord, thinking they can be happy without Him, and come to the material world. This is a place where people are determined to forget God: "One convinced against his will is of the same opinion still."
- To revive Krishna consciousness in this world is the greatest challenge — no easy thing.

### Krishna Protects the Devotee Who Takes Up His Mission

- Lord Krishna had so much opposition, but of course He was victorious, because He is the Lord. So we should understand that if we take up this adventuresome spirit, in the same mood, in the footsteps of the Lord, it may not be so easy — but at the same time, Krishna offers special protection to such devotees. He empowers them (number one, because they are doing His mission), and He protects them.
- **"rakhe krsna mare ke, mare krsna rakhe ke"** (Bengali saying — if Krishna wants to kill you, no one can save you; and if Krishna wants to save you, no one can kill you). The perfect example is **Prahlada Maharaja.**
- These pastimes repeat era after era, millennium after millennium, time and time again in the history of the world. In this age, Krishna appeared as **Lord Chaitanya**, the most recent incarnation of God, and He brought the Yuga Dharma of chanting the holy names of the Lord. So His pastimes are taking place in modern times through the members of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, spreading the chanting of Hare Krishna.
- The adventure is to become trained up in the science of Krishna consciousness, go out, and by the Lord's mercy receive some power to preach Krishna consciousness and feel the protection of the Lord. **This is adventure.**

## The Marseille Story: The Motorcycle Gang and the Gypsies

*(Example is higher than precept.)*

### The Hell's Angels Encircle the Sankirtan Party

- Very early in the preaching in France, the speaker took a Sankirtan party of ten boys (brahmacharis) to the south of France, to a place called **Marseille** — a rough-and-tumble area with a lot of immigrants who were poor, dissatisfied, looking for work, unengaged; a place of crime. Preaching, distributing books, and chanting there was difficult.
- One day they were chanting very blissfully along the main promenade; some people were stopping, some clapping, and they were selling a few books. Suddenly a motorcycle gang of **Hell's Angels** came — an infamous motorcycle gang. Not on little scooters, but big bikes you can hear ten blocks down the road. These men wear big leather jackets, are covered in tattoos, have big earrings, one eye that works, mustaches, scars all over their bodies; they carry knives, daggers, pistols; they fight and are engaged in organized crime, and they like to cause trouble.
- There were ten (later maybe fifteen) of them, and they wanted to make fun of the party. They rode their bikes up onto the curb, onto the boardwalk, and circled around and around the devotees. A big crowd gathered to see the fun — what they would do with these "poor little innocent skinny Hare Krishnas" (skinny brahmacharis, not so well-versed, scared out of their wits).
- They parked their big motorcycles in a ring around the ten devotees, got off, and started walking toward them with baseball bats, clubs, and **brass knuckles** (a thing you hold with brass balls on it, so that when you hit somebody it really hurts).

### Faith Under Test — Take Shelter of Nrsimhadeva

- Our philosophy is: if Krishna wants to kill you, no one can save you; and if Krishna wants to save you, no one can kill you. "I liked that part." So here is the real test of your faith — is it just something you repeat in class, or do you really have *shraddha*, do you really believe it?
- When we come into danger in this world, Prabhupada told us to take shelter of an incarnation of God — of **Lord Nrsimhadeva.** So the speaker (who was playing the drum and singing) started chanting.

#### Aside: Nrsimhadeva Is Not Mythology

- People think Nrsimhadeva is mythology. A couple of years ago the speaker did a program at a prestigious university in India. A boy said, "Swami, this is all fairy tales, this is all mythology, this didn't really happen — get real."
- The speaker replied: "Young man, do you think that **Sri Vyasadeva** — the compiler of the Vedas, a self-realized soul in full control of his mind and senses, respected through millennia by your forefathers — do you think he was wasting his time writing down fairy tales? He was writing **Purana** — the history of the universe as it is. What do you know? You're just a young 19-year-old boy; you've only experienced a little life here in Delhi." (He heard later that the boy became a devotee.)

#### Aside: "Scratch the Surface" (Chicago)

- One time Prabhupada told us in Chicago: "In my country, India, you just scratch the surface under anyone, and there's Krishna consciousness." All of you have this Krishna consciousness underneath.

### The Roly-Poly Cook Rolls Over the Leader

- Back to Marseille: the faith was tested. There was no way to run — they were encircled by heavy bikes, and the bikers were coming with leather jackets and clubs. The speaker closed his eyes and thought, "Well, maybe we better do something" — Krishna gives the intelligence.
- They had one big roly-poly boy, the cook (who "ate all the maha"). The speaker told him: "Come over here. This big guy is the leader. When I give the sign, you fall on the ground and roll — roll like [hell] — knock him over, then run and push his bike over, and then just keep running." The boy asked, "Maharaja, will Krishna protect us?" — "Yes." (The speaker was only 20 years old, a devotee for one year.)
- The guys came closer and closer. Halfway through the mantra, the speaker gave the sign. That boy followed orders — he dropped to the ground and, just like a steamroller, rolled (they never expected it) and actually knocked the main guy down. "There's our opening — go for it!" The cook, following the second instruction, knocked over the motorcycle, then ran so far away you couldn't even see him.
- They all ran, jumped over the main guy's body (he was trying to get up, but he'd hit the ground pretty hard — "a big bump here"), and ran down the crowded street.

### Hiding in the Bakery Attic

- The gang picked up their leader, parked their bikes on the side, and came looking for the party. It was a rainy day, so their footprints were there.
- The speaker went into a **bakery** and told the baker, "Look, we're in a really hot spot — we have some bad guys chasing us." The baker said, "Okay, get up in the attic and be quiet." They climbed a ladder into the attic.
- The gang went store to store. The speaker heard them come into the bakery: "Have you seen these weirdos with bald heads and bed sheets on, singing?" The baker said, "No, never seen him." — "You didn't hear him singing down the street?" — "You know, I'm half deaf; can you speak up a little bit?" That convinced them.
- Then they went into the butcher shop next door, and the **butcher** started arguing with them. He came out with his meat cleaver and said, "You leave those Hare Krishnas alone — they're good people." (The speaker, overhearing: "Jeez, a bad guy [a butcher] saying we're good people.") So they beat him up — "unfortunately, they had to get it out of their system" — and then went away, yelling, "We'll find them! We'll get them!"

### Camping by the River — The Gypsy Encampment

- The party stayed a few hours in the attic, then came down and made their way through the back alleys of Marseille. They had been staying in a hotel, but the speaker thought the gang might inquire and find them there, so he said, "Boys, we're going to camp outside the city next to the river; we won't have any problem there." They spent the day on the river — having class, chanting — planning to go to the next town the next day.
- That evening they went to sleep. At about one o'clock in the morning the speaker heard a big commotion — "Oh my God, they're back." He woke, looked about a hundred meters down the river, and saw an encampment with a big noise, and heard a devotee crying, "Help me!"
- He ran there — and lo and behold, it was a group of **gypsies** (wandering nomads). One of the boys had been walking that evening by the side of the river and had met a beautiful gypsy girl. ("I'm not going to go into details — just imagine in your mind for a few moments.") The brahmachari was not initiated and didn't have full control over his mind, and the girl was beautiful; they had gone back to the gypsy camp and disappeared into a tent, and the leader of the gypsies had found them.
- Gypsies are tough, unscrupulous people — resilient. They travel, are not much liked in Europe, and are always asked to move on. They had a dagger, a gun to the boy's head, and a noose, and were saying, "Which one do you want?"

### "We Are Brothers" — Making Peace

- The speaker came on the scene: "I'm the leader." — "You're going to get it too." — "Wait a minute, hold up, whoa. Let's have a powwow here. Let's have an *ista-gosthi*. We're brothers." They looked at him.
- "Yes — my teacher told me that the gypsy people originally came from India. **Prabhupada says the gypsies originated from India** — during some particularly chaotic time, that tribe left India and moved to Europe, and because they couldn't find a fixed position, they were always moving. So actually, we're brothers — we also come from India." — "No you don't." — "The people of India adopted me. I spend more time in India than in my own country (which is true), and I've taken the Indian name." — "What is your name?" — "**Indradyumna Swami.**" — "That sounds Indian." — "Look, we're brothers, and when there's a fight between brothers, it can be resolved." The leader said, "That makes sense — we're brothers."
- "This boy is new; he just joined a couple of weeks ago; he doesn't know our principles; he's not very strong; he's made a mistake — but it's better than a gun. We're brothers; give us another chance." The leader is thinking. "We have the same way of life — we have a camp down there; we're just like you guys: you like to sing and we like to sing; you like to dance and we like to dance; you like good food and we like good food." — "Yeah, our brothers. I saw you on the street; your singing is like our gypsy singing." — "Because we're brothers, Baba, we come from India."
- The leader said, "Sit down and have some dinner with us." The speaker looked — there was a sheep turning on the fire — and made an excuse ("little dysentery, belly-belly"), and got out of that.
- He thanked them for chastising the boy and asked to apologize to the whole gypsy village. They gathered everyone, and the speaker stood up in front of the whole camp — the women and everyone — and said, "I want to apologize on behalf of my community that one of our members has not acted properly. I can imagine sometimes members of your community don't act properly." — "Yeah, especially the teenagers." So they had some common ground. He embraced the leader, they shook hands, and the leader said, "It's all forgotten, don't worry." The speaker took the boy back by the ear ("I'm sorry I slapped you so hard, but we had to make a little show there"), and put him to bed: "I'll talk to you in the morning."

### The Motorcycle Gang Returns — Prabhupada's Tape

- Morning came (around 6:30–7 o'clock, the sun risen), and what woke them was the motorcycle gang, coming from the other direction. They had somehow found out where the party was and were coming with their motorcycles down to the river.
- The speaker thought, "It worked the first time; it's got to work the second time." He was reminded of a tape of Prabhupada.
- **Prabhupada's tape (Los Angeles, 1971):** Prabhupada was leaving America in 1971. He was ill, and his plan was to come to India for Ayurvedic treatment and then come back and continue his preaching in the West. He said to the devotees in Los Angeles, "I'm an old man; I may not come back." You couldn't hear a pin drop — the devotees were stunned that Prabhupada would say this. But then Prabhupada said: **"In my absence, Lord Nrsimhadeva will protect you."** And he said it with such confidence, such *vijnana*, such realization, that it resounded in the speaker's heart as a truth.
- How do we understand the higher principles, the philosophy, the realizations? When our spiritual master speaks, he speaks with realization; we want to come to that point.

### Our Scriptures Speak the Truth (Vedanta-sutra)

- This is actually a fact — it is not mythology. We are doing the work of the Lord. In His absence He comes, meets opposition, and defeats it; but in His absence the work still has to go on.
- Now that you've come to the human form of life, now is the time for **self-realization** — everything takes second place; education, career, retirement, old age must all be done. But the **Vedanta-sutra** speaks to us from the past: **"athato brahma jijnasa"** (now, therefore, one should inquire into Brahman/the Absolute) — your first business is the science of self-realization.
- Our scriptures speak to us; they speak the truth. So if we take up this mission of the Lord in His absence, we are going to meet opposition — but because we are doing the work of the Lord, He'll protect us. **"One who does good, my friend, is never overcome by evil"** (echoing *Bhagavad-gita 6.40* — "na hi kalyana-krt kascid durgatim tata gacchati").

### "Sing With Me" — The Nrsimha Marching Song

- Taking that to heart, the speaker said to the boys, "Come on, we're going to face them." They said, "Swamiji, look at them — they've got bigger baseball bats, bigger iron rods; one of them is carrying a torch with kerosene fire on it — they're going to burn us to death, put kerosene on us; look, one guy's got a machete."
- The speaker said, "Sing with me": **"tava kara-kamala-vare nakham adbhuta-srngam / dalita-hiranyakasipu-tanu-bhrngam / kesava dhrta-narahari-rupa jaya jagadisa hare"** (the Nrsimha verse from *Jayadeva's Dasavatara-stotra* — "O Keshava, O Lord of the universe, O Lord Hari, who have assumed the form of half-man, half-lion! All glories to You! Just as one easily crushes a wasp between one's fingernails, so the beautiful pointed nails of Your lotus hands tore apart the wasp-like body of the demon Hiranyakashipu").
- "It sounds like a march. If the International Society for Krishna Consciousness ever has to go to war, this will be our marching song."

### The Best Defense Is a Good Offense

- Like the **All Blacks** in New Zealand — before a football (rugby) game, they come out and do the *haka* (some kind of scary performance); the speaker has heard that sometimes the other teams cancel the game because of it.
- So this is what the devotees did — in their brahmana underwear (a few boys had a gamcha on), having just gotten out of bed ("it's like a nightmare"). The speaker lined up all ten — one skinny guy, then the fat guy (everyone looking at the fat guy, who was the most scared of everybody) — and they marched forward.
- The speaker recalled from his days as an athlete (American football) the words of the famous coach **Vince Lombardi**: **"The best defense is a good offense."**
- The gang was marching toward them, and the devotees marched faster toward the gang. The bikers went "woo" — "What? These little skinny guys in their underpants are coming at us, singing a marching song?" Our boys got the *shraddha*; they were empowered. Ten guys, "gumptious," with no weapons, sleepy-eyed, *sikhas* flying, not even having brushed their teeth — and the gang started backing away.
- "I couldn't believe it. I turned to the boy and said, 'You see — Nrsimhadeva.'" As they got closer, the bikers' faces went [pale]. "Chant louder!" The fat boy chanted loud, and the bikers dropped their weapons (which the devotees kept as **booty** — "war booty; I still have one in my home in Warsaw — the baseball bats on the wall, the meat cleavers there"), got on their bikes, and zoomed away.

### It Was the Gypsies Who Scared Them Off

- The devotees got together: "It works! *Jaya Nrsimhadeva!*" They were all patting each other on the back. But after about 30 or 40 seconds, watching the bikers go — "those cowards, those bullies" (my dad always told me that bullies are actually the scariest cats on the play field) — one boy said, "Maharaja, look behind us."
- The speaker looked behind, and **the whole gypsy village was right behind them**, marching with their meat cleavers and baseball bats; and the tallest gypsy (about six foot five, "this wide," scars everywhere) had a big noose. It was 20 or 30 men, with the ladies behind them too. It wasn't the devotees — it was the gypsies who scared the hell out of the motorcycle guys, and they never came back. (The speaker heard they rode their bikes all the way to Paris.)

### It Was All Krishna's Mercy (BG 9.22)

- The speaker told the boys: "It's the same thing — whether we do it or they do it, it's all Krishna's mercy. Krishna sent the solution to the problem; He inspired those men to come and protect us. How else could it happen?"
- In the service of the Lord, Krishna says: "For My devotee, I provide what he needs to do his service for Me; I protect what he has, and I provide whatever he lacks." (Echoes **Bhagavad-gita 9.22** — "yoga-ksemam vahamy aham" — I carry what My devotee lacks and preserve what he has.) At the moment of protection, Prabhupada said very clearly in that lecture: **"In my absence, Lord Nrsimhadeva will protect you"** — because we are taking on the service of the Lord.

### "When Did I Ever Say Preaching Is Easy?"

- One time Prabhupada told the speaker personally: he came back from Sankirtan after a difficult day, and Prabhupada asked, "How was it?" — "Prabhupada, it was difficult." Prabhupada slammed [the table]: **"When did I ever say that preaching is easy?"** — "You never said that." — "So tolerate." — "Yes, sir."

### This Is Real Adventure

- These experiences — this is adventure. Adventure isn't going to Disneyland and riding on the little play rides through Pirates of the Caribbean, where some wax figure comes out and a plastic sword goes up — "Oh, Mommy, it's so scary." That's not real adventure. **Real adventure is taking the risk to go out and having faith that in the difficulties that will arise (because this is the material world), Krishna has your back — He'll protect you.**

### Two Weeks With a Sheep

- The gypsies considered it a great battle (although there was no fighting) and wanted to celebrate: "Come back, we'll cook the biggest sheep." The speaker said, "Look, these boys have been trained in India to cook for the last 10 years." The leader looked and said, "But that guy's only 20 years old." — "Yeah, but his dad worked in the armed forces." — "Oh, okay, all right." — "Let us cook the feast. Give us the sheep, because winter's coming; we need the wool; we can make some jackets."
- So they gave a sheep. For two weeks the party had to take care of a sheep — they had him in the van, traveling with a sheep, and he was passing dung all over the car. "Don't worry, boys, we'll use the wool."

### The Feast, the Kirtan, and Ajnata-Sukriti

- They went back and cooked the feast. Good cooks made a fantastic feast, and the gypsies took prasadam. The first time you take prasadam — because it is transcendental (*prasada* means mercy; it is offered to the Lord, so it has a spiritual quality) — it just makes you feel light-hearted, as devotional service does. We're happy when we chant, when we hear philosophy, in the association of devotees, and when we take good prasadam; we rise above the modes of nature by the mercy of Krishna in His various forms.
- The boys cooked a knockout feast — about 27 preparations. "This is your original food." The gypsies gorged themselves on gulab jamun, jalebi, sandesh, rabri; and with all that sugar they became intoxicated and began dancing. Then the devotees took their harmonium and mridangas and took over, and there was a **four- or five-hour kirtan** where the gypsies were just chanting. They ate so much and chanted so much — all because they rendered some **ajnata-sukriti.**
- **Ajnata-sukriti** means unknowing devotional service, which is also accepted by the Lord. Just like a child who, in childhood innocence, takes some money from his father, buys something, and gives it to the dad on his birthday — the father appreciates that child's sentiment. So ajnata-sukriti means you may do some service unknowingly for God, but you still get the credit and the reciprocation of Krishna's mercy.
- The gypsies had the feast of their life, and a five-hour kirtan. The speaker sat down and gave a lecture on Bhagavad-gita — and they all fell asleep, every single one. "These gypsies are nice people, but the philosophy is not for them." So he woke them all up: "Let's have some more kirtan." Late into the night they had more kirtan. The gypsies said, "Maras, let's go get those bikers!"

### The Blood Brotherhood

- The next morning they had to go back to their camp and get ready to leave. The gypsies all came over with flowers, gave donations, brought bags of rice, and were all crying — after 24 hours' association, their hearts had been so touched by the mercy of Lord Chaitanya that they were feeling some emotion for the Vaishnavas. This is the mercy of Lord Chaitanya, the deliverer of the most fallen.
- The leader came up: "Maras, you are my brother." — "Yeah, that's what I said in the beginning." — "We have a tradition that when we become brothers, we change blood. Brothers share the blood. We want to make you a real gypsy; we want gypsy blood to flow through your veins." ("Oh my God" — the speaker, trying to become a *pukka* brahmana.)
- There was no way to say no — all the men were there, and to refuse would have been very dishonorable and offensive to them. They took out a knife: "Take your shirt off." He cut himself, blood flowing; then he cut the speaker ("I have the scar, but I can't show you because ladies are here — a big scar"). "This is the hardest part in the last two days." Then he grabbed the speaker, chest to chest.
- "So if you see some irregular habits in me — if I eat with my left hand, or I swear in public — it's because one quarter of my blood is gypsy blood." Actually, the speaker respected those men, though of course we don't condone much of their lifestyle (they drink, they steal, so many bad habits).

## The Krasnoyarsk Story: Making Brothers of the Gypsies

- When the speaker went to **Krasnoyarsk in Russia**, there was a gypsy camp there, and the devotees had been having a lot of trouble — the gypsies were coming and stealing from them. Nobody could put their shoes out for the Sunday feast and expect them to still be there; people weren't coming to the temple anymore because their shoes were being stolen by gypsies.
- "Let me take care of this." The speaker went by himself to the gypsy camp. They were yelling at him, spitting: "What are you doing here?" — "Take me to your leader." They took him to the leader, and he sat down.
- "What do you want?" — "I'm your brother." — "No you're not." — "Yes." — "Prove it." (He proves it as before.) — "So you are my brother; we will feast together; one sheep is over there." — "I'm not very hungry right now; we can feast later." — "Right, then we will sing and dance."
- They did their gypsy dance, and the speaker had to do the gypsy dance ("I won't show you what it's like"), and drink the gypsy wine. They talked into the night, and the speaker came back the next morning: "Our brother has come!"
- He became really good friends with the leader, sitting there for hours listening to all his problems (the leader was in charge of the village): "Half the men in our village are in jail, and a quarter of the rest are dead." — "You have to change your habits. I'm also gypsy, but I have good habits." — "What are your good habits?" — "No meat eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, no gambling." — "Never!" — "Okay, but we can live together as brothers." The leader said, "We must learn, despite our differences, to live together as brothers."
- "So don't steal our shoes anymore." — "..." — "It's obvious — everyone in your village is wearing our shoes. No more stealing, no more spitting." — "No, no, I understand now; we are brothers." — "You come to my camp." — "What? You come with the leaders to our camp; we're brothers." — "That's a big scar." — "I've got a lot of your blood in my veins." — "All right, I will come; let's go."
- The leader called: "Get the temple authorities together, we're going to have a powwow here between the gypsies." (The devotees: "Don't bring the gypsies here!" — "Don't worry.")
- They walked out of the gypsy camp — the speaker in front, all the gypsy leaders and people following, very curious; the devotees terrified. They got into the temple (fortunately there was a lot of prasada, as it was noon time), sat down, and discussed: "We're all from India, from the same tradition; we like to sing, dance, and eat; we've helped each other in different circumstances; there should be no more conflict here; let's work together and help society by spreading India's message around the world."
- Since that day, in this part of Russia, there has never been an instance where the gypsies have stolen shoes or come into the temple [to cause trouble]. When they walk by the devotees they go, "Hare Krishna, brother," and they often come to the Sunday feast — in fact the devotees have to cook so much for it, because the gypsies come to the feast (and they invite the devotees too).

### These Are the Modern-Day Pastimes of Lord Chaitanya (CC Adi 9.41)

- These are the modern-day pastimes of Lord Chaitanya. "This is not me — this is Guru Maharaja, this is Srila Prabhupada's mercy, that he's allowing us this service to spread the Sankirtan movement."
- It is Lord Chaitanya's instruction that anyone who is born in Bharat, in India, should make his life perfect and act in such a way as to make the whole world perfect, by following this perfect culture (**Chaitanya-caritamrita, Adi 9.41** — "bharata-bhumite haila manusya-janma yara / janma sarthaka kari' kara para-upakara").

## The Dagestan Story: The Muslim Wedding

### Preface: Respect for All Bona Fide Religions

- (Banter: "I have so many stories, I could tell one more... that was the tiny one; I have seven. I'll tell one more, and the next time you invite me back — if you're not too scared. Are you scaredy-cats? Okay.")
- The speaker states his respect for all religious institutions, which he learned from his spiritual master: any bona fide religion is enacted by the Lord. We understand it is a matter of time, place, and circumstance — that the people of that time adopted the tradition that was best for them to make progress. We know we have the most complete, perfected spiritual tradition, but we always respect our Christian brothers, our Muslim brothers, our Jewish brothers, and our Buddhist brothers; they have taken their chosen path, and if they are sincere they will make advancement.
- He relates an encounter with a Muslim community, but not in a derogatory way — it is in no way meant to criticize the Islamic community, because he has many Muslim friends, and many of the mullahs in the Emirates are his very good friends, with whom he has long philosophical discussions on the Quran and the Bhagavad-gita.

### Crossing the Border Into a Civil War

- This is an adventure of the speaker, his Godbrother **Bhakti Bhringa Govinda Maharaja**, **Uttama Shloka** (his disciple), and **Sri Prahlad** (a good companion and friend). They were traveling in the Muslim countries spreading Krishna consciousness — "I never thought it was possible; you could go to the land of Islam and preach openly like this." They went to Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, and **Dagestan.**
- When they went to Dagestan there was a war — a civil war going on — but they were so fired up. ("This is some years ago; now I'm a little more careful where I go.") They didn't have visas to enter the country. Foolishly they thought they could sneak across the border. The border guard stopped them; with a civil war going on, and them in their dhotis, they could be taken for spies.
- "Show me your documents." The speaker showed an American passport. "What is this?" The speaker said: **"There is no God but Allah, and Mohammed is His prophet."** The guard said, "Come on in! Our country — no visas. Salaam alaikum." (These are "mantras" the speaker learned — not from the Vedas, but they are in praise of God, so it's fine; and in that tense situation: "Allahu Akbar, alhamdulillah.") They were escorted into the country by the soldiers and continued on their way.

### The Festival on the Football Field

- They had to reach the capital before midnight, because there was a **curfew** — any cars driving in the capital after curfew were blown up, no questions asked (bazookas, RPGs). So they had a deadline and drove fast; they had a little extra time and were thirsty. Around 8 o'clock at night, in the dark, there was a huge fair — a big festival going on. "Surely we can get some water there."
- They drove down into the area — it was on a football field. They got out, and one man took their car and parked it (so there was no way to get out fast). They walked down, and it was the **Muslim community**, all dressed in their Islamic dress, and the devotees dressed as they were — and there is a history of Hindu-Muslim tension. They walked into the floodlights on the football field and realized it was a big Islamic festival: Islamic writing, singing, and dancing.

### "We Are Muslims From India"

- The leader of the village came up: "Who are you — Hindus?" ("To those who serve Me with love and devotion I give the intelligence by which they can get out of difficult problems" — **Bhagavad-gita 10.10**.) These were simple village people, so the speaker said: "No, we are not Hindus; we are Muslims from India." — "You are?" — "Yes, I'm Sheikh Swami, and this is Sheikh Maharaja (Bibi Govinda Maharaja), and this is Sheikh Prahlad."
- "You don't know — you're offending us. In India this is how the mullahs dress." — "Where is your beard, with no mustache?" — "No, no, we don't do that in India anymore; that is all [old]; this is the new Islamic." — "Oh, we didn't know. So you will dine with us; we have one sheep." ("I must have offended a sheep in my last life or something.") — "No, no, we're doing Ramadan; we've already eaten at sunrise, we fasted all day." — "Ramadan is not now." — "In India, Ramadan is now." — "You Indian Muslims are very strange." — "We are also thinking you are strange, but let's not argue; we are brothers." — "Yes, we are brothers; come."

### 10,000 Muslims Chant Hare Krishna

- He got up to the stage — there were about 10,000 people — with a translator. "Ladies and gentlemen, we are very fortunate that at the marriage ceremony today [an Islamic boy and girl getting married], Allah has sent some of our brothers from India — India mullahs. This is Sheikh Swami, Sheikh Maharaja, and Sheikh Prahlad; they are our special guests today, and we will ask them to please oversee the marriage."
- The speaker didn't know how to participate. "Part of our tradition is we have to sing to inaugurate the ceremony. Do you know how to sing?" — "Yeah." — "Do you know this Islamic song, or this?" He didn't know them. "No, I don't know those, but I know the Islamic song in India for glorifying Allah." — "What is it?" — **"Hari Krishna, Hari Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hari Hari; Hari Ram, Hari Ram, Ram Ram, Hari Ram."** ("I swear to God — Bibi Govinda Maharaja was right by my side; he tells this story too; Prahlad and Uttama Shloka were there.")
- The speaker told Prahlad to run back to the van and get the accordion, the drums, and the kartals. There was a booming loud system; the bride and groom were on opposite sides of the field; this was the introduction to the whole event, where the Indian mullah opens the ceremony.
- Prahlad (a really good singer) was at the microphone; the speaker played the drum; Uttama Shloka as well. "We're going to sing in praise of Allah in the Indian tradition." He got all the thousands of people: "So, in praise of Allah, and Mohammed — blessed be his name — please repeat after me: Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna" — and **10,000 Muslims said, "Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna."**
- He went through the whole mantra three or four times. "This is the melody we use." They wrote it down in **Arabic** and put it on the screen — a huge screen — and there was the **maha-mantra in Arabic.** "Wow, this is a great day."
- "Usually in the Islamic tradition we have our dancing, isn't it?" — "Yes, yes." — "So I want you all to get out of the stands and we'll all dance together." They all came, and Prahlad led one of his melodies; the people were swooning, all responding — 10,000 voices: "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare." It went on for 45 minutes. "What about the marriage?" The speaker turned to the man: "What about the marriage?" — "It can wait; we could do this all night." He stopped after about an hour, and the people were just standing there like "Oh" — they all loved it and wanted more.

### The Sannyasi Performs the Wedding

- It was time for the marriage. Now they really respected the devotees, because they had created a very auspicious atmosphere — which religion is meant to do. The bride and groom came out, and the speaker (the sannyasi, "who never touches a woman, never speaks to a woman, never looks at the face of a woman") was supposed to join their hands: "May I have your hand, please? You're looking very beautiful today." — "Thank you, Sheikh Swami." ("I wish I had this recorded — no, maybe we shouldn't record it.")
- Then he took the boy and joined their hands for eternal matrimony, in love and praise of Allah, and Mohammed his messenger, praise be his name. They sat down, and he took the veil off her face: "Look at your husband." He made a few mistakes, and the man asked, "Have you been trained as a mullah in doing weddings?" — "Well, I only did four or five; I'm not so good at it." — "Well, I'll take over." So the man took over, and they did the whole marriage ceremony — different from our Vedic weddings, but very elaborate and beautiful. Then all the family members came.
- The speaker asked the bride, "So you're very happy?" — "Yes." — "Can we sing some more?" — "I'd like that very much; my wedding." — "Prahlad, get back on the stage." And they had another kirtan for about an hour.

### Hiding the Mutton and the Endless Weddings

- Then it was time for the feast. In Ramadan you can't eat during the day, but at night you can. "You ate early a bit; now it's two o'clock in the morning, you can sit down and eat." They served a big piece of mutton. "Oh, look at that over there — isn't that wonderful?" And the speaker put it under the table, onto a mat, with his feet. The man looked: "Oh, you ate that very quickly." — "Oh, it's very good, really good." — "I'm just looking around..." (It was little pieces like that.) Somehow they got out of that.
- They stayed until about four o'clock in the morning, with the kirtan going on the whole time. The speaker thought, "We better get out of here before somebody gets suspicious" — but they were innocent villagers. "We have to go now." — "No, you're not going anywhere." ("Uh-oh, they figured us out.") — "You are not going anywhere; get your people here." ("Uh-oh — Prahlad, Govinda Maharaja, Uttama... what's up?") — "We have another wedding for you to go to." — "Well, at least we missed the curfew in the town."
- So they went to the other wedding, and the whole scene repeated. At the end of that, at 12 noon, "We have another wedding." — "No, in India we're only allowed to do two weddings within 24 hours." — "Okay, but you must come back again, Sheikh Swami and Sheikh Maharaja." — "For sure we're going to come back; you can count on it. But thank you very much. Hare Krishna. Allahu Akbar, alhamdulillah." And they drove off.
