## The Verse and Its Setting

- Reading from **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Canto 7, Chapter 7 ("What Prahlāda Learned in the Womb"), Text 38**.
- The verse's purport establishes the core theme: the Supreme Personality of Godhead, in His **Supersoul (Paramātmā)** feature, always exists within the core of the hearts of all living entities. He is the well-wisher and friend of all.
  - There is no difficulty in worshiping the Lord. So **why don't people engage in His devotional service? Why are they so addicted to unnecessarily producing artificial paraphernalia for sense gratification?**
- The Lord is Supreme — no one is equal to Him, no one greater — yet if one is a devotee, the Lord is easily obtainable.
- **The Lord is compared to the sky**: the sky is vast, yet within the reach of all — not only human beings, but even the animals. In His Paramātmā feature the Lord is the best well-wisher and friend.
- **Vedic confirmation** ("Sayojo Sakayo" — two birds in the tree image): the Lord in His Supersoul feature always stays in the heart along with the living entity, so friendly that He remains there so one can always contact Him without difficulty. We contact Him simply by devotional service.

## The Ease of Approaching Krishna vs. the Great Endeavor of Going to Hell

- The nine processes of devotional service make the Lord instantly accessible: **śravaṇaṁ, kīrtanaṁ, viṣṇu-smaraṇam, pāda-sevanam, arcanam, vandanam, dāsyam, sakhyam, ātma-nivedanam.** By any or all of these one immediately comes in touch with the Lord.
- **Going to hell, by contrast, requires great endeavor.** To go to hell by illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling, and intoxication, one must first acquire many things:
  - For **illicit sex** — arrange money and brothels.
  - For **meat-eating** — arrange many slaughterhouses.
  - For **gambling** — arrange casinos and hotels.
  - For **intoxication** — open many breweries.
- Clearly, going to hell demands enormous effort. But to return home, back to Godhead, requires no difficult endeavor: one may live alone, anywhere, in any condition, simply sit, meditate on the Supersoul, and chant and hear about the Lord.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 9.26** ("If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, fruit or water, I will accept it") — the Lord is satisfied by the simplest offering, and one can meditate upon Him anywhere and everywhere.
- Because of inability to control the senses, one undergoes great endeavor to go to hell. But a sensible person easily obtains the Lord's favor, because the Lord is always with him.
- Thus **Prahlāda Mahārāja advises his friends, the sons of the demons, to take this path back home, back to Godhead, without difficulty.**

## Prahlāda's Two Questions — and Why a Guru Asks Questions

- In this verse Prahlāda Mahārāja asks his own students two questions:
  1. If pleasing the Lord is as easy as offering Him a leaf or a flower in devotion, **why don't people accept this easy process?**
  2. **Why are people so addicted to sense gratification?**
- Clarification: we must not think Prahlāda doesn't know the answers and is asking his students in a confused state.

## The Qualification of the Spiritual Master

- The first qualification of the spiritual master is that he has learned from the śāstra, the scriptures.
- **Upaniṣadic instruction (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad)**: to learn transcendental subject matter one should approach a spiritual master, carrying fuel (samit-pāṇi) for sacrifice. The symptom of such a spiritual master: he is expert in understanding the Vedic conclusion and is always engaged in devotional service.
- The Sanskrit word **"śrotriyam"** means expert in understanding Vedic knowledge — not merely knowing it, but understanding and realizing it.
- **Jñāna vs. vijñāna** — knowledge vs. realization of knowledge. By acting on knowledge one becomes realized.
  - *Gurukula example*: In youth the mind is sharp, so children memorize many verses; as people age, memory fails ("My memory is failing me"). Speaker's travels in America: Indian parents bring their children forth to quote ślokas — the children recite "like the wind," but when asked "What does it mean?" there is a blank face. Memorizing ślokas is not enough; a spiritual master must realize the knowledge and impart it into the hearts of his disciples.
- **Prabhupāda's paraphrase**: "Guru means heavy with knowledge." When he shares that knowledge with his disciple, the disciple becomes illuminated — becomes Krishna conscious.
- A spiritual master changes the hearts of his submissive disciples; hearing knowledge from the guru brings a change of heart.
  - We sing this each morning (**Śrī Guru-vandana / Śrī Gurv-aṣṭaka theme**): "He who has given me the gift of transcendental vision is my lord, birth after birth (*janame janame prabhu sei*). By his mercy, divine knowledge is revealed in the heart."
- **Hearing is even better than reading.** Reading is good, but hearing the **śabda-brahma** — the transcendental sound vibration from the lips of one's spiritual master — has something mysterious about it.
  - *Advantage in Kali-yuga*: tape recorders and iPods, by which one can hear knowledge from self-realized souls. Speaker's habit: he sneaks behind devotees wearing iPods, pulls the earbud out to hear what they are hearing — sometimes shocked, sometimes pleased.
- We would consider it odd if the spiritual master could not explain the Absolute Truth in a logical, concise, and convincing way. **The main duty of the guru is to speak — and to set a good example.**

## The Nature of a Brāhmaṇa and the Varṇas

- In Vedic culture the brāhmaṇas would sit, preach, and after a long session, eat. The brāhmaṇa was typically portly ("he'd have a belly") — not interested in jogging or lifting weights, but sitting, counseling, preaching, then prasādam, then preaching, then prasādam.
  - *Prabhupāda story*: Once he preached all day, so absorbed he forgot to take prasādam; at night, feeling hunger, the devotees cooked purīs and sabjī, and Prabhupāda took prasādam with great relish. "All day long preaching and some prasādam — this is the life of a devotee."
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī**: "If one has life, he will preach." Preaching comes natural for a brāhmaṇa — whether born a brāhmaṇa or become one by training and initiation.
- Illustration of natural tendencies of the varṇas (each has an innate "knack," to be used for Krishna):
  - **Śūdra** — good with the hands; a gardener with a "green thumb," a fixer/maker with a "golden thumb." Examples: disciple **Viśvakarma** ("he has the golden thumb"); **Nityānanda Caraṇa**, who can fix anything — "whatever's broken, you give it to him; it comes back better than it was before." (He is also a brāhmaṇa, a humble "valuable jewel" on the festival program, off fixing something and too humble to want applause.)
  - **Vaiśya** — likes to milk the cows; the relish of grabbing the teats and the knack of it ("one, two, three, four").
  - **Kṣatriya** — "if there's no fight, they're depressed," but they fight for dharma, to protect religious principles, devotees, and deities — not ruffians. Examples: **Śrī Hanumān**, who likes to go on Harināma and protect the devotees; **Rakṣaṇa** — "if Rakṣaṇa's not there, we start getting nervous." These tendencies come natural, but are to be engaged for Krishna.
  - **Brāhmaṇa** — always clean; uncomfortable when there is dirt, so always cleaning. Example: **Caitanya Līlā** — very clean, nicely dressed (*śucinam*), even on tour where the bathrooms are as they are and only a half-bucket of water is allowed.
- **Dirt is compared to ignorance.** A brāhmaṇa cannot stand dirt, nor ignorance; his main duty is preaching to destroy ignorance.
- The spiritual master is a brāhmaṇa and Vaiṣṇava — in the eyes of the disciple, the best devotee. He sets the example and is always preaching, by words and by example — but **preaching is his main duty.**
  - This is exactly why Prabhupāda lectured practically every day, once, twice, or thrice — there is an equal amount of Bhāgavatam classes, morning walks, and Bhagavad-gītā classes on the folio.
- **Analogy of the sun**: just as it is impossible to conceive of the sun without heat and light, it is impossible to think of a guru without knowledge and realization.

## Cāṇakya Paṇḍita: Beauty Without Knowledge

- **Cāṇakya Paṇḍita's Nīti-śāstra verse**: *rūpa-yauvana-sampannā viśāla-kula-sambhavāḥ / vidyā-hīnā na śobhante nirgandhā iva kiṁśukāḥ.*
  - **Kiṁśuka** = the red flower seen in India, used for Durgā-pūjā / Śiva-pūjā — beautiful but with no smell or fragrance.
  - Meaning: persons born in a high family, endowed with youth and beauty, do not actually appear beautiful if they lack knowledge — just as the beautiful kiṁśuka flowers are not splendid because they lack fragrance. The kiṁśuka is quite beautiful, but no one cares for it (except Durgā worshipers) because there is no smell.
  - Applied: a person very beautiful, handsome, rich, but "dumb" is not considered attractive — no one wants to marry them; everything is there, but the knowledge is missing. **The knowledge has to be there.**
- So a spiritual master means one who knows the **siddhānta**, the conclusion — **Brahma-saṁhitā 5.1**: *īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaḥ / anādir ādir govindaḥ sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam* ("Krishna is the Supreme Controller, the primeval Lord, cause of all causes").

## Why a Real Guru Must Be a Vaiṣṇava

- A spiritual master by nature must be a Vaiṣṇava.
- There are spiritual masters for the **yogis** and for the **jñānīs** — but how far can they take you? Only as far as gaining mystic powers, or teaching you to merge into the Brahman. But that is not the siddhānta of all knowledge.
- **We are not meant to be mystics, nor to merge into God; we are meant to render devotional service to the Supreme Īśvara, Krishna.**
- So if you take the trouble to find a spiritual master, surrender, and follow instructions, you had better hope he is a Vaiṣṇava — otherwise you only get the **bronze or silver medal, not the gold**; you come in second or third place, which is not as good as first.
  - *Wimbledon example*: Yesterday were the Wimbledon tennis finals between **Federer** (Swiss) and **Nadal** (Spanish), both top players. Federer won; the other came in second — "it was almost like he won," but it is second place, and he gets half the money and half the fame.
  - So after all your penances, austerities, and prayers, you get mystic powers or merge into the Brahman effulgence — but after many years the desire again arises in the heart, because **the true glory of the soul is that he is an eternal servant of Krishna.** To reach that stage one must be initiated by a Vaiṣṇava guru.
- Therefore **a real guru is a Vaiṣṇava guru** in the ultimate sense.
- Such a guru is **Prahlāda Mahārāja** — beyond question, because he is stated to be one of the **twelve mahājanas**. He is not just a guru but a mahājana.
  - *Prabhupāda's poetic tribute*: "My spiritual master, he was no ordinary spiritual master... he saved me." (We do not think of the spiritual master as ordinary; Prabhupāda speaks poetically to glorify.)
- Prahlāda Mahārāja is thus the **crest jewel of spiritual masters** — so why is he asking his students questions? From one angle it looks reversed: a guru should answer questions, yet he is asking them ("If devotional service is so easy, why don't people take it? My dear students, why are people addicted to sense gratification?").

## Why Prahlāda Asks: To Get Them Thinking, and to Teach Us How to Inquire

- Prahlāda asks to **get them thinking**, because materialistic people are so absorbed in sense gratification they do not have time to think about these things.
- We get a glimpse into the mind of a materialistic person through **Śukadeva Gosvāmī's teaching**: "Persons who are materially engrossed, being blind to the knowledge of ultimate truth, have many subject matters for hearing in human society, O Emperor" — people think and ask about so many things, but never Krishna, never questions of a transcendental nature.
- **In Vedic times people were pure**, so they knew how to ask the right questions to get the right response.
  - When **Mahārāja Parīkṣit** asked Śukadeva Gosvāmī "What is the duty of one who is about to die?" Śukadeva was very happy: "My dear king, your question is glorious because it is beneficial for all kinds of people. The answer is the prime subject matter for hearing, approved by all transcendentalists."
- **Nowadays** the questions people bring are seldom deep: "Why do you shave your head? Why don't you wear blue jeans and a t-shirt? What is the clay (tilaka) on your forehead?"
- Things are so topsy-turvy, upside-down, in Kali-yuga that the teacher has to take the part of the student and teach us how to ask the right questions — **we don't even know how to ask.**
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.34** (*tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā*): the guru-disciple relationship is submissive inquiry and service — "question, question, question, and then answer, answer." We often don't even have questions; the absence of questions is not because we have understood everything, but because we are still in ignorance and don't know how to inquire.
- So great souls take the position of the student to model inquiry: e.g., **Sanātana Gosvāmī** asked Lord Caitanya *"ke āmi"* — "Who am I?"
- Chain of exemplary inquiry:
  - The sages of **Naimiṣāraṇya** inquiring from **Sūta Gosvāmī**;
  - **Mahārāja Parīkṣit** inquiring from **Śukadeva Gosvāmī**;
  - **Rūpa and Sanātana** inquiring from **Lord Caitanya**;
  - Even **Lord Caitanya** inquiring from **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** and **Rāmānanda Rāya** — to show us the initial steps in Krishna consciousness.
- So Prahlāda asks "Why do people search for material happiness if it is so troublesome?" — and later in the chapter he himself takes the teacher's position and answers.

## The Answer Foreshadowed: Where Is the Happiness in Material Life?

- Later in the chapter Prahlāda explains how difficult it is to gain material happiness — "Where is that happiness in life?" The stages of life:
  - **First 25 years** — spent studying intensely in school.
  - **Next 25 years** — spent working hard to maintain family and home.
  - **Next 25 years** — spent trying to ward off disease and old age.
  - If you survive to **75**, whatever time is left you are always in anxiety: "When will death come?"
- Speaker applies it to the audience by stage:
  - "I'm at stage three." Some are at **stage one** — just school, working so hard, "the only relief is the tour."
  - Some have completed school and married — "Is life a bowl of cherries? Somebody forgot to tell you that when you get married you have to make money, and money doesn't grow on trees." Most of us are not Bill Gates; at the end of the day we're counting pennies, pence, shillings — not millions.
  - Some are at the speaker's stage, "just beginning to live your life so that you don't get sick and get old too fast."
- **Prabhupāda's poem**: "Struggle for existence, the human race — only hope is divine grace."

## The Positive Side of Struggle: Humility Leads to Surrender

- It is a sad story, but there is a positive side: as a result of all this struggle we become a little humble. After being defeated many times, you become humble — "That's material life; it makes you a little humble."
- **In humility you turn to God.** According to **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura**, *dainya* (humility) is the first quality in surrendering to God.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Śaraṇāgati** — the seven divisions / opening songs describe the struggle of life "from the cradle to the grave," using himself as an example; after all that struggle he develops humility, which helps him turn toward God (*dainya* = humility). The stages of surrender in Śaraṇāgati:
  1. **Dainya** — humility (struggle of life makes us humble).
  2. **Ātma-nivedana** — dedicating oneself to God, accepting the Lord as one's soul and maintainer.
  3. **Avaśya rakṣibe Kṛṣṇa** — gaining faith in Krishna as protector.
  4. **Bhakti-pratikūla-bhāva-varjana** — renouncing conduct unfavorable to devotional service.
  5. Beautiful songs about **activities favorable to devotional service.**
  - But it all begins with the frustration of material life.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 7.16** (*catur-vidhā bhajante māṁ janāḥ sukṛtino 'rjuna*): "O best among the Bhāratas, four kinds of pious men begin to render devotional service unto Me — the distressed (ārta), the desirer of wealth (arthārthī), the inquisitive (jijñāsu), and the searcher after knowledge (jñānī)."
  - **Prabhupāda's comment**: *ārta* — the one who is distressed — is the main group who surrender to Krishna. Frustration has a good side, but it takes a long time to come to surrender; although suffering, people take a long time to surrender.

## The Nature of Addiction and Its Cure

- Why does surrender take so long? Because, as the verse says, **they are addicted.**
- **Dictionary definition of addiction**: dependence on a behavior or substance that a person is powerless to stop — like smoking or drugs. It is not good for you; because you are addicted you can't stop; you are actually suffering but cannot stop.
- Likewise, people pursuing sense gratification are only suffering, but because of addiction they cannot stop.
- **How doctors deal with addiction — two ways**: (1) stop the drug, and (2) replace it with something better. "Stop smoking and chew gum."
- The parallel in Krishna consciousness: **stop sense gratification — there is no alternative but to stop — but do something better.**
  - **Bhagavad-gītā 9.27** (*yat karoṣi yad aśnāsi yaj juhoṣi dadāsi yat / yat tapasyasi kaunteya tat kuruṣva mad-arpaṇam*): "O son of Kuntī, all that you do, all that you eat, all that you offer and give away, and all austerities you perform — do that as an offering unto Me." **That will cure our addiction. Simple.**
- Therefore at **initiation** the guru says: stop all the nonsense and do the good things.
  - Stop **meat-eating, illicit sex, intoxication, gambling** — they are really bringing suffering, so stop.
  - And **kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ** — chant all day long. (As followers of Lord Caitanya, we don't merely sit and chant all day; we sit and chant *and* go out to preach the glories of the holy name.)

## No Compromise

- **If you are strict, you'll make it; if you compromise, you get the bronze or silver medal.** You cannot compromise.
- *Milk-fast (payovrata) story*: A devotee on a strict milk fast (**payovrata** = only milk) was also eating sandeśa, reasoning that "sandeśa is a milk product." He extended this to burfī and ice cream — "this is all milk product, so I'm also going to take." **No — you can't compromise.**
- You also cannot compromise on your **prescribed number of rounds**, or you'll slide back into bad habits.
- *Dieting statistic*: It is a known fact that people go on a diet and lose weight, but **70% of dieters start eating again and put back the same weight and more.** We don't want to become like that.
- We want to **march forward with success, never look back and think we left anything behind, and never envy the position of the materialistic sense enjoyers.** Success is guaranteed if there is no compromise and one pushes forward with enthusiasm.

## Gīta-vali and the Joy of the Holy Name

- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Gīta-vali** — unlike Śaraṇāgati (which reveals the frustration of material life and the need to surrender), Gīta-vali deals exclusively with the **joyful process of Krishna consciousness — chanting Hare Krishna.**
- One line: *"kṛṣṇa-nāme anurāga to hobe he"* — "The life of the living being easily becomes successful when we become attached to the holy name of Krishna. Without affection for Krishna, life is simply useless. And at the end of life, by the mercy of Gaura, such a chanter will obtain the beautiful vision of Rādhā-Śyāma in Vṛndāvana."

## Conclusion: Give Them Something Better

- So Prahlāda Mahārāja is asking: this process is so easy — chanting, dancing, feasting, and we become Krishna conscious — why don't people take it? Whereas material life (sense gratification) is so hard to achieve and gives no lasting pleasure, because they are addicted.
- **We have to give them something better** so they can stop their addiction and experience the natural, healthy life of Krishna consciousness. We must help them overcome their addiction to "this horrible sense gratification."
- It is very easy: they simply have to hear and chant **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.**
- **This is the life of tour devotees**: just sit and chant Hare Krishna, and go out on the streets and dance to the holy names — then our lives become perfect, and others' lives become perfect. It is possible that the whole world can become Krishna conscious.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** (*Gurudeva! kṛpā-bindu diyā / and the "Hari Hari" mood*): "Drink the pure nectar of the holy name of Krishna, and thus satisfy the soul of Bhaktivinoda; except for the holy name there is nothing within all the fourteen worlds that is our life and soul." Chant Hare Krishna.
- **Closing instruction**: Let's finish class, take prasādam, and go out and chant Hare Krishna.
