## The Verse and Prabhupada's Purport (Bhagavad-gita 2.11)

- The class reads the verse and Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 2.11** — *aśocyān anvaśocas tvaṁ prajñā-vādāṁś ca bhāṣase / gatāsūn agatāsūṁś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ*: "While speaking learned words, you are mourning for what is not worthy of grief. Those who are wise lament neither for the living nor the dead."
- Points from the purport:
  - The Lord at once takes the position of the teacher and chastises the student, indirectly calling him a fool. Kṛṣṇa says: you are talking like a learned man, but you do not know that one who is learned — one who knows what is body and what is soul — does not lament for any stage of the body, neither living nor dead.
  - As later chapters clarify, **knowledge means to know matter, spirit, and the controller of both.**
  - Arjuna argued that religious principles should be given more importance than politics or sociology, but he did not know that knowledge of matter, soul, and the Supreme is even more important than religious formalities. Lacking that knowledge, he should not have posed as a very learned man.
  - The body is born and is destined to be vanquished today or tomorrow; therefore the body is not as important as the soul. One who knows this is actually learned, and for him there is no cause for lamentation regardless of the condition of the body.

## Krishna Calls Arjuna a Fool

- In this very famous and important verse, Kṛṣṇa — the supreme authority, the Supreme Personality of Godhead — is calling His young disciple Arjuna a fool.
- **"Young" because** before the Battle of Kurukṣetra Arjuna was the friend of Kṛṣṇa. Actually he is Kṛṣṇa's **eternal friend**; the pastime of speaking the Bhagavad-gītā goes on between them eternally, and at this very moment it is taking place in some particular universe within the material world.
- To make the speaking of the Gītā possible, **Lord Kṛṣṇa temporarily covered Arjuna with illusion** so that Arjuna could take the position of a student and Kṛṣṇa could speak transcendental knowledge.
- **Prabhupāda:** As soon as Arjuna surrendered to Kṛṣṇa as his spiritual teacher, the relationship became more formal. "Talks between the spiritual master and disciple are very serious discussions." Friends joke and laugh, but the relationship between teacher and student is serious. Now, in all seriousness, Arjuna surrenders to Kṛṣṇa as his spiritual preceptor — and the first thing Kṛṣṇa tells him is that he is a fool.

### The Guru Must Point Out the Defect

- One surrenders to the guru, and the first thing the guru says is, "You are simply nonsense, you are a fool." In the material world one does not appreciate being called a fool, but Arjuna welcomed it, because that is the relationship: **the spiritual master must point out the defect in the disciple.** If there were no defect, why would he be a disciple? Everyone in this material world has a defect — otherwise he would not be here.
- **Lord Caitanya's humility:** A devotee once told Lord Caitanya, "Mahāprabhu, You have so much love for Kṛṣṇa." Mahāprabhu replied in all humility, "If I had any love for Kṛṣṇa, what would I be doing here? I would be in the spiritual world."
- So Arjuna welcomes this chastisement from his guru, Kṛṣṇa — the **ādi-guru**, the original spiritual master.
- **The patient and the doctor:** One goes to a doctor because one knows something is wrong. No one likes to go to a doctor, but at some point we realize we must, because there is some pain. It is actually a **relief** when the doctor says "This is the problem." Half the anxiety of being sick is not knowing what it is, how it is to be cured, or how long it will take. When the doctor says "Here is the problem, here is the medicine" — "Oh, thank you, doctor." This is the mood of a disciple receiving chastisement from his spiritual master.

## We Are Fools: Having Exchanged the Superior for the Inferior

- A devotee realizes he is a fool simply by looking around at the mess he has gotten himself into. If we are so intelligent, what are we doing in the material world?
- **Milton** (the great English author) said this is nothing more than a fool's paradise — *"It is a folly to be wise where ignorance is bliss."*
- **What we gave up:** We were once in the spiritual world, whose nature is that life is **eternal, full of knowledge, and full of bliss** (*sat-cit-ānanda*). Everyone is fully illuminated with full knowledge of Kṛṣṇa, their eternal relationship with Him, and their service to Him. No one grows old — everyone stays "sweet sixteen" — and everyone is always happy in the varieties of service to the Lord.
- **What we have now:** Not an eternal life. Even as we have been sitting here, many cells of our body have died and been replaced. Every moment we are growing old, and with old age comes guaranteed **disease**, and then **kaput — death.**
- **The hourglass:** It is like the hourglass with sand; you turn it and it starts running. If we really looked at it we would realize how little time is left, but everyone is ignorant of the passing of time and the inevitability of death.
- So we have given up **that** (eternal life, full of bliss and knowledge) for **this** (temporary life, full of ignorance and suffering). Is that intelligent?
- **Cāṇakya Paṇḍita:** "One should not give up the sure for the unsure." We have given up the sure for the unsure — that means we are fools.
- **The Deutschmark exchange:**
  - If you have 100 Deutschmarks and someone offers 500 for them, you make the exchange — that is intelligent. You only give up something if you get something better.
  - But the reverse: if you have 500 Deutschmarks and exchange them for one Deutschmark, you are 499 short — that means you are "crazy, cuckoo."
- In the spiritual world we had a loving relationship with the Supreme Lord — in one of the five relationships: **neutrality (śānta), servitorship (dāsya), friendship (sakhya), parenthood (vātsalya), or conjugal love (mādhurya).** Somehow or other we exchanged that for this. That makes us fools — we have given up something far superior for something inferior.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 8.20** — *paras tasmāt tu bhāvo 'nyo 'vyakto 'vyaktāt sanātanaḥ*: there is a world transcendental to this world; when all in this world is annihilated, that world remains as it is. We have given up the eternal for the temporary — a far graver mistake than exchanging 500 Deutschmarks for one. As mad as that sounds, how much more mad it is to exchange our loving relationship with Kṛṣṇa for the temporary so-called loving relationships of this world.

## Approaching the Spiritual Master With Humility

- One who knows this approaches his spiritual master with all humility, at his command. There are many nice examples of this. "We have our precept and we have the example" — sometimes it is easier to understand through example, and it is said that example is higher or better than mere theory. That is why we follow in the footsteps of great devotees, who are themselves the perfect example of the teachings of Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, and Caitanya-caritāmṛta.

### Sanātana Gosvāmī's Humility (Caitanya-caritāmṛta)

- In **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** we may study the life of **Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī**, who nicely showed the principle of humility when he accepted Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu as his spiritual master — just as Arjuna accepted God as his spiritual master.
- **"They were more fortunate than me" — no:** One might think Arjuna and Sanātana were more fortunate. Not necessarily. **sākṣād-hari** — the living spiritual master is the bona fide representative of Śrī Hari/Kṛṣṇa, passing on the same message and setting the same perfect example. One is just as fortunate today to take shelter of the guru-paramparā as one was 5,000 years ago (Kṛṣṇa) or 500 years ago (Lord Caitanya).
- **Electricity through a wire:** It does not matter where you plug into the electricity — you get the full benefit. Whether one person plugs in here and another there, it is the same current. Is one more fortunate than the other? No.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.2** — *evaṁ paramparā-prāptam imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ*: this knowledge of Kṛṣṇa consciousness was spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead and comes down through the guru-paramparā, the disciplic succession; the saintly kings understood it that way. Everyone who takes shelter of the guru-paramparā has equal opportunity to achieve the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa.
- **The art of taking shelter (500 years ago):** When approaching Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the first thing Sanātana Gosvāmī did was put some grass between his lips.
  - In India this is a sign of humility. Grass is the lowest thing — on the ground, always stepped on, and it never complains. It is a symbol of humility; it is the lowest, and everyone takes advantage of it.
  - Sanātana approached Mahāprabhu and said: many people consider me very learned, but actually I am a fool, because despite all my learning I do not know who I am. He was well versed in astronomy, astrology, Persian, Sanskrit, and mathematics — yet he said, "Although I know all these things, I do not know the essential thing: **Who am I?**"
- That is the most important knowledge, because unless you know who you are, you do not know what to do to make yourself happy.
- **People today** have so much knowledge, but only about the body — they can tell you everything from the tip of the head to the bottom of the toe, but nothing about the soul, who they really are. Sanātana was saying likewise: "I have so much material knowledge, but I don't know who I am."

### Do Not Consider Material Qualifications

- **Prabhupāda's point:** When approaching the spiritual master, we should not consider our material qualifications. What actually attracts Kṛṣṇa is **the devotion of the soul.**
- **The businessman and the sweeper woman (Bombay):**
  - A businessman came for Prabhupāda's darśana, wanting Prabhupāda's favor for a business deal (they were constructing the temple in Bombay, and he wanted them to buy bricks or cement from his corporation). To impress Prabhupāda, he wrote out a check for $1,000: "Swamiji, here's a check." Prabhupāda simply said, "Hare Kṛṣṇa."
  - A few minutes later a woman who was sweeping the hall — knowing a sādhu was in the room — peeked in. (In India everyone, from street sweeper up, is trained to know the benefit of even a moment's association with a devotee: **satāṁ prasaṅgān** / *"lava-mātra sādhu-saṅge sarva-siddhi haya"* — even a moment's association with a devotee can give perfection. That is the beauty of Vedic culture and our good fortune.) She thought, "Let me please the sādhu," went outside, picked a rose, came in, and said, "Swāmījī, please accept this rose." Prabhupāda was so pleased: "Oh, Hare Kṛṣṇa, thank you. May Kṛṣṇa's mercy be upon you, may Kṛṣṇa bless you."
  - Prabhupāda was not attracted by the businessman's material qualifications ("I have big money, do business with me"), but was very much attracted by that poor woman's ten-rupee rose, because it was given with love and devotion.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 9.26** — *patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ...*: Kṛṣṇa is pleased with the tiniest devotion. He does not say "offer Me a huge feast with ten million sweet-balls"; He says "offer Me with love a fruit, a flower, a leaf." It is that devotion that attracts the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- Sanātana Gosvāmī knew that Mahāprabhu would not be impressed with material qualifications (learning, etc., which are temporary) but would be pleased with a little sign of humility — "I'm so intelligent, I know I know nothing."

### Material Qualifications Can Be a Hindrance

- Sometimes one's material qualifications get in the way of devotional service.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.8.26** (Kunti Devi's prayer, being studied that morning) — *janmaiśvarya-śruta-śrībhir edhamāna-madaḥ pumān / naivārhaty abhidhātuṁ vai tvām akiñcana-gocaram*: "My dear Lord, Your Lordship can easily be approached, but only by those who are materially exhausted. One who is on the path of material progress — trying to improve himself with respectable parentage, great opulence, high education, and bodily beauty — cannot approach You with sincere feeling."
- One can become very proud or attached to his material qualities and miss the real goal of devotional service; and it may be difficult to train such a devotee.
- **The violin school (Berlin, 1800s):** A famous teacher opened a school for violin. Two students approached.
  - The first had been playing for about ten years, but self-taught, with many bad habits (though he could play). The teacher told him: for you it will be **10,000 Deutschmarks.**
  - The second had never played one minute in his life and knew nothing. The teacher told him: for you, **500 Deutschmarks.**
  - The first protested: "You're charging me 10,000 and I know how to play, and him only 500 when he knows nothing?" The teacher replied: "Yes — because you have so many bad habits, developed over so many years of playing without a teacher. For that new boy, no problem — I teach him A, B, C, D, E, F, G."
- Sometimes one's material talents are an obstacle. Of course one can and should use his talents and qualifications in Kṛṣṇa's service — but in a humble way, giving the results to Kṛṣṇa under the direction of the spiritual master. **Bhagavad-gītā 7.8** — Kṛṣṇa says "I am the ability in man." If one realizes all ability has been given by Kṛṣṇa for His service, then it is not an obstacle.

## Approach the Spiritual Master to Solve the Problems of Life

- Like Arjuna, everyone should approach a bona fide spiritual master, a bona fide teacher, to solve the problems of life. There are many problems; in fact, **life is basically a problem.**
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.14.58** — *padaṁ padaṁ yad vipadāṁ na teṣām*: there is **danger at every step.**
- **The disciple who died:** Two days ago one of the speaker's disciples died in a car accident, and Śrīla Prabhupāda's words came to mind ("you never know when we have to go"). Life is very dangerous at every step. Everyone thinks, "These things happen to other people, not to me."
- **Gopāsvāmī dāsa, doctor in France:** He often works in the emergency ward, and says most people coming in say the same thing: "I never thought this would happen to me. This only happens to people on the news broadcasts or in the newspapers."

### The Most Urgent Problems

- We have to set priorities and tend to the **most urgent problem.** What are the most serious problems? **Birth, disease, old age, and death.** These must be tended to immediately.
- **The battlefield:** When the wounded are lying there, the doctors go around treating the **most serious cases first.** Similarly, in life the most serious problems are not "how to get to work" or "how to pay the bills" (they'll give you another month) — the serious problems are that we have to take birth, get diseased, grow old, and die.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura:** "Upon the arrival of old age, material happiness becomes impossible" — and then just around the corner is death. These are no fun; they are the most serious problems and should be given the most attention immediately.

### Use the Youth for Kṛṣṇa Consciousness — Prahlāda Mahārāja

- Especially when one is young — with good health, strength, vigor, clarity of mind, and intelligence — we should not waste those valuable things on sense gratification, but use them to become Kṛṣṇa conscious.
- **Prahlāda Mahārāja** told his friends: "My friends, you shouldn't be playing frivolous sports; you should be chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa." They said, "Oh Prahlāda, we'll become Kṛṣṇa conscious when we're old."
  - Prahlāda — a great **mahājana**, a very intelligent little boy who was Kṛṣṇa conscious at five years of age (more special than a boy who can play violin at three) — replied: "You want to become Kṛṣṇa conscious when you're old? What is old? Old means just before you die. You may be old right now — you may die tomorrow. So you are old men right now." The boys agreed: "You're right, Prahlāda — what is this nonsense?"
  - Their teachers **Ṣaṇḍa and Amarka** asked, "Who has taught you this chanting?" and took Prahlāda to his father, **Hiraṇyakaśipu.** Even to his father Prahlāda preached: "O best of the asuras, I have heard that the best thing to do is to leave household life and go to the forest and take sannyāsa; the best forest is Vṛndāvana" — he was always preaching.
- So one should utilize this valuable human form of life to become Kṛṣṇa conscious, to solve the most serious problems. It may not seem they are the most serious problems — that is because we are in ignorance; when you are attached, in māyā, you cannot see clearly.

### The Man Hanging in the Well (Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's Favorite Story)

- **A man walking through the jungle** — slippery, full of vines — slipped and started to fall into a well, but grabbed a vine going over the well and hung there.
- Looking around he saw:
  - On either side, a **white rat** and a **black rat** eating the vine.
  - Below, many vipers, cobras, and snakes waiting for him to fall.
  - Above, a big **beehive** with many bees swarming around his head, from which came a **drop of honey.**
- He tasted the honey — "Mmm, that's yummy!" — even while crying "help, help." Suddenly a man nearby threw him a **rope**: "Grab the rope!" But he said, "No, I think I'll wait for the next drop of honey." So he fell — and the snakes had a feast.
- **The meaning:** We are in the forest of material enjoyment, in a very dangerous situation. The **white rat is daytime, the black rat is nighttime** — day and night eat away at our lives; with every rising and setting of the sun, one's duration of life decreases, except for one engaged in chanting the glories of the all-blissful Personality of Godhead. At the bottom of the well is **death.** The **spiritual master has kindly thrown the rope** of his mercy — the rope of his transcendental instructions — which can save us and pull us out so we are not devoured by the snakes of death. But we think, "No, there's some more honey coming." **The honey is sense gratification.** One's intelligence is bewildered by desires for sense gratification, so though the rope is there, we think "there's some honey still left," and we lose our chance.

## The Spiritual Master's Duty: Knowledge of Matter and Spirit

- Saved from the dangerous situation of material existence by the mercy of the spiritual master, we are now trained by him in all the **64 arts of devotional service** mentioned in **Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu** (*The Nectar of Devotion*).
- Essentially the spiritual master's duty is to **enlighten us with knowledge.** That is our relationship with him: we approach with **paripraśna** (submissive inquiry) — how to engage in devotional service — and he enlightens us with **divya-jñāna**, transcendental knowledge (cf. BG 4.34).
- That knowledge has two categories: **knowledge of matter and knowledge of spirit.**
- **"Guru is teaching about matter? Why? He is my spiritual master, not my material master."** He teaches both subjects, because **unless you become detached from matter, you can never understand what is spirit.** As long as you are attached to this world's so-called pleasures, you can never really understand spirit.
- **What does he teach about matter?** Not E = mc², not H₂O, not molecules and atoms. He teaches the **real nature of the material world.**
- **Bhagavad-gītā 8.16** — *ābrahma-bhuvanāl lokāḥ punar āvartino 'rjuna / mām upetya tu kaunteya punar janma na vidyate*: from the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery wherein repeated birth and death take place. That is the real understanding of this world.
- Materialistic people think the material world is real and the spiritual world is an illusion, but the real fact is that **material life is the illusion and spiritual life is real.** We have everything mixed up — "back to front, upside down, turned around." We think this is real and the spiritual life is for old ladies, babies, and sentimental fools. No — this is the illusion (in the sense that it is temporary and you cannot enjoy), and the reality is the spiritual world.
- So the spiritual master has to teach about matter — **māyā, "that which is not"** — to convince us. That is why Prabhupāda's purports contain so much about the material world.

### The Real Business: To Train Us to Love Kṛṣṇa

- Ultimately the spiritual master's real business is to **train us to love Kṛṣṇa** — "that's the fun part."
- **The progression of bhakti** (from Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu / *Nectar of Devotion*) — *ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu-saṅgo 'tha bhajana-kriyā / tato 'nartha-nivṛttiḥ syāt tato niṣṭhā rucis tataḥ / athāsaktis tato bhāvas tataḥ premābhyudañcati*: the stages are **śraddhā** (faith), **sādhu-saṅga** (association of devotees), **bhajana-kriyā** (practicing regulative principles under the spiritual master), **anartha-nivṛtti** (cleansing the heart), **niṣṭhā** (steadiness), **ruci** (taste), **āsakti**, **bhāva**, and **prema.**
- We are in the initial stages — developing faith, associating with devotees, practicing regulative principles, cleansing our hearts. But that is not the goal. The goal begins to be realized when we become steady and develop some taste for the holy names, a little love for Kṛṣṇa. **Prabhupāda:** "One should learn the art of crying for Kṛṣṇa." Our actual goal is the stage of **pure devotional service.**
- **What really attracts Kṛṣṇa:** The fact that we are trying to give up our material desires is nice, but that is not ultimately what attracts Kṛṣṇa and keeps His attention. **What really attracts Kṛṣṇa is your love for Kṛṣṇa.** He is very kind to His devotees because of that loving sentiment.

### Bhakta-vatsala — Not Jñānī-vatsala or Yogī-vatsala

- You never hear "jñānī-vatsala," "yogī-vatsala," or "bhukti-vatsala."
  - Is He **bhukti-vatsala**, very kind to the materialist sense-enjoyers? He smashes their heads so they come to understand "I cannot be happy here."
  - Is He **yogī-vatsala**? Like a mother saying "give me, give me" — "okay, take it, now go play, leave me alone" — Kṛṣṇa fulfills the yogis' desires for mystic powers (a few planets or universes to throw around, walking on water), which have nothing to do with pure devotion.
  - Is He **jñānī-vatsala**, lover of the jñānīs who want to merge into the Absolute Truth? "All right, merge."
- No — He is **bhakta-vatsala**, the lover of His devotees.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.11** — *ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham*: "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly." The type of love Kṛṣṇa is most attracted to and reciprocates most fully is **pure devotional service.** One who has that pure devotion always has Kṛṣṇa's association.

### Those With Pure Devotion Get Constant Association

- **The residents of Vṛndāvana** live with Kṛṣṇa and play with Kṛṣṇa.
- **King Yudhiṣṭhira / the Pāṇḍavas:** It is amazing that although Kṛṣṇa is **Mukunda** (the protector of His devotees), your spiritual master, and the Lord of your life, and though He is the director of all your activities (individual and family), He sometimes even carries out your orders as your messenger; although He is the great Personality of Godhead, He appears as your intimate friend. This is the type of relationship we ultimately desire — to always have Kṛṣṇa's association.

### Those With Material Desires Rarely See Kṛṣṇa — the Demigods

- For those who still have material desires, Kṛṣṇa is rarely, if ever, seen — like the **demigods.** They are devotees of Kṛṣṇa (He has given them very responsible services), but they are still hanging on to some material desires, so they do not get His constant association like the Pāṇḍavas or the residents of Vṛndāvana; they see Him only once in a while.
- **Whenever there is a disturbance** created by the enemies of Indra (the asuras), the various incarnations of the Lord appear to protect the devotees. The demigods get into trouble, the demons attack, and then Viṣṇu comes — "What's the problem? Demons" — and He goes back; they see Him only for a few moments.
- **The churning of the ocean of milk:** When the demons and devas churned the ocean of milk to get the nectar and there was no conclusion to the struggle, Kṛṣṇa came as **Mohinī-mūrti**, the most beautiful woman, bewildered the minds of the asuras (they were just looking), took the pot of nectar, gave it to the devas, and left — that was her mission, a momentary appearance. Then the asuras started to fight the devas to get the pot back, and Kṛṣṇa came again on **Garuḍa** — "chop, chop, chop" — and was gone. The demigod devotees can see Kṛṣṇa only momentarily, because there are still some desires left in the heart.
- **Nara Muni:** On the path of perfection, meditating in the dark jungle, doing austerities and penances, at one moment Lord **Nārāyaṇa** appeared: "Yes, I have come for a brief moment, just to give you an impetus to achieve Me, for I can hardly remain before those who are incomplete in devotion." Then He disappeared. The Lord appeared only to give a little taste of pure devotional service.

### The Goal: The Same Devotion as the Great Devotees

- All of us would like to be like the Pāṇḍavas or the residents of Vṛndāvana — always with Kṛṣṇa. That should be our goal. But we must have the same devotion as those great devotees, which entails becoming freed of **lust, anger, and greed.** That will come one day; be patient.
- **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.23** — *yasya deve parā bhaktir yathā deve tathā gurau*: to one who has full faith in the instructions of his spiritual master and in Kṛṣṇa, the full import of Vedic knowledge is revealed. So if we keep serving our Guru Mahārājas and Lord Caitanya, one day that goal will be there for us.
- **Be patient — the first step in the right direction:** Even a first step in the right direction is better than going quickly in the wrong direction. **Confucius:** "The longest journey begins with the first step." By Prabhupāda's mercy, after millions of years going in the wrong direction, we have been turned around and are making our first careful steps in the right direction. As we say in America, **"slow but steady wins the race."**
- If we are careful and attentive to our **sādhana-bhakti** — chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa — and work very hard to help our spiritual master, Śrīla Prabhupāda, and Lord Caitanya in the saṅkīrtana movement, then it is just a question of time before that pure devotion wells up within our heart and we find ourselves back in the spiritual world.

## Questions and Answers

### "Why Should I Believe Krishna Consciousness?"

- A girl today asked, "Why should I believe this — why should I believe anything?"
- **Submissive hearing is required.** If one hears submissively, one can be convinced about Kṛṣṇa consciousness; but if one has a challenging attitude, you can never convince anyone. English saying: **"A man convinced against his will is of the same opinion still"** — if he does not want to be convinced, he never will be.
- **The scissors and the knife (Prabhupāda's story):** Two men argued over whether scissors or a knife is better for cutting. The man with the knife picked up the man with the scissors and threw him in the lake. He couldn't swim and began to drown; as he was going down, he still made his fingers go like scissors — and then drowned. Unless a person is submissive, it is very difficult to convince him.
- **For the submissive:** preach very intelligently and convince through the philosophy, point by point, logically — beginning from the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th chapters of Bhagavad-gītā. "I've never met a submissive person who hasn't been convinced with the philosophy of Bhagavad-gītā."
- **Convinced by the superior taste:** Another way one is convinced is by the **superior taste** of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. We give up something inferior if we get something better. Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the **positive alternative** to the miseries of material existence. As soon as one starts chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, one can practically experience that this is better — by comparison.
  - **The fruit at the market:** You pick up two fruits, compare them, and take the better one. We are saying Kṛṣṇa consciousness is better than material existence — but you have to try it.
  - **Prabhupāda's jar of honey:** I can tell you how nice the honey is, but unless you open it and taste it you will never know. Licking the outside of the jar — although you are very close — you will never get the taste. Simply watching the devotees is not sufficient; you have to try the process yourself. The devotees' main business is chanting, so you also chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, and then you will see why all these boys and girls are chanting — because they are getting that higher taste (*paraṁ dṛṣṭvā nivartate*, BG 2.59). Following regulative principles is not difficult for us, because we realize those (forbidden) activities are in themselves a source of misery and pain.
- **Give them prasādam:** "Do your friends come here? Do they chant? Do they take prasādam? Just keep giving them prasādam." **Prasādam is Lord Caitanya's secret weapon** because it purifies — it is absolute and transcendental — so in due course it purifies the heart so one has the intelligence to understand the philosophy. That is why people drink prasādam milk in Vedic culture: prasādam is purifying, and **milk nourishes the finer cells of the brain**, so one has a brain for understanding philosophy. Nowadays they drink beer and Coca-Cola — so what state the brain is in. Prasādam nourishes the soul and the brain so they can understand.

### The Doctor Sending a Nurse — Can We Have Faith in the Helper?

- **Question:** We say the guru is like a doctor, but sometimes a doctor sends nurses or student doctors to give treatment, and the patient has no faith in these helpers; the nurse may hurt the patient badly, and the patient thinks, "If the doctor did it, it wouldn't hurt so bad."
- **Answer — same procedure, same result:** If one follows the guru's instructions perfectly, the same result will be there. If the doctor says "one plus one is two" and the student says "one plus one is two," the same truth is there. If the doctor says "apply the ointment like this" and the trainee applies it the same way, the same result follows — because it is the **doctor's knowledge** that solves the problem.
  - Analogy: if I am fixing a car and train my son to fix it by the same science, mechanically doing the same thing, the same result will be there.
- So we can have the same faith if we see the student following the same procedure/instructions perfectly. **The student eventually becomes the teacher** — where do teachers come from? They were once students. In the absence of the teacher, the qualified student takes the role and functions in the same way.
- **Example:** In the absence of Śrīla Prabhupāda, his movement continues to grow and build, because his followers and grand-followers are nicely following his instructions. So you can have faith.

### Prabhupada's Dreams Are Reality

- These are things Prabhupāda dreamed about — but he knew they were a reality; there was just some time separating the dream from its manifestation.
- **A pure devotee's dream is not a loose material dream.** Our dreams are concoctions — we take a piece of gold and a mountain seen during the day, hallucinate, and put them together to "see a gold mountain at night." Most of our dreaming is concoction or spent material speculation. But everything a pure devotee does is **absolute reality**, so even his dreaming is purely Kṛṣṇa conscious.
- **Yoga-nidrā / Mahā-viṣṇu's dream:** This whole material creation is described as the dream of **Mahā-viṣṇu**, who lies down in the Causal Ocean and dreams. It is not an illusory state of forgetfulness like our dreaming. Everything the Lord does — past, present, and future — is absolute, so even His dreaming is a state of reality; when He dreams this material cosmic manifestation, these events actually happen.
- Similarly, the pure devotee lives in that same state of pure Kṛṣṇa consciousness, absolute reality — whether awake during the day or "sleeping" at night, he never loses touch with absolute reality; there is no state of forgetfulness for a pure devotee. **Prabhupāda:** "There was never a time when I ever forgot the lotus feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa." So everything the pure devotee thinks is reality — it may just be a question of time before that reality manifests for the vision of ordinary people.

### Prabhupada Was Never Alone (the Park Bench)

- **The famous story:** When Prabhupāda was first visiting America, he sat down on a park bench. A reporter for the *Village Voice* happened to walk by and sat down next to him. At first he thought this man was all alone — "a lonely man from India." But when he engaged in conversation, it was obvious that Prabhupāda had a great mission; he was not like so many derelicts or lonely people. As the Beatles sang, "Look at all the lonely people" — but Prabhupāda was **not lonely; he always has Kṛṣṇa with him.**
- **Govinda in the heart:** According to the *Questions and Answers* by Harikeśa Svāmī, the ordinary soul has the **Paramātmā** (the four-handed Viṣṇu) in his heart; but when you become a pure devotee, then **Lord Govinda, Śyāmasundara**, comes into your heart. Prabhupāda had Govinda — not merely Paramātmā, but Śyāmasundara in the heart.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 10.10** — *teṣāṁ satata-yuktānāṁ bhajatāṁ prīti-pūrvakam / dadāmi buddhi-yogaṁ taṁ yena mām upayānti te*: "To those who serve Me with love and devotion, I give the understanding by which they can come to Me." That "I" means Kṛṣṇa Himself — He is always in touch, always within the vision of His pure devotee.
- **Prabhupāda's reply to the reporter:** The gentleman asked, "Do you have any followers? Do you have a place to live?" Prabhupāda said, "Yes, I have thousands of followers." (To one devotee he once said, "As my followers, some know it and some don't," because ultimately everyone is getting benefit from Śrīla Prabhupāda's presence on this planet, the books he left behind, and the movement he gave — a shelter for everyone, directly or indirectly.) "I have many followers, many temples, many schools, many farms" — everyone is getting the benefit.
