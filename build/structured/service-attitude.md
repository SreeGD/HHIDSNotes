## Opening: An Arrival Address at the Festival

- The speaker (a visiting sannyasi and godbrother of Indradyumna Swami) explains that Maharaj requested him to give an arrival address.
- This reminds him of **Srila Prabhupada**, who used to give arrival addresses whenever he visited any temple or any place.

## Report from Canada: The Rathayatra Festivals and the Youth Bus Tour

- He has just come from Canada, where he participated in the Rathayatra festivals in Montreal and Toronto — also very ecstatic festivals, but (he notes with a compliment to the hosts) "not as nicely organized as your festival."
- One thing he must mention: for the first time he experienced the **Hare Krishna Youth Bus Tour**.
  - About 30–35 grown-up gurukulis and about 20–22 matajis.
  - "You should have seen them dancing — it's unbelievable." So the local matajis should not think they are on top of the world.
- Anecdote of the street kirtan:
  - When the tour went out, the whole traffic stopped.
  - One of the matajis led the kirtan, and many passengers on the street stopped.
  - He noticed a girl, maybe 16 years old: at first she danced a little; then she looked at "our matajis" (who were quite dominant/prominent in their dancing); then she started crying — perhaps thinking, "Wow, this would be nice, to live such a nice life."

## "One Who Has Life Can Preach"

- Visiting this festival reminded him of **Srila Prabhupada's** saying: *"One who has life, he can preach."*
- This is what inspires him about the festival: "You have life. Therefore so many things are happening — and it will happen more and more."

## Prabhupada's Vision of a World-Traveling Sankirtan Party

- The festival reminded him of Srila Prabhupada's wish to have a world-traveling Sankirtana party.
- Perhaps this traveling festival tour might eventually develop into an international, world-traveling Sankirtana party — "traveling from country to country. This would be really ecstatic."
- Why only Poland? Why should only Polish people benefit in such a tremendous way? What about all the others — even Mongolia?

## Accepting the Reception; Respecting Devotees as Vaishnava Culture

- On being received: he first thought maybe he was doing something wrong, because the reception was "like a Vyasa Puja."
- But then he accepted the principle: this is actually our Vaishnava culture — **to respect devotees**. "Prabhupada taught us like this."
- He identifies an unfortunate development in the society: "some devotees lose respect for other devotees."
- He felt truly inspired — not because he is being honored, but because the devotees gave such a nice example of how to respect other devotees. "That should be spread all over the movement — to really genuinely respect other devotees."

## Associating with Devotees Is the Basis of Happiness

- Citing **Srila Prabhupada's teaching** (paraphrased): if a devotee learns how to associate with other devotees — with more advanced, equally advanced, and less advanced devotees — he becomes happy in Krishna consciousness.
  - The implication: if we do not follow this principle, we will not become happy in our Krishna consciousness. "It is such a valuable principle."
- **Indradyumna Maharaj** has been painstakingly, for so many years, training and educating devotees in this way — "and that's why you are happy."
  - Proof of that happiness: "Otherwise, how can you dance like this at seven o'clock in the morning? Just see what other people are doing at seven in the morning — you are dancing. There must be some happiness." And today they will continue dancing on the street.

## Preaching Spirit and Attracting Others

- These things are inspiring: there is life, there is a preaching spirit, devotees are exercising Vaishnava principles so nicely.
- We should invite more and more people into our society, and constantly meditate on how to attract them.
  - "Attraction is there" — the question is how to facilitate their joining this movement.
  - There are so many different ways to involve people in Krishna consciousness; "that also should be our constant meditation."

## Question 1 — How to Cultivate Respect for Devotees and Avoid Losing It

- **Question:** You spoke about the importance of respecting devotees, and now that respect has been lost. How can we cultivate respect and avoid losing it?
- **Answer:** We can only avoid losing respect for devotees **by maintaining a service attitude.**
  - This ties back to the point about how to associate with more advanced, equal, and less advanced devotees — it is only possible on the basis of a service attitude.
  - **Toward more advanced devotees:** Obviously, "I want to render service to them."
  - **Toward equally advanced devotees:** The service is, "Let us spread Krishna consciousness together; let us serve Krishna together." That is friendship — very close friendship. When equally advanced devotees associate, they have only one thought: how to serve Krishna together, how to spread Krishna consciousness together.
  - **Toward less advanced devotees:** How to render service to them so that they can make advancement in Krishna consciousness — by teaching, and by creating facilities for them to render service.
- With a service attitude always present, there is no question of thinking "I am more advanced than others" or "I am superior to others."
  - The service attitude means: "I am the servant of everyone." That is the actual Vaishnava mood — "I am the servant of everybody."

## "Dāso 'smi" — The Servant's Response, Contrasted with the Mayavadi

- When people bow down to a sannyasi, he should answer **"Dāso 'smi"** — "I am your servant."
- The wrong response: "Yes, I am your master; bow down a little longer." That is how the Mayavadis think.
  - Mayavadis, when they take sannyas, think, "Now I am Narayana, I am Vishnu."

## Story: Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in Jagannatha Puri

- When **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** visited Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya in Jagannatha Puri, Mahaprabhu took on a very humble attitude.
- Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya was known as a very learned scholar, and Mahaprabhu requested him to give some advice, some instruction.
- Context — the ecstatic symptoms: In Jagannatha Puri, the temple guards had wanted to beat Mahaprabhu up and drag him out, because He was displaying ecstatic symptoms (falling on the ground) that they did not understand. But Bhattacharya could understand that this was not an ordinary person — these were genuine ecstatic symptoms.
- So Sarvabhauma first glorified Mahaprabhu, witnessing His ecstatic symptoms. But then he said: "Since you are asking, I would like to give you one advice:
  - "You should not have taken sannyas. First of all, you are a young man — not good.
  - "Second, it is not good for a Vaishnava's humility, because when you accept sannyas you are being glorified — you even think that you are Vishnu. This is not good."
- Mahaprabhu listened very patiently, but later explained that this is **not the purpose of Vaishnava sannyas.** Vaishnava sannyas is just the opposite: now he thinks, "I am the servant of everyone."

## The Vaishnava Sees Paramatma in Every Living Entity

- It is explained that a Vaishnava thinks Paramatma is present in every living entity. "This is why I have to respect every living being — because of Paramatma's presence."
- That is the only possible way not to lose our respect for other Vaishnavas: **by cultivating a service attitude.**

## Service Training on the Festival Tour; Having "No Time" to Lose Respect

- A beautiful feature of this festival tour: all the devotees are being encouraged, trained, and educated in how to render service.
- He was surprised to learn that programs happen almost every day — sometimes every day, sometimes every second day. As a former manager, he knows what that means, and he thought it impossible: to pack everything, move to the next city, do Harinam and the whole morning program, prasad, and then the whole program again in the evening. "Impossible." Definitely very, very exhausting.
- But "try to tolerate this, because by this you will develop a service attitude more and more."
- The key principle: "We should have no time to lose respect for other devotees."
  - "Only people who have too much time run into such mental conflicts. They have to search for some reasons to lose respect for other devotees. We have no time for this — always engaged in service."
  - That is the only possible way to maintain a respectful attitude.
- Qualification: not service according to our own whims, but **under the guidance of advanced devotees.**

## Question 2 — Respecting Godbrothers and God-Cousins

- **Question:** Giving respect to senior devotees is quite easy, but among god-cousins and godbrothers, sometimes "I feel like I want to strangle" [them]. Why is there such a dichotomy, and what to do?
- **Answer:** That is definitely much more difficult. But again — if we take to the service attitude.
- **Practical example — prasad time** (why it can "feel like strangling someone"):
  - I sit down and expect to be served.
  - I tell the devotee serving prasad, "Give me a little bit," but he gives a lot.
  - When I want to stop him, I put my hand over the hot khichri — "Okay, it's enough" — and he gives a full scoop of hot khichri right on my hand. Then you feel like strangling that devotee.
  - **The solution:** In order to avoid this, I should not sit down and expect to be served. Instead, "I take the bucket and I serve."
- General principle: Just by rendering service to other devotees, one can counteract the danger of developing this kind of disrespectful attitude.
- The same applies to envy: "When I'm envious of another person" — rendering service "kills envy just at the root."

## Closing

- He stops here, offering: "Srila Prabhupada ki jai," and thanks the devotees for their kind reception.
- On the reception: "It was embarrassing but inspiring at the same time — like poison and nectar at the same time." He calls it a very special shower.
- The MC closes: chant japa now; deity greeting at nine o'clock; Maharaj is invited to sit with the devotees to chant japa together. "Hare Krishna."
