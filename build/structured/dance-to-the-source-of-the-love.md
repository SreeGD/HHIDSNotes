## Opening: Arriving Amid Kirtan and Honoring Radhanath Swami

- The speaker has just arrived in the midst of a very ecstatic kirtan led by his dear godbrother, His Holiness Radhanath Swami.
- He says with all certainty that he is very happy to be back in the devotees' association again, and very happy to have Radhanath Swami Maharaj's association.
- Like many present, he is in awe of the great preaching Radhanath Swami is doing, particularly throughout America.
  - Maharaj is spending more and more time in America with his world-famous book, *The Journey Home*.
  - With the help of others, he is meeting the higher echelon of society — which the speaker considers "taking preaching to a higher level that's really never been done before."
  - He is pioneering the Krishna consciousness movement among the important people of society: the leaders, the leadership, on the highest level.

## Prabhupada in Paris: "Bring Intelligent People for Me to Preach To"

- Story from the speaker's early days: they were in Paris with Srila Prabhupada. The speaker was leading a Sankirtan group, and Prabhupada was visiting their temple.
- He brought his Sankirtan group into Prabhupada's room one day. Prabhupada was speaking, then said: **"Why don't you bring some people for me to preach to?"**
  - The speaker protested: "But Srila Prabhupada, these are the Sankirtan devotees, and every morning you're preaching to the temple devotees and the congregation."
  - Prabhupada repeated: "Why don't you bring some people for me to preach to?"
  - Present were Yogesvara, Bhagavan, and other devotees — some 300 devotees from all over France, Germany and similar places. They were at a loss for words.
- Prabhupada said a third time: **"Why don't you bring some *intelligent* people for me to preach to?"**
  - He did not mean the devotees were unintelligent — he wanted to preach to the higher echelon of society.
  - The devotees had never thought that way, so they had to scramble.
- The next day they gathered such people: a professor from the University of Stockholm, a famous author, a poet, a politician, and a famous rock star.
  - When these people came in to see Prabhupada, "he was just beaming."
  - After several days of many such people coming in, Prabhupada said: "Thank you very much... this is the best preaching."
- Prabhupada then quoted the verse that whatever a great man does, the common man follows in his footsteps, and whatever exemplary acts he performs, the whole world pursues.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 3.21** — *yad yad acarati sresthas tat tad evetaro janah*: "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow; whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."

## Preaching to the Leaders of Society

- Radhanath Swami is setting the best example by traveling throughout the country and meeting so many important people; whatever these people do "will filter down."
- Unfortunately, the nonsensical ideas and sinful activities of such leaders also filter down — yet these leaders are capable of wonderful things as well.
- In their hearts they are servants of the Lord.
  - **Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 20.108** — *jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya-dasa*: the constitutional position of the living entity is to be the eternal servant of Krishna.
  - When such people come in contact with a potent devotee, that dormant service attitude comes out.
- In the years to come, the speaker predicts "big things happening here in America with this type of preaching," and says we should all follow suit. (He asks for more applause for Radhanath Swami.)

## Prabhupada's Personal Instruction: "Preach Boldly, Have Faith in the Holy Names"

- Story: The speaker was going to France to join the devotees at the fledgling temple in Paris. He had come over from New York with Prabhupada on the plane, spent a few days in London, and it was time to leave.
- He had always wanted a personal instruction from Prabhupada.
  - He recalls that Prabhupada himself had received a personal instruction from his own Guru Maharaj: if you ever get money, print books, and preach this Krishna consciousness movement in the English-speaking language.
- As they said goodbye in Prabhupada's quarters, the speaker was inwardly wishing Prabhupada would give him "an instruction I could base my life on."
- Prabhupada said: "So you go to France, and you help them, and you get my books translated into French, and you distribute them all over the country, and meet important people like that."
- Then Prabhupada stopped, leaned over, looked him straight in the eye, and said: **"And you preach boldly, and have faith in the holy names."**
  - It went deep into the speaker's heart — like when Hanuman opens his chest and reveals Sita and Rama seated within. He likes to think that if he could open his own heart, those instructions would be etched there: *preach boldly and have faith in the holy names.*
- Then Prabhupada said, "To encourage you, I'll give you a gift."
  - He reached into his dresser drawer and took out one of his old dhotis, saying, "I had this for one year," and gave it to the speaker.
  - Trembling, the speaker received it. Prabhupada said: "Always remember that a gift from a Vaishnava is a very special thing."

## The Courage and Purity Needed to Preach to Leaders

- The speaker has tried to follow the instruction to preach boldly, but reflects that it takes a lot of courage to approach leaders.
- For anyone who has preached on the streets or within society, the greatest challenge is to approach people in prominent positions — charismatic, very intelligent, and possibly much influenced by the material energy — and present Vedic culture to them. It is never easy.
- What it requires:
  - A lot of purity — "Purity is the force."
  - Intelligence.
  - Charisma.
  - The mercy of one's spiritual master.
- The speaker sees Radhanath Swami's preaching as fulfilling Prabhupada's order to preach boldly and have faith in the holy names, and thanks him for the example he sets for everyone, including his godbrothers and godsisters.

## The Theme: Dance to the Source of Love

- The theme of the program is "dance to the source of love," which is Krishna.
- Chanting, dancing, some *harikatha* (hearing) and hearing will awaken our *prema-bhakti* (loving devotion).
- Central-most is the chanting of the maha-mantra: **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.**
- Walking into the room, it was evident how potent those three names — Hare, Krishna, and Rama — are to elevate everyone to the transcendental platform; the devotees looked very joyful.

## The Potency of Sound (the Radio Analogy)

- Sound by nature has great potency.
  - There is a shloka in the Vedic scriptures that says: **by sound one becomes conditioned, and by sound one becomes liberated.**
- We can understand the potency of sound even in our conditioned state:
  - Turn on a radio and turn the dial: someone is singing the blues — how he lost his girlfriend or she lost her boyfriend. The sound vibration makes you remember the same thing happening to you, and you become sad. In a moment your consciousness is affected by sound entering the ear and the heart.
  - Turn the dial further: a happy song. (John Lennon said, "Take a sad song and make it better.") Immediately you forget your blues and become joyful. What happened? Sound.
  - Turn the dial further: a news broadcast — the Democrats debating the Republicans — and you become angry. What is it? Sound.
- Sound vibration entering the ear and heart affects your consciousness and how you perceive life — so potent. By sound we become conditioned and by sound we become liberated. That is the potency of *mundane* sound.

## The Potency of Transcendental Sound

- What, then, is the potency of *transcendental* sound — the sounds we are chanting, the Hare Krishna maha-mantra? This is coming straight from Goloka.
- Prabhupada analyzes that the sound of Krishna's name, specifically the maha-mantra, is so potent that:
  - It can destroy all the karmic reactions that have been infesting the heart for millions of lives.
  - It can uproot material desires and throw them to a distant place.
- According to Haridasa Thakura, the ultimate, real benefit of chanting Hare Krishna is that it awakens *krsna-prema* (love of God) within the heart.
- Who would not chant Hare Krishna, knowing the benefit of simply letting that sound enter the ear and the heart?

## The Meaning of "Mantra"

- The very word *mantra*: *ma(n)* means mind and *tra* means to deliver.
- So *mantra* means a sound vibration that delivers us from material consciousness.
- Material consciousness is basically: "I am this body," the purpose of life is material enjoyment, there is no soul, there is no God in control, etc.
- Just by chanting, all that illusion is gone.

## Mantra in Previous Ages: Restricted to the Qualified

- In previous ages, only a small, select group of people could chant mantra and benefit from it.
  - Mantras were chanted by the priests (brahmanas), and to some degree the kshatriyas — the higher orders of life — but generally it was the business of the priests because they were qualified.
- If one who was unqualified chanted a mantra, it had no effect.
  - "Unqualified" meaning one in the mode of ignorance or passion, given to sinful activity and materialistic goals.
  - Such chanting was like giving knowledge to a fool, or like pouring ghee on ashes: after the fire is out you pour ghee on ashes and nothing happens. Ghee is meant to be poured on the fire.
- So if someone unqualified in Vedic culture — who did not live a pure life of simple living and high thinking — tried to chant, regardless of which mantra, it would have no effect. Only the brahmanas, sometimes kshatriyas, the rishis and yogis would chant.

## Kali-yuga's Concession: The Yuga-dharma of Chanting

- When Kali-yuga arrived, a special concession was made because everyone had become very fallen — yet the goal of life remained the same.
  - **Vedanta-sutra 1.1.1** — *athato brahma-jijnasa*: now that we have come to the human form of life, this is the time for self-realization, culminating in pure devotion and service to Krishna.
- Although in previous ages only a small select group could chant mantra and benefit, the Lord decided that the *yuga-dharma* of this age would be chanting the maha-mantra — **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare** — and suddenly anyone, by the Lord's grace, could chant.
- This is the Lord's discretion: but for the Lord, no one can enunciate *dharma* (religion). It is up to Him to decide, and therefore in each age there is a different process of dharma / self-realization.

## The Yuga-dharmas of the Four Ages

- **Satya-yuga:** meditation.
- **Treta-yuga:** ritualistic sacrifices.
- **Dvapara-yuga:** temple worship.
- **Kali-yuga (the present age):** chanting mantra, specifically the maha-mantra — the "great chant for deliverance."
- All the benefits one gets from chanting all the other mantras are contained in that one mantra, Hare Krishna.

## Ramanujacharya and the "Secret" Mantra

- Chanting became something public, as demonstrated in the life of Ramanujacharya, a little over a thousand years ago — the great Acharya, head of the Sri Sampradaya.
- When Ramanuja was young he had several gurus enlightening him — a diksha (initiating) guru and shiksha (instructing) gurus.
- He went to one of his teachers, who said: "Young man, I have a very special mantra for you. This mantra is so powerful that all the illusion of material life will be dissipated —
  - just as the sun rising in the morning dissipates all the fog (fog so thick that even huge blowers cannot clear it, yet the rising sun does),
  - and just as darkness is dissipated by the rising of the sun.
- "But it is very special, and you have to keep it to yourself. You can't tell anyone. It's very powerful." The teacher then whispered the mantra in his ear.

## The Heart of a Vaishnava

- What did Ramanujacharya do? Being a great Vaishnava — a Sri Vaishnava (another Sampradaya, who worship Lakshmi-Narayan) — his response flowed from the heart of a Vaishnava.
- What is the heart of a Vaishnava? It is demonstrated by Bhaktivinoda Thakura, one of our Vedic acharyas, who prayed:
  - "When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest, and with a lowly heart I'll go out to preach the divine command?" (mood of Bhaktivinoda Thakura's prayers).
- Like **Prahlad Maharaj**, a Vaishnava is not satisfied with his own liberation.
  - He is a liberated soul even while living in this world; his consciousness is always with Krishna through devotional service.
  - So he is always thinking, "What about all the poor fallen souls rotting in the material world?"
- Story illustrating this mood: The speaker was sitting with Prabhupada when one of his godbrothers said, "Srila Prabhupada, sometimes I feel so sad for the suffering of the conditioned souls in this world."
  - The speaker thought: "Wow, that's a noble sentiment. Prabhupada is going to bless him."
  - Prabhupada shot back: **"Why *sometimes*?"**

## Ramanuja Broadcasts the Mantra from the Pole

- In the heart of the great Vaishnava-acharya Ramanuja, he took the mantra in the right ear (mantras are always chanted in the right ear).
- He then went straight to the marketplace, climbed a very tall pole, and from the top started screaming the mantra at the top of his lungs:
  - **"Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya"** (repeated) — "I offer my obeisances unto Vasudeva, the Supreme Personality."
  - "Come everybody, everybody listen... Chant! Chant!" And the people began to chant.
- His guru heard that he had done this and called him back, very angry: "I have given you this secret mantra for *your* liberation. It is so powerful that anyone can be delivered with this mantra."
- Ramanuja's realization: since anyone can be liberated by it, "let me chant it to the whole world so everyone can benefit from chanting these names of God."
- Thus mantra chanting became available — because the Lord can make the adjustment. It is His prerogative to make an adjustment according to the age, for how people can make advancement in spiritual life.

## Sukadeva to Parikshit: The Ocean of Inauspiciousness and the One Good Quality

- Shastra confirms this. Shukadeva Goswami is speaking to King Parikshit — the king who renounced his kingdom and came to live on the bank of the Ganges, because he knew his time had come and he would soon be leaving his body. Through the king, the sage is instructing all of us.
- Shukadeva said: "My dear king, this age of Kali, which has just begun, is an age of inauspiciousness — an *ocean* of inauspiciousness."
  - Analogy: imagine a beautiful ocean; if you are swimming in it you can get lost in the huge expanse of salt water. Now imagine swimming in an ocean of *inauspiciousness* — quite poetic, but in an alarming way. ("Where'd you go today?" "Mom, I was swimming in an ocean of inauspiciousness.")
  - If you have ever been out to sea as a sailor, you know how vast the ocean is; such is the ocean we live in during Kali-yuga — the iron age of quarrel and hypocrisy. A little scary.
- But the Lord gives a positive alternative. Our acharyas describe this temporary place of suffering but do not leave us hanging — they always give the positive alternative, here in the last two lines.
- The complete verse: "My dear king, this age of Kali, which has just begun, is an ocean of inauspiciousness — but there is one good thing left in this age." What is it? "By singing these very potent transcendental names of God — which are actually none different than God — one can achieve perfection and go back home, back to Godhead."
  - **Srimad-Bhagavatam 12.3.51** — *kaler dosa-nidhe rajann asti hy eko mahan gunah / kirtanad eva krsnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet*: "Although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is one good quality: simply by chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra one can be freed from material bondage and promoted to the transcendental kingdom."

## Demigods Lining Up to Take Birth in Kali-yuga

- Such is the concession that we hear the demigods on the higher planets are lining up, praying to take birth in Kali-yuga.
- Some sages do not even want to *say* the name "Kali-yuga," because there is "a lot in the name."
  - Just as when you say the name of some infamous criminal or politician your mind feels polluted, some sages will not even utter "Kali-yuga."
- Yet the demigods on the heavenly planets are lining up and praying to take birth here — because of the presence and power of the holy name.

## Why the Name Is So Powerful: The Name as Incarnation

- Why is it so powerful? Because the name, fame, form, pastimes, and paraphernalia of the Lord are all absolute.
- Prabhupada made it very poetic: when you chant Hare Krishna, it is as if Krishna is dancing on the tip of your tongue.
- **Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 17.22** — *kali-kale nama-rupe krsna-avatara*: in the age of Kali the holy name is the incarnation of Krishna.
  - Because the holy name is so powerful, the sound vibration is considered an incarnation of God.
- This is the beauty of Vedic culture: we have insight not only into the Lord Himself, but into His many expansions and incarnations who incarnate in this world.
  - They incarnate in order to defeat the atheists and re-establish dharma.
  - **Bhagavad-gita 4.8** — *paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya ca duskrtam / dharma-samsthapanarthaya*: "To deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants, and re-establish the principles of religion, I appear millennium after millennium."
- So here is an incarnation — the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.

## Chanting Properly Requires Understanding the Glories of the Name

- In summary, if we want to chant properly and get the full benefit, we have to understand the glories of the holy name.
- If we chant without understanding the philosophy and science of chanting Hare Krishna, and without reading about the lives of the great devotees who fully surrendered to the chanting, then our appreciation will not be great, and we will not chant properly — with full attention, in a mood of prayer, begging for service.
- Analogy of the child and the mother's ring:
  - A mother takes her ring off to wash the dishes and it falls to the ground. Her little child finds the ring but has no knowledge of the value of a diamond — she looks at it, plays with it, puts it in her mouth, and swallows it.
  - The mother comes: "Oh, my ring! My husband gave me that — I almost lost it!" and carefully puts it back on.
  - One person had no knowledge of the value of the gem; the other had full knowledge.
- Therefore we need festivals like this — festivals of the holy name — where we not only chant and dance in ecstasy, but also hear the philosophy of the holy name.
- We can study books such as *Namamrta*, compiled by the BBT, containing all of Prabhupada's quotes about the holy name.
  - Prabhupada glorified the holy name so often that it is a big, fat book — *Namamrta*.
  - For connoisseurs of the holy name (which we all should be), it is one of their favorite books: how to chant Hare Krishna. We have to hear about it.

## More Than a Mantra: A Prayer of the Heart ("My Friend, My Friend, My Friend")

- Actually the holy name is *more* than a mantra — more than a sound vibration that delivers us from material consciousness. That is roughly how Prabhupada first presented it in the Western countries when he first came. But it is more like a prayer of the heart.
- Story: Prabhupada was giving a Bhagavad-gita class with the devotees in Boston, and a guest raised his hand: "Swamiji, you're always stressing the chanting of Hare Krishna... what does it mean? Over and over and over — what does it *really* mean?"
- Prabhupada could have given his classic translation:
  - "My Lord, my dear energy of the Lord, Srimati Radharani — please, please, please engage me again in Your devotional service." That is what we are saying when we chant.
  - It is not a mantra or a technique merely to control the mind — it is a prayer of the heart.
- But this time Prabhupada said something different. He said to that boy (Michael): "What does it mean — Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare? It means: **my friend, my friend, my friend.**"
  - The speaker heard this on a tape; it was Prabhupada's translation at that particular time.
- It is really a prayer from the heart. We are calling out in distress: "Please lift us up from this material world and place us again at Your lotus feet with devotional service." In desperation we call out to the Lord.

## Concluding Story: The Holy Name on the Plane to Durban

- The speaker concludes with a story from a recent trip to South Africa about how one person took shelter of the holy names in distress.
- They were flying from Johannesburg (in the north) to Durban, along the central coastal region of South Africa.
  - The airport had recently been moved from downtown Durban up the coast to a place called La Mercy.
  - The speaker had always thought that was not a good place for an airport: it is far away, and — having chanted japa up there many times — he knew it was very windy. Why build an airport there?
  - He had been away from South Africa for a year and a half while they built it.
- As the plane came in for landing, descending lower and lower, a violent wind shook the plane.
  - The speaker yelled out, "Hare Krishna!"
  - The man behind him: "Allahu Akbar!"
  - The lady across: "Jesus Christ!"
  - The Buddhists: "Ah, ah, ah, ah..."
  - He is not exaggerating: South Africa is a multi-racial, multi-ethnic country — they call it "the Rainbow Nation" — with people of all faiths, denominations, and ancestries, so everyone called out the name of God they were used to calling.
- Then from the cabin: "Abort! Abort! Abort!" The co-pilot said not to land — it was too dangerous — and the plane went back up.
- The man sitting next to him challenged him: "Do you really think there's a God out there hearing you — way up in the sky beyond the moon — who's going to make sure this little tiny machine doesn't crash? Do you believe that? Are you crazy?"
  - Speaker: "Yeah, I firmly believe that, sir."
  - "What?"
  - "Yes. Actually, God is present in the sound of His name, and the holy name is feared by fear personified."
  - The man pressed against the window: "Did you say fear *by* fear personified? God is present in the sound of His name?" Then: "That's sentimental rubbish. You guys are just crazy. I would never do something like that. Foolishness, sentiment."
- The plane came down again. The wind was even stronger — you could see it in the trees. This time the man was holding onto his seat, white as a ghost. Again: "Abort, abort" — the second time.
  - The speaker said to the stewardess: "This is dangerous. Why don't we just go back to Johannesburg? I'll take a bus down here."
  - The stewardess: "Our pilot is very professional, very experienced. He will make the right decision."
  - Speaker: "I don't know..."
- The pilot came on: "Ladies and gentlemen, we're going to make one more attempt." They came in higher this time, but the wind grew stronger and the plane began shaking even before the descent.
- The man said: "Well, if I did have to do it, which of those names of God should I sing?"
  - (The speaker notes: a preacher is always looking for an opening, like a boxer looking for one.)
  - Speaker: "All these names are absolute. Just as you have your personal name, your middle name, your family name, and your girlfriend has a pet name for you — so God has unlimited names, and He is present in all of them."
  - "Absolute? Which one should I sing?"
  - "Why don't you try the Hare Krishna song? That's my favorite."
  - "Yeah, I can see. Teach it to me."
  - "Okay: Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare..." The man interrupted: "I'll be dead by then — the thing will crash!"
  - "Okay, just say 'Hare Krishna.' Can you say it? Say it — if you want to save the plane, say it."
  - The man: "Hare Krishna."
- And then the wind died down, and the plane landed smoothly — like a snap of the finger. Everyone was relieved.
  - The speaker looked at him: "See, it works."
- As they taxied to the runway, the speaker asked: "So now do you believe there's a Lord beyond the clouds, behind the moon, maybe watching and protecting us?"
  - The man: "Maybe."
  - Speaker: "Well, you've made a lot of progress — from being an atheist to being an agnostic. Congratulations." (A big step from "no God" to "maybe there's a God out there.")
- The speaker wished him well on his path, and asked: "If I get in trouble again, what are you going to sing?"
  - The man: "Hare Krishna."
- The talk closes moving into kirtan (Hare Krishna / Nitai-Gaura-hari-bol).
