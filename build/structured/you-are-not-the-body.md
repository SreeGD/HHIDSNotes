## Opening: Reading Bhagavad-gita 2.18

- The class is based on *Bhagavad-gita As It Is*, translation and commentary by Srila Prabhupada, Chapter Two, text 18.
- **Bhagavad-gita 2.18** — *antavanta ime dehā nityasyoktāḥ śarīriṇaḥ / anāśino 'prameyasya tasmād yudhyasva bhārata* — chanted with word-for-word synonyms:
  - *antavanta ime dehāḥ* — all these material bodies are perishable/have an end
  - *nityasya uktāḥ śarīriṇaḥ* — of the embodied (eternal) soul, said to be eternal in existence
  - *anāśinaḥ aprameyasya* — never to be destroyed, immeasurable
  - *tasmāt yudhyasva bhārata* — therefore fight, O descendant of Bharata
- **Translation:** "The material body of the indestructible, immeasurable and eternal living entity is sure to come to an end; therefore, fight, O descendant of Bharata."

### Purport read from the book

- The material body is perishable by nature. It may perish immediately or after a hundred years — it is only a question of time. There is no chance of maintaining it indefinitely.
- The spirit soul, by contrast, is so minute it cannot even be seen by an enemy, to say nothing of being killed. As mentioned in the previous verse, it is so small that no one can measure its dimension.
- From both viewpoints there is no cause for lamentation: the living entity as he is cannot be killed, nor can the material body be saved for any length of time or permanently protected.
- The minute particle of the whole spirit requires this material body according to his work; therefore observance of religious principles should be utilized.
- In the **Vedanta-sutras** the living entity is qualified as light, because he is part and parcel of the supreme light. As sunlight maintains the entire universe, so the light of the soul maintains this material body. As soon as the spirit soul is out of the body, the body begins to decompose — therefore it is the spirit soul which maintains the body. The body itself is unimportant.
- Arjuna was advised to fight, and not to sacrifice the cause of religion for material bodily considerations.

## "Refresher Course" — Back to Basics

- Analogy from Srila Prabhupada's Mac computer: In "the world of Microsoft" (Seattle), he is learning the Macintosh, with its many interesting buttons, widgets, and "far out things." He recalls buying it in a big San Diego store where the staff wore black T-shirts reading "above Vista."
- Among the buttons there is a **refresh button**: when doing email or web pages, sometimes you want to clear everything and get back to basics, so you push refresh.
- Similarly, in Krishna consciousness we periodically need a **refresher course** — "back to basics," "back to square one." He plays with the Monopoly reference ("Do not pass Go") to arrive at "back to square one."
- Today's class is such a refresher: the Second Chapter of the Gita, the "teen verses" — 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 — on how **we are not the material body**.

## Jnana vs. Vijnana — Knowledge vs. Realized Knowledge

- Anticipating the reaction "Oh no, another class on 'we're not the body,'" he stresses the difference between **jnana** (knowledge, memorizing) and **vijnana** (realized knowledge).
- Example: The day before, a proud mataji said her son knows three chapters of the Gita and can recite the slokas — but when asked the translation, the child had a blank face. It is nice that children learn the verses, but nicer to know and to *realize* the translations.
- The goal is to capture the essence, the spirit — to understand what the verse is actually saying.
- To *realize* "I am not the body" is profound; it takes many years, perhaps lifetimes. If we actually realized we are not the body, we would never be disturbed — the senses would never be agitated, the mind would never be disturbed. This is the foundation of Krishna consciousness.

## "The Body Is Unimportant" — and the Balancing Truth

- Prabhupada says in the purport this is a **revolutionary concept in Western society**: the body is unimportant.
- One might object that this seems fanatical, because in one sense the body *is* important. This is illustrated by the story of **Sanatana Goswami**.

### The Story of Sanatana Goswami's Disease

- Sanatana Goswami, traveling through the jungle, contracted a disease that produced terrible oozing sores/boils on his body, leaking pus.
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — the Lord Himself — came every day to see him and, out of great transcendental affection, embraced him, continuing to embrace him even when the wet pus came onto the Lord's own body. "Love is blind" — the Lord so loved His pure devotee that He did not even consider the oozing sores.
- Sanatana Goswami became deeply embarrassed and resolved to give up his body by throwing himself under the wheels of the **Ratha-yatra** (Jagannatha's chariot) cart when the festival took place in a couple of weeks.

### Digression: The "Juggernaut" and Sati

- The English word **"Juggernaut"** (from Jagannatha) came into the dictionary from the British witnessing people in Puri give up their lives under the chariot wheels. The Puri-vasis understood — though not to be broadcast widely — that to leave one's body at the feet of Jagannatha yields a very high destination.
- **Sati:** Westerners find it hard to understand a wife ascending her husband's funeral pyre. When a childless wife's husband died "untimely" (we always consider death untimely, except perhaps at 101), she would jump into the ignited pyre.
  - The understanding: she would go where her husband was, considering the pain of the fire less severe than the pain of separation. This was not mundane material attachment — the husband was her **pati-guru**, helping her advance in Krishna consciousness by example and teaching; she was attached to him as her shelter and guide on the path.
  - Vedic-age women received *no diksha* separately; they were trained from a very young age to be very attached to their husbands (who were training them in Krishna consciousness).
  - As Prabhupada says, when the spiritual master leaves, disciples cry in grief at the separation — but life must go on.
  - Note on abuse: In time some women were *forced* to ascend the pyre for financial and other reasons; it became ugly, so the British outlawed it. Yet in close quarters the practice's original meaning can be understood.
  - (Interruptions from a phone call — from Giridhari Swami — and crying babies; the speaker jokes about locking his phone.)

### Prabhupada's Father and the Sadhus' Natural Death

- Prabhupada once told them that his father, **Gour Mohan De**, in old age contracted a long-term illness.
- What sadhus traditionally did toward the end of life — once free of all attachments, wholly attached to God, and having done their duties/preaching but now incapacitated — rather than slowly waiting for the end, they would **give up eating**, take only a little water, then gradually give up water too, thus dying a natural death.
- This could be called a type of suicide, but "there was no reason left" — a devotee has no attachments to this world; the only reason he stays is to continue the spiritual master's mission.
- We also saw Srila Prabhupada in his last days give up food and water as the end neared. Such things are for great souls.

### The Lord Claims Sanatana's Body

- Because the Lord is **avikina-surat** (all-knowing, omnipresent) — qualities that distinguish God from us, who are neither all-knowing nor everywhere — Krishna in Sanatana Goswami's heart understood his plan.
- The Lord approached him: "I understand you are planning to give up this body." When Sanatana confirmed it, the Lord said he could not, **"because that body doesn't belong to you — it belongs to Me."** As a surrendered devotee (His shishya/disciple), the body is the Lord's.
- Analogy of **initiation beads (kanthi-mala)**: Prabhupada said these neck beads are "like the dog collar on the dog of my guru." Woof woof — sit, jump, eat, catch the ball; "yes sir": chant 16 rounds, follow the principles, open this temple, distribute books, "we're sold out." Devotees are sold-out servants of Guru Maharaja — but with a very nice master who takes good care of them, just as the Lord takes good care of the surrendered soul.
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.22** (*yoga-kshemam vahamy aham*): "For My devotee who has surrendered everything to Me, I provide whatever he needs and I protect whatever he has." Devotees don't mind being sold-out servants of Guru and Krishna.
- The Lord told Sanatana He had many plans for that body — to establish His movement and spread the teachings of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — "so don't do it."

### Reconciling "Unimportant" and "Important"

- So we know we are not the body, and Prabhupada says the body is unimportant — but this is essentially an instruction to *materialistic* people, stressing the more important principle of the soul. We cannot carry it too far.
- The body *is* important if we use it nicely in the service of the world and Krishna. We maintain body and soul together so we can execute the instructions of the spiritual master; in the course of time the body will perish.
- The body is also important because the **human form of life** can take us "from A to B to C to D" — back home, back to God. Of all species, the human form is most important because of **buddhi** (intelligence), the special gift God gave humans: with it we can perceive our deeper spiritual nature, understand who God is, and understand our eternal relationship with God.
- **Car analogy:** You may not be your car, but you take good care of it because it takes you to the office and brings you home. Similarly, though we are not the body, we take good care of it.

## The Sun Analogy — Spirit Is More Powerful Than Matter

- For the materialist, accepting "I am not the body" is very foreign and difficult. The purport gives the example of the **sun**.
- How vast is this one universe — leaf through any science magazine and see the distant galaxies and black holes, incomprehensibly vast — yet it is only a "mustard seed" in a huge bag of unlimited universes making up the cosmic manifestation.
- Vedic shastra says our universe has many planets, but one star — the sun — lights up the entire universe. All is darkness, but one sun lights everything; its heat and light reach far distant places, though it is only one tiny globe in the huge dense mass of the universe.
- **Application to the body:** Our body is big; consider an elephant's body — what manipulates it? One tiny spiritual spark. How can something one ten-thousandth the tip of a hair's dimension have such an effect on a huge dense mass of matter? Just as the tiny sun, by its mere presence, produces heat and light to the distant corners of the universe.
- The soul, situated within the body, is tiny and minute, "hovering in the region of the heart." Because of that tiny particle of spirit soul, there is consciousness from the top of the head to the tip of the toe.
- Therefore **spirit is more powerful than matter.** Matter is subject to destruction, but the soul by nature is eternal.
- **Bhagavad-gita 2.20** (paraphrased): the soul is never born and never dies; having once been, he never ceases to be — unborn, undying, primeval; he is not slain when the body is slain — because matter is inferior to spirit.

## How Does the Powerful Soul Become Entangled in Matter?

- A logical question arises: if spirit is infinitely more powerful than matter (which is temporary and subject to destruction), how does the soul come under the influence of the inferior energy, identify with matter, and thus suffer? How does that particle of soul become completely bewildered by maya and take on a material body?
- **Two answers come to mind:**

### Answer 1 — The Marginal Energy (tatastha-shakti)

- There is the Supreme Soul (Krishna, the Supersoul/Paramatma) and the finite soul (the jivatma). The Supersoul never comes under the influence of the material energy.
- But the tiny jivatma is described as **tatastha-shakti** (marginal energy). There is the material energy, the spiritual energy, and the marginal energy — which is us. We are spiritual, but because we are such tiny particles, being marginal we can come under the influence of *either* the powerful material energy or the powerful spiritual energy. We are "on the line" and can come under the control of both.

### Answer 2 — Rebellion Places Us Under Maya

- When we become rebellious to the Lord, the Lord places us under the jurisdiction of the material energy (maya).
- **Prabhupada's "playing hooky" story:** As a little boy, Abhay would sometimes play hooky (skip school). (Aside: he checks whether the Indian children even know the word "hooky.") Because young boys grow big and strong and parents can no longer catch and spank them, his mother **Rajani** could not catch little Abhay to "give him the sauce."
  - So she hired a man named **Damodara** — the village man who would catch all the boys playing hooky, tie them with a rope, and pull them to school.
  - The speaker jokes that their own congregation reportedly hired such a "Damodara" with a rope; how embarrassing to be pulled by a rope into your classroom.
- **The analogy:** When the living entity becomes rebellious, the Lord engages **Mayadevi**, who takes us to "school" — we come to the material world, the school of life, to learn that we cannot be happy separate from Krishna.

## Maya's Phantasmagoria and Our Own Attachments

- Mayadevi creates a whole "phantasmagoria" of material life designed to keep us bound. Our *attractions* to the material world are what bind us life after life after life.
- The three modes — **goodness, passion, and ignorance** — are described in *Bhagavad-gita* terminology as **ropes** (the word *guna* meaning both "quality" and "rope") that bind us to the material world.
- We cannot blame maya. Yes, she creates the phantasmagoria, but it is because of *our own strong desires* to enjoy separately from Krishna that we are bound. It is our own attachments to this world of birth and death that keep us here.

### The Guru and the Tree Story

- A disciple went to his guru complaining that his lack of progress was Mayadevi's fault: "She just will not let go of me... it's all maya's kingdom and she's keeping me bound. Please speak to *her* instead of always chastising *me*, then we could finish this business."
- The guru called him "you rascal" and left. The next day during class the guru disappeared; the boys heard him outside crying, "Let go! Let go of me! Why don't you let go of me?" — and found him embracing a big tree, telling the tree to let go.
- The boy said, "Guruji, the tree is not holding you — *you* are holding the tree. Just let go." The guru immediately let go and said: "Yes. In the same way, maya is not holding on to you — *you* are holding on to maya. Let go."
- The illusion is very tempting, but it is an illusion. As **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati** said, "the whole show was only temporary." We should not be tricked by anything maya offers.

### Even Demigods Are Bewildered

- In the first chapter / first verse of the **Srimad-Bhagavatam** (1.1.1) it is described how Mayadevi's kingdom is such that even the demigods are sometimes confused or attracted.
- Example given: just as sometimes you see land on water and water on land (a mirage-like reversal), the Lord's energies are such that even a great demigod can be tricked. So we must be careful.

## Maya's Two Tricks

- Prabhupada said maya has **two tricks up her sleeve**, at opposite ends of the spectrum:
  1. We think we are the body.
  2. We think we are God.
- Both are powerful and must be overcome — the attraction to the body and its pleasures, and (for those who advocate it) the philosophy that we are God.

### The Yogi Who Mistakes the Supersoul for Himself

- Prabhupada told them once in Detroit: a yogi practicing meditation becomes purified and at one point gets a glimpse of the **Supersoul** within the heart — and wrongly concludes, "There I am; that is me."
- Without the direction of a bona fide Vaishnava spiritual master, even spiritual life can be risky; one can fall short of the goal.
- Had the yogi a Vaishnava guru, he would clearly understand: "That is not me — that is the Lord in my heart," and the next step is to stop being a hatha-yogi and surrender to the Lord as a **bhakti-yogi**.
- **Bhagavad-gita 6.47** (paraphrased): Of all yogis, one who worships Krishna with devotion is the highest.
- Therefore at every step we need the direction of a bona fide Vaishnava so as not to fall prey to the material world's temptations.

## Question & Answer

### Q: What did Prabhupada do when he played hooky?

- Called "the toughest question I ever had." One time little Abhay took some matches, was playing with them, burnt his dhoti, and got a small scar/burn mark on his knee/leg — mentioned in the *Lilamrita*.
- "Playing hooky" means being "up to no good." In fact, **everyone in the material world is up to no good** — even what we call "good."
- The concept of good and bad in this world is misleading; it is all bad, even what we consider good. Living in the mode of **goodness** as a devotee is helpful — a stepping stone toward transcendence — but merely being "good" means one takes another birth in the mode of goodness, which still means birth, disease, old age, and death. From top to bottom, everyone in the material world is "up to no good." So we don't want to play hooky — we want to go back to school (Krishna consciousness) and back home.

### Q: What are the various steps to come out of the increments of maya?

- One of them "you're doing right now." There are **nine processes of devotional service**: *shravanam, kirtanam, vishnoh smaranam, pada-sevanam, arcanam, vandanam, dasyam, sakhyam, atma-nivedanam* (hearing, chanting, remembering, serving the lotus feet, deity worship, offering prayers, servitude, friendship, and offering everything to the Lord).
- Of the nine, **hearing and chanting are the most effective**, because our condition is compared to that of a **sleeping/slumbering man**.
  - When sleeping, you forget everything important; you dream, but the dream world is unimportant fantasy. In material life we are slumbering in a world of fantasy; we want to wake up to the reality of Krishna consciousness.
  - **How do you wake a sleeping man? Through sound.** So the slumbering soul is awakened through sound. Therefore, of all the senses in regard to spiritual life, the most important is the sense of **hearing**; we deliver ourselves from material existence primarily through hearing.

#### Prabhupada's Burglar Analogy

- If you are sleeping and a burglar enters, you cannot see, smell, taste, or touch him — all those senses are of no value. But if he makes a slight improper move and bangs something, you can wake and be alerted to the danger.
- **The speaker's son's real experience:** His son, wife, and children were sleeping upstairs. The son woke because he *heard* a burglar — heard the knife cut the screen window, heard the glass being cut (they cut a hole rather than smash it), heard someone come through and walk, heard the television being lifted. He was alerted to the danger.
  - Instinctively he got up; his wife asked where he was going. "There are burglars downstairs." She said that was all the more reason *not* to go. Choosing "the better of two evils," they cut their losses and reported it to the police.
  - The police said it was very good he did not go downstairs: in the same extended area there had been similar burglaries, and several times the burglars, when confronted, murdered the people. "Discretion is the better part of valor."
  - The point (aside from the story's drama): we can be alerted to the danger of material existence, and the most effective sense is hearing. Just as we are doing now — if we could only do just this, we could be delivered.

#### A Devotee for Each of the Nine Processes

- For each of the nine processes there is a corresponding devotee who perfected it.
- **Maharaja Pariksit** was delivered simply by sitting at the lotus feet of the spiritual master (Shukadeva Goswami) and *hearing* the Srimad-Bhagavatam.
- This is so glorified that even today in India there is the seven-day recitation of the Srimad-Bhagavatam (**Bhagavata-saptaha**). His Holiness **Lokanath Maharaja** is expert at this: he comes to a temple and for seven days recites the Bhagavatam — a "summary," from **shraddha** (initial faith) up to **prema-bhakti**, the Tenth Canto describing Krishna's ecstatic pastimes with His liberated devotees in the spiritual world. One can be delivered just by hearing, sitting.
- **Bhagavad-gita 7.1** (*tat srinu*): Krishna tells Arjuna, "Don't be restless, don't let the mind wander; I am speaking to you." "This Gita is enough." Prabhupada told them once that if you simply heard and served the Gita, that would be sufficient for your deliverance in Krishna consciousness — though he kindly gave us many other scriptures as well.
- The hearing process is so potent: it is said in the Vedas that **by sound one is conditioned, and by sound one becomes liberated.** Therefore we sit every day and chant *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare.*
- Even if you are not inclined toward philosophy (which requires **sumedhasah**, intelligence — and much of our intelligence has been weakened or destroyed by our activities in this world), you could just sit and hear the three words *Hare, Krishna, and Rama* in the form of the **Maha-mantra**, and that alone would be sufficient to deliver you from all ignorance to full spiritual enlightenment.

#### Scriptural Support for the Holy Name

- **Siksastakam, verse 2** (paraphrased): "O my Lord, Your holy name alone can render all benediction upon the living entities, and thus You have hundreds and millions of names like Krishna and Govinda. In these names You have invested all Your transcendental energies, and there are no hard and fast rules for chanting them; You have made approaching You so easy." But — in the mood of an aspiring devotee — Mahaprabhu says, "unfortunately I have no attraction for them." The secret is simply to develop attraction for this one activity of **kirtana**, chanting.
- Therefore it says in the Eleventh Canto — **Srimad-Bhagavatam 12.3.51** (*kaler dosha-nidhe rajann asti hy eko mahan gunah / kirtanad eva krishnasya mukta-sangah param vrajet*): "O King, this age of Kali is an ocean of faults, but there is one saving grace — simply by the *kirtan* of Hare Krishna one can be freed and attain the supreme destination."

#### The Ease of Resisting Temptation by Chanting — Hercules and the Sirens

- We do not have to do what **Hercules** did. To test his strength in resisting temptation (in the *Odyssey*), there was a bay guarded on either side by extremely beautiful women with exceptionally beautiful voices.
- When explorers sailed through the bay to plunder that region's riches, they would hear the beautiful girls, sail to the shore to enjoy with them — but the girls were expert warriors who killed and chopped up any sailor who landed. It was said no one could resist the temptation of their voices and forms; no one had ever passed through.
- Hercules decided to prove how strong and controlled he was and took a boat through the bay — but he could not resist, and his men had to tie him to the mast; only by being tied to the mast did he survive the ordeal.
- But in Krishna consciousness we can resist any temptation maya offers **not** by tying our hands to a post — we simply have to chant *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare.*

#### Examples of the Power of the Holy Name — Haridasa Thakura

- **Haridasa Thakura** (the Namacharya) was tested twice by a beautiful woman:
  1. Envious people, disturbed by his purity, hired a **prostitute** to approach and cause him to fall. She came — a very beautiful girl — but by *hearing him chant*, she surrendered to him as his disciple.
  2. **Mayadevi herself** came and proposed to Haridasa. He said yes, he would fulfill her desire — but first he had his vow to fulfill: to chant **300,000 names a day**, which took about 18 hours. "As soon as I finish my chanting, I will surely enjoy with you; in the meantime, sit and wait patiently."
     - He chanted all night; she sat waiting and naturally heard the sound vibration of Hare Krishna from the lips of a pure devotee. She had to wait three nights (she had to go back to her "work" as Mayadevi each morning), coming each night. At the end of the third night she too **surrendered** and became the initiated disciple of Haridasa Thakura.
- These illustrate the power of the holy name — the answer to the question of how we begin the process of devotional service to overcome maya: the very first thing we hear when we come to Krishna consciousness is to **chant the holy names**. We encourage newcomers, intermediates, and advanced devotees alike to chant the holy names.
