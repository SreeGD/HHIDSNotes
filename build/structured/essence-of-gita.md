## This Is Not New — One of the Oldest Traditions

- When people see devotees chanting and dancing or distributing books, especially in America, they may think this is "some new religious tradition" — since Hare Krishna has "only been around 35–40 years here." But this could not be further from the truth.
- **The Krishna conscious movement is one of the oldest spiritual traditions known to man — at least 5,000 years old.** It is authoritative because it is based on the ancient scripture **Bhagavad-gītā**, spoken by the Lord Himself when He was present on this planet 5,000 years ago.
- **A spiritual tradition, to be authentic, must be based on real scripture.** Just as the Christians have the **Bible**, the Muslims the **Qur'an**, the Jewish tradition the **Torah**, and the Buddhists the **sūtras of Buddha** — all authorized because the knowledge comes from God Himself.
  - **"dharmaṁ tu sākṣād bhagavat-praṇītam"** (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 6.3.19): "Religious principles are enunciated directly by the Supreme Lord." No one but the Lord can enunciate a genuine religious tradition; we are too fallen and imperfect.
  - "**To err is human**" — that alone disqualifies us from starting a religion.
- **Etymology**: The word *religion* comes from the Latin **re-ligio**, meaning "to reconnect with God." The word **Jehovah** carries the same connotation — to again make contact with God.
- Any bona fide spiritual tradition has a divine scripture coming directly from the Lord of the spiritual world.

## "Why Did You, an American, Join an Eastern Religion?"

- The speaker lived in America in 1970 and did not come back for almost 30 years, so "sometimes I forget I'm American." On the Sri tour, people ask: "You're an American — why have you joined an Eastern religion? We have Christianity; this is a Christian country."
- **Response 1 — Religion has no direction**: Religion is neither Eastern nor Western, North nor South; **it comes from the spiritual side.** If Krishna consciousness is called "Eastern" because it came from the Eastern part of the world, then **Christianity also came from the East** — the Middle East is closer to India than to America — and so did Islam and Buddhism. It appears that most, if not all, of the great spiritual traditions came from the Eastern part of the world.
  - Krishna says in Bhagavad-gītā that He is the **seed-giving father of all living entities** (**Bhagavad-gītā 14.4**). So every living entity — not only human beings, but animals, birds, fish, etc. — is part and parcel of God, and we all have our original home in the spiritual world.
- **Response 2 — Personal background**: "Were you born a Christian?" — "Yes, my parents were Roman Catholic; I was an altar boy." The speaker still carries a Bible: "I don't feel I've abandoned it; I feel I've **evolved** in my understanding of God."
- **Response 3 — Every question was answered**: "Because any question I ever had about spiritual life was answered in Bhagavad-gītā." He was inquisitive as a boy (religious upbringing), and became more inquisitive after several tragedies — his **father died when he was 17.** He open-mindedly researched various traditions and groups, and fortunately came in contact with the **Bhagavad-gītā.**

## The Purpose of Human Life

- **"athāto brahma-jijñāsā"** (Vedānta-sūtra 1.1.1): now that we have the human form of life, it is the time for spiritual introspection. The human form, with its advanced intelligence, affords the opportunity to understand our deeper spiritual nature — to understand **who is God** and **our intimate, eternal loving relationship with Him.**
- **This has always been the purpose of life** — 5,000 years ago, right now, and 5,000,000 years from now. Human life is specifically made for spiritual realization. Other species lack the intelligence to ask such questions and find such answers. Along with the human form comes the **responsibility** to understand who I am, who God is, and my relationship with Him.

## Why So Many Scriptures and Religions?

- At different times, places, and circumstances in human society, the Lord has revealed spiritual knowledge **according to the ability of the people of that time to understand it.** Therefore there are many different scriptures and religions.
- **Objection**: If there is one God, why are there so many different traditions? **Because there are different types of people, at different levels of spiritual advancement.**
- **School analogy**: In first and second grade we learn "2 + 2 = 4, 4 + 4 = 8, 8 + 8 = 16" ("I'll stop there — a senior moment"). Later grades bring algebra, trigonometry; high school, then university, master's, PhD, physics, and so on — deeper and deeper.
  - All the mathematics teachers know the whole subject; the teacher of the first-graders also knows quantum mechanics, physics, trigonometry, algebra — but he teaches **according to time, place, and circumstance**, according to the level of his audience. Hence different spiritual traditions and different levels of understanding about consciousness.
- **If one makes a comparative study with an open mind, he will find that Bhagavad-gītā is the "PhD" on the science of God consciousness** — any question about spiritual life can be answered to full satisfaction.

## The Questions of a Transcendentalist

- What questions do transcendentalists ask? **Who am I? Where have I come from? What happens at the moment of death? Does God exist?** These are the seekers of the truth.
- Let us take one question and see how Bhagavad-gītā satisfies the audience.

## "Who Am I?" — I Am Not This Body

- Krishna addresses this in the very beginning of Bhagavad-gītā, with a concept revolutionary in Western society: **"You are not this body."**
- **Mirror illustration**: When you wake and look in the mirror, however attached, happy, or disappointed you are with the reflection you have been seeing for 15, 20, 35, 40 years, you are convinced "there I am." But Krishna says no — **you are the spark of life within the body.**
- Krishna describes the body as **yantra** (**Bhagavad-gītā 18.61**, *yantrārūḍhāni māyayā*) — a machine, a very sophisticated machine made of material energy.
  - *Mac computer story*: The speaker just got a Mac, switching from PC to Macintosh after many years — "it's like changing gurus." Everyone said "You can't make the switch, it's too hard" — but he learned it in three days. When he went to buy it, the Apple store salespeople wore T-shirts saying **"Above Vista"** (a jab at Microsoft's Vista). A very sophisticated, advanced machine — but **without someone to operate it, it is useless. Any machine is only as good as the person who operates it.**
- So Krishna describes the body as a machine made of material energy (matter), **operated by the conscious driver within — the soul. That is who we really are.**
- **Intuitive proof from language**: We say "**this is my** hand, **my** arm, **my** head, **my** leg, **my** body" — not "I hand, I arm, I head, I body." The body is *mine*; it belongs to me. So **who am I?** — "I'm the body." — No; you said it's *your* body, it belongs to you. Who are you?
- With all our Western progress, the biggest politicians, professors, and scientists cannot answer this question. So **how advanced is our modern society really?** Materially we may be advanced; spiritually we are falling way behind.
  - *Toyota analogy*: When a friend drives by in a Toyota, you say "There goes my friend." If another says "You mean the Toyota?" — "Hello?" — no, our friend is *driving* the Toyota. Likewise, we know so much about the body: a person could speak for hours about it ("six feet tall, brown hair, green eyes, a little mole here..."), but if asked to "speak about the soul," there is nothing to say. "I'm like the soul man."

## What Happens at the Moment of Death?

- Human life is meant to understand that the body is here today, gone tomorrow, but the soul continues.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 2.20** (*na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṁ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ...*): "For the soul there is never birth nor death; nor, having once been, does he ever cease to be. He is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, undying, and primeval; he is not slain when the body is slain."
- People at universities and clubs sometimes say, "Who cares? Eat, drink, and be merry, for tomorrow he dies — and it's all over." Maybe, maybe not. We should inquire where we are going at the moment of death.
- **Don't be like the ostrich**, putting its head in the sand and pretending death doesn't happen. It is the end of everything in this life, and does not always come in old age.
- **Apartment / stadium analogy**: If the city decides to tear down your apartment building to build a football stadium and gives you six months' notice, do you put your head in the sand and hope it won't happen? No — an intelligent person reads the classified ads and arranges another apartment ("I'd like to rent your apartment five months from now"), signs the contract, so that when the bulldozers come he is safely out. To ignore the fact and make no preparation is not intelligent. **So we should inquire about death.**
- We are already very concerned with the future — working hard in school for a career, putting money aside for old age — but we mistakenly think the future ends with this body.
- **Bhagavad-gītā 2.13** (*dehino 'smin yathā dehe kaumāraṁ yauvanaṁ jarā / tathā dehāntara-prāptiḥ...*): "As the embodied soul passes, in this body, from boyhood to youth to old age, the soul similarly passes into another body at death."

## The Logic of Reincarnation

- *Manipal challenge*: A student in Manipal, India, challenged: "What is the proof that we change bodies? Who has ever come back and said 'it was like this'?" The speaker's logic: **we are already changing bodies within this very lifetime,** so it is not difficult to understand that when the final body is finished, we continue into another body.
  - **The five-year-old body**: "I was playing with trucks, she was playing with Barbie dolls." Where is that five-year-old body now? Completely gone.
  - **Medical science**: Every seven years every cell in the body changes — so every seven years you have a new body. If you are 21, you have already changed your body three times.
- Right now we sit in these bodies; one day we will get old (fortunate to live to 80 or 90), look completely different, have a completely different body — yet friends will still recognize you. **Something changes (the body), something remains the same (the soul).**
- Just as we change bodies every seven years and continue to live (we don't die), so at the final demise of this body we continue to exist. **This is logic.**

## Where Do We Go? — Consciousness at Death Determines the Next Body

- **Bhagavad-gītā 8.6** (*yaṁ yaṁ vāpi smaran bhāvaṁ tyajaty ante kalevaram...*): "Whatever state of being one remembers when he quits his body, that state he will attain." **Whatever desires you have cultivated in this life will take you to your next body.**
- **How many bodies to choose from?** You don't have a *choice* — it depends on how you have lived.
  - *University analogy*: After high school you can't just choose your university; it depends on how you studied, your grades, your qualification — whether you go to the local junior college or to Harvard or Oxford. Similarly, how you live cultivates certain desires, and **what is most dear to you at the moment of death, you "catch on to," and it takes you to your next body.**
- There are **8,400,000 species of life** — some higher, some middle, some lower.

## The Mechanics of Rebirth

- At the moment of death the soul is propelled into the womb of a new mother. When a man and woman unite, the spirit soul takes shelter of a particle of male semen, enters the womb of the mother, and creates a pea-like body the first night.
- **Because of the presence of the soul, the body grows.** Dead things don't grow; only living things grow. So the sages, yogis, and Indians say **life is present from the moment of conception** — because of the soul the embryo grows. He is born and passes through infancy, youth, middle age, old age.
- **What is death? Death simply means the soul is separated from the body** — a big mystery in Western culture, but very scientifically explained in the Vedas.

## "Next Time I Want to Be Bill Gates" — Even the Best Case Is Duality

- The Manipal student became convinced of reincarnation: "Yes, I understand — I actually like your philosophy, because **next time I want to be Bill Gates.**"
- Response: Let's take that best-case scenario. There is an upper crust, a higher echelon — a few who are rich, famous, beautiful, and have much knowledge (the entertainers, politicians, professors we see on TV) — the "haves" versus the "have-nots." Many people cherish these material desires.
- **But even if you had it all — even if you had it now** (as in the song, "I want it all, and I want it now") — you still have to understand that **this world is a world of duality.**

## Everyone Wants Happiness — the Intrinsic Nature of the Soul

- We may be born in different countries, speak different languages, have different skin colors and ethnicities, but **we all have one thing in common: we all want to be happy.** No one (except a madman) would say, "No, I don't want to be happy."
- **It is the intrinsic nature of the soul to be blissful** — that is our natural condition.
  - Just as the intrinsic nature of **sugar is sweet**, of **salt is salty**, of **water is wet**, the intrinsic nature of the soul is to be blissful. We want bliss, happiness — not distress, anxiety, suffering, misery, tragedy.
- We are doing everything we can to get happiness and avoid distress. **But there is a problem: we are looking in the wrong place.**

## The Lost Keys — Looking in the Wrong Place

- *Prabhupāda's story of the lost keys*: A man drove his car into a dark garage and dropped his keys in the darkness. Unable to find them, he walked 50 meters down the street to search under a bright streetlamp. A friend came to help; after ten minutes the friend asked, "Where exactly did you drop them?" — "In my dark garage, 50 meters up there." — "Then why are you looking here?" — **"Because the light is better."**
- **"All that glitters is not gold."** We think we can have happiness in this world — this is called **Māyā** ("that which is not"). It *appears* there is happiness here, but it is only a **perverted reflection** of the real happiness of the spiritual world from which we come. It looks real, but does not give the satisfying result.
- *Apple-reflection analogy*: On a clear, still day you go on a picnic; there is an apple tree, and looking in the water you see the apples reflected — such a good reflection that you reach in and get "a mouthful of water." **We are tricked by Māyā into thinking there is happiness in this world.**

## Seeing Through the Ears — Hearing From Self-Realized Souls

- We have to hear from the self-realized soul, from the **sādhu.** We cannot see the world through our own eyes.
- **Indian saying: "The saintly person sees through his ears"** — he hears from self-realized souls who are beyond the illusion, and comes to the same conclusion.
- We must be convinced: **this is not our real home; our real home is the spiritual world.** That conviction begins our search for the positive alternative.
- We could speak all night about the material world, but everyone has as much experience of it as the speaker does. **If it doesn't work, we should look for another alternative** — don't keep making the same mistakes over and over.
  - *Broken microphone analogy*: If the mic isn't working, you don't just keep waiting — two minutes, five hours, a day, two weeks, a year, five years — "it doesn't work; you should get another microphone." So if something doesn't work, look for an alternative. **Does material life work?** "Yes, I'm handsome, powerful, beautiful" — but **for how long?** Nowadays you're lucky to live to 80 (a centenarian may reach 116) — but how many more months? **Time and tide wait for no man.** When death comes, you leave everything behind.

## What You Keep: Spiritual Advancement Carries Over

- But if you have used your life to cultivate spiritual knowledge, live a pure life, and gain self-realization, **your destination is very bright.**
- If not a completely self-realized soul, you may be reborn — but **into a family of saintly persons, where you get the opportunity to continue on the spiritual path.** If you made 51% advancement, you begin next life at 52%.
- **Materially, you leave everything behind; spiritually, you keep whatever advancement you have made.**
- *Egyptian pyramids*: The Egyptians believed in reincarnation but didn't have the whole picture — they buried their kings with horses, soldiers, gold, and wealth, thinking they could take it to the next birth. But **now we dig it up and enjoy it ourselves — where are they?**
- So we should utilize the things of this world, take what is necessary, live a simple life — we don't have to live like cats and dogs; we can have a nice life — **but not think that this is the real center of existence**, and know the day will come when we leave.

## The Final Examination

- Where do we go? It **depends on our consciousness.** As students we study hard because we know there will be a final examination.
- **The final, final examination is the moment of death.** If you have utilized your life properly — struck the balance between accepting what is necessary to keep body and soul together, and using the greater part of your time for self-realization — there is a good chance that when you leave this world you will have **no attachments.**
- **If even a little attachment remains, you have to come back and take another body.** But if no attachments to this world remain and all your attachments have gone to God, then you are **immediately transferred to the spiritual world and never come back again.**

## Take Your Pick: Two Worlds

- **The material world is temporary, full of suffering and ignorance; the spiritual world is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss.** So take your pick.
  - *Shopping analogy*: When a lady goes shopping and takes two mangoes, she weighs the differences, the pros and cons, and chooses. When spiritual knowledge is presented logically, scientifically, and clearly, you can see the difference between this world and what the spiritual world offers.

## No Time? The Concession for the Modern Age

- Most of us find it hard to find time — we are caught up in a huge, complicated society, no longer the "**simple living and high thinking**" of the days of yore, with just a little time to think.
- People think: "After that, I think I should become more spiritual — but what do I do? I don't have time to do as the ancients did: to go to the forest (**vana**) and practice yogic meditation in seclusion where the mind is peaceful, thinking of the Lord in the heart; to perform penances and austerities to become detached and absorbed in transcendence; to follow all the rules, regulations, and rituals of ancient scriptures; to read all those books. I just want to sleep when I read the newspaper, tired from working all day. So what do I do?"
- **A concession has been made for those born in modern times.** In days of yore there were saintly persons concerned about those who would be born in their future — just as today there are philanthropists, vegetarians, and environmentalists concerned about what the earth will be like for our children's children (a bleak picture with global warming).
- **In India, thousands of years ago, saintly persons were concerned about the spiritual emancipation of people who would live now** (e.g., "March 16th, 2007"). They understood that we would not be capable of practicing spiritual life with the same intensity as in days of yore. So they studied the scriptures and discovered verses prescribing the chanting of the holy names.
  - **Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa — "harer nāma harer nāma harer nāmaiva kevalam / kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā"**: In the age of Kali there is no other way, no other way, no other way for self-realization than the chanting of the holy name of the Lord.
  - These sages, with their mystic vision, foresaw the people who would live in Atlanta, New York, Paris, Sydney, and prescribed the most effective, sublime, and easy means: **chant the names of God — Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.**

## Judge a Thing by the Result

- It is a long way from start to finish. **As Confucius said, "The longest journey begins with the first step."**
- Someone sincere can follow the path according to the authority of scripture — no speculation, guesswork, "maybe, perhaps." It has all been set down; **the spiritual "MapQuest" was given thousands of years ago.** We just try it and get the result.
- **"phalena paricīyate"** (Sanskrit): "We judge a thing by the result."
- If anyone begins — even out of curiosity — by chanting Hare Krishna, then **the things said tonight will not remain mere knowledge; they will become realization.**
  - **There is a difference between knowledge and realization.** It is one thing to say "I am not the body"; it is another to realize, "Wow, I am not the body, I am a spirit soul" — **"ahaṁ brahmāsmi."** These things come gradually.
- **By chanting Hare Krishna, all the knowledge comes** — the satisfaction, the peace, and the happiness we were looking for in the wrong place. Through **bhakti-yoga** we come in contact with Krishna, with God, who is the reservoir of all pleasure — and we become happy.

  - **Cāṇakya Paṇḍita in the Nīti-śāstra**: You can only give up something if you get something better.

## The Higher Taste Is the Holy Name

- **Padma Purāṇa — "nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ..."**: There is no difference between the name of God and God Himself. All His transcendental energies, all His glories, all His bliss are present in the sound of His name. He has made approach to Him very easy in this age — through chanting His name.
- **The successful chanter will, without doubt, one day achieve perfection and go back to the spiritual world.**
