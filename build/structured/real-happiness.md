## Kīrtana: Glorifying God by Singing His Holy Name

- The evening's final performance is **kīrtana** — a Sanskrit word meaning **the glorification of God by singing His holy name.**
- God is one — there is one God — but He has many names in the different languages of the world.
  - **Sun analogy:** there is one sun, but in different parts of the world people call the sun by different names. Similarly there is one God, but different languages have different names for Him.
- If you went to India on vacation (instead of, e.g., Pobierowo) and wanted to refer to God, you would use the name **Krishna.** Krishna is a very appropriate name for the Lord, because it means **the most attractive** or **the most beautiful person.**

## God Is Present in the Sound of His Name

- The ancient scriptures of India and the great self-realized sages explain that God is **absolute**, so **He is present in the sound of His name.**
- In the material world, an object and its name are different.
  - **Water example:** if you are thirsty and merely say "water, water, water," the word and the substance are different, so the name does not quench your thirst.
- But God is so merciful that He **is present in the sound of His name** — so when you say **Hare Krishna, you are actually having the association of the Supreme Lord.**

## We Are All Hankering for the Lord's Association

- We are all hankering for that association. At one time we were all in the **spiritual world with the Lord**, but for one reason or another we came to the **material world**, where we are separated from God.
- Here we **foolishly try to find happiness in material things**, but material things cannot give lasting happiness — "they are here today and gone tomorrow." What we are really hankering for is the happiness of associating with God.
- **Objection answered:** Someone may say this is impossible because God is so far away. True — we are in the material world and He is in the spiritual world. But **by His mercy He is also very near — again, present in the sound of His name.**

## The Lord Is Ever-Youthful and Blissful — So His Name Makes Us Happy

- In a few moments, when these names of God are sung in Sanskrit, you will see how happy the boys and girls become — because **the Lord is non-different from His name, and He is the most joyful person.**
- **Correcting a Western misconception:** In Western culture we often depict God as very old, grey-haired, and sickly, holding up the earth. But the **Vedic scriptures** describe that, although God is the oldest person — the Father of all creation — He is nevertheless **eternally youthful.** He does not have a material body like ours; He has a **spiritual body, ever-youthful and always blissful.**
- So if you associate with God, as we should, **you also become very happy.**

## Everyone Wants to Be Happy — and Kīrtana Is the Easiest Means

- One thing everyone in this world can agree on: **we all want to be happy.** We differ on so many things, but here is one point of agreement.
- Therefore the ancient **Vedic scriptures of India recommend the process of kīrtana — chanting Hare Krishna — as the easiest and most sublime means of awakening our love for God in this age.**

## Chanting Is for Everyone, Not Only for India

- Although this chanting originated in India, it is **not meant only for Indian people** — it is for Polish, German, English, American, and Japanese people alike, because ultimately all of us are **spiritual beings, part of God's family.** In the ultimate sense we are not Polish, German, American, or Japanese — "that is not who we really are"; we are spiritual persons.
- All over the world, people who chant this song immediately become happy.
- **Pizza analogy:** Don't think, "This is something from India, it won't make me happy." Here in Poland everyone likes pizza — but pizza was not invented in Warsaw; it comes from Italy. Yet Polish people love pizza. Even if something comes from another country, if it is good for you — what to speak of chanting God's name — you can embrace it and become happy.

## The Mahā-mantra Is a Transcendental Sound from the Spiritual World

- The audience has already seen how nice it is — on the beach and on the streets — "the same song over and over again":
  - **Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.**
- Some of the devotees have been chanting for **30 or 40 years** and never get tired of it, because it is a **transcendental sound vibration** — spiritual, coming from the spiritual world.
- **Contrast with material music:** A favorite song from the radio can be sung for a few days or months, but then you get tired of it, because it is a **material sound vibration** that does not touch the heart, the soul. But this chant **touches the soul, wakes the soul up, and makes the soul happy, because it brings us into contact with God.**
- The effect: it becomes very blissful — you will see the boys and girls, and even the children, dancing.

## Teaching the Audience to Chant Along

- At every festival, IDS looks out and sees people trying to chant but not knowing all the words — so tonight he will teach the words so they can chant along and experience the same joy. ("Dobrze? — Okay.")
- He teaches the song **two words at a time**, call-and-response, and the audience repeats after him:
  - **Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare, Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.**
- He gently encourages more enthusiasm — "They did better in Reval, we were there two days ago; a little more enthusiasm" — and leads several rounds.
- Between rounds he adds interactive touches: "Now look at the person sitting next to you, see if they're smiling." "Some of you are a little shy, so we'll chant one more time." (He also invites everyone to look at how nice it is.)

## The Children's Dance Competition

- The festival is presented as a **family event**, so there is also a dance competition for the children.
- **For the girls:** IDS has **10 silk saris**, brought back from India (Bombay, a couple of months earlier). The **10 best little-girl dancers** will be called on stage, given a sari, and **dressed** in it, to take home and keep all year for **next year's Festival of India.** And if you don't win tonight, tomorrow **15 saris** will be given.
- **For the boys:** since boys can't wear saris, the **best boy dancer** gets a beautiful **flower garland**, "which brings good luck." So the boys can dance too.
- Closing: "So here we go." (The kīrtana and competition begin.)
