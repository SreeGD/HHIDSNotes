## The Verse — Prahlada's Prayer (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.9.28)

- The class is on **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Canto 7, Chapter 9 ("Prahlada Pacifies the Lord with Prayer"), Text 28** — *"evaṁ janaṁ nipatitaṁ prabhavāhi-kūpe / kāmābhikāmam anu yaḥ prapatan prasaṅgāt / kṛtvātmasāt surarṣiṇā bhagavan gṛhītaḥ / so 'haṁ kathaṁ nu visṛje tava bhṛtya-sevām."*
- **Translation:** "My dear Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, because of my association with material desires, one after another, I was gradually falling into a blind well full of snakes, following the general populace. But Your servant Narada Muni kindly accepted me as his disciple and instructed me how to achieve this transcendental position. Therefore, my first duty is to serve him. How could I ever leave his service?"

## The Purport — Serve the Spiritual Master First

- As seen in later verses, even though Prahlada Maharaj was directly offered all the benedictions he might have desired, **he refused to accept them from the Supreme Personality of Godhead.** On the contrary, he asked the Lord to engage him in the service of His servant, Narada Muni. **This is the symptom of a pure devotee.**
- One should serve the spiritual master first — not bypass the spiritual master and desire to serve the Supreme Lord directly. "That is not the principle for a Vaishnava."
- **Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu advised that one become the servant of the servant of the servant of the Lord** — that is the process for approaching the Supreme Lord. The first service should be rendered to the spiritual master, so that by his mercy one can approach the Supreme Personality of Godhead to render service.
  - While teaching Rupa Goswami, **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu said (CC Madhya 19.151): "guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja"** — one can achieve the seed of devotional service by the mercy of the guru, and then by the mercy of Krishna. That is the secret of success: one should try to please the spiritual master, and then one should attempt to please the Lord.
  - **Vishwanath Chakravarti Thakur also says (Gurvāṣṭaka 8): "yasya prasādād bhagavat-prasādo"** — one should not attempt to please the Lord by concoction; one must first be prepared to serve the spiritual master, and when qualified, he is automatically offered the platform of direct service to the Lord.
- Therefore Prahlada proposed that he be engaged in the service of Narada Muni; he never proposed to be engaged directly in the service of the Lord. "This is the right conclusion." He said, in effect, **"How can I give up the service of my spiritual master, who has favored me in such a way that I am now able to see You face to face?"**

## Initiation as a Second Birth

- Today these devotees are taking initiation into our **Brahma-Gaudiya-Vaishnava-Sampradaya.** This initiation process has been compared to a **second birth** — "you're being born again."
- The **first birth** is into the womb of material nature, into the womb of our mother. Although people tend to celebrate their birthday, "it's really not so much a tradition in our Gaudiya-Vaishnava culture" — "but anyway, it's happening; we want to honor the Vaishnavas."
- **Birth into this world is nothing wonderful — it's essentially a fall-down,** to a greater or lesser degree:
  - **To a lesser degree** if you take birth as **Lord Brahma.**
  - **To a greater degree** if you take birth as **Indragopa** — the tiniest insect ("like an amoeba, the tiniest creature").
  - But whether you're a first-class prisoner, a second-class prisoner, or a third-class prisoner, **you're still a prisoner** — and to be in jail is a fallen condition. So we are all in a fallen condition; "there's nothing wonderful."

## Birth Guarantees Death — The Bad Bargain

- Birth in this world means we're going to die. **Krishna says in the Gita** that for one who has taken birth, death is sure; and after death, one is sure to take birth again — the wheel of **samsara.** "So it's a very bad bargain: with birth, you're guaranteed at least one thing in this life — death."
- **Analogy — two sides of a coin:** One side is heads, the other tails; you can't have one without the other. Before a football or rugby match they flip a coin to see who gets the ball first; every side hopes to win the toss (better to be on the offensive), but only one team gets the advantage — "so one team's happy and the other side is sad."
- Life is like that: we take birth — a new beginning, a fresh start, a hope, an aspiration that "this time I'll be successful." "Some lessons we never learn." Then suddenly, without warning, as if we're not prepared, there's the shock, the tragedy of death.

## Why a Devotee Is Always a Little Sober

- Therefore a devotee is always a little sober. Devotees are happy by nature.
- **Personal recollection — Sridhar Swami:** "My wonderful god-brother Sridhar Maharaj — they were both wonderful god-brothers — he was the jolly swami, even up to the point of death." The speaker was with him almost to that point (not when he passed away). He was so transcendental, so convinced that he was going to be with Prabhupada, with Krishna — "he had a smile on his face to the end."
- But although we're jolly, we're also a little sober — sobriety, because we know everything is temporary. **Bhagavad-gītā 8.15: "duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam"** — this is a temporary world, full of so many miseries.
  - So we don't become overjoyed in good times, nor depressed in bad times. We're equal-poised. We have **one duty** — as Krishna instructs Arjuna, to fix our mind on Krishna and go back home, back to Godhead. Anything can happen in this world.
  - **Murphy's law** — "whatever will go wrong, will go wrong. I don't know; it's not in the Bhagavad Gita, but maybe we should add it as an appendix."
- The sadhus are helping us; though they appeared centuries or thousands of years ago, they left us their wonderful realizations in the form of their writings.
  - **Chanakya Pandit says:** give up the association of evil persons, partake in the association of saintly persons, perform pious deeds day and night, and **"smara nityam anityatām"** — always remember the flickering (temporary) nature of this world. If you do like that, you'll always be a little sober.

## The Positive Alternative — A Solution Even Against Death

- However dismal the situation — the prospect of disease, old age, and death is ahead of us. "We've only experienced one or two things: birth and disease. Now old age is coming, and then death will come."
- Nevertheless, we retain hope in Krishna consciousness, because in Krishna consciousness we have in our hands a solution to those problems. The **karmis** have no solution, but we always have the positive alternative, even against all-devouring death.
  - "Oh, what is your solution, sir?" — In the heavenly planets they have the **soma-rasa** to become like immortal, but that runs out after a while: after your **punya** (piety) runs out, they don't give it to you anymore. "Sorry, no more drugs."

## The Formula for Immortality

- **Bhagavad-gītā 18.55: "bhaktyā mām abhijānāti yāvān yaś cāsmi tattvataḥ"** ("this is real poetry"): Krishna says one can understand Him as He is only through the process of devotional service; and when one is in full consciousness of Him through devotion, he can enter the kingdom of God.
- And what happens if you enter the kingdom of God? **"There's only one entrance; there's no exit."** In another verse Krishna says that the person who enters into the spiritual sky never returns — "never returns to Melbourne, Australia, in the cold weather."

## The Two Primordial Mysteries — Birth and Death

- That auspicious **second birth** — into Krishna consciousness, where there is no return — is what we're witnessing today.
- As mentioned in a series of lectures given in **Sydney,** there are two primordial mysteries that always attract people: **when somebody comes into the world, and when somebody leaves.**
  - **Birth:** Nowadays it's fashionable for all the relatives to come and watch the mother give birth — "Oh look, wow, he's so cute, kuchi-kuchi." "No — Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama… **may this be your last birth in this world.** Happy birthday, may this be your last birth." That's the blessing we give on birthdays. ("Amoraj, can you give me my birthday? Very good — may this be your last birth.")
  - **Death:** When someone leaves, "it's like a magnet — everyone surrounds the bed." The rich man is leaving, and everyone's around his bed thinking, "I wonder if I'm in the will." But a Vaishnava is leaving, and his beloved god-brothers and god-sisters are there to see him off with **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare.**

## The Benediction of Guru and Krishna

- "We're thanking you all for being here today to witness this spiritual birth, this second birth into Krishna consciousness, because this birth is very auspicious."
- **"I love this verse"** — one of the first the speaker memorized when the verse book by **Rohini Nandana** came out: everyone gets a mother and father, birth after birth; but if one gets the benedictions of **Guru and Krishna** — the spiritual father (the guru) and the eternal, original father (Krishna) — he can conquer the material energy and return back to Godhead, by the mercy of the Lord.

## Respecting Our Material Parents

- This verse does not mean we disrespect our parents. Our parents are actually like our **first gurus** — our first **shiksha-gurus.** They conceive us and bring us into the world "with the idea of fast exit" — to deliver us to our eternal guru, our spiritual master, and to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- So even as devotees, we shouldn't neglect our material parents, because had they not taken care of us, we wouldn't have had the opportunity to take to Krishna consciousness.
- **Personal story — argument with his mother at seventeen:** "I was a teenager, and we were having a very strong argument over the dinner table. I was winning, because I was a big boy and I was seventeen, and my father had passed away, and my poor mother was trying to raise us children. I had no education in Krishna consciousness." She broke down crying: "Son, don't you remember how I changed your diapers for months and years, and how I fed you from my breast milk?" She went on and on, "and I just broke down and said, 'Okay, Mom, I'm sorry, what do you want me to do?' — 'I just want you to take out the garbage.'"
- So we should respect our mundane mothers and fathers because they enabled us to come to the point of choosing to be devotees.

## Praying for Parents — Even the Unfavorable — and the Story of His Mother

- Even if our parents are not favorable, we should still pray for them — because of the gifts they gave us — like we pray for all living entities to become Krishna conscious.
- **The speaker's mother:** "As you know, my mother was a scientist. If you read the diary, she was atheistic — maybe agnostic at best: 'Something's out there,' she said. We never agreed on philosophy."
  - Toward the end she found she had cancer, and then she had a change of heart — she started reading all the books he had sent her (which she had put in the garage).
  - By the time she left, on her deathbed, his brother was crying and lamenting, and she said to him: **"Don't lament for me; I'm not this body — I'm a spirit soul, jivatma. I'm going to Krishna."** She died with the name of God on her lips. "So now she's in a position to bless me. This is the power of a devotee's prayer."

## The Debt to the Spiritual Father

- We respect our parents — but how much more respect, then, do we have for our **spiritual father,** who takes upon himself the responsibility, the burden, to take the disciple back to Godhead? "That is the greatest challenge — to go back to Godhead." The spiritual master makes it his business.
  - Just as the mother and father make it their business that their children grow up (and later the children are free), the guru doesn't rest until his disciples are back to the spiritual world. That may even mean he has to take birth again. He thinks like this at the moment of initiation.
- **At initiation, disciples are thinking:** "Oh, I'm getting initiated into the family of Prabhupada and ISKCON; I've waited for this day for years; I love the ceremony, the garlands; I'm getting a new name; this is wonderful."
- **The guru is very sober,** thinking, "I'm taking on another burden — but it's a burden of love." ("So don't feel guilty when you have children; it's a burden, but a burden of love.")
- The guru is also taking his vows. Disciples take their vows; the guru takes a vow also, in his heart (he doesn't publicly announce it — "we're only saying this for clarity's sake"): **"Yes, I am taking these devotees as disciples, and I promise Krishna, I promise my spiritual master — by his mercy I'm doing his service — I promise Krishna that I will take them back to Godhead."** So we are indebted to our spiritual father.

## The Seed of Bhakti — Planted Through Sound

- What is the process of taking birth in the material world? It all begins by the father planting the seed of life into the womb of the mother.
- In Krishna consciousness, it begins at initiation, when **the spiritual master plants the seed of bhakti into the heart of the disciple.** How does he do that? **Through sound.** When the disciple hears the spiritual master chant, he opens his heart — **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare.** By hearing from the spiritual master, the spiritual master grants him birth into transcendental knowledge.
- If you chant this mantra, even if you're not very scholarly, everything will be revealed to you in due course of time. That's the beauty of Krishna consciousness. **Bhagavad-gītā 10.10:** to those who serve Him with love and devotion, Krishna says, "I give the understanding by which they can come to Me."
  - Even if one is illiterate, that is not a deterrent in Krishna consciousness. Most important is **faith in Krishna.** **Shvetashvatara Upanishad 6.23:** for one who has faith in the spiritual master and in Krishna, all the purport of Vedic knowledge is revealed.

## The Greatest Gift — This Mantra Reveals All Knowledge

- When you get this mantra and chant it very attentively every day — with some gratitude and affection, and eventually love — all the ignorance is destroyed, and you become fully enlightened, Krishna conscious. "It's the greatest gift you can receive; you cannot receive any greater gift in this lifetime than what you're getting today."
- This mantra will reveal to you all the knowledge of Krishna consciousness — how to get out of this world, the key to enter into the spiritual sky.
- **The value of knowledge:** "As the moon is the ornament of night, as a pious king is the ornament of a kingdom, as a chaste wife is the ornament of a man, so knowledge is the ornament of everything." This is the king of all education.
- Of course, you should strengthen it by reading Prabhupada's books. **Prabhupada said, "If they don't read all my books, they'll fall down."** But even if you're illiterate, if you just have faith in this instruction — the most important instruction the spiritual master gives (according to Prabhupada's purport) — you chant your **sixteen rounds** and you're faithful in following the principles.
  - **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakur said, "I see no reason why all my disciples can't go back to Godhead in one lifetime."** The onus is on the disciple.

## Waking Up the Sleeping Soul

- This loud chanting wakes us up. It also "keeps the clouds at bay": when the devotees were in anxiety — "Oh Mahaprabhu, the clouds, we're not going to get [to chant]" — **Lord Chaitanya just raised his kartals and those clouds would run away so fast.**
- We're in a sleeping condition. **Nursery rhyme:** "It's raining, it's pouring, the old man is snoring; he bumped his head and went to bed and couldn't get up in the morning." "That's not a shloka from Chanakya — that's just a silly little rhyme. But we *did* bump our head; it's a long fall from Vaikuntha."
- **The four types of sleep:**
  - **A snap** — Srila Prabhupada's definition of a devotee who takes a few winks in the middle of the day, just after prasadam or "maybe sometimes during class." ("Amrita Kaley was snoring through my class at the Melbourne city hall the other day, sprawled all over — a little angel.")
  - **A nap** — like ten minutes.
  - **A sleep** — a lot of six hours in Krishna consciousness.
  - **Comatose** — "the demigods are deciding if you can stay or you can go."
- But the deepest sleep is the **sleep of ignorance** — like a zombie, the walking dead. People not enlightened in Krishna consciousness are awake but actually asleep — daydreaming and night-dreaming; their whole life is illusion. Whereas devotees are wakeful in the day and also wakeful at night — sometimes devotees have wonderful dreams of Krishna.
- **This comatose condition is described by Bhaktivinoda Thakur ("Jiv Jago"):** *"jīv jāgo, jīv jāgo, gorācānda bole, kota nidrā yāo māyā-piśācīra kole"* — "How long will you sleep in the lap of the witch called Maya? How long will you sleep?"
  - "Sir, I don't like to sleep." The speaker heard **Prabhupada say one time at New Mayapur, "I don't like to sleep, because sleep means forgetfulness of Krishna."**

## How to Wake a Comatose Soul — Transcendental Sound

- How do you wake a sleeping man? **Through sound.** "All right honey, time to get up, go to work; Lali, up for school."
- But how do you wake a soul who's in a comatose condition, deep ignorance? **Shabda-brahma, transcendental sound: Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare.**
  - *(Aside about a child being carried out:)* "Don't take him too far away — he's waited millions of births to hear the Bhagavatam, and you're taking him away. Okay, sit next to you — that's better."
- "This is the sound you're hearing today from our disciplic succession."

## What You See When You Wake Up — Love of God

- What do you benefit by waking up? A materialistic person wakes up and has the opportunity to fulfill his "nasty material desires" — he goes to school, he goes to work.
- But when your **third eye** — your divine eyes — are awoken, what do you see? When **Krishna blessed Arjuna,** "By My mercy I give you divine eyes to see the **virata-rupa** [universal form]."
- When you wake up in Krishna consciousness, ultimately what do you see? **Chaitanya Charitamrita (Madhya 22.107):** "Pure love for Krishna is eternally established in the hearts of all living entities. It is not something to be gained from another source. When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, the living entity naturally awakens." Awakens to what? **Love of God — that is the real treasure of life that's waiting for all of us.**

## The Treasure Hunt — More Than a Spanish Galleon

Recently, a few months ago, they discovered a **Spanish galleon** — "they won't say where; it's all politics now, some say off the African coast, some say off the Spanish coast" — with billions of dollars of gold coins. The government gets half, and whoever did the expedition gets half — "enough to keep them pretty [comfortable] for the rest of their life."

Those who know the value of this mantra — to destroy your karma, burn it to ashes, uproot your material desires, and give you **prema** — when they chant, they're very careful. "It is a treasure."

## The Story of the Astrologer and the Buried Treasure

- The **Chaitanya Charitamrita** tells the story of the astrologer who came to the house of a poor man and saw that the man was so unhappy, so sad. Looking at his hand, the astrologer said, "Why are you so unhappy? Your father has left you a vast amount of wealth." "Oh, where is it?" "I can tell you."
- "I'll tell you where *not* to go first:
  - Don't go to the **south,** because a **wasp** will sting you.
  - If you go to the **west,** a **ghost** will haunt you.
  - If you go to the **north,** a **snake** will bite you.
  - That leaves the **east** — yes, you go to the east."
- The man asked the meaning:
  - **South = the wasp of karma-kanda** — performing spiritual activities for some material benefit.
  - **West = go to America; one ghost will haunt you — mental speculation:** "Life comes from matter, there is no life after death, there is no God in control — and I am speaking such scientific jargon that you must accept what I'm saying; you layman, you fool." "Yes, sir."
  - **North = the snake** — there the yogis in the Himalayas are doing the yogic asanas for mystic powers, "but that's like child's play, playing with the material energy in some subtle form; we're not interested."
  - **East:** "Sir, if I go east, what will I find?" — **"You go to Gauda-desha, you go to Mayapur, you go to Sridham Navadvipa, you go to the house of Sri [Chaitanya], where he revitalized, rejuvenated — a renaissance of the holy names of Krishna: Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rama."** Only in the east, only in Bengal.

## Prabhupada's Own Mission — and the Sweets of Bengal

- **Like Prabhupada said:** "I went to the West, I fulfilled my spiritual master's desire; then I had my own mission." ("I was shocked when I heard this.") Once he was successful in satisfying his guru, he wanted to go back and revive Krishna consciousness in Bengal, because that's where the Lord appeared — He chose to appear amongst the Bengalese. "You would hardly imagine it now — it's all a communist state, they're forgetting their culture. But thank the Lord He did appear in Bengal."
- **Because in Bengal they have lots of sweets.** "I've noticed in my travels around South India, you get that little preparation at the end; and in Gujarat you get some **shrikhand** — 'Is there anything more?' 'No, Swami, just shrikhand.' Yeah, but in Bengal we get **rasmalai,** and **burfi,** and **sandesh,** and **rasgullas**… Thank the Lord that He appeared in the Bengali culture."
  - So at the Sunday feast we don't just have the yogurt preparation; we have all the nice sweets. "I saw now the sun is coming — confirmed: the Lord likes sweets. A little Kanai, He likes the sweets."

## What You Get from the Yuga Avatar

- You go to the east, and what will you get from the Yuga Avatar? **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.5.32: "kṛṣṇa-varṇaṁ tviṣākṛṣṇaṁ sāṅgopāṅgāstra-pārṣadam / yajñaiḥ saṅkīrtana-prāyair yajanti hi su-medhasaḥ"** — the same Lord who appeared in Dvapara Yuga came again in Kali Yuga, in a golden form, chanting, dancing, and distributing prasadam. So that treasure house you'll get in the form of **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare.**

## Only Sixteen Words — But Absolute

- At one of the programs someone said, "You're speaking so much about this chanting of Hare Krishna, but I counted — it's only 16 words." "Yes — 32 syllables." "What you can get out of just 16 words — you said everything, all our hearts' desires will be fulfilled — that doesn't make sense."
- "No — but you have to understand the nature of these words." **Chaitanya Charitamrita (Madhya 17.133), quoting the Padma Purāṇa: "nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ"** — the name, the fame, the form, the paraphernalia of Krishna, "it's all absolute." **Krishna is dancing on your tongue when you're chanting the Maha-mantra.**
- And what will you get? **Chaitanya Charitamrita (Ādi 17.22): "kali-kāle nāma-rūpe kṛṣṇa-avatāra / nāma haite haya sarva-jagat-nistāra"** — in the age of Kali, the holy name of the Lord [is the incarnation of Krishna, and from the name comes the deliverance of the whole world].
- "We hear these verses and tend to think, 'I've heard that.' No — but we should meditate on that. So valuable: you'll get Krishna Himself."

## Chant and One Day You Will See the Lord

- **Like when Mahaprabhu gave Raghunatha das Goswami the sila (stone) from Govardhan** — what did he say? "That's a stone. We say **sila** out of respect." He said, **"If you worship this stone in the mode of goodness — transcendental goodness — one day you will see the son of Maharaj Nanda in that stone."**
- So if you chant this mantra — **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare** — one day you'll directly see the Lord. "So it's very precious."

## Guard the Treasure — Precious Things Attract Thieves

- Because it's precious, you have to guard it — "whenever something is precious, valuable, there are always thieves who want to steal it."
- **Prabhupada said we want to decorate our deities very gorgeously, but we shouldn't use real jewels.** Sometimes in places like Africa, ignorant people think the jewels are real.
  - **The story in Nairobi:** They came one time and thought the deities' jewels were real, so they attacked the deities with machetes to get the jewels. One **pujari** fought with them; they cut off his arm — "but he still fought with one arm and beat four of them away." Later that devotee died of some disease — "I'm sure he went back to Godhead."
- Something precious — maybe some small or private deities in your home — but "we have a gate, like you have a gate here." Someone asked, "Why the gate?" "Well, Krishna is very beautiful and gorgeous, and people may be envious." "He got the point."

## The Thieves Within — Lust, Anger, and Greed

- We have something very valuable in our hearts — **Krishna-prema,** being slowly developed through the chanting of Hare Krishna. It's the most beautiful thing, and you have to protect it from the thieves.
- Who are the thieves? There are some "bad guys" in the heart. ("The devil made me do it.") **There's lust in your heart, there's anger in your heart, there's greed in your heart.**
  - It's very embarrassing — "like if you go into public and you have… when you're a teenager, you got the pimples. I remember I used to put band-aids here: 'What happened?' 'Oh, I was in a fight' — some pimple was there." It's very embarrassing.
  - So for a devotee who wants to offer his heart to Krishna, he doesn't want a dirty, unclean pot. There's still lust in the corners, some anger, some greed — a very embarrassing situation. There are some bad guys there ready to take the treasure.
- "Sometimes treasures are stolen — don't be naive. There's plenty of people going around the neighborhood in cars looking for houses to hit up when the people are away on vacation."

## Protecting the Treasure by Keeping It Confidential

- One way to protect the treasure is to keep it somewhat **secret.** The word **gupta** means hidden or secret.
- **The gopis:** the word **gopi** comes from *gupta.* The village girls of Braja keep their love for Krishna a little secret because of their seniors.
  - In Braja there's that **parakiya-bhava** rasa — the girls are not married to Krishna, and Krishna is not married to the girls, but they are deeply in love with each other. Vedic culture was very strict: an unmarried boy and an unmarried girl couldn't associate. On the transcendental platform, this enhances the love.
  - So at best, when Krishna was going to the fields, he could just look at the girls, and the girls would look — "just so that the elders wouldn't see." They had to keep their love hidden, and therefore they weren't discovered. Sometimes **Kutila and Jatila** would find out — "but that was also to enhance the pastime." So the gopis keep their love for Krishna a little hidden.
- In Vedic culture a disciple would not discuss openly who his guru was, what his mantra was, or what his realizations were. **These are affairs of the heart — a little confidential, a little secret, something that you cherish** — not for just everyone to see what is in your heart. "It doesn't mean you didn't preach, but those confidential matters were the business only of your friends and close associates."
- Of course, in these times, on the order of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, we proudly declare who is our founder-acharya — **His Divine Grace Om Vishnupada Paramahamsa [Srila Prabhupada]** — and we tell the whole world what our mantra is: **Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare.** But again, they're affairs of the heart, so a little confidentiality is there. We have to protect the treasure.

## Building a Wall — The Four Vows

- How do we protect a treasure? One way is to build a wall around it. So that treasure house of Krishna consciousness, we protect by building a wall — **the four vows.** A wall has four sides, so there are four vows:
  1. **No meat-eating.**
  2. **No intoxication.**
  3. **No illicit sex.**
  4. **No gambling.**
- If you strictly follow these rules and regulations, it will protect the treasure of Krishna consciousness in your heart. There's so much to be gained by being strict in your vows. It's not always easy, but the spiritual master has confidence that at the time of initiation you're ready to say yes to your vows and keep them — "otherwise he wouldn't give the initiation."

## Being Practical About Falldowns — Be Strict Like Devavrata

- "To be frank": in the course of Krishna consciousness the vows may be broken, but we should not let that exception become the rule. If it does happen, then you pick yourself up and go forward and say, **"I will not do that again."**
  - "To be practical, because I have many disciples and they speak to me confidentially, so I know these things are happening." A Muslim friend said, "If you fall down eight times — after three times, this is a sign of insincerity." So then we have some real work to do.
- Really we should be like **Devavrata (Bhishma),** who took that terrible vow — a story in the **Mahabharata** — saying to his father, "Pita, I shall not marry," so that his father, who was a king, could marry a girl (it's a long story). When he said that, the demigods [honored him as] one who has taken a terrible vow.
  - "Of course, *we* think abstaining from illicit sex is a nice vow — but the karmis are thinking, 'What a terrible vow!'" So be strict in your vows.

## Inheriting the Mercy — and the Debt

- It's a wonderful treasure we're receiving, and actually we're indebted to our spiritual master that he gives us this mantra.
- **In Vedic culture, when your father passes away, you inherit his wealth — and you also inherit his debts.** In Western culture it doesn't happen like that; you just get the money, not the debt. But Vedic culture means if your father died, you got the money but also the debts. **"The father who dies with debts is an enemy to his children."**
- So we've inherited the great mercy of our spiritual master — and we also have a great debt. He's taking our karma; he's taking the burden of responsibility; he's taking our hand.
  - When you get back to the spiritual world, **the guru is waiting for you** (as Prabhupada told Bhavananda). He takes your hand and takes you into the **kunja** (the grove), and he puts your hand into the hand of Krishna, and then he leaves you alone. As Prabhupada said in the purport: first we serve guru, but eventually guru takes us and gives us to Krishna. ("Of course, always we're serving our guru eternally.")

## The Mark of a Good Disciple

- So we have a great debt, but it's a blessing in disguise, because we can engage our body, mind, and words in trying to serve the mission of our spiritual master. **This is a good disciple** — he feels grateful: "My spiritual master has given me so much; I want to help him in his mission."
  - *(Aside about a bird/pigeon:)* "The bird has also come for the grains — but you're hearing, so you can stay. You see the pigeon? Hare Krishna Hare Krishna…"
- There are good disciples and foolish disciples — just like there are good students and foolish students. Unquestionably the spiritual master is qualified, because he's in good standing; **but the onus is on the disciple to become qualified.**
- **You can judge the guru by the quality of his disciples.** So we want to advance so that we can give glory to our spiritual master.

## The Example of Radhanath Swami's Disciples

- "I often think of **Radhanath Swami** when I travel around and meet his disciples — he's such an elevated sadhu." ("How many of you have met Radhanath Swami or seen him? That's an elevated devotee.") He trains his disciples so nicely that wherever the speaker goes, "I don't have to ask the question 'Whose disciple are you?' — I can see it in their eyes: such etiquette, such humility, such a service attitude."
- So there's an example of the disciple giving credit to the guru — "it's almost as if they're mirrors or reflections of the spiritual master."
- **The onus is on the disciple to give glory to the spiritual master by making nice progress — and that we can do; it's not difficult.** What pleases our spiritual master?
  1. **First,** to become fixed up in Krishna consciousness.
  2. **Secondly,** to share our good fortune with others.
- **Preaching was the mission of Krishna, the mission of Lord Chaitanya, the mission of Srila Prabhupada** — it is our mission, and it will be the mission of our children, and our children's children's children. And this gives the greatest pleasure to the spiritual master.
