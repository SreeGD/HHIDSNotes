## Introduction: The Appearance Day of Srila Narottama Das Thakura

- The speaker feels privileged and honored to visit the community in **Alachua** on the auspicious **appearance day of Srila Narottama Das Thakura.**
- He feels "very unqualified even to mention his name or speak of his pastimes and transcendental qualities, which are unfathomable" — but it's our duty to say something, so he asks the grace of Srila Prabhupada to glorify this great Mahajan, this devotee of the Lord.
- For many in ISKCON this is one of the biggest days. We generally think of Janmasthami or Radhasthami — appearance days of the Lord and of Nrisimhadev — as the big days. But without the mercy of great acharyas like Narottama Das Thakura, "we would be in complete ignorance of the Lord and his transcendental pastimes."
- There are many such great devotees, but Narottama is very dear to the hearts of Vaishnavas and ISKCON members "because he's a great hero." **Everyone needs a hero** to look up to, to follow, and to be inspired by.

## "I Want to Be Just Like You" — Acharya Teaches by Example

- Once a disciple said to Srila Prabhupada, "I want to be just like you." Another devotee objected, "That's offensive — you can't be like the spiritual master."
- Prabhupada said, **"No, that's a very nice aspiration,"** because for the disciple the spiritual master is the best example of how to engage in the process of Krishna consciousness. "Yes, you be like me." **Acharya means one who teaches by example.**
- We have many heroes in our Guru-parampara. Narottama stands out as an ideal example of a devotee, "as Srila Prabhupada has trained us."

## ISKCON Is a Missionary Movement — Prabhupada's Mission

- We must never forget that ISKCON is a **missionary movement.** Prabhupada has an intimate, loving relationship with Krishna in the spiritual world (which we are not meant to speculate on), but we know very clearly his mission, personality, and desires in this world — he made it very clear.
- Many times Prabhupada said, **"If you want to please me, distribute my books"** — meaning preaching. He spent every day, hour, minute, and second of his life pushing forward the same Sankirtan movement of **Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.**
- As his Guru Maharaj said, "This preaching will do good for you, and it will also do good for others."

## Our Two Goals

- The result of faithfully following Srila Prabhupada, Lord Chaitanya, and Radha-Shyamasundar is that, hopefully within this lifetime, one will awaken **pure love for Radha-Shyamasundar.**
- We have two goals:
  1. Give our heart and soul to the Sankirtan movement of Lord Chaitanya, to fulfill the desires of Srila Prabhupada as he outlined them. "That will give him great pleasure."
  2. Become a pure, unalloyed devotee of the Lord — this also pleases him.
- We have the example in the personality of **Sri Narottama Das Thakura** — a great stalwart preacher in Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement who "took over" after the Lord and His associates wound up their manifest pastimes.

## The Three Who Continued the Mission

- The responsibility to continue that mission lay on the shoulders of three divine personalities:
  - **Sri Narottama Das Thakura**
  - **Shyamananda Pandit**
  - **Srinivas Acharya**
- All under the guidance of their **shiksha-guru, Jiva Goswami.** They were the ones chosen to continue the mission that "God himself had started." They demonstrated perfectly the art of preaching.
- Narottama was "a preacher from A to Z," and simultaneously a very deep, *rasika* devotee — "it wasn't one or the other."

## "Why Not Both?" — Rasika Depth and Preaching

- **Story:** The speaker and Sri Prahlad once went to **Tamal Krishna Goswami** with an important question — "should we do this or should we do that?" He said, "Why one or the other? Why not both?"
- In our movement we have these two aspirations:
  - To become very deep, *rasika* devotees, relishing the mellows of pure devotional service, giving pleasure to Radha and Krishna through **ahaituki, apratihata** — unmotivated, uninterrupted, pure devotional service.
  - And using that purity to spread Sri Krishna Sankirtana.
- **"Preaching is the essence. Books are the basis. Purity is the principle... and purity is the force."**
- **What type of purity?** Not just having no material desires left in the heart — *cheto-darpana-marjanam*, no lust, no anger, no greed. "But that's basically liberation, nirvana, cessation of material desires."
- Real purity is a very intense desire to please our spiritual master and Sri Sri Radha and Krishna. "That was the purity that Narottama had, and therefore he was chosen among all the others. Many are called, but few are chosen."
- This morning the devotees heard a beautiful lecture by **Chaturatma Prabhu**, who (as many do) has a special affection for Narottama. This evening the speaker will describe how this great Vaishnava acharya realized his mission on earth by the mercy of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.

## Bilas Manjari Descends as Narottama

- The intimate associates of Lord Krishna accompanied Him in His pastimes in Vrindavan 5,000 years ago — most of them in female forms, as gopis.
- They came again 500 years ago in the pastimes of **Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (who is non-different from Krishna), in male forms.
- **Bilas Manjari**, who serves Radha and Krishna in a very intimate way in Sri Vrindavan Dham, descended in the pastimes of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu as the great devotee **Narottama Das Thakura.**

## Lord Chaitanya Predicts Narottama's Appearance

- Before winding up His pastimes, Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu predicted Narottama's appearance. During a kirtan on the banks of the **Padma River**, He kept calling out, "Narottama! Narottama! Narottama!"
- There was no one named Narottama among His associates. **Nitai** wondered; suddenly Mahaprabhu, chanting the name, fell unconscious on the ground.
- Nitai whispered in His ear, "Mahaprabhu, who is Narottama?" Mahaprabhu opened His eyes: "The great devotee — he will appear after we finish up our pastimes, and he will continue the mission of Krishna consciousness, this Yuga-dharma of chanting Hare Krishna."
- So it came to pass, by the will of the Lord, that he appeared in **Keturi**, grew into a fine young Vaishnava, and eventually received his training in Krishna consciousness from none other than **Sri Jiva Goswami in Vrindavan** — along with his close friends **Shyamananda** and **Srinivas Acharya.**

## The Books of the Goswamis Must Go to Bengal

- The comradery between these boys was extraordinary. They studied the **Bhakti-shastras** and the teachings of the Goswamis, engaging in wonderful service and pastimes in Vrindavan.
- As a means to reciprocate with Sri Jiva Goswami, they agreed to take the books of the Goswamis to Bengal. At that time books were being written in Vrindavan but not published as today — no big printing presses — everything was copied by hand.
- The Goswamis were so prolific under the instruction of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Every time a new book came out in the assembly of devotees, it was held up to huge applause: **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, Hari-bol! Ujjvala-nilamani, Hari-bol!** And **Kaviraja Goswami's Chaitanya-charitamrita, Hari-bol!** — and everybody would run to read the one copy.
  - Aside: "How fortunate we are — we have the books on our bookshelves. Do we read them?"
- Jiva Goswami wanted these books (only one copy of each) to go to Bengal, where the Bengali Vaishnavas were thirsting for this nectar. The idea: the books would go there, be copied, and come back. So the boys had this very serious responsibility.

## The Books Are Stolen by King Birambir

- On the way, **King Birambir's** astrologer told him that a very precious cargo was coming down the road — "more valuable than millions of chests of gold, silver, rubies, and diamonds."
- Birambir sent his soldiers, who in the middle of the night stole the trunk of books. In the morning the boys woke and the books were gone.
- Bear in mind, the Goswamis had written these books over many years — "a veritable treasure house of knowledge, gone." The boys were devastated. *(This is where the morning class ended.)*

## The Voice from the Sky — Sent Out on Sankirtan

- According to **Bhakti-ratnakara**, as they lamented, a voice came from the sky, which **Srinivas Acharya** heard: **"The books will be returned. Do not fear."**
- Hearing this divine oracle, Srinivas Acharya felt somewhat pacified, knowing the Lord had a plan to retrieve them. Without further ado he said, "All right, boys, out on Sankirtan."
  - "Like Prabhupada was like that — 'If you've got time, chant Hare Krishna.'"
- So Shyamananda and Narottama left the divine association of Srinivas Acharya.

## The Honorific Titles

- How these boys served their spiritual masters so nicely is a whole class in itself. At their graduation ceremony in Vrindavan they were awarded august titles:
  - **Narottama** received the title **Mahasaya.**
  - **Shyamananda** — very erudite in the scriptures — was given the name **Pandit** (Shyamananda Pandit).
  - **Srinivas** — a little older, so illustrious — received the name **Acharya** (Srinivas Acharya).
- "They were boys, but they were blessed with these honorific titles. And they were tight — their friendship was based on Krishna consciousness, which goes very, very deep."

## Friendship Based on Something Sacred

- We all hanker for relationships, and friendship is one of them. If we don't develop strong, intimate, loving relationships among our godbrothers and godsisters (godbrothers with godbrothers, godsisters with godsisters), we'll search for that satisfaction outside ISKCON. **"No man is an island unto himself."**
- If those relationships are based on sense gratification (we like the same clothes, the same music, the same drugs), the result is described in **Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.5.30** — *"matir na krishne..."* — "because of their uncontrolled senses, persons too addicted toward sense gratification gradually glide down to hellish conditions of life."
- "Real friends don't let their friends eat meat" — the speaker's favorite American bumper sticker, "even more than Obama... even devotees have the Obama."
- Friendship should be based on something sacred — **service to guru and Krishna.** A real friend is one who encourages you to give up sense gratification, as difficult as it may be, and take up service to Guru and Gauranga. You may not like the same clothes or the same prasadam, but if we have a strong desire to work together to please the spiritual master, then the friendship is unlimited — it goes deeper. **Anandambudhi-vardhanam** — unlimited, ever-increasing bliss/love.
- We can hardly understand how intimate and loving was the friendship between Narottama, Srinivas, and Shyamananda.

## Attachment and Separation

- To the degree that you're attached to something, when you lose it you feel separation. If it's some little thing, "okay, just a little bit." But when something very dear to your heart is lost, "the pain of separation is equal to the attachment that you have."
- We don't just have attachment to God — we only know God by the mercy of our spiritual master, so our attachment to the spiritual master is very deep. And we can only really understand our spiritual master by serving in the association of our godbrothers and godsisters, so they too are very dear to us.
- Narottama writes: **"Hari, Guru, Vaishnava"** — Hari (God), Guru (spiritual master), Vaishnava (the devotee), the Bhagavata, the Gita. Sometimes he stresses the **association of devotees** — it's so important for our progress.

## Relishing Association — and Going Out to Preach

- These boys demonstrated how they relished each other's association: doing kirtan, hari-katha, taking prasadam together, worshipping the deity — and **going out among the masses "and pressing the flesh"** (as they say in this country — shaking hands), befriending the fallen conditioned souls and giving them an opportunity too.
- As **Jesus** said, "It's better to give than to receive." When we share Krishna consciousness we genuinely experience the **higher taste.** We won't experience it fully if we just share it among ourselves — to go higher and higher we have to go out and "press the flesh in Gainesville and Jacksonville and all those horrible places." "This is where the mercy comes and you get the higher taste."
- We all have our **sankirtan partners.** The speaker remembers the friends he went out with in the 1970s — "sometimes my eyes well up with tears" — the austerities they underwent as young boys, how they weren't disturbed, and how much they enjoyed traveling and preaching Krishna consciousness.
- So who can imagine the feelings of separation as Narottama and Shyamananda had to leave the association of Srinivas Acharya? "We only understand when we get to that level."

## Keturi — Where Narottama Was Born

- They left and headed for **Keturi**, part of Greater Bengal — then East Bengal, now part of **Bangladesh.**
- The speaker has had the great fortune of going there. Unfortunately, because of the Islamic government and Muslim populace, "they've torn everything down practically — you have to see it with your imagination." Still remaining:
  - The palace of Narottama's father.
  - The lake from which **Mother Janhava** took the water to cook the big feast at the first **Gaura Purnima.**
  - Some remnants and shalagram grounds of Narottama.
- This is where Narottama was born — where **Bilas Manjari** took birth — "so for us it's as good as Mecca, as good as Bethlehem."

## Coming Home a Great Sadhu — Santosh Surrenders

- Narottama left home at a young age. He was supposed to be the next king, but like many great personalities in our disciplic succession — such as **Raghunath Das Goswami**, who would have inherited vast wealth and prestige — he renounced it all for the nectarean service of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. He struggled to reach Vrindavan and get his Krishna conscious education.
- Coming back after a long time, the whole village came out to receive him (with Shyamananda). "Just like in our Civil War, when the boys came back — *When Johnny Comes Marching Home Again, hurrah, hurrah.*" His fame preceded him "just like the sunlight comes before the sun planet." They were very proud of him — his studies and pastimes in Vrindavan.
- The only sad part: his father **Krishnananda Dutt** and his mother **Narayani** (the king and queen) had passed away, and his uncle also. But his uncle's son — his nephew, **Santosh** — was the reigning regent, the king at that time, who had inherited all that vast wealth, prestige, and honor.
- But Santosh was **Krishna-bhakta.** Though surrounded by wealth, internally his mood was that of **Shikshashtakam, Verse 4** — *"na dhanam na janam na sundarim kavitam va jagad-isha kamaye / mama janmani janmanishvare bhavatad bhaktir ahaituki tvayi"* — "O Lord, I have no desire for wealth, followers, beautiful women, or poetry; just give me causeless devotional service at Your lotus feet, birth after birth. That's what I'll be happy with."
- When his nephew Narottama returned, Santosh ran to the perimeter of the town and fell down at his feet, giving him the whole kingdom: "My life is yours."
- **The lesson:** If you get that sadhu association, don't do anything else — just fully surrender. Like Prabhupada told **Vishnujana Maharaj:** "So many lifetimes you've been experimenting; just give one lifetime to Krishna. There may be so many before and after where you've done everything — but surrender to Krishna, just give this one lifetime, and see what happens." "That was the mood of Santosh."

## Narottama Becomes the Prominent Spiritual Master

- Narottama and Shyamananda stayed in Keturi and mercifully began giving their **bhakti-shastri classes**, enlightening the townspeople in the science of Krishna consciousness, regardless of who they were. As a result, Narottama became the prominent spiritual master in the village.

## Shyamananda Departs for Utkala

- **Bhakti-ratnakara** relates that after some time **Shyamananda** left for his own town, **Utkala** — his *Prabhupada-desh.*
  - Aside: "What is your Prabhupada-desh? Gainesville? Okay, Alachua — that's just a few souls that are liberated. You've got to move on: Gainesville, Jacksonville, Miami."
- Shyamananda considered that his service to Jiva Goswami would be fulfilled when he liberated all the people of Utkala. There was a whole scene of separation between him and Narottama — crying.

## What Is Krishna Consciousness? — "Crying, Crying, Crying"

- **Anecdote:** A devotee asked **Gour Govinda Maharaj**, "Could you just summarize what is Krishna consciousness?" His answer: "Crying, crying, crying, crying, crying."
- Not the crying of lamentation, but the deeper crying — feeling separation, as in **Shikshashtakam, Verse 7** — *"yugayitam nimeshena..."* — "Feeling Your separation, O Govinda, I am considering a moment to be like twelve years; tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain; I feel all vacant in the world."
- "That is the success." If, after many years of chanting and a lifetime of service, toward the end of your life you can chant one holy name purely, and a little tear comes from your eye, goes down your cheek, and falls onto the ground — "that's success." The feeling, the emotion comes — love for Guru and Gauranga.

## Narottama's Pilgrimage to Meet the Last Associates

- Narottama remained preaching for some time, then had a very strong desire to tour all the holy places, with the intention of getting the **darshan of the last remaining associates of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** who were still alive.
- Mahaprabhu had passed away 25, 30, or 40 years earlier, and only a few remaining **parishadas** (associates) were left. Gauranga, Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhar — "they all left," and of their intimate associates, just a few remained. They were all **uttama-adhikaris.**
- Just as Prabhupada had his "lifetime in preparation" before coming to the West, Narottama thought: "To prepare myself, I want to go get the dust of the feet of these great Vaishnavas" — and to see the places of the pastimes of Lord Chaitanya.

## Arrival at Navadvip — The Kirtan Vanishes

- First he went to **Navadvip.** As he came in, **Bhakti-ratnakara** describes him lamenting: "If I had just been born a little earlier, I could have had the association of so many great souls — and I just missed seeing Lord Chaitanya." Thinking like this, he fainted unconscious on the ground at the perimeter of Navadvip.
- When he woke from his ecstatic state, he saw a huge kirtan party — hundreds, thousands of the residents of Navadvip going through the streets in great ecstasy and bliss, chanting the names of Krishna, playing kartals, beating mridangas, blowing bugles, waving flags. **Navadvip was alive with the Sankirtan movement of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.**
- Suddenly, everything stopped and disappeared. The whole of Navadvip was plunged into **vipralambha-bhava** — the mood of separation from Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Though He had disappeared years ago, the predominant mood now was not the tumultuous kirtan of His presence, but weeping and crying in separation, 30 years later. "How strong was their attachment to the Lord that they could feel such deep feelings of separation."
- Looking around Navadvip, what did Narottama see? "The Ganges was no longer flowing. The calves were not sucking milk from the teats of their mothers; the mothers stood motionless with the grass still un-chewed in their mouths. Bees were stunned sitting on the tops of the lotus flowers. The birds in the trees had long ago stopped singing. And all the people stood like statues."

## The Old Sadhu Who Had Been Waiting

- Narottama walked around in a daze. Suddenly one very old sadhu — **Sukalambhava Brahmachari** — bent over, unable to straighten up, walking with a cane, came up and asked, "Young man, what is your name?"
- "My name is Narottama Das Thakura. I've come from Keturi."
- That old man, though crippled, suddenly began to dance in ecstasy — twirling, singing, laughing: **"You are Narottama Das Thakura! You were predicted by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — that you will carry on the banner of Sankirtan. I know Mahaprabhu called out your name before he left. We've been waiting for you! Where have you been?"**

## Meeting Dhamadara Pandit — "The Name of a Devotee Is as Good as the Name of God"

- He took Narottama to meet various transcendental personalities. First, **Dhamadara Pandit**, who sat down with him and spoke Krishna-katha — in the form of discussing all the devotees of Lord Chaitanya.
- **"It's as good to chant the name of a devotee as it is to chant the name of God."** How could that be? Because they are intimately entwined in love.
  - **Jesus Christ** said, "My Father and I are one" — meaning not the same person, but united in love.
  - You'll never see a picture of Krishna alone. **Pushkara Prabhu** never paints Krishna alone — there are always some swans, deer, gopis, or cowherd boys in the background. Devotional service means the Lord, His servant, and the process of devotional service.
  - Analogy of the married man (*grihastha*): when you think of the wife you can't separate her from her husband — there's love there, an intimate relationship. "They're two halves of the same body, and they say the lady's the better half." The wife does the rituals at home to keep it sanctified while the man works in the material world. So when you think of Krishna you think of Prabhupada, and when you think of Prabhupada you can't help but think of Krishna. To sing the name of either is just as purifying.
- Dhamadara Pandit began relating the pastimes of the associates of Lord Chaitanya who had recently left this world out of separation. But as he went on, Narottama, instead of becoming enlivened, lamented more: "Oh, I missed him? By how much time? Ten days — he passed away ten days ago." Associate after associate, until by the end Narottama was again unconscious on the ground, so devastated.

## The Dream at Navadvip

- That night Narottama slept under a tree and had a dream — more like a vision. (Great souls don't dream nonsense as we do; "this is more like a vision — sleeping, but the vision is there.")
- One by one, all the associates of Mahaprabhu that Dhamadara Pandit had described that afternoon came and embraced Narottama, saying: **"You alone will execute the mission of Lord Chaitanya."**

## Shantipur and Kadaha — Achyutananda and Mother Janhava

- Next morning he went to **Shantipur**, where he met **Achyutananda**, the son of **Advaita Acharya**, still living, very old. They had some sweet hari-katha and remembrances of Gaura-lila.
- Then he went to **Kadaha (Khardaha)**, where he took darshan of the wife of Lord Nityananda — the great Maharani, **Vaishnavi Thakurani, Mother Janhava Mata**, the most prominent Vaishnava living at that time — and her son, **Virachandra.**
- **Mother Janhava** encouraged him: "Narottama, go very quickly to Jagannath Puri. There are still a few great devotees there, but they're going to leave very soon." She "just pushed him out of the house — go immediately, get the darshan of these great devotees."

## Jagannath Puri — Tota Gopinath and Haridasa Thakur's Samadhi

- Then he went to **Puri**, where he met **Mamu Thakur**, a disciple of **Gadadhar Pandit**, who took him to see **Tota Gopinath** and described how **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu had given up His pastimes by entering into the body of the Tota Gopinath deity** — the deity He had personally found in the sand dunes of Jagannath Puri. "He entered into the knee — there's a crack there, you can see it, if you give 100 rupees."
- Then he went to the **Samadhi Mandir of Haridasa Thakur** — and what did he do? "Crying, crying, crying, crying."

## The Dream at Haridasa Thakur's Samadhi — Mahaprabhu's Promise

- There in Puri, Narottama fell asleep next to the Samadhi Mandir of Haridasa Thakur and had a wonderful dream: he was watching the **Ratha Yatra** parade going down the main road in Jagannath Puri.
  - Aside: The speaker has had the good fortune to be there — "our little Ratha Yatra seems like kindergarten." Three huge carts, millions and millions of people, "as it has been since the very beginning."
- In the dream Narottama stood on the side watching this huge festival — "Jaya Jagannath, Jaya Baladeva, Jaya Subhadra." Suddenly the cart of Jagannath passed, and to his amazement, who was dancing in front of the cart? **Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.**
- In the dream, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu saw him, came over, embraced him, and took his hands, saying:
  - **"Narottama, through you I will manifest a new type of kirtan that will mesmerize the entire universe. Through your kirtan I will reveal my mission, explain my message, and reveal my very deep and divine pastimes. Narottama, don't you worry about anything. Now and in the future, anyone who takes shelter of you will get the shelter of my feet and easily attain prema-bhakti."**

## Why This Day Is So Important

- Therefore this is one of the most important days for the members of the International Society. "What are we here for? When all is said and done, what do you hope to achieve in this lifetime? Some **prema-bhakti.**"
- Prabhupada will be very happy — "like the child grows up and takes over the business, daddy's happy." We're going through our growing pains, but in the end, if we have a little **bhava** in our heart for Radha and Krishna, Prabhupada will be very pleased: "That's my son. Yes, my daughter, you have done well."
- And how do we get it? By Mahaprabhu's promise: anyone who takes shelter of Narottama gets the shelter of Mahaprabhu's feet and easily attains prema-bhakti.
- Therefore we should never neglect to observe this day (or Narottama's disappearance day, which falls in **Kartik**). For some devotees the calendar is: "Janmasthami is coming, Radhasthami is coming, Nrisimha's appearance day, Narottama Das Thakura's appearance day — let's hold a huge feast, 108 preparations, all come to the temple, chant and dance and have tumultuous kirtan," just like Janmasthami. "We are the servant of the servant of the servant, many times removed. This is how we begin — by honoring our great acharyas."

## The Promise of Sri Ramchandra Kaviraj — and Awakening in the Temple

- Holding his hands on the street of Ratha Yatra, Mahaprabhu also said: **"Very soon you will be joined by a special devotee, Sri Ramchandra Kaviraj. He's a disciple of Srinivas Acharya, and together the two of you shall propagate the chanting of the holy names."**
- Narottama woke and found himself in the temple of Lord Jagannath. The pujari was doing arati; Narottama rushed forth to see Jagannath, and a garland from Jagannath fell down over his neck — giving divine blessings for the mission ahead. Stumbling out of the temple, he realized his mission on earth and felt blessed to achieve it by the direct instruction of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
- **(Comparison to Chaitanya's own transformation):** Just like Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — who grew up, had His school and did His teaching, then went to **Gaya** to perform the funeral ceremony for His father, where He met **Ishvara Puri** and was initiated into the chanting of Hare Krishna, then began chanting like a madman, exhibiting all the ecstatic symptoms of love of God, and came back to Navadvip so transformed that nobody recognized Him, "like an Avadhuta, a transcendental mad personality" — so too Narottama was now transformed.

## Katwa — Gadadhara Pandit and the Muslim Kazi

- Narottama proceeded to **Katwa**, where he was greeted by a very intimate associate of Lord Nityananda, **Gadadhara Pandit.**
  - Who is Gadadhara Pandit? "Oh, that's another class." Suffice it to say he is **the effulgence of Srimati Radharani** — a very empowered devotee, a fearless preacher.
- **Story of Gadadhara and the Kazi:** In Katwa there was once a very powerful Muslim ruler who hated Hindus and Vaishnavas — plundering, abusing, and torturing them, and stopping them from performing any religious activities. The Vaishnavas were terrified of him. **Narottama-vilasa** describes that if the wind just breezed past him and touched a Vaishnava's body, they would jump into a sacred river immediately to purify themselves.
- But Gadadhara Pandit was very courageous. One time he walked right into the Muslim king's palace late at night, screaming, "Kazi! Kazi! Come down here! I want to speak to you!" The soldiers and family members were alarmed. He shouted, "Chant the names of Hari! Hari! Hari! Or I will cut off your head!"
- The Kazi came downstairs with a sword to kill him — but seeing his divine form, he had a change of heart and saw him as the embodiment of compassion. "Sadhu, what are you doing here?" Gadadhara: "Dear Kazi, Gora has come to deliver you. Everyone is chanting His names but you. Chant Hari! Hari!" The Kazi: "Okay, okay, look — just go home, and I promise you, tomorrow I'll chant Hari Hari." Gadadhara started dancing in ecstasy: "You said it! You said the names Hari! Hari! You're delivered! Hari-bol! Hari-bol!" — and they let him out, no problem.
- "This is a Vaishnava — taking risks for the Lord."
- When Narottama came to Katwa, the first person he met was Gadadhara, who embraced him and whispered in his ear: **"Narottama, I've been waiting for you. Soon all your desires will be fulfilled. Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu will come and dance in your kirtan. Wait and see."** — a prophecy.

## Ekachakra — Lord Nityananda in the Guise of a Brahmana

- To get the mercy of Lord Chaitanya, Narottama first had to get the mercy of **Lord Nityananda.** So next he went to **Ekachakra.**
- Who greeted him there? **Lord Nityananda in the disguise of a Brahmana.** ("These are very deep, esoteric pastimes, but just pure nectar, because we have full faith. No problem.")
- The Brahmana asked, "Who are you, young man? Where have you come from, and why are you here?" Narottama: "I've come to see the pastime place of Lord Nityananda — exactly where He danced in ecstasy." The Brahmana: "I know everything. You come with me."
  - "Here he herded the cows. Here he played like Lakshman in the pastimes of Ramlila. Here he played like Balaram, with a club and a plow." Suddenly that Brahmana manifested his form as **Lord Balaram**, with His plow. Then Balaram turned into **Nitai**, who said to Narottama: **"You are my favorite. All of your desires will soon be fulfilled."**
- And what did Narottama do? "Typical — he fell unconscious."

## Back to Keturi — Lord Chaitanya Reveals the Granary Deity

- After all this — "a lifetime in preparation" — Narottama came back to Keturi, received with great pomp, adoration, and affection. In his humble kutir he thought: "Now I have received so much mercy. Exactly how should I begin to execute this mission? What's the first step?"
- He fell asleep, and Lord Chaitanya appeared to him in a dream:
  - **"Narottama, I've been waiting for you to return to Keturi. I've hidden myself in a granary. There's a wealthy man in this town of Keturi who has many granaries, but he can't use them because they're infested with venomous snakes. In the biggest granary, I'm staying there under the grain, in my deity form as Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, along with my wife, Vishnu Priya. Come and rescue me. And then, have the artisans carve five beautiful Krishna deities, arrange a glorious Gaura Purnima festival in my honor, and invite all my associates who are still living, their disciples, and their disciples' disciples, and anyone who's taken an interest in this Sankirtan. Invite them all for one big Mahamahotsava."**
  - Narottama: "How am I going to do that?" Mahaprabhu: **"Don't worry, I'll empower you."**

## "Along with the Order Comes the Ability"

- Every devotee knows this: no one ever feels qualified. "Who are we to do anything in Krishna's service? We're experts at sense gratification." But we have faith — Prabhupada once said, **"Along with the order of the spiritual master comes the ability to execute it."**
- So never be afraid to accept any responsibility on behalf of your spiritual authorities — "this is the transcendental mystery. If you just agree to do it, you'll get the mercy. You'll be empowered to do it. It's not us — it's him."

## Rescuing the Golden Deity from the Snake-Infested Granary

- Narottama woke immediately and started running around Keturi asking about granaries. Everyone said, "Yes, but you can't go — there are too many snakes." He found the owner: "Are you the owner of these granaries?" "Yes, sir." "What's the biggest one?" The man pointed. Narottama started walking toward it: "I'm going in." The man: "No, no, you can't! There's a curse and dangerous, venomous snakes — people have gone in and been bitten; the thieves who came to steal the grain are littered as corpses."
- But Narottama wasn't afraid — he had the order of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. "One who has the order of Guru and Gauranga is not afraid. He's just a servant." As Krishna said to Arjuna: **"I've already decided the fate of this battle. You just be my instrument and take the glory."**
- As he came, the snakes were hissing "like a beehive." He opened the door: **"Out, snakes!"** Suddenly all the snakes came slithering out — thousands and thousands of them — and slithered away into the forest, never to be seen again.
- He dug deep into the granary and found a **solid gold deity of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.**

## The Speaker's Darshan of the Golden Deity

- **Personal account:** The speaker has seen this very deity — "at the house of **Ganga Narayan Chakravarti**, about four hours north of our Mayapur community. It's a transcendental open secret. If you want directions, I'll give them after class."
- He went there with **Radhanath Swami**, spending hours in the home of Ganga Narayan Chakravarti — "the chief disciple of Narottama Das Thakura." The deity, solid gold, had been kept for a hundred years in a bank vault, but the pilgrims protested, wanting darshan, so to this day he remains in that house — guarded by "one old guard with an old shotgun from 1927, falling asleep." "I don't know how that deity still remains there, but he's there. Beautiful. Pure gold."
- The visit: They had an ecstatic kirtan outside the house, dancing and twirling; the people looked on. They asked for darshan — "Oh, no, no, no." They began another kirtan (Hari, Haraya Nama); seeing how Radhanath Swami dances, the people relented: "Yes, yes, come, have darshan of Gauranga." Left alone with the deity and all the **shalagram-shilas** of Ganga Narayan Chakravarti, the speaker "was drooling." Eventually the pujari gave him one — the **Varaha / Nrisimha shalagram** worshiped by Ganga Narayan Chakravarti (now in a bank vault in Poland — "No, you worship him!").
- They took the feet of that deity onto their heads, embraced it, prayed, and cried before it — "to give us mercy to become the proper representatives of Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement, and to help Narottama, Srinivas, and Shyamananda push the preaching." They spent hours there; Radhanath Swami looked at the deity without blinking. "So instead of touching the feet of the deity, I was touching Maharaj's feet. It was such nectar — I could have stayed there forever."

## The First Kirtan — A New Style That Shames the Gandharvas

- Along with the deity of **Vishnu Priya**, Narottama brought the deities in a huge procession back to Navadvip. (Navadvip is not far from Keturi — "if you could fly by the crow, it's just five or six hours" — though now, being a Muslim country, you need a visa.) He took the Gauranga deity back to Keturi, put him on an altar, and just stood there staring at the deity for days on end.
- At the end of that period, the Lord empowered him — and Narottama started singing. It is described by the acharyas as the **first time he ever sang.** As predicted by Lord Chaitanya in the dream at Jagannath Puri (next to Haridasa Thakur's samadhi), he was empowered to do a new style of chanting and dancing that would mesmerize the entire world.
- Alone, in front of the deity, he sang a new type of kirtan — very sweet — and danced in a new style. It is described that "even the **Gandharvas** were put to shame" (the Gandharvas being very sweet singers and very good dancers). As he finished, a tumultuous sound of "Jai, jai, jai" came from the sky, and he was inundated with flowers from **Svargaloka** ("those flowers are very sweet"). From that day on he worshiped that deity personally.

## Hankering for Srinivas — and the Story of Ramchandra Kaviraj

- After some time Narottama thought, "Now I have this deity — what's the next step?" He hankered for the association of **Srinivas Acharya**, who was like the Sankirtan leader/organizer, while Narottama was the bhakta — chanting, dancing, crying.
  - Analogy: "Srinivas was like Tamal Krishna Maharaj — the general, the organizer — and Narottama was like Vishnujana Maharaj, the kirtan."
- Suddenly a message came from Srinivas Acharya: **"I'm married, I've had children, and I've initiated a disciple who's very near and dear to my heart. His name is Ramchandra Kaviraj."**
- **How Srinivas got Ramchandra Kaviraj as a disciple:** Srinivas (a renunciate at the time) was sitting near his ashram in Braj when a wedding procession came by. The door was locked, so he couldn't go back inside; he had to stand on the stairwell and watch.
  - A boy had just married a girl "as beautiful as all the ladies of heaven combined" — beautiful, young, educated, shy, chaste, possessed of all wonderful qualities. Her husband, **Ramchandra**, sat next to her, very happy — fresh from the **vivaha-yajna**, on the way to her home to bid farewell to her parents before going to his.
  - Srinivas called out to the charioteer, "Stop! There's a black snake around your neck, fool!" Ramchandra: "What did you say?" Srinivas: "It's a black snake of death — spiritual death — around your neck. That woman is too beautiful. If you make her your wife, you'll make no progress in Krishna consciousness; you'll never become an enlightened soul."
  - Caveat (the words of a sadhu): "Time, place, and circumstance. I'm not making a general principle here. Different strokes for different folks." Narottama himself says (in **Prema-bhakti-chandrika**), *"grihe thako, vane thako"* — whether one is a *grihastha* or a *sannyasi*, if in his heart he wants to serve Lord Chaitanya, "give me the dust of his feet." This was a particular instance — Srinivas wanted to deliver this great devotee.
  - Ramchandra thought for a minute, got off the chariot, and told the charioteer, "Take her back to her father. Finish this marriage." He walked over and fell at the feet of Srinivas Acharya: "Sir, may I become your disciple?" "Yes, come in." He became so renounced — and, more importantly, such a **bhakta** — that he was the nearest and dearest to his Guru Maharaj, "master and disciple in a loving relationship."

## "One Soul in Two Bodies" — Ramchandra Given to Narottama

- Narottama got a message from Srinivas to meet him in **Vidura (Viduri)**, a nearby town. There Narottama explained to his bosom friend how he had gotten the deity of Lord Chaitanya, how he was arranging for five other deities to be installed, about the dream from Lord Chaitanya, and how they were going to have a big festival at Gaura Purnima in Keturi. "Srinivas, you have to help me."
- Srinivas said, **"I'll help you. I'll give you my most valuable possession — I'll give you Ramchandra Kaviraj. He will be like your second self. You will be like one soul in two bodies."**
- So they began working together — Ramchandra Kaviraj, Narottama, Srinivas. "This is what we hanker for — these personal relationships, this type of friendship, preaching Krishna consciousness."

## Planning the Keturi Festival — Establishing the Siddhanta

- News spread of this big event: they would install six deities, have a big kirtan, sumptuous prasad, and **sit down to discuss the philosophy of Lord Chaitanya** — to determine "what is the real siddhanta, what is the real tattva."
- By that time so many years had passed that there were different **deviations and schisms**; nobody knew the real philosophy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu or the real process of devotion — everyone was confused.
- So Narottama proposed an **ishta-goshthi**, where all the **Mahants** would sit down, discuss, and come up with the actual philosophy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — the *tattva*, the *siddhanta*, how it is to be performed — following what the Goswamis established.
- **Santosh** built huge kirtan halls, huge kitchens, and huge residences; "it began looking like Svargaloka." Finally **Shyamananda** came, and the three boys were together again — "their joy knew no bounds."

## Mother Janhava's Dream — The Secret of Panchatattva

- Some distance away, **Mother Janhava**, the wife of Lord Nityananda, was engaged in her transcendental pastime with many followers and disciples, preaching. One night she had a dream of Lord Chaitanya, who told her to make sure that all the great devotees came to this festival being arranged by Narottama, Shyamananda, and Srinivas.
- He said: **"One thing will happen there, but you keep it secret. I will also come with the Panchatattva and dance in great transcendental ecstasy."**
- Janhava Mata woke, tears flowing from her eyes "like torrents of rain," and began dancing in great ecstasy — because Lord Chaitanya's appearance in Keturi meant she would once again see her beloved Lord Nityananda.

## The Transcendental Armada Assembles at Keturi

- The day of the festival arrived. Great souls — devotees from **Braj, Orissa, Bengal, and even South India** — began arriving on boats on the river, "like a transcendental armada." Everyone assembled.
- Only one illustrious personality was still missing: **Mother Janhava Mata**, the most esteemed Vaishnava at that time, and the special guest. **Santosh** had made a very special, beautiful, sturdy boat for her, waiting on the other side of the Padma River. Finally she arrived with her associates and took the boat across.
- There was an amazing reception — tumultuous kirtan, chanting and dancing, flower garlands, and arati. She arrived in the morning, and the kirtan went on all day and all the next night. ("We have our 24-hour kirtans now — a new thing in ISKCON, which we plan well ahead. This wasn't planned; they were just so happy to see her.")
- Santosh arranged it so nicely that every guest had a special room and a group of dedicated devotees to take care of them — to wash their clothes and clean their rooms. These servants were themselves great exalted souls, great Vaishnavas and Thakuras, yet took great pleasure in doing menial service. "It was like a competition of transcendental love."

## Installation of the Six Deities — Srinivas Bows to Every Vaishnava

- The next day the festival began with the installation of the six deities. The head priest was **Srinivas Acharya.**
- Before starting the abhishek — although he was a great Vaishnava and Thakura whom everyone worshiped — Srinivas went and bowed down at the feet of **every Vaishnava** who came to the festival (there were thousands of them), very humbly: "Prabhu, please accept my humble obeisances. Give me your mercy. I am a very fallen conditioned soul."
- The installation started with the abhishek — it took hours and hours (the abhishek, the dressing, the bhoga).

## Naming the Deities — Janhava Mata's Song

- Then Narottama came forth and gave names to each of the deities:
  - The deity found in the granary — **Gauranga.**
  - The deity of Krishna — **Vallabhi Kanta.**
  - Another — **Sri Krishna.**
  - Another — **Braja Mohan.**
  - Another — **Radha Raman.**
  - Another — **Radha Kanta.**
- And **Janhava Mata** made up a little shloka/song, very dear to the followers of Narottama Das Thakura, which everyone sang: **"Gauranga, Vallabhi Kanta; Sri Krishna, Braja Mohan; Radha Raman, He Radhe; Radha Kanta — Namaste."**

## Narottama's First Public Kirtan — Empowered by Janhava's Glance

- Then arati started; the deities were dressed; a huge arati. And who did they ask to sing? **Narottama.** He had not revealed his special type of kirtan yet, saving it for the right moment.
- He came out and began singing a new melody and dancing in a way no one had seen before. The thousands of Vaishnavas were mesmerized — "you could hear a pin drop" — watching Narottama alone chanting and dancing before his beloved lords.
- **Janhava** gazed at Narottama Das Thakura with such love and mercy that her glance empowered him. As a result, one of the greatest incidents in Vaishnava history took place.
- He began singing **shuddha-nama** — the pure chanting of the holy names — in such a melody that it would melt the heart of even a hard-hearted person, and with his particular type of dancing. He started very sweet and very slow, and gradually the Vaishnavas started moving, accompanying him, and trying to learn the dance.

## Raghunandana Thakur Worships the Kirtan Paraphernalia

- **Raghunandana Thakur** went into transcendental ecstasy, seeing the Yuga-dharma Sankirtan — the very dharma that would flood the entire earth and universe throughout Kali-yuga — manifesting before his eyes.
- He took some **chandan, incense, a lamp, and flowers**, and he went and worshiped **every kartala** in that kirtan (because it is **tadiya** — non-different from the Lord, part of His paraphernalia): offering the incense, the flower, the lamp, and putting chandan on every single pair of kartals. Then he worshiped all the **mridangas**, then all the **trumpets**, then all the **flags** — "all part of the paraphernalia of Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan."
- When that was done, he went and worshiped **every Vaishnava** present at the festival — with incense from the feet up to the body, then the lamp, then the flower, then chandan on the forehead of every devotee. And then they all fell at his feet. "There was this competition for service, for glorifying, for worship."

## The Meaning of Kirtan

- This kirtan is meant to be the mood of **loving exchanges** — between the devotees, between the spiritual master and his disciples, and with the Supreme Personality of Godhead. "This is kirtan — the highest ecstatic mood of devotional service, with all the emotions."
- **Jiva Goswami says in his Bhakti-sandarbha** that when kirtan is performed — even among neophyte devotees — because it is blessed, "even a neophyte devotee can experience deep spiritual emotion while having congregational chanting of the holy names." "The holy name is so merciful."
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakur** says that whenever he hears the chiming of the kartals and the playing of the mridanga, his bliss knows no bounds, and he runs — "from whoever he is" — to the kirtan. "Who will not come to the kirtan? This is our saving grace — kirtan."

## Prabhupada: "You Came Running"

- One time Prabhupada said to a group of disciples: **"My Guru Maharaj sent you all to me, to help me spread this ISKCON movement. You are all representatives of my Guru Maharaj — he sent you."**
- One day a devotee said, "Prabhupada, I'm very low. I committed so many sinful acts. You don't know how sinful I was." Prabhupada said: **"That's all right. But as soon as you heard the chanting of the holy name, you came running. Whatever you were — when you heard the chanting of the holy names, you Western boys came running. Therefore I have accepted you."**

## The Intensity We Must Have

- The devotees were honoring and glorifying each other; the emotions were coming forth. **Rupa Goswami** says: as young girls are attracted to young boys, and young boys to young girls — "we know what that means" — in the same way, "my Lord, with the same intensity, let me be attracted to you in a deep, transcendental, spiritual way."
- The boy-girl attraction in society is very prominent — "lust makes the world go round." But that same intensity, **dovetailed**, is how eager we have to be for Krishna consciousness — not some lackadaisical "How are you doing, man? Okay." "We have to come together and do these activities: hari-katha, kirtan, discussion, cleansing the heart, praying for these spiritual emotions. We can only do it with each other." "Many hands make small work" — it takes the congregational chanting of the holy names to achieve perfection in Krishna consciousness.

## Narottama's Ecstatic Kirtan and the Glories of Lord Chaitanya

- When Narottama was so surcharged by Janhava's glance, he began dancing like a madman. **Narottama-vilasa** describes how, in that kirtan, his face shone like millions of beautiful moons, his graceful limbs danced so ecstatically, and his teeth looked like rows of pearls. Everyone realized they had never heard this type of kirtan, these melodies, or this type of dancing before.
- In the middle of the kirtan Narottama began describing the glories of Lord Chaitanya: "My Prabhus, listen — who is this **Gauranga Mahaprabhu**, the Yuga-avatar? He has descended in the age of Kali to inundate the world with **prema.**" He described the *lilas* and the beauty of Lord Chaitanya.
- So expert was he in this *rasa* that he began describing the **mood of separation** from Lord Chaitanya. Narottama was always in the mood of separation (he had just missed Chaitanya's pastimes and was always lamenting), and according to our acharyas that is the highest mood — **"separation makes the heart grow fonder."**
- As he spoke of this mood, the kirtan became even more intense, the rhythm more powerful, and suddenly everyone in Keturi jumped up and danced like him. The intense feelings of separation from Lord Chaitanya became unbearable; everyone was crying. As **Gour Govinda Maharaj** said, it was almost as if they would give up their bodies in separation from the Lord.

## The Panchatattva Appear

- At the peak of that kirtan — that **muddha vipralambha-bhava** — suddenly **Lord Chaitanya appeared** and began dancing in the kirtan. The devotees, many of whom had never seen Lord Chaitanya (including Narottama), stood spellbound. The Supreme Personality of Godhead, the Yuga-avatar, the benedictor of love of God — "He was there. It wasn't a dream. It wasn't a vision. He *was* there." (Revealed now to Mother Janhava — "the secret was over.")
- Suddenly **Lord Nityananda** appeared and started dancing with Him. Then **Advaita Acharya** and all his sons — "seeing their father again" — then **Gadadhar** and **Srivas.** The kirtan went beyond any boundaries "that can be imagined by our tiny brains." Everyone saw it — "a rainstorm of transcendental bliss." Even villagers who happened to be walking by saw the **Panchatattva** dancing.
- Lord Chaitanya could not resist the loving devotion of Narottama Das Thakura. **Narottama induced the Lord to come from Goloka** — "Goloka Premdan." **Harinama Sankirtan has the power to attract Krishna Himself.**

## Holi and the Feast — Mother Janhava as Acharya and Mother

- The bliss went on for hours, and then suddenly (it being late) the Panchatattva disappeared. The devotees went mad looking for the object of their love; most fell to the ground unconscious.
- **Janhava** was inundated with grief, but somehow collected herself, and to get everything back on track she said, "Now let us perform Holi." She took the holy dyes and began sprinkling them on all the deities — blue, red, green, yellow.
- The devotees came back to life. Seeing the deities, they saw the Lord who had appeared before them as the deities: "My dear Lord, you're not a statue — you're directly the son of Maharaj Nanda." Then the devotees took the colors and threw them at each other in a mood of loving reciprocation. It is described that the whole sky became clouds of different beautiful colors — "not like the Holi they have today, where downtown you make a mess." It went on through the entire night, up to Mangala Arati.
- The devotees realized they were hungry — they hadn't eaten, so absorbed in the kirtan and katha that they forgot to eat and sleep. So **Mother Janhava**, the Supreme Ishwari, said, "Okay, gentlemen, I'll go and cook." She went to the kitchen and asked for volunteers; there were so many — hundreds of devotees helped her. She made up the plates for the deities and offered the prasad to the six deities herself, and it is described that she saw the deities eating and having fun.
- Then the devotees were all fed, then the servants, and finally she was the last one to take. "So she's a great acharya, and at the same time a mother to all the devotees."

## Mother Janhava and the Dacoits and Brahmanas on the Road Home

- After the festival, Mother Janhava went back to Braj, but promised the Keturi devotees she would come back.
- **The dacoits:** On the way she came again, and some dacoits heard she was coming. Knowing that, as a spiritual master, she must have much money — and envious that a woman was being worshiped — they plotted to steal her wealth and kill her. They waited until Mother Janhava and her entourage had taken rest one night under a tree, then hid about 40 yards away in the dark. When everyone was sound asleep, around 11:30 at night, they took out their lances, swords, and knives and began running toward her with raised weapons. They ran and ran and ran — but after eight hours the sun came up and they found themselves still in the same place. Realizing she was a supreme goddess, they put down their weapons, crawled on their hands and knees with tears in their eyes, begged forgiveness for their sinful mentality, and fell at her lotus feet: "Please accept us." She picked them up, embraced them, and accepted them as disciples then and there.
- **The Brahmanas:** A little further on the road were some "smarter" Brahmins who had also heard she was coming and performed a special ceremony to kill her, worshiping **Bhavani (Goddess Durga)** in her ferocious feature for the power to kill Mother. There were ten or twelve of them. That night Bhavani appeared in the dream of each and every one of them, threatening that if they touched a single hair on Mother's head, she would take her chopper, chop off their heads, and drink the blood from their torsos. They all woke, ran to where she was, and surrendered; she forgave them and initiated them.
- Therefore the acharya (Janhava) is described as **the compassionate energy of Lord Nityananda.**

## Closing

- "There are so many other pastimes — hundreds of thousands of volumes. I would love to speak all night," but the speaker did not sleep the previous night (for various reasons), had a long day, and a flight.
- "So tomorrow we can continue — a lot more nectar — with the pastimes of Srila Narottama Das Thakura and his transcendental associates."
- **"All glories to Sri Thakur Mahasaya — Narottama Das Thakura ki jaya!"**
