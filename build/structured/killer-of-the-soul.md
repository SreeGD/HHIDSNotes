## Opening — The Rare Value of Godbrother/Godsister Association

- The speaker expresses gratitude for the association of so many godbrothers and godsisters at **New Raman Reti (Alachua)**.
  - In the early days (the 1970s, in France, Germany, Italy, Spain) it was all godbrothers and godsisters — enlivening association.
  - Now, as the movement has grown and devotees have taken responsibilities worldwide, that association has become rare: "one or two maybe in Russia, one in China, one in Malaysia, zero in the Antarctic."
  - The unique feature of the New Raman Reti community is that so many godbrothers and godsisters live there. For someone who comes from "the frontiers of Krishna consciousness" off the beaten track, it is enlivening. "Thank you so much for your association."

## The Verses — Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya-lila 1 ("The Later Pastimes of Lord Chaitanya")

- Reading from **Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya-lila 1**, "The Later Pastimes of Lord Chaitanya" — three verses given (two without purports).
- **First verse** (the holy name's power): "*Tomara nama lana, tomara karila nindana... sei nama ha'ila tara muktira karana*" — even those who took Your holy name in order to blaspheme You, that very holy name became the cause of their deliverance.
- **Second verse** (Text/Translation 196): "*Jagai-Madhai haite koti-koti guna, adhama patita papi ami dui jana*" — **"We are millions and millions of times more inferior than Jagai and Madhai; we two are more degraded, fallen, and sinful than they."**
- **Third verse** (with purport): "*mleccha-jati mleccha-sevi kari mleccha-karma, go-brahmana-drohi-sange amara sangama*" — **"Actually we belong to the caste of meat-eaters, because we are servants of meat-eaters; indeed our activities are exactly like those of the meat-eaters. Because we always associate with such people, we are inimical toward the cows and the brahmanas."**

## The Purport — Two Kinds of Meat-Eaters and the Degradation of Leadership

- **Two kinds of meat-eaters:** one born into a family of meat-eaters, and one who learns to eat meat by associating with meat-eaters.
  - From **Sri Rupa and Sanatana Goswami** (formerly named Dabir Khas and Sakara Mallik) we learn how one takes on the character of a meat-eater simply by association.
- **India's leadership:** Presidential offices are occupied by many so-called brahmanas, yet the state maintains slaughterhouses and makes propaganda against Vedic civilization.
  - The first principle of Vedic civilization is the avoidance of **meat-eating and intoxication**.
  - By supporting cow-killing (for a fat salary) and not protesting, such so-called brahmanas are degraded to the platform of **mleccha** (a meat-eater) and **yavana** (one who has deviated from Vedic culture) — even though born in brahmana families.
- **The king/president as God's representative:** The ruler must be the representative of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
  - **Maharaja Yudhishthira**, when he ruled Bharata-varsha (formerly this entire planet, including all seas and land), took sanction from authorities like **Bhishmadeva and Lord Krishna**, and thus ruled according to religious principles.
  - Today's heads of state do not care for religious principles: if irreligious people vote for a bill against the shastra, it is passed, and the rulers become sinful by agreeing to such abominable activities — classing themselves among the mlecchas.

## Invocation and the Glory of Chaitanya-charitamrita

- Invocation verses recited: **"om ajnana-timirandhasya jnananjana-shalakaya..."** (pranama to the guru who opens the eyes blinded by ignorance); **"sri-chaitanya-mano-'bhishtam sthapitam yena bhu-tale, svayam rupah kada mahyam dadati sva-padantikam"** (pranama to Srila Rupa Goswami, who established the Lord's desire on earth); **"jaya sri-krishna-chaitanya prabhu-nityananda, sri-advaita gadadhara srivasadi-gaura-bhakta-vrinda"** (the Panca-tattva mantra); the Hare Krishna maha-mantra; and **"vanchha-kalpatarubhyash cha kripa-sindhubhya eva cha..."** (pranama to the Vaishnavas, who are wish-fulfilling trees and oceans of mercy).
- The **Chaitanya-charitamrita** was the favorite book of **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**, who lectured from it more than any other because of its profound importance among the Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
- It records the pastimes of **Sri Krishna Chaitanya Mahaprabhu**, the *Yuga-avatara* and the combined form of the divine couple Radha and Krishna — **"Sri-krishna-chaitanya radha-krishna nahe anya"** (Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is none other than the combined form of Radha and Krishna). He appeared to flood the entire planet with **Krishna-prema**, and is described by Rupa Goswami as the most merciful incarnation of the Lord.

## Rupa and Sanatana's Humility — Nitya-siddhas Showing Us the Danger of Association

- In this verse, Rupa and Sanatana Goswami are *apparently* implicated in sinful activity by bad association — but this is an expression of their **Vaishnava humility**.
- They are **nitya-siddhas** — eternally perfected souls who never come under the influence of material nature, even while within the material world. They are **jivan-mukta** — **"jivan-muktah sa ucyate"** (a liberated soul even while living in this material existence).
- **The lotus-flower analogy:** In lakes in India, Malaysia, and Indonesia, the lotus grows in the water with its roots deeply embedded in the mud, yet the flower itself always stays a few inches above the water and never really touches it (perhaps the leaves touch, but not the flower).
  - Like Jesus said, "to be in this world but not of this world." A devotee lives in the material world but his heart is with Krishna — **"you go where your heart takes you"** — so in due course he returns to the spiritual world.

## Two Types of Liberated Souls Who Descend — and Prabhupada's Special Position

- Referencing a lecture by **Gour Govinda Maharaja** in Bhubaneswar: there are two types of liberated souls (nitya-siddhas) who come to the material world:
  1. One who comes of his own accord — like **Jesus Christ**.
  2. One who comes on the express wish/instruction of the Lord — like **Srila Prabhupada**, who said, "I did not want to come to this nasty place, but Krishna ordered me: 'You go down there and write books on the science of Krishna consciousness, and I will arrange everything else.' I am not inclined toward austerity."
  - "Many are called, but few are chosen."
- **Prabhupada's position is very special** because he came to continue the very mission of the Lord in the Lord's absence — founder-acharya of ISKCON, which has spread love of God worldwide.
  - **Business/eldest-son analogy:** When you grow old and want to hand over a business you gave your heart and soul to, you don't give it to just anyone — you hope your eldest son will take it over (as in India, "Bharat and Sons").
  - **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** revived the Krishna conscious movement, which the material energy had covered for 400 years after Chaitanya Mahaprabhu; he is the pioneer of Krishna consciousness in the modern era. Worried about who would carry on, he made a specific plea to Chaitanya Mahaprabhu to send an intimate associate — an "aide-de-camp" (only a personal associate can carry on the mission).

## Anxiety of the Older Generation — The Transcendental Baton

- The older generation is always in anxiety that the movement will continue. The speaker notes he and his godbrothers/godsisters are now the **"troisième âge"** (French, "the third age") — officially senior citizens with perhaps 10–15 years left. Even at 50 or 60 they wonder how it will all go on — an anxiety they did not previously consider.
- In the mood of the acharya (though not with the same purity), they hope the next generation will "carry the baton."
  - **Relay-race analogy:** The baton passes from the first runner to the second, third, and fourth — the fourth being the **anchor man**, the fastest, who catches up any gaps. **Guru-parampara** (disciplic succession) is a transcendental baton handed to each generation of Vaishnavas; every generation historically has its ups and downs.
  - They hope the **guru-kulis** ("guru coolies," an affectionate name) will take the baton in a few short years and carry on the missionary activity.
- **This is a missionary movement in essence.** Prabhupada said Krishna consciousness is blissful when it is expanding. When Prabhupada traveled the world, he did not ask, "How advanced are you in prema?" (that is confidential) — he asked: **How many books have been distributed? How many temples have you opened? How many devotees have you made?** This is the essence of the movement.

## The Crown Jewel — Humility — and the Goswamis' Eternal Identities

- Rupa and Sanatana were entrusted by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — his direct disciples, trained specifically to go to **Vrindavana** (our home seat), discover the lost holy places of Krishna's pastimes, establish Deity worship, and write books on the science of Krishna consciousness.
- Yet they display the **crown jewel of all Vaishnava qualities — humility**. Despite their paramount greatness historically in ISKCON's lineage, they consider themselves the most fallen; painfully, they state "we are fallen because we have become implicated in sinful activity by bad association."
- They can never actually be touched by material energy, because in the spiritual world:
  - **Rupa Goswami is Rupa Manjari** — the close associate of **Lalita Devi** (the guru of the gopis), Radharani's chosen friend and advisor to the gopis.
  - **Sanatana Goswami is Rati Manjari (also called Lavanga Manjari)** — Rupa Manjari's closest friend, and considered a personal extension of the body of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.
  - Cited from **Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika**: "*sa rupa-manjari-prakhya... rati-manjari... lavanga-manjari...*" — Rupa Manjari's closest friend, known as Rati Manjari and Lavanga Manjari, appeared in the pastimes of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu as Sri Sanatana Goswami.
- These liberated, ecstatic devotees are never touched or affected by matter. For our benefit — to show conditioned souls the danger of bad association — the Lord placed them in that situation, just as He did with **Prahlada Maharaja** or **Arjuna**. We should not misunderstand.

## Historical Background — Turbulent Times and Employment Under Hussain Shah

- Historically, the Goswamis' family hailed from **Karnataka** in South India, then moved to Bengal (in what is now **Bangladesh**). They were born into turbulent times.
  - **What Vaishnava is not born into turbulent times?** We think now is the worst point in history (America's wars in Afghanistan and Iraq), but history repeats itself. The speaker recalls the 1960s — the Vietnam War, the protests. It is like that every time.
  - Their turbulence: a society dominated by **Muslims**, who had conquered most of North India (and parts of the South) and were imposing their beliefs and culture on the Hindus. It was said that if a Muslim simply sprinkled water on you, you became a Muslim — not found in shastra, but a traditional practice by which many were converted (all people of India were originally of Vedic culture; converts were then ostracized by the orthodox brahmin community).
- Despite this, Rupa and Sanatana excelled in both material and spiritual education, becoming leaders in their brahminical communities — yet they became employed in the service of **Hussain Shah** and thus associated with meat-eaters.

## The Story of the Mason — How Sanatana Was Made Chief Minister

- Hussain Shah ordered his principal mason to construct a memorial to one of his battle victories (the speaker thinks at Vidyanagar, where he conquered a Hindu king), somewhere in the district of Nadia.
- When completed (a six-story, impressive structure), the king climbed to the top with his ministers and the mason and praised it. Out of **humility**, the chief mason said, "Actually, sir, it's not such a great thing; I could have done better."
  - The king became furious: "You could have done better? This means you're saving your talent for my successor!" He threw the mason off the top — "splat." Very cruel.
- Now without a chief mason, and with more victories to commemorate, the king told his chief minister: go to **Ramkeli** (famous as the home of India's best masons) and bring back the best mason — "and if you're not back in 24 hours, I'll have your head."
- The nervous chief minister went in disguise. Unable to decide, with time ticking, he encountered **Sanatana Goswami** in the market.
  - Prabhupada tells us the Goswamis were perfected devotees yet endowed with many material qualities. **Sanatana Goswami was a great diplomat** — intelligent, expert in politics and diplomacy (he later escaped from prison), and knew many languages and sciences (mathematics, astronomy, astrology). The Lord bestows different abilities on His devotees.
  - By his special discernment, Sanatana studied the disguised minister's character and understood he was a big minister in disguise, probably looking for a mason. He approached: **"Sir, have you found your mason?"** The shocked minister ("How did you know?") concluded, "You're so intelligent, you must be the best mason in town," bound him, and took him to Navadvipa to the king.
- The king could immediately tell from Sanatana's uncallused hands, free of a worker's marks, that he was no mason. Hearing that the minister took this most intelligent man for a mason, the king said, "My chief minister is such a fool," and **cut off the minister's head**. To Sanatana he said, "You shall be my chief minister, because you're so sharp and intelligent." That is how Sanatana became chief minister in Hussain Shah's government.

## Sanatana's Refusal, the Threat, and Forced Association

- Understanding the **power of association** — that a moment's association with the material energy can be a diversion for a devotee, while a moment's association with devotees can be a stroke of good fortune for a conditioned soul — Sanatana immediately refused: "I'm not interested in your politics, diplomacy, and ruling this country."
- Hussain Shah threatened: if Sanatana would not become chief minister, Rupa Goswami head of the treasury/finance, and their nephew **Anupama** in charge of minting coins, he would **kill 10,000 brahmanas within 24 hours**.
- Considering time, place, and circumstance, Sanatana agreed. Thus Rupa, Sanatana, and Anupama became employed in the association of the Muslims — technically in the association of "mlecchas and yavanas," and considered themselves most unfortunate.

## What "Mleccha/Yavana" Means — Meat-Eating as Violence

- **Mleccha** means a meat-eater; **yavana** means one not leading a pure lifestyle as described in Vedic culture. Mleccha-association is especially condemned because meat-eating is one of the **greatest sins** in Vedic culture, owing to the violence involved. A primary principle of Vedic culture is **ahimsa** (non-violence).
- Killing is never pleasant. Modern society hides the killing process, but the videos exist — **PETA** (a society against cruelty to animals) on YouTube shows horrific footage of how animals are killed. "If people had to kill for their own meat, the amount of meat-eating would be greatly reduced."
- **The McDonald's advertisement** (about 20 years ago): it showed children going into the garden and picking hamburgers from trees — children being so influenced they'd think meat comes "from the trees, like fruit." If people had to kill the animal themselves, most could not do it — but now it's all industrialized.

## The Meaning of "Meat-Eating" — The Soul Ripped From the Body

- To eat meat means that the **soul is ripped from the body** of that animal (or human), and no living entity wants to experience this trauma.
- Therefore the **Bible** concludes: **"Do unto others as you would like them to do unto you"** — the Golden Rule. Are they applying it? Every living entity should be allowed to live out its natural, destined life.

## Indira Gandhi and the Karma of Rulers

- Prabhupada notes India's so-called leadership sanctioning animal slaughter. This was really introduced by **Indira Gandhi**, who officially made it law that **Gomata (the mother cow)** could be slaughtered and even transported overseas.
  - Prabhupada said the Vedic culture was destroyed more by Indira Gandhi than by the Muslims and the British combined, because of the laws she introduced.
  - Facing overpopulation, she introduced a sterilization program (making men impotent by an operation — a vasectomy); her people had quotas. Because of the quotas, they went to the holy places and performed the operation on the **sadhus** (easy to grab), including many holy men in **Vrindavana**. She also promoted cow slaughter and taxed liquor sales (so the government profited and promoted liquor).
  - "It's no surprise how she died — live by the gun, you die by the gun." She was murdered by her own security.
- **The king's karma:** In Vedic culture the ruler receives **one-sixth of the subjects' pious activities** (if he performs yajnas, serves brahmanas, protects cows, etc.), but also **one-sixth of their sinful reactions** if he encourages sinful activity. So Prabhupada said it is no surprise that in modern days politicians sometimes die violent deaths.

## The Body as a Corpse — The Doctor and the Morgue

- Prabhupada put meat-eating in perspective: **what is a body? A body is a corpse** — the word itself brings chills.
- **Story of Dr. Sunil Mohan** (South Africa), a famous ENT specialist and EMT friend of the speaker: as a student in Johannesburg he got ahead of everyone by begging the mortician to let him practice on bodies in the **morgue** at night, performing extremely difficult nose operations and making his mistakes on corpses — now one of the famous doctors in the world "because he cheated a little bit."
  - He also explained that medical students were each given **one corpse for a whole year** — dissecting it head to toe (anatomy), returning it to the freezer each night. "This is how they do things in the medical world."
  - The speaker's reflection: "What if somebody wanted to eat one of those things?" A dead body is a corpse — so people are eating corpses.

## The Provision for the Attached — The "Mamsa" Mantra

- Vedic culture provides a means for everyone to make a progressive step, from wherever they start (lowest, halfway, or advanced). For one attached to eating flesh: wait until the animal dies naturally, then eat.
- For the impatient, there was a strictly-restricted provision: take the goat, and whisper a mantra in its ear. The speaker doesn't know/care to memorize the shloka, but its essence: **"In this lifetime I am killing you, with the agreement that in the next lifetime you will eat/kill me."**
  - The **bija** (seed) of that mantra was **"mamsah."** Just as our Gayatri mantra has a bija ("aim"), this mantra's seed was "mamsah." **Meat in Sanskrit is "mamsa" — "mam sah" means "me, you"** ("me he/you") — i.e., "this lifetime I kill you, next time you will kill me."
- Chanting a mantra brings one to one's senses and wakes one up (**"jiva jago"** — Hare Krishna, waking up). Eventually the person comes to his senses: "Is it really worth it to eat the corpse and then next lifetime be killed?"
- The Vedic scripture states that if one partakes particularly of the **cow**, it is not just one lifetime: **Manu-samhita** says that for as many hairs as there are on the body of that cow, you must take birth and be slaughtered in the same way. Cows are quite hairy — "that's really *mamsa* — me, you — not just one birth." The speaker wonders where **Colonel Sanders** is now — "one of billions of chickens."

## Why the Sadhu Must Speak Strongly — And the Conspiracy of Slaughter

- **Sadhu** means one who cuts our material attachments by words, because our attachments are overwhelming. He cuts through the ignorance by **divya-jnana** (transcendental knowledge). Sometimes we must speak strongly.
- **The boy in Durban:** He said, "My hands are clean, Swami — I'm not killing the animals, I'm just eating them later" (the speaker could see his fangs).
  - The speaker replied: **"You're part of a conspiracy."** Like in a crime — someone makes a plan, hires a killer, buys the gun, and the killer shoots. When it comes to court, not only the trigger-man but the one who came up with the idea, the one who drove the van, and the one who disposed of the evidence are all implicated. So in the business of slaughter, everyone who participates becomes implicated and suffers.
- A **Vaishnava's heart is pained** to see people suffering; he doesn't think "I'm not part of that," but "what about all my brothers and sisters?" His happiness is in seeing them become free from this ignorant way of life. Though aloof from it (it is our dark past), we remain very much involved because we see the suffering of humanity.
- **Global karma:** Prabhupada, in a purport of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, attributes **World War I and World War II** not to the actions of Nazi Germany and Mussolini's Italy, but to the **global karma incurred by the mass slaughter of animals** organized in the 20th–21st centuries (previously the slaughter was not on such a mass, organized scale). This pains us — so we don't just hide in our communities or go to the Himalayas; discussing Prahlada Maharaja, we discuss these things soberly, knowing the alternative.

## The Positive Alternative — Krishna Consciousness

- The alternative is Krishna consciousness: **no matter how sinful you were, if you chant Hare Krishna** — Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare — there is hope.
- The sublime message is really the **Vrindavana pastimes of Radha and Krishna and Their associates** — that is what Krishna consciousness is truly about. It is not merely cleansing the heart of unwanted things; it means becoming involved one day in the beautiful pastimes of the Lord.
- To bring people to accept and practice this philosophy, we must try to **close down the slaughterhouses and places of intoxication, prostitution, and gambling** — not by violent means (as some do), but by purifying the hearts of the conditioned souls.

## "Atmahani" — The Killer of the Soul

- One who partakes of flesh to maintain his body is called **"atmahani"** (a term from the **Sri Ishopanishad** purport). Prabhupada said a meat-eater is called *atmahani* — "not a sweet name" — it means **the killer of the soul**.
- One who partakes of meat, fish, or eggs is the killer of the soul. Of course you cannot literally kill the soul, but **you can kill the soul's chances for self-realization** — the golden opportunity for Krishna, especially in this age when the Lord's mercy is available. Meat-eating is so polluting that it covers the consciousness.
- **Illustration — "you are not the body":** We never say "I hand, I arm, I body"; we say "**my** hand, my arm, my body" — so I am the *possessor* of the body. Who is the "me"? Tell this to someone sharp and pure and he says, "Aha, that makes sense." But tell it to an *atmahani* and he says, "I don't understand — it's so simple I can't catch it."
- Therefore, along with the purificatory process of chanting Hare Krishna, at initiation we agree to the four regulative principles: **no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, and no gambling** — because those things cloud the consciousness.
  - **Fog analogy:** The beautiful sun is shining, but when the fog comes in you can't see its beauty. Krishna consciousness is just common sense. Prabhupada said it is **"simple for the simple-minded and crooked for the crooked-minded."** It is obvious that we are spirit souls and that there is God — but for the *atmahani* civilization, "duh, we can't understand."

## Change Is Always Possible — Bell Metal into Gold

- Even one implicated in these activities — not just this lifetime but for many lifetimes — can have a change of heart, all by the mercy of **Gauranga**.
- As **Sanatana Goswami** said: **as bell metal can be changed into gold by a chemical process, so a fallen conditioned soul addicted to sinful activity can have a change of heart** — he can rise to the stage of brahmana, and even become more than a brahmana, a **Vaishnava**, by proper training, a Krishna conscious lifestyle, and **harinama-diksha** (initiation).
- There is always good news, always the positive alternative, in Krishna consciousness. As much as we decry the material energy and the materialistic way of life, we always leave the audience with hope.

## Contrast — Jean-Paul Sartre and the Missing Light

- Unlike the devotees, **Jean-Paul Sartre**, the French philosopher: reading his works, one might think he had read the *Bhagavatam* — he had the whole material energy figured out (how people suffer). But he didn't know the "bright light," the light at the end of the tunnel; he didn't know there was a positive alternative.
- The Gita's answer he lacked: **"paras tasmat tu bhavo 'nyo 'vyakto 'vyaktat sanatanah"** (*Bhagavad-gita 8.20*) — beyond this miserable world, which is sometimes created and destroyed, there is another eternal world, full of knowledge, that remains when all this is destroyed.
- Not knowing this, Sartre's philosophy (his *siddhanta*, his conclusion) took him to the point of taking his life — "take a gun and kill himself." One of the greatest philosophers of all time, brilliant — "if only he had met the devotees." We are fortunate that Prabhupada came and gave us hope.

## The Principle of Association — The Chameleon

- Rupa and Sanatana said they were "fallen" because the *association* of such persons was detrimental to spiritual progress — not that they were actually engaged. The principle: **we take on the qualities of those we associate with.**
- **The chameleon analogy:** When the speaker was young you could still mail-order lizards, snakes, frogs, and spiders. His brother **Roger Kumar** was fascinated by spiders, snakes, and scorpions and saved up to send away — every two weeks a package would arrive (a tarantula, a black scorpion from Ethiopia, snakes) to their mother's horror.
  - They also got **chameleon lizards**. As kids they were fascinated: put the chameleon on a green paper and it turns green; on reddish-brown paper it turns reddish-brown; back on green, green again — for hours. We are like that — we take on the color of our association.

## Spirit Is More Powerful Than Matter — Yet We Can Be Overwhelmed

- We are tiny spirit souls (a "touch of the shakti"), the **same quality as God but not the same quantity**. The all-powerful Lord is never influenced by association, but though we are spiritual we can be overwhelmed.
- **Spirit is more powerful than matter:** The soul is one ten-thousandth the tip of a hair in dimension, yet it manipulates the huge body of an elephant (or a large human) — because spirit is more powerful than matter.
  - Krishna says: **"one who does good, my friend, is never overcome by evil"** (*Bhagavad-gita 6.40*) — spiritual goodness.
- **Yet the material energy is so powerful it can overwhelm:** **"daivi hy esha guna-mayi mama maya duratyaya"** (*Bhagavad-gita 7.14*) — this divine energy of mine, consisting of the three modes of material nature, is very difficult to overcome. Even an advanced transcendentalist, a yogi or jnani, has the odds against him. Only for a **devotee** are the odds in favor, because he has the mercy of **Hari, guru, and Vaishnava**. But even a devotee can be overwhelmed — this is the fear of the Vaishnava.

## The Fear of a Devotee — Prahlada Before Nrisimhadeva

- This fear is expressed by **Prahlada Maharaja** (who of course can't actually be overwhelmed, but speaks for our benefit) before **Nrisimhadeva**.
  - The Lord was still in an angry mood, having just killed Hiranyakashipu, wearing a garland not of roses but of the **intestines** of Hiranyakashipu, eyes red with anger, wedge-like ears pointed, mane spattered with blood — fearsome to non-devotees.
  - But "love is in the eyes of the beholder": Prahlada saw Him as his savior, in a mood of loving devotion, crying tears of ecstasy — "You're so beautiful."
- Prahlada said (paraphrasing his prayer): **"I am not afraid of Your fearsome tongue, Your shrill voice which sends elephants fleeing to distant places, Your blood-soaked mane, Your garland of intestines, or Your sharp nails which have bifurcated the body of my father. What I am afraid of is that, by the influence of the material energy, I may forget Your lotus feet."** This is the fear of a devotee — if any fear is left in our hearts, it should be this type.
- Prabhupada told the speaker's godbrother **Kulashekhara Prabhu** in London: **"Your problem is you're not afraid of maya."**
- Before leaving Los Angeles (Rukmini-Dvarakadhisha) to spend time in India, Prabhupada prayed a long time. Asked what he was praying for, Prabhupada said, **"I was praying not to fall into maya"** — again, as an example for us to follow.
- If demigods, so advanced yet maintaining some material desires, can be overwhelmed, what about "us tiny, puny earthlings"? So a devotee always prays not to be overwhelmed — but there is nothing to fear if you know where to take shelter.

## Take Shelter in the Greatness of the Ocean — The Crabs at Stinson Beach

- **Story at Stinson Beach** (the speaker's old surfing haunt): Prabhupada was walking with disciples. There was a colony of crabs, and suddenly they all exited "left" into the ocean (crabs move left or right, backwards or forwards).
- Prabhupada asked his **Bhaktivedanta Institute** scientists why the crabs went into the ocean. **Bhakti-svarupa Damodara** gave a technical answer — the crabs' senses feel the movement of feet on the ground transferred through electrical impulses and chemicals in the sand to the brain.
- Prabhupada said: **"No — because they sense that danger is coming, and they are very small, so they know they can take shelter in the greatness of the ocean."** "Sometimes the truth is so simple it just knocks you over."
- In the same way, we are very small and can be covered by the material energy — or we can take shelter in the source of the material energy, **Krishna**. "All that is material and all that is spiritual is coming from me" — the material energy has a purpose but is controlled by the Lord.
- The second part of the verse (*BG 7.14*): **"mam eva ye prapadyante mayam etam taranti te"** — this divine energy is very difficult to overcome, **but one who takes shelter of Me can easily overcome it.**

## End on a High Note — The Positive Alternative

- Like the performing arts, one takes the audience through different moods — down and then up — but the art is always to **end on a high note.**
- The high note: there is a positive alternative. Though we can be overwhelmed and spend millions of lifetimes aimlessly wandering — **"brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva"** (*Chaitanya-charitamrita* — wandering through the universes, a fortunate soul...) — our destiny can be changed in the association of the Lord and His devotees. Falling, falling, falling... then you meet the devotees, meet your guru, take shelter of the Lord, and start going up. "That's Krishna consciousness — that's where the smile begins."
- **The reporter in Poland:** She said, "You guys have an answer for everything — do you have any problems in life? I won't believe you unless you tell me one." The speaker: "Yes, our problem is we're smiling so much that sometimes our jaws ache."

## This Is a Dangerous Place — Distraction and Being Swept Back Out

- This is a very dangerous place — believe the shastra: **"padam padam yad vipadam na tesham"** (*Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.14.58*) — danger at every step. There are accidents, disease, thieves — but the most dangerous thing is to **forget Krishna** and get entangled again in the network of material existence.
- We can be distracted and lose sight of the "golden" goal, swept back into the ocean:
  - **Sri Lanka tsunami:** People stood on the shore wanting to see what a tsunami was, and the ocean knocked them off their feet and pulled them out. That can happen at any moment.
  - **The driver and the fly:** The speaker sent a trusted driver (a good driver, no parking ticket in 20 years) to Paris for a shipment of sankirtan books. He crashed the car, the books burned up, and the speaker visited him in the hospital. How? "There was a fly in the Volkswagen van, and the car went off the cliff." A single little fly caused it.
  - **Ajamila:** A *pakka* brahmana boy, walking from home toward the forest, happened to see a shudra couple embracing (common today — worse on billboards). Never having experienced such a thing, his mind got diverted, and eventually he fell away from his brahminical life into sinful activity — from one little deviation.
- Just as we were "swept off our feet" into Prabhupada's wave of mercy by some small incident (a Harinam party, receiving a book, coming to the temple), we could be swept back out into the ocean. How careful we must be.

## Therefore, Live in the Association of Devotees

- Therefore we live in the association of devotees, and every morning we pray from the heart — and we cannot make exceptions ("except this one, and that one"). **Any devotee, wherever they are, their association is more valuable than millions and millions of non-devotees.**
- We must be reminded of this because **familiarity breeds contempt**, especially in big communities — we get used to each other and can become overwhelmed, not because of them but because of our own devious consciousness. We must always keep things in proper perspective.
- Prabhupada said: **you cannot become Krishna conscious outside the association of devotees.** "Outside/inside" doesn't necessarily mean physically in the community — you can live miles away, but if you consider the devotee community your family, you are safe. If someone thinks they can become Krishna conscious outside the association of devotees, **"they're hallucinating."**
  - We must identify with the "body" — the **International Society for Krishna Consciousness**. Prabhupada said he created this movement **for association**, because of the dangerous nature of the material world. We should not take that for granted; we should worship that principle — the association of devotees is our way of coming into the shelter of the ocean, the shelter of Krishna's lotus feet.

## The Strength of Association — Both Ways

- By that association, even if you don't want to become Krishna conscious, you are "forced to" — it is that strong.
- But it works both ways: a very strong devotee who associates intimately with non-devotees can fall down — **even a maha-bhagavata can fall down by hearing the philosophy of Shankaracharya** (Mayavada). So, however advanced we may or may not be, we need the association of the Vaishnavas.
- Just a little association can give us perfection — what to speak of living in a greater community of 300 members, where our chances of becoming Krishna conscious are better. So we should live **"packed up tight."**
  - **Russia example:** It is packed up tight there — a room meant for three has 20 brahmacharis sleeping in a line on the floor (one turns over, everyone turns); a class in such a room may have four or five hundred people.
  - It is less so at Alachua because of the beautiful spread-out land, but in principle we want to be packed up, giving our allegiance to Prabhupada and his movement, which he described as **his body**.

## Tolerating Friction and Purifying the Heart

- If there are disturbances, just tolerate — there will be friction because we are not pure yet, so there will be some friction of natures.
- **Chanakya Pandita** says: like a clap of thunder in a storm, like two rams butting horns, like the funeral of a sage in the forest, and like a fight between husband and wife — they all start out in grand fashion but the outcome is very insignificant. We may have our differences, but we don't differ on the general underlying principles — the philosophy and practice of Krishna consciousness.
- So tolerate, and purify ourselves of our own **anarthas** by: every morning offering obeisances to the illustrious Vaishnavas in whose association we live, and by chanting, dancing, having festivals, and taking prasadam together. The combined effect purifies the heart very quickly — to the point that **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati said: "I see no reason why all my disciples cannot go back to Godhead in this one lifetime."** That is possible, especially if we come together and have **kirtan** — that's the essence.

## Closing Verse and Jayas

- The great devotee **Kaviratna** concluded (verse in the **Padyavali**): **"One who daily sings the glories of Yashoda's son Krishna — which are as cooling as sandalwood and camphor — is not troubled by the days of Kali-yuga; for him, at every step, there is a torrential flood of the sweetest nectar."**
- Closing jayas: *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita ki jaya! Sri Vaishnava-sanga ki jaya! New Raman Reti dhama ki jaya! Gaura-bhakta-vrinda ki jaya!*
