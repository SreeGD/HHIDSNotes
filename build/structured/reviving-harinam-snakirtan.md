## Introduction: Why Harinam Sankirtan Matters

- Spoken as a short talk at the Russian Festival on the importance of **Harinam Sankirtan**.
- This subject is very important because Harinam Sankirtan is the very means by which we advertise our **International Society for Krishna Consciousness** and the process for becoming God-conscious in this age.
- We are all, technically, followers of **Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu**.
  - Although our ultimate goal is to serve the divine couple, **Radha and Krishna**, in the groves of Vrindavan, no one can actually understand the divine couple — or the process of achieving love for Them — without the grace of Lord Chaitanya.
  - This is one reason that, before chanting the **Maha Mantra** (*Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare*), we first invoke the mercy of Lord Chaitanya and the Pancha Tattva by chanting the **Pancha Tattva mantra**.

---

## Lord Chaitanya, the Yuga Avatar, and the Process for This Age

- Lord Chaitanya is the **yuga avatar** — the incarnation of Krishna who delivers the *dharma* for this age, the **yuga dharma of chanting Hare Krishna**.
- Therefore all devotees of Krishna, regardless of which particular *sampradaya* they follow, should actually be following Lord Chaitanya.
- **Prabhupada's principle:** "If many instructions are given, the most important instruction is the last one."
  - God has appeared in many ages and given many instructions, but His most recent, last incarnation — Lord Chaitanya — is the most important, and everyone should follow Him.
- It has been predicted (whether in *shastra* or by one of our acharyas) that in this age, eventually **all the sampradayas will unite under the banner of Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement.**
- As the yuga avatar giving the process for self-realization in this age, Lord Chaitanya had a specific way of delivering His message of Godhead: **through Harinam Sankirtan.**
  - Every day, Lord Chaitanya would rise early with His devotees and followers. They would gather the drums, the cymbals (*kartals*), bugles, flags, and banners, and go throughout the towns and villages of **Nadia**, chanting Hare Krishna.
  - For scholars, Mayavadis, and logicians, Lord Chaitanya would meet one-on-one and discuss.
- But most people are not renunciates, logicians, or yogis. Most people are common people.
  - Intellectuals form only a very small part — maybe one percent — of society.
  - What about the rest of the people, the "you-and-me's," common people? We too are Krishna's devotees, deep down in our hearts.
  - What process did Lord Chaitanya have to attract the mass of people to Krishna consciousness?

---

## The Meaning of "Harinama" and the Birth of Street Chanting

- **"Harer nama harer nama harer namaiva kevalam / kalau nasty eva nasty eva nasty eva gatir anyatha"** — quoted from the **Brihan-naradiya Purana**: in the age of Kali there is no other way, no other way, no other way to become self-realized and God-conscious than chanting the holy name of Hari.
- **Harinama** means *Hari* (God) and *nama* (name) — literally, chanting the name of God. It means "He who takes away all inauspiciousness."
  - In practice we use "Harinama" to mean going out on the streets and chanting: "Let's go on Harinama" — gathering the drums, cymbals, and banners and singing on the streets.
  - But strictly, *Harinama* means the name of Hari; it does not necessarily mean congregational chanting.
- **Before Lord Chaitanya's appearance:** devotees had their bead bags, sat in the jungle, and chanted Hare Krishna. There was no congregational chanting — no groups going on the streets chanting and dancing.
- It was **Lord Chaitanya and His followers who introduced this mass chanting of Hare Krishna on the streets.**
  - When Lord Chaitanya first started chanting, He chanted with His devotees in the house of **Srivas Thakur** (Srivas-angan). It was a private affair at night.
  - Because of the Muslim government, things were restricted and had to be kept somewhat secret, so they chanted with the doors locked and the windows closed.
  - But out of His infinite compassion for all fallen souls, Lord Chaitanya decided to take the Harinama out of Srivas Thakur's house and onto the streets.

---

## A Spiritual Revolution: A Sannyasi Singing and Dancing in the Streets

- This was like a spiritual revolution. Nobody did things like that — especially sannyasis.
- **What a sannyasi was expected to do:** stay in the ashram, study the shastras, practice penance and austerity, speak on the scriptures, sometimes travel to holy places, engage in debate about the Absolute Truth, eat very little, sleep very little, and keep practically no material comforts.
- Suddenly the people of Navadvip and Nadia saw these devotees — and later a sannyasi, because Mahaprabhu took sannyasa — singing and dancing, laughing, rolling on the ground with tears flowing from His eyes.
  - They asked, "What kind of sannyasi is this?" They criticized Him as a sentimental person gone a fool, or worse, someone gone crazy.
  - When Lord Chaitanya passed through **Benares (Kashi)**, the strict Mayavadi sannyasis heard about Him and wrote Him off as a madman: "What kind of sannyasi is singing and dancing in the streets and not sitting in the ashram studying Vedanta?"
- Little did they know He wasn't really a sannyasi — He was the **supreme enjoyer, God Himself**, mercifully in the disguise of a sannyasi, distributing the yuga dharma.

---

## A Process for Everyone — and the Persecution It Provoked

- The people loved it. Here was a process they could relate to and by which they could make spiritual advancement — not just the 1% who went to the mountains or lived in ashrams.
- Now, all of a sudden, everybody — **man, woman, child, fool, dog** — could make tangible advancement and actually achieve perfection.
- So many people joined the Sankirtan movement. **"Power to the people."**
- Mahaprabhu was heavily criticized — by the Mayavadis, the smarta brahmanas, the envious and jealous, and even the Muslim government became involved.
  - As a result, some of His followers were actually persecuted.
  - **Haridas Thakur** was thrown in jail for the very serious "crime" of singing Hare Krishna in the streets.
- **Summary of the history:** Harinam Sankirtan began 500 years ago with the advent of Lord Chaitanya. It was — and to a certain degree still is — one of the principal methods of advertising the teachings of Lord Chaitanya, which now manifest as the International Society for Krishna Consciousness.

---

## Srila Prabhupada Brings Sankirtan to the West

- **Srila Prabhupada** — Lord Chaitanya's emissary, disciple of **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**, and Lord Chaitanya's emissary for spreading Krishna consciousness all over the world — spread the teachings this same way when he came to the West: he had his followers go out on the streets and chant.
- **But in the very beginning there was no Harinam.** The only preacher was Prabhupada, because no one else was qualified.
  - A few boys joined him in the Bowery, but it wasn't clear whether they were serious about Krishna consciousness or about continuing their materialistic ways.

### Story: The "Do-Not" Shop

- One time after a lecture, Prabhupada took a little stroll for fresh air, walking a bit down from **Matchless Gifts at 26 Second Avenue**. There was a shop selling donuts (little round pastries with holes, chocolate, sugar) and coffee.
- Some of Prabhupada's followers, who lived with him and had attended the lecture, had gone there for a coffee break and a cigarette break.
- Prabhupada stopped and asked, "What is this shop?" The devotee said, "It's a donut shop."
- Prabhupada said: **"No, it's a *do-not* shop. Do not be my student and attend these places where you're drinking coffee and smoking cigarettes and talking *prajalpa*. This is not Krishna consciousness."**
- But Prabhupada had to tolerate it, because those were the only people he had.

### Only One Preacher; Word-of-Mouth Beginnings

- Only a few people joined in the beginning. Prabhupada couldn't send them out preaching — they didn't yet represent the movement — so he had to train them up.
- For the first few years there was no Sankirtan, no one going on Harinam, no one distributing books. There was just one preacher: **His Divine Grace Srila A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada.**
- People came to his lectures by word of mouth: "Did you hear there's a really cool Swami preaching on the Lower East Side about mystical things from India? And he cooks good food. It's a good place to come down from an LSD trip after staying up all night."
  - The hippies would take LSD at night, and as they were coming down from their hallucinations, they'd go to the morning program, stare at Swamiji, have something good to eat, and then go out and smoke marijuana the rest of the day.
- These were the humble beginnings of the movement that is supposed to change the world — the spiritual revolution meant to give some sense to the world's misdirected civilization.

---

## The First Street Chanting in the West

- One day Prabhupada — who was never new to taking risks — said, "All right, I want you all to go out on the street and chant Hare Krishna in the neighborhood."
  - You could hear a pin drop. The devotees were shocked to be told to go out on the street, among their old friends and acquaintances, in their devotee garb, singing Hare Krishna.
- Only one person had a shaved head. A couple were wearing dhotis pinned together — they didn't really know how to put a dhoti on, so it looked like a big diaper.
- There is an old video (from perhaps the first week) taken out at 26 Second Avenue / Matchless Gifts: they have a big bass drum going "boom, boom, boom," and they look very self-conscious, going out and wondering how long it would all last.
  - **"Not the most potent Harinam party ever."** But somehow or other it works.
- They attracted a few likewise persons, and eventually things grew.
  - None of us were any different when we joined. A number of those first devotees are now stalwart leaders and preachers in our movement.
- Despite its uncultured, unprofessional look, there was always the element of devotion and love for Srila Prabhupada, and that attracted sincere and intelligent people.

---

## The Rise of Book Distribution — the Brihat Mridanga

- In the initial stages, that kind of transcendental advertising — going out on Sankirtan and having ecstatic Sankirtan parties — became the main way of spreading Krishna consciousness.
- As the *Back to Godhead* magazines and the books came, devotees began distributing them as part of the Sankirtan party.
  - Because we are preaching in different circumstances than ancient India, we have to be a little innovative in presenting the message — **without compromising.**
  - So, along with the Harinam (congregational chanting), devotees started distributing books. That was something new.
- Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan parties did **not** distribute books and magazines — there was no *Back to Godhead*, no *Sri Ishopanishad*. In those days there was no paper as we know it, only palm leaves on which scripture was written very slowly. They weren't distributing palm leaves to the farmers or to the girls milking cows or the cowherd boys — it was just singing.
- Srila Prabhupada, the founder-acharya of ISKCON, had these ideas, and they worked.
- **Book distribution evolved into a separate entity in broadcasting the message of Lord Chaitanya.** It became, in effect, the **Brihat Mridanga** — the *mridanga* that could be heard not just a few city blocks away, but all over the world.
- Prabhupada pushed this concept because it was the order of his guru:
  - On a walk at **Radha Kunda**, **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura** told Prabhupada, **"If you ever get money, print books."**
  - Prabhupada is quoted as saying, **"If you want to please me, distribute my books,"** and there is the famous maxim: *"distribute books, distribute books, distribute books."*
- It is history now how the movement spread and how so many devotees came as a result of book distribution.

---

## Reviving Harinam Alongside Book Distribution

- Many devotees feel that book distribution should never have grown **at the expense** of the Harinam Sankirtan party. Gradually the Harinam party faded, and in some places it is almost non-existent.
- But it doesn't have to be like that. Harinam Sankirtan remains a very potent, very attractive way of spreading the Sankirtan movement. There is no reason for it to go into oblivion.
- Like all activities in Krishna consciousness, it can go on side by side. The **nine processes of devotional service** go on side by side; we may stress one over another, but that doesn't mean the others go out of existence — they are all important.

### Story: Tamala Krishna Goswami — "Why Not Both?"

- We once asked **Tamala Krishna Goswami** a question: there was a choice of doing one or two services, and we were torn between the two alternatives, unable to decide.
- Goswami Maharaj gave a very nice answer: **"Why this or that? Why not both of them? Just find the balance."**
- Life is always about finding the balance between two things — the yin and the yang — and normalcy.

### Book Distribution as the Main Anga, Harinam as Complement

- Book distribution will always be the main *anga* for propagating the Sankirtan movement, because that was very much Prabhupada's mood. But it can be complemented and embellished by continuing or reviving Harinam Sankirtan.
- Especially now: whereas before there were only a certain number of devotees, in a country like Russia there seem to be unlimited devotees.
  - At this festival there are **3,000 devotees**; at the Ukraine festival there were **4,500 devotees.**
- Not everyone can necessarily go out and do book distribution; and even those who do can still find time to do Harinam Sankirtan and add even more flavor to the movement.
- Everyone can come together at certain times — once or twice a week, or on special days — and go out on Harinam Sankirtan, **reviving the original trademark of Lord Chaitanya's Sankirtan movement.**

### The Taste of Harinam

- Those of us still doing Harinam never abandon it. We continue with the other *angas* (book distribution too), but such a taste was there that we just couldn't give it up.
- We see how potent it is in attracting people to this movement — it's good for the *karmis*, but it's also good for us.
- There is nothing devotees enjoy more than going out with other devotees in a joyful mood, singing and dancing, distributing the holy name, and relishing the reaction of the public, which is generally favorable. It's every devotee's favorite activity.
- Just to keep our own devotees, we have to have Sankirtan parties, because it very easily gives the higher taste that keeps us in Krishna consciousness.
  - **"Param drishtva nivartate"** (from **Bhagavad Gita 2.59**): by experiencing a higher taste one gives up the lower — Sankirtan gives that higher taste, which is important for our own progress, what to speak of spreading the movement.
- So the leadership should organize Sankirtan parties regularly, so that devotees will be happy and the public will be informed. That, in essence, is Sankirtan.

## Harinam vs. Book Distribution: Presenting Krishna Consciousness "As It Is"

- Although Harinam and book distribution are very similar, they are a little different.
- **Book distribution** is not always direct:
  - We go to certain places to meet people, generally dressed in non-devotional clothes — and rightfully so. Prabhupada gave permission for this in a **purport in Srimad Bhagavatam**: it is okay to dress in *karmi* clothes in order to distribute the books.
  - Often we don't directly say it is about God or Krishna, the Supreme Personality of Godhead — many people would recoil. Instead we may say it's about yoga, or that it will bring peace and happiness.
  - So book distribution has a certain personality, a certain way of doing it.
- **The difference with Harinam:** when we go out on Harinam, we present Krishna consciousness **as it is** — as it is in the spiritual world. We're not hiding anything; we're revealing what spiritual life in Krishna consciousness actually is: chanting, dancing, a little philosophy, and feasting.
  - Who is not attracted to these principles — singing, dancing, a little talking, socializing, and some feasting? We're just saying: **do it in a spiritual way.**
  - That's what people are looking for. They're not looking to work; they're looking to enjoy. If we can do it better than they can, then join us. As we say in English, **"If you can't beat them, join them."**
  - We are enjoying — or should be enjoying — more than the common person, because we're enjoying on the transcendental platform: **anandambudhi-vardhanam** (from Lord Chaitanya's **Shikshashtakam**), where the ocean of pleasure is always increasing, never deteriorating.
- That's Harinam Sankirtan: not hiding anything, just like the spiritual world. It is meant to attract and melt the hearts of the conditioned souls, to show them what **Goloka Vrindavan** is — where all walking is dancing, all talking is singing, and there's a festival every day.

## Doing Harinam First-Class: The External Standard

- Because we are presenting the highest culture, the highest way of life, the most wonderful alternative, **Harinam Sankirtan has to be first-class.**
- We don't want to go out just to chant, show the people where it's at, and make a little money. We have to organize and plan very carefully to actually represent the spiritual sky, both externally and internally in our hearts.
- We're going out there to attract them, so our presentation has to be **as good or better than anything else people see in the city.**

### We Are Competing With the Advertising World

- Whether we like to say it or not, we are advertising — and we have a lot of competition.
  - There are advertising companies analyzing the minds and brains of people, studying what will attract them to spend their money: big billboards, fancy arrangements in store windows, the internet, radio, and TV.
  - People walk around with big eyes: "I need this," "I have to buy that."
- **We can't compete** with a kirtan party of four or five brahmacharis in wrinkled clothes — a red sock and a blue sock, a broken drum, unpolished kartals — not into the chanting, who can't wait to get back and have dinner. We won't win a competition like that.
- **Playful exaggerated example:** A bigger Harinam party of men and women goes past the shops. All the *matajis* are looking at the dresses and jewelry in the shop windows — then the kirtan party comes along, "Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna," and pretty soon all the matajis are drawn away from the store windows. (He notes he's exaggerating, but the point is clear.)
- **Jesus said, "You're either for me or against me."** So the first thing is that if we're going to do Harinam — and we should, having spoken of the need — we have to do it professionally.

## Attention to Detail: Dress, Instruments, Invitations, and Melodies

### Dress Your Very Best

- Everyone has to be dressed very nicely — just as in the Christian church people put on their best dress when they go to church on Sunday.
- When you chant Hare Krishna, you're with God — He's dancing on the tip of your tongue — so when you go on Sankirtan you should dress your very best. It's not a difficult thing.
- **Reminiscence of the old days:** When he first joined, the ladies had only one color — yellow. Every lady wore a light yellow sari; there was no other option. They didn't have saris from India; it was cloth bought in a store downtown.
  - The men also wore yellow. Everyone wore yellow for a while — "we looked like a flock of canary birds." Maybe the temple president just liked yellow.
  - The householders wore sashes — the top piece, like the sannyasis wear — worn on the side.
- Now devotees have a choice of 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 saris, so wear a nice sari.
- As the tradition was started on the **Polish festival tour**, the ladies wear **gopi dots** (face makeup) done nicely, and the men are dressed very *pukka* — including **same-colored socks** (no outrageous colored or checkered coats, nothing oversized).
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura said, "Man's glory lies in his common sense."**

### Nice Instruments, Invitations, and Melodies

- Use your imagination with the instruments: cover the mridangas in colorful cloths, shine the kartals, put new ribbons to tie the kartals together.
- **Appeal to the Russian devotees:** You are very artistic people — famous for Russian ballet, gymnastics, the Olympics, and the most beautiful churches in the world. So use your imagination and make it very blissful and attractive, within our culture.
- **Have a nice invitation.** The days of the black-and-white invitation are over. With technology, from your own computer and a common printer, you can print a really nice colored invitation. No temple is so poor it can't afford this. Make it attractive, as Krishna is all-attractive.
- **Sing nice melodies.** You don't always have to imitate **Sri Prahlad** — he is a musician, a vocalist, artistic, and came up with wonderful melodies. But sometimes when those melodies are chanted here, they become a "perverted reflection": the holy name is there, but the melody has gone astray.
  - If a melody is too complicated, don't sing it. Sing what you're comfortable with.
- We're getting very specific, but **Prabhupada said, "Attention to detail is a sign of love."** So Harinam is really getting down to the details so that it looks all attractive — more attractive than any other presentation — with flags, banners, and a nice sound system.

## The Art of Attracting the Senses of the Public

- He has seen many wonderful Harinam parties here, and many DVDs.
- **Never turn your back on the people.** If you're talking to someone and you turn your back on them, that's considered an insult. **Sankirtan is all about communication.**
- On our Sankirtan program and festival tour — which in many ways has become a standard in Sankirtan — we never group together in a circle with our backs to the people just having our own kirtan.
  - We're not chanting for ourselves; we're chanting for the glorification of Krishna and to attract people.
  - So he does not recommend getting into circles with your backs to the people, because then they're no longer connected.
- **Dance nicely, sweetly, in choreographed patterns.** Generally the ladies are quite good at that, and the boys are terrible at it.
  - But by the mercy of Lord Chaitanya (paraphrasing the **Chaitanya Charitamrita**): *even a blind man can see the stars, even a deaf and dumb person can recite elegant poetry, and even a lame person can climb mountains.* So with a little practice the boys can also get together some nice choreographed movements.
- This is all meant to attract the senses of the public. **It's all about sense control** — **"Vacho vegam manasah krodha-vegam, jihva-vegam udaropastha-vegam..."** (from Rupa Goswami's **Upadeshamrita / Nectar of Instruction**, text 1: one who can control the urges of speech, mind, anger, tongue, belly, and genitals is a *dhira*).
- **How we attract each sense:**
  - Their **eyes** — by the beautiful display of spiritual ecstasy before them.
  - Their **ears** — by the sweet melodies of the holy names.
  - Their **tongues** — by sometimes distributing *prasadam*.
  - Their **mind and intelligence** — by invitations, accompanying book distribution, announcements about short talks on the philosophy, or announcing an upcoming festival program.
- It's always nice to do the Harinam program in conjunction with an **upcoming event** — a Sunday feast, a program in a hall with a visiting senior devotee. It adds meaning and makes it more attractive, because you're advertising an upcoming event. Always good to do Harinam with a purpose.

### The Unpublished Harinam Manual

- We could go on and on; there are many details. By the mercy of Lord Chaitanya, the devotees have developed a nice Harinam party on the summer festival tour in **Poland**, and there are several videos — "seeing is believing," so you can get your ideas just by watching those DVDs.
- **Sri Prahlad** and the speaker (mostly Sri Prahlad) put together a **Harinam manual** many years ago — almost ten years ago — which is still yet to be published. It's on their hard disk, and several leaders in the movement have it.
  - Prahlad specifically went to the scriptures and gathered many quotes on Harinam, basing the book on quotes from Prabhupada.
  - It goes into great detail. It's a classic and deserves to be published and distributed — maybe it's high time it was.

## The Internal Mood: Compassion and Sharing Our Good Fortune

- This is really a 60-minute seminar, and he could speak for hours. In closing: beyond the external appearance, there is the **internal mood** of Harinam Sankirtan, which is really the essence and ultimately what attracts the people.
  - If all the externals are there but the internal mood is not right, then the formula won't really work.
- **What is the internal mood?** In the heart of every individual, we're out there to **share our good fortune with others.**
- Because of that, we can easily tolerate the scorching heat, the freezing cold, the wind, the temptations of the store windows, and our own restless minds.
  - We can tolerate all that out of a sense of duty, because we've been ordered to go on Sankirtan.
  - But after some time we should actually become purified and develop a **spirit of compassion.** We can't just sit by and watch the world go to hell.
- We've been delivered by the mercy of Prabhupada and Lord Chaitanya and are relishing Krishna consciousness — we can't just watch the multitude of people spiraling downward into hellish conditions.
- The world is in dire straits, a very precarious condition. What are we doing? Not just singing and dancing and having a good time — **we have a message which can save the world.** Sankirtan is a very serious, very sober thing.

### Story: "Spiritual Solutions to Material Problems in Modern Society"

- One time Prabhupada asked one of the speaker's godbrothers to write a book: **"Spiritual Solutions to Material Problems in Modern Society."**
- We are trying to educate the world how to live a sane, relatively peaceful existence while utilizing this opportunity of the human form of life to go back home, back to Godhead — and thereby solve all their problems.
- People are suffering even in the best, wealthiest countries. Don't be in any illusion — people are suffering from all angles.
  - When they try to solve their problems through science, technology, and various theories, the problem just gets worse.
  - We have the solutions — not "we," but Prabhupada, our acharyas, and Krishna.
- That is the real reason we go out: to help others and share our good fortune.

### Bhaktivinoda Thakura's Mood of Humility

- This is exemplified in the words of **Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura**: **"When will my compassion for all fallen souls manifest, and with a lowly heart, in a mood of humility, I'll go out to preach the divine command?"**
- If you go out with that mood — everything internally like that, and everything externally very nice — then it is a magic formula. It has to work, because it's transcendental.
  - Some people may not react immediately, but it plants a seed in their heart which will eventually fructify.
  - So, immediately or in the future, we're relieving people of the burden of material existence.

---

## Conclusion: Sankirtan as the Highest Welfare Work

- Therefore **Rupa Goswami describes Sankirtan** — of which Harinam is an integral part — **as the highest welfare work.**
- So it's not a question of "Should we do Harinam? Do we have enough time? Do we have enough money? Maybe some other devotee should do it." No — **we have to do it.**
- We have to do all forms of preaching; that makes everything complete. **Sankirtan means the complete glorification of the Lord, and that includes Harinam Sankirtan.**
- It must go on. It must be revived — for your benefit and for the benefit of the whole world.
- Concluding with the words of **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura**: **"This preaching will do good for you, and it will do good for others."**
- *Thank you very much. Spasiba. Hare Krishna.*
