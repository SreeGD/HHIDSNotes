## Best Place, Best Time, Best Purpose

- We have come to Vrindavan for the best place, the best time, and the best purpose.
- **The best place** — not only on this earth planet, not only in this universe, but in the entire creation of Krishna, the best place is Vrindavan, "because this is the playground of God."
- **The best time** — anytime is good to visit Vrindavan, but especially the month of **Kartika**.
- **The best purpose / reason to come** — to learn as much as we can about the divine couple, Sri Sri Radha and Krishna, and how to serve Them with pure devotion, *amala-bhakti*.

## We Have Come Here to Learn — Our First Day of School

- "While in Vrindavan we're all ears." The definition of a *sadhu*/*isadu* is not one who merely observes everything as it manifests, but "one who hears about the divine truth and everything is revealed to him."
- We are like students who have just come to school for the first time.
- **Personal story:** The speaker recalls being about six years old on his very first day of school. He couldn't sleep the night before — his school bag, pen, paper and clothes all laid out. He was so eager to learn, and also scared, that he couldn't sleep. So too, this is "our first day of transcendental school in Kartika."
- The *param gati* (supreme goal) is to develop pure love for the divine couple, but to do that we first have to learn about Them and learn how to serve Them properly.

## The Braj-bhasha Saying — Vrindavan as a Classroom

- There is a very beautiful saying in **Braj-bhasha** — which is *not* Hindi, but the language of the local people of Braj. ("So many wonderful things to discover in Braj.")
- The saying: **"In the transcendental classroom of Sri Vrindavan Dham, love is the teacher, Radha and Krishna are the students, all the gopis who are listening attentively are their classmates, and they are learning the great knowledge of relishing lila (pastimes) together."**
- Some things must be repeated over and over — like the Hare Krishna mantra — so the speaker recites the Braj-bhasha verse again.
- "And that's why people come to Vraj."

## Srila Prabhupada's Love for Vrindavan

- Our own beloved Srila Prabhupada lived in Vrindavan many years as a sannyasi; as soon as he gave up all so-called material activities, he "ran to Vrindavan."
- But Prabhupada did not come as a *kshetra-sannyasi* — a sannyasi who comes to a holy place to remain in one place doing his *bhajana* until he dies. That is noble, but it was not Prabhupada's path.
- For Prabhupada, Vrindavan was **just a stop on the way** in his service to his Guru Maharaj, **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura**. From the very beginning he came here intending to go to the West — ultimately to bring everyone home to Vrindavan: either here in Gokula Vrindavan, or, for the most fortunate souls, to Goloka Vrindavan in the spiritual sky. He is our leader, to take us here and ultimately there.
- **SB 5.13.8 (purport)** — Prabhupada made it very clear, hoping "one becomes elevated to the transcendental world and returns back home, back to Godhead."
- "Like father, like son" — we can never *be* like Prabhupada, but we can imbibe his mood, which is what he very much wants.
- **Vrindavan Days** (memoir by Hayagriva Das, one of Prabhupada's first and most cherished disciples) recounts Prabhupada saying: *"I lived very simply in Vrindavan, content to be in Rupa Goswami's presence. Now I have hundreds of temples to go to, but I still like Vrindavan best of all."*
- Even while preaching in the West, Prabhupada always hankered for Vrindavan (as read in the *Lilamrita*): *"Here I am now sitting in New York, the world's greatest city, but my heart is always hankering after that Vrindavan."*
- **SB 4.29.39–40 (purport)** — Why did he love Vrindavan? He writes: *"In a holy place like Vrindavan, if one gets the chance to hear from pure devotees, then the cultivation of Krishna consciousness is very easy."* Vrindavan is actually meant for developing love of God, and therefore so many devotees accumulate here now (you can't even get through the traffic anymore).

## The Mercy of the Dham — Even Sinful People Are Welcome

- Vrindavan is so merciful. The Dham is merciful. As we know, the Dham is an expansion of Lord Balarama, who in our Gaudiya philosophy is known as one of the original spiritual masters.
- Vrindavan is so merciful that it even opens its arms to sinful rascals — it is recommended that even sinful people come here.
- **SB 3.20 (purport, text 4)** — Prabhupada writes: *"Vrindavan — any person may go there, and even if he is sinful, he will at once contact an atmosphere of spiritual life and automatically chant the names of Krishna and Radha."*
- As preachers, we all know how difficult it is to get someone to chant Hare Krishna. But such is the power of the Dham: even if a low-born sinful rascal comes here and spends some time, in this atmosphere he will call out, **"Radhe Shyam!"**

## Prahlada Maharaj Recommended Even His Demon Father Come Here

- Millions of years ago, **Prahlada Maharaj** — one of the twelve *mahajanas*, the authorities on devotion — recommended that his father **Hiranyakashipu** come to Braj/Vrindavan.
- His instruction: *"One should go to Vrindavan, where only Krishna consciousness is prevalent, and one should thus take shelter of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."*
- In that purport Prabhupada wrote something wonderful: *"In our Krishna conscious movement, we therefore invite all the elderly persons of the world to come to Vrindavan and stay there in retired life, making advancement in spiritual consciousness, Krishna consciousness."* — Prabhupada is inviting the whole world.

## Prabhupada Invites Us to Stay Permanently

- By reading Prabhupada's letters to disciples, the speaker found something amazing: Prabhupada invites us to stay here. "At the end of this Kartika you can't go home, because you're already home."
- **Letter to a disciple, November 9, 1975:** *"Anyone who can stay permanently is welcome. I want that as many devotees stay permanently and become brajabasis. This will be very inspiring."*
- **SB 4.8.42 (purport)** — *"Unless one is very advanced in spiritual life, it is recommended that he stay in such a holy place as Prayag, Mathura and Vrindavan."* Those who have not yet achieved perfection in spiritual practice are recommended to live in holy places and do devotional service.

## Rupa Goswami's Prayer in the Stava-mala

- Last night the speaker was reading Rupa Goswami's **Stava-mala**, where Rupa Goswami reveals his heart:
  - **"O Vrindavan Forest, was there ever anyone in this world who did not quickly attain supreme transcendental bliss by serving you? Therefore I surrender to you and most humbly petition you. Be kind and personally disclose to me the best way to receive the darshan of your worshipable Lord, Sri Sri Radha and Krishna."**

## The Question: How Does One Properly Enter Vrindavan?

- For all Gaudiya Vaishnavas the question is: how to properly enter into this playground of Krishna?
- We have often heard that one cannot simply buy a plane ticket or train ticket and enter into Vraja. It is not just being physically present here.
- **Krishna Book, Chapter 38** — very revealing: *"One cannot go to Vrindavan by purchasing a ticket. The process of going to Vrindavan is shown by Akrura. Akrura's journey to Vrindavan is exemplary. One who intends to visit Vrindavan should follow the ideal footsteps of Akrura."*
- "Follow the leader" — just as one follows Bada Hari Prabhu after Kartika ends. So let us hear briefly about Akrura's coming to Vrindavan; we have much to learn from his example.

## The Background of Akrura's Mission

- 5,000 years ago in Mathura, the evil king **Kamsa** was sending a demon every day to kill Krishna in Gokula.
- However, he was unsuccessful in every single attempt, because Krishna is **Ajita** — unconquerable.
- **Plan A / Plan B:** In management, if Plan A doesn't work you go to Plan B. So Kamsa changed his plan: he decided to invite Krishna to Mathura and kill Him there. He was confident that his two wrestlers, **Chanura and Mushtika** — big, powerful men — could very quickly and easily kill Krishna, and bring Krishna and Balarama "back to Mathura."

## Akrura the Hidden Devotee

- One thing Kamsa did not realize: Akrura was in fact a great devotee of Krishna.
- He was very humble, like devotees are — *Gupta*, meaning hidden. He kept his love for Krishna hidden in his heart, like the gopis.
- The gopis are young girls who love Krishna more than anyone has ever been loved before, but by social custom they cannot show that love — so they are *Gupta*, hiding their devotion.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilasa** — devotees are very humble: "They don't show their japa-mala. They don't proudly announce who is their guru. They keep *Gupta* — they keep these secrets close to their hearts."
- Akrura was so excited that he had to keep his ecstasy from manifesting — his body began to grow (horripilation), tears of love burst into his eyes, perspiration — he had to control it all. He clearly agreed: "Yes, I will bring those two boys, Krishna and Balarama, back to you."

## Akrura's Humility (Revealed in the Bhagavatam)

- The Bhagavatam reveals Akrura's mentality: he felt very unqualified, very low.
- This is how Vaishnavas feel — "because when you realize something's very big, you feel very small." One sign of a realized devotee is that he realizes how great Krishna is and how small he is; so humility comes naturally.
- Humility is not something you can imitate ("I'm so fallen, I'm worthless"). It is a matter of realization — when you actually realize that God is great and you are very small, then humility naturally comes from the heart.
- Akrura thought: *"I'm just a wretched materialistic person, engaged in sense gratification."*
- **This is the first instruction on how to come to Vraj:** humility. And if we don't have it, we must at least desire to desire to have it — "that's good enough. You can still get in."

## The Same Humility in All Great Devotees

- We see this humility in all true Vaishnavas — they may differ in personality and in the way they serve Krishna, but they all have this humility.
- **Prahlada Maharaj (7th Canto):** When asked by Lord Brahma to approach Lord Nrsimhadeva, Prahlada — himself a *mahajana* — expressed his humility:
  - **"How is it possible for me, who had been born in a family of Asuras, to offer suitable prayers to satisfy the Supreme Personality of Godhead? Even until now, all the demigods headed by Lord Brahma and all the saintly persons could not satisfy the Lord by streams of excellent words. Although such persons were very qualified, being in the mode of goodness, then what is to be said of me? I, Prahlada, am not at all qualified."**
  - Conclusion: "Unless one is very meek and humble, it is very difficult to make progress in Krishna consciousness."
- **Krishnadas Kaviraja Goswami** (author of the *Chaitanya Charitamrita*) speaks similarly: "I am more fallen than a worm in stool."
- **Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** — one of the first things He mentions in the **Shikshashtakam** is humility (the verse **"trinad api sunichena"**). Mahaprabhu said we should wear that verse "like a garland around our mind" — plug it fully into the mind so it protects like a bag.
  - **Chaitanya Charitamrita** (Mahaprabhu's proclamation: *"Udvahu kariya kahon, shuno sarva-loka…"*) — **"Raising my hands I declare, everyone please hear me: string this verse on the thread of the holy name and wear it on your neck for continuous remembrance, so that you never forget."**
- So we very humbly admit: I'm unqualified, I don't have any good qualities, I can't do anything. This is a prerequisite.

## How Can One Who Admits Weakness Be Strong Enough to Advance?

- The question: how can a person who admits apparent weakness be strong enough to advance in Krishna consciousness?
- **The reply:** A devotee knows that the Lord's mercy is greater than his or her disqualifications.
- So although Akrura felt very fallen, he was confident that when he got to Vrindavan, Krishna would uplift him. That is the hope of every devotee — what other hope is there? — that we can be elevated to the transcendental platform.
- Even after 50 years, many in this first generation are not crying when they chant Hare Krishna. We are fully dependent on the mercy of Srila Prabhupada, Lord Chaitanya, and Radhe-Shyam in the long run — the devotee completely depends on the grace of God, confident that at some point God will raise him and give transcendental service and love for Krishna.

## Rupa Goswami's Padyavali — Crying Out for Rescue

- **Padyavali, verse 61:** **"The elephant of my mind is drowning in the waters of material illusion, strongly held by the ferocious crocodile of the threefold misery; it anxiously cries with fear in its heart. O Lord Hari, please rescue it with your glowing Sudarshan chakra, which cuts the sufferings of even the demigods to pieces."**
- The beauty of Rupa Goswami is that by his example and his teachings he can take us **from Shraddha to Prema** — from the beginning steps of Krishna consciousness all the way to pure love of God.
- We are all *rupanugas*. Prabhupada is a pure *rupanuga*, and we follow in Prabhupada's footsteps. So in many ways we owe everything to Rupa Goswami, who revealed the real glory of Vrindavan. Over the next 30 days we will quote Rupa Goswami many times, to take shelter of his lotus feet.
- Prayer: **"For the purification of my physical body, I bathe regularly using water; however, for the complete purification of myself and my existence, I bathe in the nectar of the words of Sri Rupa Goswami."**

## Akrura's "Hope Against Hope"

- Humbled but desperate, Akrura figured: Krishna is all-merciful, so there is some hope even for me. He expressed this in the Bhagavatam — one of the speaker's favorite "hope against hope" verses:
  - **"Maivam mamadhamasyapi syad evachyuta-darshanam / hriyamanah kala-nadya kvachit tarati kaschana"** — **"But enough of such thoughts. After all, even a fallen soul like me can have the chance to behold the infallible Supreme Lord, for one of the conditioned souls being swept along in the river of time may sometimes reach the shore."**
- **Analogy of the river:** In a river, all the sticks and leaves are washed along together toward destruction; but sometimes the waves push one of those little leaves or sticks to the shore, where it is safe. So too a conditioned soul swept along in the river of time may sometimes reach the shore of Vaikuntha, of Goloka.
- What a moment that would be — to come face to face with Krishna! That is why we do all the austerities, penances, vows, rules, regulations and chanting: it all concludes when you come face-to-face with Radhe-Shyam. We have no other desire in life. If something does not facilitate that, we kick it to the side; if something is favorable to that, even if a little difficult, we embrace it. We are in this movement for one purpose: to go back home, back to Godhead, and to associate with Prabhupada in his perfected service to the Supreme Lord. "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna" is all we think about.

## Chanting From the Heart — Madhava's Meditation

- This is what it means to chant from the heart, as Madhava is always encouraging us to do.
- Once a devotee asked Madhava Prabhu what he is thinking about during his very deep kirtans.
- Understanding the temporary nature of this world and the golden opportunity we have to chant Hare Krishna to get out of it, one calls upon the Lord with feeling: *"Please take me out of this world of birth and death. Fix me as an atom at your lotus feet."* This is kirtan.
- Madhava Prabhu answered: *"I'm trying to meditate on that — that it's like my last kirtan."* Then the mood becomes true, and one prays, "O Lord, please take me out of this cycle of birth and death and place me like an atom at your lotus feet."

## Story of the Christian Priest

- The speaker saw a video of a famous Christian priest, elderly (about 86), giving a sermon. In the middle of the sermon he left his body — apparently a heart attack.
- In the video he is glorifying God, and then, as death comes, he calls out, **"Oh, monseigneur!"** — which means in English, **"Oh, my Lord!"** He died with a smile on his face. It brought tears to the speaker's eyes.
- "That's how we should do kirtan" — "Oh, monseigneur!"

## Akrura's Meditation on Krishna's Lotus Hand

- Approaching Vrindavan, Akrura thought about what he would do when he crossed the frontier — and what would happen when he fell at Krishna's lotus feet:
  - **"api angri-mule patitasya me vibhu…"** — **"When I have fallen at his feet, the Almighty Lord will place his lotus hand upon my head. For those who seek shelter in him, because they are greatly disturbed by the powerful serpent of time, that hand removes all fear."**
- The distance from Mathura to Vrindavan was not that great, and Akrura meditated: "What will happen when I fall at His lotus feet? O Lord, will You place Your lotus hand on my head?"
- The important part is the lotus hand of the Lord on your head. What else would you want?

## Story of Prabhupada's Slippers (Paris)

- One time Prabhupada was visiting the first temple in Paris (Fontenay-aux-Roses). Every morning he would come down, take off his slippers, and go into the temple room to give class.
- One time the speaker took his slippers and held them in his lap (there were about 20 devotees present; he kept one of the slippers/held them).
- When Prabhupada came out after the lecture, the speaker very carefully placed them on Prabhupada's lotus feet. Prabhupada rubbed his head and said, **"Good boy."**
- "That's enough for a lifetime. He said, 'Good boy.' He rubbed my head. Good boy. I tried to be a good boy." These blessings of Prabhupada are enough for a whole life.

## The Fragrance of Krishna's Lotus Hand

- The Puranas call the lotus flower found in the **Manasarovara** lake **saugandhika** — a pristine, pure, crystal-white water lotus with a particular, very beautiful scent.
- We speak of Krishna's lotus hand, and the acharyas say it also smells very nice, like that white *saugandhika* lotus.
- They say Krishna's lotus hand acquired the fragrance of this particular flower from this particular lake **by coming in contact with the beautiful faces of the gopis.** Krishna touches the faces of the gopis, and so his hand carries the scent of that beautiful white lotus flower from Manasarovara.

## What Will We Do When We Enter Vrindavan? — Akrura's Example

- At this point Akrura is meditating: "Now, when I just cross over the frontier into Vrindavan, what should I do?"
- **Contrast with us:** We come from New York, from Sydney, from Moscow, thinking: "Oh, I want to go to Vrindavan — I'm going to go to my room, take a hot shower, go to the MVT, and have pizza."
- What is Akrura thinking? The Bhagavatam — *Grantaraj*, the king of scriptures — reveals it:
  - **"I will alight, I will get off of my chariot and bow down to the lotus feet of Krishna and Balarama. Their feet are meditated upon by great mystic yogis striving for self-realization."**
  - And he says: **"I will also offer my obeisances to the Lord's cowherd boyfriends and to all the other residents of Vrindavan"** — all the Vaishnavas.
- Then Akrura thinks: when Krishna sees me, He will know I am not merely a minister of Kamsa — He will know I am a devotee; Krishna will know my heart.

*(The recorded lecture ends here, mid-thought, on Akrura's confidence that Krishna will recognize his devotional heart.)*
