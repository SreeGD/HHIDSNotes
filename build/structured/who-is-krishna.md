## The Verse: The Material Energy Emanates From the Lord

- Today's class is on **Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.5.31** — Brahma answering Narada's question about the substance of the material creation: *"All the material manifestations of the universes are therefore situated in His powerful material energies, which He accepts self-sufficiently, although He is eternally without affinity for the material modes."*
- The subject matter of the verse is the **material energy** — more specifically the material world — which, as Srila Prabhupada states emphatically in his purport, emanates from the Supreme Lord.
- The material nature is **not** working independent of the Lord; it works completely **under His direction**.
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 9.10** ("mayadhyaksena prakrtih suyate sa-caracaram"): Krishna tells Arjuna that "this material energy is working under My direction, O son of Kunti."
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 10.8** ("aham sarvasya prabhavo mattah sarvam pravartate"): "all that is material and all that is spiritual comes from Me — it emanates from Me."
- Therefore the ultimate source of the entire material manifestation is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Krishna.
- Krishna creates this material world as **a sort of prison** for those souls unfortunate enough to want to leave the spiritual world.

## Why Would an All-Good, All-Merciful God Create a Prison of Suffering?

- A common question from those without spiritual knowledge: if God is all-good and all-merciful, why has He created this prison-house of material existence, which is basically a place of suffering?
- Even so-called happiness here, Prabhupada said, is simply **a momentary relief from distress**.
- Within the material world the Lord has created **three types of miseries**:
  - **adhyatmika** — miseries caused by one's own body and mind
  - **adhibhautika** — miseries caused by other living entities
  - **adhidaivika** — miseries caused by natural occurrences (under the control of the demigods)
- At any given time everyone is suffering from one, or a combination, of these.
- **Example — even in the Bhagavatam class itself** we experience all three:
  - The floor is too hard; even with a cushion you must keep shifting; a cushion that is too soft makes the speaker sink in and grow sleepy.
  - We cough, we get a headache — the **body/mind** disturbs us.
  - The mind wanders during class: thinking about a fight with one's wife, a debt, where to go on sankirtan, whether we'll get some maha-prasadam — the conditioned mind.
  - **Adhibhautika** — mosquitoes; visiting a grihastha couple's house the day before, the speaker noticed a big can of mosquito spray, because at night everyone is disturbed by bugs and bed bugs.
  - **Adhidaivika** — earthquakes and other natural occurrences; sometimes too hot, sometimes too cold.
- When we get a little relief from these miseries we call it "happiness," but that is not happiness.
- Real happiness is **ananda** — described as *ananda-buddhi-vardhanam* — something always increasing, always expanding, ecstatic, in relationship to Krishna, not to the material energy.
  - The material energy **cannot** give happiness. What gives happiness is Krishna, or Krishna's devotional service.
- What we call "happiness" is only the tiny break sandwiched between one misery and the next.

## Prabhupada's Analogies for Material "Happiness"

- **The prisoner on the pole** (Prabhupada's example): in old England / New England they would tie a prisoner to the end of a long pole and slowly lower him under the water. Held under, he would be gasping; when pulled up he'd gasp for air and think, "Oh, everything's okay now, everything's so nice." Then back under, then up again — "Oh, happiness!" But that is not happiness; it is a momentary relief from distress.
- What we call happiness in this world is a temporary relief from the frustration and anxiety of material existence.
- Prabhupada described material life as **hankering and lamenting**:
  - Hankering for something material — "I want it, I need it, I have to work hard for it… I've got it!" — but then it goes.
  - Everything can be enjoyed only for a short duration: a few moments, days, years; hardly anything lasts a lifetime.
  - Then lamenting — "Oh, it was so nice: that girl, that car which got smashed, my house which burned down." We spend half our time hankering and the other half lamenting.
- Prabhupada didn't even give material life enough credit to say "hankering, enjoying, and lamenting" — the tiny iota of happiness is sandwiched between a big thick piece of hankering and a big thick piece of lamenting.
- **The dry cheese-sandwich story:** A sandwich originates in America (just as pizza originates in Italy); in Europe making one is always guesswork — like foreign attempts at Croatian recipes, close but not the real thing. Before travel, devotees made the speaker a cheese sandwich. On the plane he opened it: the bread was thick on both sides with only a tiny sliver of cheese, a little butter, a tiny piece of tomato, no salt — and nothing to drink. So dry it was practically just a bread sandwich. Material life is like that: a tiny bit of happiness with hankering and lamenting on both sides; you bite in and the pleasure is missing.

## Why the Living Entity Comes to the Material World

- This world is a type of **punishment** for the living entity who leaves the spiritual world and tries to be the enjoyer.
- Krishna does not *want* to create this prison, but He is obliged to.
  - **Government analogy:** a government doesn't want to build prisons — taxpayers must pay to build and maintain them — but it is forced to because certain citizens develop a criminal mentality. Similarly, some citizens of the spiritual world somehow develop a criminal mentality.
- This is described by **Jagadananda Pandit**, a great Vaishnava spiritual master (in the *Prema-vivarta*, found in the Vaishnava songbook):
  - — **"krsna-bahirmukha hana bhoga-vancha kare, nikata-stha maya tare japatiya dhare"**: "As soon as one becomes inimical to Krishna and desires sense gratification, he is immediately struck down by the illusory energy of the Lord and sent to the material world."
- **Thunder-and-lightning analogy** (given by Prabhupada): thunder and lightning actually occur simultaneously. From a distance you see the lightning first and hear the thunder later, because light travels faster than sound.
  - The speaker's father taught him a Navy trick: count the seconds between lightning and thunder to gauge the storm's distance (five counts = about five miles). But right at the spot, lightning and thunder happen at once.
  - So too: the instant the living entity becomes inimical to Krishna in the spiritual world, he is struck down by the illusory energy **immediately** — there is no room for rebelliousness there.
- **Good-leader analogy:** just as a good temple leader who detects some nonsense (a devotee not following principles, some politics) takes care of it immediately rather than letting it fester into a poisonous issue — or as you treat a bodily infection at once — as soon as enviousness of Krishna infects the heart of the living entity, he is sent to the material world to act out the role of enjoyer.

## Imitating the Lord: The Child, the Mud, and the Perverted Reflection

- The living entity becomes envious of Krishna and wants to imitate Him.
- **The little girl who wants to cook:** a small child watches her mother frying puris in ghee and says, "Ma, I also want to do." The mother refuses — "You're too little, the ghee will burn you" — but the child pulls the sari, insisting. So the mother takes her outside, mixes water into the dirt to make mud, and says, "You play here, you can imitate me out here but not there." The girl makes mud pies, pretends to bake and cook soup, and offers it to her friends. She isn't really cooking — if you tried to eat it, it's just mud — but she is given the facility to imitate her mother for a while.
- Similarly, the Supreme Lord creates the material world so the living entity can come and, for some time, try to imitate the Lord. It is not the same as the Lord's activities — it is like mud.
- The material world is described as a **perverted reflection** of the spiritual world; in a reflection you see the original object but there is no real substance.
- **The apple tree reflected in water:** on a very clear, still day a lake becomes a mirror and you see the apples and the tree reflected — you might even be fooled. But if you try to bite the apple in the water, there is nothing; you must pick the real apple from the tree.
- So one who tries to enjoy here can only imitate the Lord and never gets real satisfaction. Because he originally tasted the real nectar in the spiritual world, eventually he comes to his senses and realizes, "This is only imitation; I can't really enjoy here."

## The Real Purpose of the Prison: Rectification, Not Just Punishment

- The purpose of the material world is ultimately to bring us **back to the spiritual world** — not simply to punish.
- **Prison analogy again:** a prison is not ultimately meant to punish but to rectify the criminal so he has a change of heart and comes out a good citizen who follows the laws. Likewise the Lord creates this prison so the living entity can come, try to imitate Him, become frustrated, have a change of heart, and return to the spiritual world as a good citizen.
- So is God cruel? If we understand the material world as a place of rectification rather than mere punishment, we can understand: **God is so kind.** Although we rejected Him, He has never forgotten us; He is rectifying our deviant mentality.

## Proof That the Lord Never Forgets Us

- The proof that the Lord never forgets us, though we forget Him, is that He accompanies us into the material world as the **Supersoul**.
- "A friend in need is a friend indeed." Your real friends are those who never abandon you.
  - When a person has money and fame he has many "friends" who glorify him and ask him for money; but when the money and fame are gone those friends abandon him. The few who stick around even when you are down and have nothing are your real friends — they want to help you even then.
- **How low can the living entity fall?** All the way down to an **indra-gopa**.
  - The highest birth in the material world is Brahma; the lowest is the indra-gopa — described as an insect but even lower, like an amoeba or a tiny germ, with practically no consciousness; it hardly knows it exists. The **Brahma-samhita** describes the range "from Brahma down to the indra-gopa."
- Yet Krishna is in that creature's heart:
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 15.15** ("sarvasya caham hrdi sannivistho mattah smrtir jnanam apohanam ca"): "I am seated in the heart of every living entity, and from Me come knowledge, remembrance, and forgetfulness."
  - Krishna does not say the heart of every *human being* — He says every *living entity*, which includes even the indra-gopa. He waits there patiently for that soul to come to the point of surrender.
- Therefore Krishna is the **best friend of every living entity**: though we fall to such degraded conditions, He remains with us and waits for us to desire spiritual knowledge.

## How the Lord Continues to Love Us

- When we finally desire spiritual knowledge, the Lord directs us to a bona fide spiritual movement like ISKCON and to a bona fide spiritual master, who — as the external manifestation of the Supersoul — enlightens us in the process of getting out of the material world.
- Even though we reject Him, He continues to love us and always tries to purify us:
  - He accompanies us as the Supersoul.
  - He gives us **shastra**.
  - He sends us **saints and sages**.
  - He sends us the **holy name**.
  - He sends us **prasadam**.
- Even low-born creatures with no spiritual consciousness are sometimes fed prasadam at the temples. At **Govardhan Puja** (mentioned three days running) the animals also receive mercy in the form of prasadam.
- **The temple dog:** every time the speaker sees the temple dog, it offers obeisances (going down like that); even if kicked away it returns, so determined, like a devotee — so it too must be given prasadam. And it renders service: it scares away bigger and smaller dogs, barks at intruders, protects the Vaishnavas. Animals have their role.
- So who can say God is cruel? We rejected Him and came here; through this world He rectifies us; and even during that rectification He comes Himself, though He is the enjoyer of ever-blissful rasas in the spiritual world, because He cares about us.
- Next time someone says, "There is so much suffering — how can there be a God?" — from these different angles you can answer and hopefully satisfy their questions.

## How the Lord Creates the Material World

- "I am the source of all that is material and all that is spiritual" (**Bhagavad-gita 10.8** again) — that is *why* the Lord creates the material world; the Vedic scriptures also describe *how*.
- In a corner of the spiritual world is the total material manifestation, the **mahat-tattva**. It is so tiny it seems insignificant next to the infinite spiritual world — the material world is finite, but the spiritual world is infinite. (This is said only as a concession to our conditioned brains.)
- The stages of creation:
  - Krishna enters the mahat-tattva as **Maha-Vishnu** and lies down on the Causal Ocean; from the pores of His skin emanate **unlimited universes**.
  - He then expands into each universe and lies down on the universal waters (as **Garbhodakasayi Vishnu**). Each universe is like a **coconut**: look inside a coconut and it is half filled with water — so half the universe is air and half is water, with the planets in the air part and a huge Garbhodaka ocean below.
  - From His navel grows a **lotus flower**, and the first created being, **Brahma**, appears on it.
  - Under the Lord's direction, Brahma creates the diversity and variegatedness within the universe, and the Lord allows the living entities to take birth in different situations.
  - The Lord again expands Himself into the heart of every living entity (as the Supersoul).
- That is a basic summary of creation.

## The Scientists' Objection — and the Cowherd Boy Who Is the Source

- Material scientists will not accept this; they challenge: "You expect us, scientific men of advanced intelligence, to believe that this whole cosmic manifestation has come from a little ten-year-old cowherd boy?"
- — **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.3.28** ("ete camsa-kalah pumsah krsnas tu bhagavan svayam"): all the incarnations are plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions, but **Krishna is the original Personality of Godhead**, from whom all incarnations come. (Incarnations come into the material world whenever there is a disturbance created by the enemies of Indra.)
- So the Maha-Vishnu, Garbhodakasayi, and Ksirodakasayi incarnations all come from *krsnas tu bhagavan svayam* — the original form of Krishna.
- And what is the original form of Krishna? **A cowherd boy**, eight, nine, ten years old, in Vrindavan:
  - He plays with His cows, dances with the gopis, eats butter, plays with the monkeys.
  - He is so mischievous — He throws butter to the monkeys and tells His mother, "Mata, your butter is useless, these monkeys won't even eat it" (they've eaten so much they can't eat any more).
  - He sneaks into the gopis' houses where the babies sleep, pinches their bottoms and makes them cry; when the gopis scold "You rascal!" He passes urine on the floor and runs away.
  - He is called **Makhan Chor** — the butter thief, a transcendental thief.
- From this little mischievous, butter-stealing boy, this whole cosmic manifestation has come: He expands as a four-armed being lying on an ocean, from whose navel a lotus flower grows, from which a four-headed person (Brahma) is created.

## Contrast With Darwin and the Big Bang

- **Darwin's version:** the first created being was an amoeba — some material elements came together under favorable circumstances and, "whoop," the first form of life appeared. The speaker retells it satirically: the amoeba is lonely, "needs a wife," so it splits into two (the other half female), they say "Hi" to each other and decide to "start evolving," and from their combination comes another who again expands into a female — and this is supposedly the origin of life within the universe.
- Compared to the Bhagavatam's account (a cowherd boy expanding into creation, a lotus, and four-headed Brahma), the scientists dismiss the Vedic version as "science fiction" — yet **their own theories are even wilder.**
- **The Big Bang / exploding rock:** they begin from the assumption of a huge piece of matter (they don't really define where it came from) which exploded, and from that explosion came the variegatedness of the universe.
- But the universe is full of **order** — from the tiny atom to the huge universal shell, everything is well organized and planned:
  - A **cell** is like a small universe — anyone who has studied physics or biology sees how well organized it is (the nucleus, the surrounding matter, the framing); a genius must have invented such a machine.
  - The planets move in their orbits and never collide; the seasons come in perfect order — summer to fall to winter to spring — for millions of years.
- How could such order come from an explosion? Have you ever seen an explosion produce order?

## The Kitchri Explosion: Order Cannot Come From Chaos

- The speaker's own experience of an explosion. One day on the sankirtan party the cook was sick and they asked him to cook — though he has never cooked before or since ("I'm expert at eating, but not cooking"). They asked for **khichri**.
- He used a pressure/steam cooker: put in water, rice, and dal, closed the lid, but seeing the little steam valve he thought "this is crazy" and **closed the hole** so the steam couldn't escape. He put it on a low fire, then a high fire to speed it up, then went outside to chant japa.
- Chanting outside, he looked over at the sankirtan van (they cooked inside the van) and saw that the pot had **exploded** from the pressure — khichri everywhere: out the windows, dripping out the doors, wall-to-wall inside; even later the driver couldn't get the key into the ignition because of the khichri.
- For weeks afterward they distributed books with little pieces of khichri stuck inside; when people said "Yuck, what is that?" he'd reply, "Oh, that's prasadam — Krishna's mercy." The boys had to fast that day — no prasadam.
- **The lesson:** from an explosion there is only **chaos**, not order. Since this universe is full of order, it cannot have come from chaos; it must have been created by a very intelligent designer.

## We Can Only Imitate the Lord's Original Designs

- We human beings only **imitate** the Lord. Our creations are copied from the originals He made:
  - A boat is designed after the **aerodynamics of a fish**; an airplane after the **aerodynamics of a bird** — and as planes get more sophisticated they look more and more like birds.
  - We get all these ideas by studying material nature.
- We are proud of the intelligence that created the imitation — but how much more intelligent is the person who created the **original**? Why can't we give Him the credit? Because of our envious nature.
- Our imitations are poor at best:
  - Birds can fly here and there, dart in and out, even stop in the air — our airplanes just drone along, boring.
  - Fish swim, turn, do backflips in the water — our boats just go straight.
- We take the credit for a very poor imitation; why not credit the Supreme Lord who made the original?

## Faith: Scientists or the Vedic Scriptures?

- Their theories are "madness" — theories at best, not knowledge, because they cannot be proven; most of what they say they cannot even prove, yet it is passed off as knowledge.
  - On the **origin of life**: no one has ever taken material elements and created consciousness or life, yet the amoeba theory is taught as fact.
- Both presentations (the cowherd boy and the exploding rock) are amazing, but to understand higher knowledge you must accept one or the other:
  - Either put your faith in the **scientists**, who have imperfect senses and limited minds, or in the **Vedic scriptures**, given by great saintly persons and sages, which emanate from the all-knowing, all-blissful, all-perfect Personality of Godhead.
- To know higher things beyond your ability to see or understand, you must put your faith in some higher authority — you will never know just by speculating.
- We put our faith in Srila Prabhupada and the Vedic scriptures; the scientists put theirs in mundane speculators.

## The Moon Story: Where Do We Place Our Faith?

- During the period when the Americans were supposedly going to the moon, one devotee found it hard to accept that they hadn't gone.
- He came into Prabhupada's room (Prabhupada was reading his own *Chaitanya-charitamrita* — he liked to read his own books, they were so nice) with a newspaper: "Srila Prabhupada, this newspaper says on the moon there are only rocks."
- Prabhupada replied that the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and *Chaitanya-charitamrita* describe the moon as a celestial planet where very elevated beings live for 10,000 years, with rivers of nectar — no rocks are described. So either we accept the newspaper or we accept the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*; one of them must be thrown out. Prabhupada then **threw the newspaper over his shoulder.**
- It is a question of where we place our faith. Prabhupada says **faith means to put your trust in something sublime** — not in some rascal scientist:
  - who eats meat, takes intoxication, engages in illicit sex and gambling;
  - who has no qualifications even from a material point of view;
  - whose motivation is ultimately to deny God and to exploit the material energy so as to increase eating, sleeping, mating, and defending.
- Their real motive for going to the moon, Mars, or Venus is to do the same things they do here: "if we destroy our environment here, we'd better have a place to go and do it all over again." Like animals, all they know is **eat, sleep, mate, fight** — and "wherever you go, your mind goes with you," so they do the same in Zagreb, Budapest, Warsaw, Berlin, New York, San Francisco, or Honolulu.
- So it is difficult for an honest, sincere seeker of the truth to put his faith in such persons; we place our faith in saintly persons and the Vedic scriptures.

## Faith Is Not Blind — But It Accepts What Is Beyond Logic

- Our faith is not blind: the Vedic scriptures are full of logical, reasonable explanations of Krishna's spiritual and material energies.
- But there are also statements that cannot be grasped by logic and reasoning — e.g. how the Lord has **four arms** (where in the skeleton and muscle would you fit another arm?), how Brahma has **four heads** (turn one and you'd bump another), or how Krishna marries **16,108 wives**.
  - These involve subtle and spiritual energies, not gross mechanical bodies; the spiritual form is beyond our ability to comprehend.
- We accept such things because we have faith in Srila Prabhupada: his character and activities are perfect, we have no reason to doubt him, and everything he has told us up to this point in our devotional service **has come true.**

## Where Are We Situated on the Path?

- We are at least coming to the stage of **anartha-nivritti**, where the heart is becoming freed of material attachments and some attraction for Krishna begins to manifest. So we have already passed successfully through a big part of devotional life.
- — **Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu** (Rupa Goswami): *"adau sraddha tatah sadhu-sango 'tha bhajana-kriya, tato 'nartha-nivrttih syat…"*
  - **adau sraddha** — our faith was awakened in the association of a Vaishnava (a festival program, a devotee met on the street) — that was a transcendental experience. Otherwise, why would we give up material life to serve a little cowherd boy?
  - **sadhu-sanga** — we took up the association of devotees.
  - **bhajana-kriya** — we took up the regulative process of devotional service.
  - **anartha-nivrttih syat** — the result: freedom from unwanted things. So by that verse we are already **halfway.**
  - The next line covers **nistha** (fixed faith/steadiness), then **ruci**, **asakti**, **bhava** (the initial awakening of love of God), and **prema**.
- Ten years ago you would never have imagined telling your friends you'd be in this movement — wearing a sari or shaving your head, wearing saffron, worshipping a cowherd boy who herds cows in the spiritual world. Yet now you do it with great faith and determination, because you have progressed scientifically along this path.

## Why We Have No Reason to Doubt the Second Half

- We have no reason to doubt the rest of the process, because we have come halfway and **seen** it work — everything Rupa Goswami said has come true; all the promises we heard on our first day of devotional service have come true.
- By contrast, **material life** gives you reason to doubt at every step:
  - Adhyatmika, adhibhautika, adhidaivika miseries — the promise of material happiness never materializes; you were never once fully satisfied.
  - You surrendered to maya, followed all the rules and regulations of the **pravritti-marga** (the path of enjoyment) — doing everything your parents, teachers, television announcers, and politicians told you — and after sixteen or seventeen years of that you were still frustrated.
- Now imagine giving sixteen, seventeen, twenty-five, or fifty years to Krishna consciousness. You may not be perfected, but you'll be well along the path — and guaranteed at least to take birth next life in a family of devotees to finish from there. Most present devotees are only five- or six-year (at most ten-year) devotees; we've been in the material world for billions of lives, so we must give the process time to work on us.

## Difficulties Trace Back to Improper Chanting

- Have you ever been disappointed in Krishna consciousness? If there was disappointment, the source was not the process but **your inability to follow it.**
- Difficulties do not come from:
  - taking prasadam (unless you eat too much);
  - the association of Vaishnavas;
  - worshipping the deity.
- You can trace back most, if not all, of your problems to the **improper chanting of the holy names.** ("I can't control my mind, this chanting doesn't work" — no; you can trace the problem to improper chanting.)
- Chanting the holy name is the **fundamental principle** of this movement — everything comes from chanting Hare Krishna, and everything else is meant to serve the holy name.
  - Even deity worship: Prabhupada said **deity worship is for Dvapara-yuga**; we do it because our minds are so restless and attracted to the material energy that we fix the mind on the beautiful forms of the Lord and become peaceful, and then with a pure mind we chant Hare Krishna.
  - One time Prabhupada said the **most important instruction** the spiritual master gives is to chant **sixteen rounds a day**.
- So if we have difficulty, it is not the process (which is perfect) but our inability to follow it properly. A sincere devotee — even a neophyte — experiences only newer and newer realizations, whereas the materialist is defeated at every step.
- Srila Prabhupada himself was perfect in his activities, his character, his motivations, and everything he said; to doubt him is foolish, because we have no reason to.

## Faith in the Future: Realization Comes With Maturity

- One day we will realize these exalted statements of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* as practical knowledge.
- **Analogy of the child and mathematics:** to a five- or six-year-old boy (like Nitai) advanced mathematics seems "way out there," but as his mind and intelligence develop he sits down and finds it simple. Right now four-headed Brahma and 16,108 wives seem far out, but as we advance one day we'll sit down and find them easy to understand. We must be patient and give ourselves time — a few years in this process versus billions of lives in the material world.
- In the meantime, have faith in **guru and Gauranga** — they will never cheat you.
  - The **materialists are great cheaters**: they have imperfect senses, they make mistakes, they fall under illusion, and they have the cheating propensity — yet they present themselves as great authorities.
  - The **Vaishnava acharyas** have none of these four defects (imperfect senses, mistakes, illusion, cheating); they are great, transcendental souls presenting the perfect process of Krishna consciousness. So it will prove true in the end.

## Prabhupada's Words on Faith in Krishna

- **"What if Krishna isn't really God?"** A disciple once pressed Prabhupada: "But just *if* — after we do all this, in the end Krishna's not really God?" Prabhupada answered that this way of life — Krishna consciousness — is so wonderful that we wouldn't change it anyway. "But," he added, "Krishna *is* God."
- **The GBC meeting (around 1971–72):** when the GBC had just been created and his leaders had been disciples only a few years, Prabhupada looked around and said, "You are now the leaders in this Krishna consciousness movement — do you really believe that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead?"
- Krishna is God, confirmed by so many great sages. In the Gita, **Arjuna quotes Narada, Vyasa, and Devala**, who all accepted Krishna as God (**Bhagavad-gita 10.12–13**), so why shouldn't we?
- **Classical-music analogy:** we accept that a certain kind of music is bona fide classical music because Mozart, Beethoven, and Tchaikovsky played it. By the same principle, we accept this is a bona fide process because Narada, Vyasa, and Devala said so — and the scriptures and Srila Prabhupada say so.

## Conclusion: Following the Process Home

- If we follow this process all the way through — beyond anartha-nivritti, through nistha, ruci, bhava, and prema — we will realize Krishna not merely as the creator (*aham sarvasya*, the source of everything) but as the **dearmost friend** of every living entity and the **most attractive and lovable person** in existence.
- We will throw out all the newspapers, radios, nonsense television programs, cinemas, and mundane philosophers and scientists, and accept Srila Prabhupada, the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, and these scriptures.
- And happily we will go **back home, back to Godhead**, and meet personally, face to face, the all-blissful Personality of Godhead.
