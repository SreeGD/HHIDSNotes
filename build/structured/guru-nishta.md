## Welcome: The Appearance Day of Śrīla Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura

- The lecture opens by welcoming all the devotees on this **appearance day of Śrīla Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura**, whose association — along with about thirty Chinese devotees — has kindly blessed the gathering.
- IDS offers *daṇḍavats* to his godbrothers scattered through the audience, and to his godsisters — including **Mother Śītalā**, "hiding way up in the back," who has written (but not yet published) a book **compiled from the various śāstras about Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura.** IDS wanted her to speak, but the subject is very deep and they may not finish even today.
- He feels **very unqualified** to speak, because on such a day the speaker is meant to fully bring forth the glories of such a personality for the audience's benefit — and Narottama is such an illustrious, historic personality in the **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava sampradāya.**

## Why This Is One of the Most Important Days in the Vaiṣṇava Calendar

- We usually consider the prominent days to be **Janmāṣṭamī, Rādhāṣṭamī, Nṛsiṁhadeva's appearance, Lord Nityānanda's appearance, Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Govinda / the Deities' appearance,** and the like — and they are indeed supremely important.
- **But without the mercy of great pure devotees like Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura, we could hardly begin to understand these Personalities of Godhead.** For many present, this is the most important day in the Vaiṣṇava calendar.

## Narottama: A Perfect Bhajanānandī and a Perfect Goṣṭhyānandī

- Narottama is especially dear to the devotees of ISKCON for **two reasons: he was a very deep rasika devotee, and he was a preacher** — a perfect **Bhajanānandī** (absorbed in personal worship) and a perfect **Goṣṭhyānandī** (dedicated to preaching).
- **Principle:** one cannot be a good Goṣṭhyānandī (preacher) unless one is himself a good Bhajanānandī.
- **Precedent — Haridāsa Ṭhākura:** Caitanya Mahāprabhu glorified Śrīla Haridāsa Ṭhākura as the **Nāmācārya** ("spiritual master of the holy name"), qualifying it thus: some devotees practice Krishna consciousness very nicely but don't preach; some preach but don't practice nicely; **"but you, Haridāsa, both practice nicely and preach nicely — therefore you are the ideal example for all devotees."** Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura is another such great example.

## His Writings Are as Good as the Vedas — or Better

- His two most famous books are his **Prārthanā** and **Prema-bhakti-candrikā.** **Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī carried these two books with him everywhere he went** — he was never without them.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** always traveled with **Narahari Ṭhākura's *Narottama-vilāsa*** (which describes many of Narottama's pastimes) — a fact Bhaktivinoda revealed in the **autobiography** that one of his sons had requested him to write before he left this world. Thus here is one great ācārya (Bhaktivinoda) carrying the writings about another great ācārya.
- Narottama's writings are considered **as good as Veda, if not better** — because he is **nitya-siddha**, an eternally liberated soul. They are "better" in the sense that they take us **even deeper** into the understanding of **prema-bhakti** as practiced by the residents of Vṛndāvana / Goloka Vṛndāvana. (You won't find much description of the activities of Vṛndāvana's residents in Vedic scripture; this is given to us by our ācāryas in the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition.)
- A devotee ordinarily quotes **śāstra**, and that is his qualification for speaking; Narottama quoted śāstra over and over in his preaching — yet his own writings are also accepted as śāstra.

## Caitanya Mahāprabhu Predicts Narottama's Appearance

- So extraordinary is Narottama that **Śrī Krishna Caitanya Himself, in full ecstasy, predicted his appearance.**
- It happened when Caitanya Mahāprabhu visited **Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī at Rāmakeli**, near where the **Padma River** flows. As usual, His recreation was going out in **harināma-saṅkīrtana** to distribute the mercy of the holy names.
  - Aside: everyone has their duties — care of the body, management — but the chief activity Lord Caitanya and His associates relished was participating in harināma-saṅkīrtana, singing this very same song (the mahā-mantra) sung at the start of the program.
- In the midst of a particularly ecstatic kīrtana, dancing and twirling among hundreds of associates, Mahāprabhu suddenly began **calling out with great emotion, "Narottama! Narottama!"** — the name of a devotee no one had ever heard of. Chanting his name, **Mahāprabhu fell unconscious on the ground.**
- **Nityānanda** bent close and heard the quiet call, "Narottama, Narottama," and asked, "Mahāprabhu, who is Narottama? None of Your associates has that name — who is he?" Swooning, a smile came over Mahāprabhu's face and He said: **"Narottama is My very dear devotee. In due course of time, when We have left this world, Our mission complete, and gone back to the spiritual world, Narottama will take his birth and continue Our missionary activity."**

## Aside: The Ācārya's Concern That His Work Will Continue

- This is the apprehension of every great ācārya — that his work will continue, that his effectiveness be carried on.
- **Preaching is never easy, never sure and certain in this world.** Inspiring devotees in Krishna consciousness is easy, but inspiring *non*-devotees to take up a regulated life of devotional service and give up sense gratification — "there are not so many people interested."
- **Paris harināma story:** Coming back from harināma-saṅkīrtana in Paris after a particularly difficult day at **Galeries Lafayette** (a big downtown department store) — people shouting, a few pushing, "a little rough" — Prabhupāda, who was visiting the temple, called some of the saṅkīrtana devotees to his room and asked, "How was the kīrtana today? Who was chanting?" IDS and his godbrother Lokṣīn Ānanda raised their hands. Prabhupāda asked IDS, "So how was the chanting?" IDS said, "Prabhupāda, it wasn't very easy." **Prabhupāda got upset, leaned over, and said: "When did I ever say that preaching was easy?"** It is not easy to convince conditioned souls who have "come here to forget Krishna."
- So an ācārya does his work hoping it will carry on after his departure. **Bhaktisiddhānta [Bhaktivinoda] Ṭhākura prayed to Lord Caitanya: "Please send someone from Your personal camp to continue my activities."** And whom did Mahāprabhu send? — the one known in this world as **Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī.**

## Mahāprabhu Entrusts Prema-Bhakti to the Padma River

- To ensure that Narottama would be empowered for his important service, Mahāprabhu stood up and **walked into the nearby Padma River.**
- As soon as His lotus feet touched the water, the river **erupted in transcendental ecstasies** — waves crashing back and forth. (Contrast: IDS notes that today the river crossing to Navadvīpa was very quiet, flat and calm — but the Padma became "like an ocean, crashing down.")
- The river personified — **Padmā / Padmāvatī** — appeared before Lord Caitanya: "My Lord, what is my good fortune that Your holy feet have touched my waters?"
- Holding the treasure in His hand, Caitanya said: **"I have brought Prema-bhakti, which I am distributing in this world. I want you to keep it until a great devotee, Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura, enters your waters — and then give it to him."**
- Padmāvatī asked, "So many people bathe in my waters every day — how will I know who is Narottama?" Mahāprabhu smiled: **"When his lotus feet touch your waters, you will feel the same ecstatic symptoms you feel now, as I have entered your waters."** Then He entrusted the Prema-bhakti to the personified Padmāvatī.

## The Appearance, Naming, and the Astrologer's Warning

- In the course of time — after the departure of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Nityānanda Prabhu, the other members of the Pañca-tattva, and many of their associates — Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura made his appearance, in the family of **King Krishnānanda Datta and his wife Nārāyaṇī.**
- An **astrologer** was called (as is typical in Vedic culture). Observing that the boy's body bore the features of a **Mahāpuruṣa** (a great soul) and casting his chart, he said, "This is a very unusual, very special person." When the king asked what to name the prince, the astrologer, shaking his head, said the only fitting name was **Narottama — "the best amongst men."**
- But he noted **one anomaly**: a certain combination of stars meant **the boy should never bathe in a river, or he would go mad** — so they must keep him away from the great Padma River near the house.

## The Grain-Giving Ceremony (Anna-prāśana)

- At six months came the **grain-giving ceremony**. They brought a cup of sweet rice, put the spoon to the young boy's mouth — and **he pushed it away.** His mother thought this very inauspicious (a child refusing grains on his grain-giving day).
- But his father said, "There must be some explanation," and investigated — discovering that the **pujārī had forgotten to offer the sweet rice to the Deity**; it was still *bhoga.* The father was proud: **"You see our boy — he will not take anything that is not first offered to Viṣṇu."** They offered fresh sweet rice to the Deity, and then the boy ate a great deal of it.

## His Real Education: Hearing the Pastimes of Mahāprabhu

- Narottama grew into a very handsome, super-intelligent boy who did well in and liked school — but his **most important education came in an unusual way.**
- Because his father was a king, various **paṇḍitas, sādhus, and ṛṣis** would visit the palace to share transcendental knowledge (a standard tradition). Every day Narottama looked forward to the sādhu coming; they would sit together under a tree, and the sādhu (Krishna Dāsa) would speak on and on about **Caitanya Mahāprabhu and His pastimes.** The boy never tired of hearing them, "because they are transcendental, and one never gets bored hearing the same pastime over and over."
- **Aside — Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī:** As a young boy Raghunātha Dāsa participated in Mahāprabhu's pastimes in Purī; after Mahāprabhu left, he went to Vṛndāvana and, by the order of Rūpa and Sanātana, stayed at **Rādhā-kuṇḍa for 42 years**, chanting *lakhs* of rounds every day and for six hours a day sitting on the banks of Rādhā-kuṇḍa **telling the pastimes of Caitanya Mahāprabhu for 32 years.** Just like us: we hear the same pastimes, philosophy, stories, and analogies again and again, yet never fall asleep — "always all ears: tell me more, tell it again."
- **The effect of hearing the Lord's pastimes from a Vaiṣṇava:** the heart becomes cleansed of all unwanted things — lust, anger, and greed — and from within the heart develops a very strong attraction to the Lord.
- For nearly **twelve years** Narottama heard from Krishna Dāsa and developed an intense love for **Caitanya, Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, Śrīvāsa** and all their associates.

## The Vision of Lord Nityānanda and the Bath in the Padma

- At the age of twelve, one night, by the Lord's invitation, Narottama had a dream. **Bhagavad-gītā 10.10** — *teṣāṁ satata-yuktānāṁ bhajatāṁ prīti-pūrvakam, dadāmi buddhi-yogaṁ taṁ yena mām upayānti te* — "To those who serve Me with love and devotion, I give the understanding by which they can come to Me." The Lord directs us on the path of devotion.
- Around two o'clock in the morning, **Lord Nityānanda** (who had already departed) appeared to Narottama in the dream — not an ordinary dream but a reality, a vision. Amazed by the effulgent form of Nityānanda, Narottama received the instruction: **"Wake up, boy, get out of bed, go down to the Padma River, and take a bath."**
- Narottama would not disobey Lord Nityānanda. He woke immediately, wrapped a **gamchā** around himself, and walked down to the Padma River as the sun was just rising. He had never bathed there (though his friends and even the buffaloes bathed and swam in it daily). This day, **with full faith in the Lord's instruction, he walked in.**
- As his feet touched the calm, peaceful water, again the water **crashed back and forth in waves**, and Mother Padmāvatī experienced the ecstatic symptoms of love of God. The personified river appeared, holding the liquid nectar **Prema** in her hand: **"On the order of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, I have been waiting for you, and I am convinced that you are Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura."** She poured that liquid Prema over his body.
- His body erupted in the **eight ecstatic symptoms of love**: his voice faltered, the hairs on his body stood up, he cried, he swooned nearly unconscious, uttering the names **"Śrī Krishna Caitanya, Śrī Krishna Caitanya."** Most amazing of all, **his body turned golden — very much like the form of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, like the form of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.**

## Mistaken for a Madman; the Plan of a Beautiful Wife

- A big crowd gathered. His parents received word that their son had gone to the river, bathed, and gone mad. **Nārāyaṇī and Krishnānanda** came to find the prince rolling in the mud of the river, calling out the Lord's names, screaming, foaming at the mouth, hair disheveled. His father cried, **"It's a ghost!"**
- They called an **exorcist** — one who by mantras drives out a ghost — but it did not work. (Interspersed: a light aside adjusting the sound system.) They took the "mad" boy back to his room in the palace and **locked the door**, not wanting the embarrassment of him running around town screaming "Śrī Krishna Caitanya." This went on for months, even a couple of years; everything in the family was interrupted.
- Speaking outside his room, his father said: **"I know what will make him peaceful — a beautiful wife. We haven't chained him to his bed, but we will chain him with the love of a beautiful woman."** Married life, he reasoned, is *śānti, śānti, śānti* — very peaceful.

## Narottama's Desperate Prayer and Mahāprabhu's Answer

- When Narottama heard this plan, he became **desperate.** He fell to his knees and prayed to Lord Caitanya with all the devotion he could muster: **"Please free me from the shackles of materialistic/family life. Let me become Your own. Let me come to You and serve You to my heart's desire."** He cried all night until he cried himself to sleep.
- **Teaching — pure prayers are answered:** Do not think your prayers will not be answered if you pray *amala-bhakti* — praying for the right, pure things, praying for **service.**
- **Aside — Bhaktivinoda Bāṇī** (the compiled instructions of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura): a devotee once asked him, "Ṭhākura, do the sages of yore — the great sages who appeared thousands, or hundreds of thousands, or millions of years ago — hear our humble prayers for devotional service? So much time and distance separate us." Bhaktivinoda closed his eyes, cried for a moment, and said: **"Yes, of course — through the transcendental medium, they not only hear your prayers, but reciprocate with them."**
- So Narottama prayed very sincerely, and this time **Caitanya Mahāprabhu Himself appeared** to him in ecstasy: **"My dear devotee, now it is time for you to go to Vṛndāvana. Leave immediately, out of this room! Go to Vṛndāvana — the middle of the night — take no possessions with you, and take shelter of Śrī Lokanātha Gosvāmī as your spiritual master, and serve Rādhā and Krishna to your heart's desire."**
- **Mystically the locks fell off the door and it opened.** With nothing more than a wrap around his waist (chādar), his **mālā**, and a water pot, the prince — who had every material thing he could ever have desired at that point in history — **walked away from it all** in the service of Rādhā and Krishna.

## The Journey to Vṛndāvana: Soldiers and the Heart Already Given

- To avoid detection, he did not take the normal route but walked **through the jungle.** A prince with soft hands (no calluses) and tender feet, fed only the best foods, now walked through dangerous jungle full of rocks and brambles — his feet and arms cut and scratched, his cloth torn, hardly eating or drinking. At one point he fell unconscious, then got up and kept walking.
- Discovering he had gone, his parents sent **ten soldiers** after him. They caught up and grabbed him: "You rascal boy! You ran away from home!" Fully absorbed in thinking of Rādhā and Krishna, Narottama folded his hands, his eyes full of tears of love, and said: **"Sirs, please consider — once you have given your heart to a lover, you cannot give that heart to someone else; that would be a violation of culture. I have already given my heart to Rādhā and Krishna and Vṛndāvana, so I could not give my heart to my family."** The soldiers mystically agreed and said, "Continue your path — quickly, quickly, because the associates of Caitanya Mahāprabhu are gradually leaving this world; go quickly."

## Arriving at Vraja: The Loss of Rūpa and Sanātana

- After months of travel, he finally reached the outskirts of Vraja, thinking: **"I am so unfortunate — the most unfortunate of any human being. I missed seeing Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Nityānanda, Advaita, all these great associates. At least I will meet Rūpa Gosvāmī and Sanātana Gosvāmī in Vṛndāvana — that is my great fortune."**
- Along the way he saw a **brāhmaṇa** sitting by the roadside, despondent and crying. Narottama asked, "Sir, could you please tell me the quickest route to Vṛndāvana, so I may take shelter of the lotus feet of Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī?"
- The brāhmaṇa looked up with eyes red from many hours of crying: **"You don't know? You didn't hear? Both Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī recently left this world — just a week or two ago — for Goloka Vṛndāvana."**
- Narottama fell unconscious on the ground; when he woke and remembered the news, he fell unconscious again; then woke once more: **"Now I have lost the association of Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī — what hope is there for me to become Krishna conscious?"** The only thing that gave him hope and made him stand up and continue was **Caitanya Mahāprabhu's instruction to take shelter of Lokanātha Gosvāmī.**

## Aside: What Real Transcendental Attachment Is

- His extreme lamentation is something we cannot even begin to appreciate, because **we have not yet really felt what real transcendental attachment to Hari, Guru, and Vaiṣṇava is.** We think we have attachment, but we don't know what real attachment is.
- **Prabhupāda's remark:** A devotee once said, "Prabhupāda, that kīrtana was ecstasy," and Prabhupāda looked at him and said, **"You do not know what is ecstasy."**

## Jīva Gosvāmī Receives Narottama in Vṛndāvana

- Somehow, in that state of extreme lamentation, he made it to Vṛndāvana. At that time there were only a few temples — three or four — one of them the **Rādhā-Govinda temple, the temple of Rūpa Gosvāmī** (still standing today, though no longer in use).
- Hearing that Narottama had come, **Jīva Gosvāmī came running like the wind** to the Govindajī temple and stood over Narottama, who lay unconscious: "Wake up, boy — wake, come, stand." Narottama opened his eyes and asked, "Who are you?" — "I am Jīva Gosvāmī." Jīva pulled him up and embraced him in loving ecstasy.
- Jīva said: **"Come, I will introduce you to a very wonderful devotee — the first of the Gosvāmīs to come to Vṛndāvana. He is very, very old now, sitting under a tree doing his nāma-japa. His name is Lokanātha Gosvāmī."** Narottama replied, "Yes — I must go and fulfill the instruction of Mahāprabhu to take initiation from Lokanātha Gosvāmī, because without initiation there is no chance I can get the shelter of Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Take me, take me."

## Meeting Lokanātha Gosvāmī and Begging for Initiation

- Jīva took him to a secluded **kuñja** (bower of trees). There Lokanātha — very old, the first of the Gosvāmīs at Vṛndāvana — was absorbed in his nāma-japa, tears flowing, chanting. Jīva said, "Gosvāmījī, I have one very nice devotee who would like to meet you; his name is Narottama." Immediately Lokanātha came out of his absorption: **"Ah, Narottama — we have all heard of you."**
- Narottama wasted no time and begged for initiation: **"I can chant and chant and chant, but I will not get Prema unless you initiate me into the glories of the Holy Name."**
- Lokanātha demurred: "How could I initiate you? You are a prince; the Lord has appeared to you in dreams; your fame precedes you wherever you go — you are the great Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura, protected by Caitanya Mahāprabhu. How could a humble devotee like myself initiate a person like you?" Narottama, crying: "No, no, don't say that — I am your humble servant." "No, you are the prince; I am your servant." Lokanātha concluded: **"I do not accept disciples."**

## Aside: Determination in Krishna Consciousness

- **One must be very determined to make progress in Krishna consciousness.**
- As quoted from **Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Padyāvalī*:** just as young boys are attracted to young girls and young girls to young boys — "and that is quite intense" — with that same intensity and determination, "my Lord, let me be attracted to Your lotus feet, but in a transcendental way." With as much determination as we once had for fulfilling our material desires, we must have the same eagerness for Krishna's lotus feet — but in a transcendental way, with a heart purified by chanting Hare Krishna.

## Narottama's Secret Menial Service

- Determined that he must receive initiation ("it is Mahāprabhu's order; I cannot disobey"), Narottama observed that every morning Lokanātha Gosvāmī went to a certain place in the forest to answer the calls of nature, leaving that dirty place behind.
- Narottama would **follow him and hide in the trees**; after Lokanātha left, he would go and **clean that place very nicely** — burying everything, putting fresh sand, bringing water, changing the pot/bucket for purifying the hands — making everything neat, clean, and orderly, then going home. Each morning Lokanātha would find everything organized and clean, and wonder: **"Who is doing this? Who is cleaning up after my stool?"**

## Aside: No Service Is Too Low — Humility Is the Crest Jewel

- We should **not be afraid of menial service.** Any service — even an iota — is "like a precious gem, after millions and millions of years of being deprived of devotional service, only serving our senses and getting no real pleasure, only suffering." We are happy just to sweep the temple, clean the latrine, wash the devotees' dishes, or wash their clothes — "this is nectar; after millions of years I get some service to Hari, Guru, and Vaiṣṇava; we cherish that."
- A devotee **does not distinguish between higher and lower service** — it is all nectar, all *cintāmaṇi.* **Śikṣāṣṭaka 4** — *na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ... mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi* — "I do not want money, I do not want illicit association, I do not want fame and prestige; all that I want is Your service, birth after birth (*mama janmani janmani*)." Not: "The service I want is to be a GBC, a sannyāsī guru with lots of followers and money, money, money" — but simply, "just give me service."
- **Humility is the crest jewel of all the devotee's fine qualities**, demonstrated by being willing — and very happy — to do any service one is asked to do.

## Lokanātha's Heart Melts: The Initiation

- This went on for several weeks. One day Lokanātha Gosvāmī came very early to catch who was cleaning; hiding in the bush, he saw Narottama cleaning the area **just as a pujārī cleans the altar.** He came out: "Narottama, what are you doing? You are a prince — why are you doing this?"
- Narottama turned, saw his Guru Mahārāja, and said: **"Guru Mahārāja, I have rejected the useless wealth of this world for the riches of devotional service to your two lotus feet."** Lokanātha asked, "But what do you hope to obtain from this menial service?" — "I want you to be my master." — "But again, how can I accept you? Mahāprabhu has blessed you in so many ways."
- Narottama's **menial service, his humility, and his intense desire — his "greed" to get the mercy of Krishna — melted the heart of Lokanātha Gosvāmī**, who said, "Alright, I will initiate you."
- On an auspicious day he gave him **dīkṣā**, performing the ceremony according to a new book that had just been compiled — the **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** (written by **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī**) — following the system given there.

## Sent to Study Under Jīva Gosvāmī

- Lokanātha was old and needed some personal service; after some time he said, "That is enough. Now you must get an education." (These are different levels of association with one's Guru Mahārāja.)
- Lokanātha called **Jīva Gosvāmī** and told Narottama: **"Now you will study the bhakti-śāstras under Jīva Gosvāmī."**

## Living at Rādhā-Dāmodara and the Story of the Deity

- Narottama lived in the sacred **Rādhā-Dāmodara temple** and observed the wonderful pūjā arranged there by Jīva Gosvāmī for his beloved Deity, **Rādhā-Dāmodara.**
- The **Dāmodara Deity** (about a hand's height) was **hand-carved by Rūpa Gosvāmī** and given to Jīva Gosvāmī. Rūpa took the stone and carved Krishna "just like He is." Where is such a description in śāstra? How did Rūpa know? — **Because he is the favorite servant of Lalitā; in the spiritual sky he is Rūpa Mañjarī, a chief assistant of Rādhārāṇī, who sees Krishna "here, there, everywhere."** So he carved this Deity of Dāmodara and gave it to Jīva Gosvāmī as his **Iṣṭadeva.**

## Narottama's Manasa-sevā and the Burned Hands

- By observing that pūjā, hearing Jīva Gosvāmī's classes, and chanting alongside him, Narottama's **prema was awakening very quickly.** He was doing **Mānasa-pūjā / Mānasa-sevā** — serving Krishna on the platform of the purified mind.
- Because he advanced so quickly, one time in a state of Mānasa-sevā he saw himself in his **eternal form as a gopī** serving Rādhā and Krishna, **assisting the sakhī Campakalatā.** Campakalatā was boiling milk for Krishna's cooking and asked **Vilāsa Mañjarī** (the eternal form of Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura) to watch the milk while she attended to some important service.
- While Vilāsa Mañjarī watched, the milk started to boil over and get wasted; she grabbed the pot to take it off the fire and **slightly burned her hands.** The burning was so hot that Narottama **came out of his mānasa-sevā** — and looked at his hands to find them **actually burned.** He went and showed **Jīva Gosvāmī** (his beloved teacher, his śikṣā-guru), who just smiled and said, **"Very nice."**

## Fellow Students and Their Titles

- While studying with Jīva Gosvāmī, two other boys joined the school — **Śyāmānanda and Śrīnivāsa.** The three became very close, their camaraderie being the real friendship of serving Guru and Gaurāṅga, and they did very well in their studies.
- On graduation, Jīva Gosvāmī gave them **titles**: to Narottama, the name **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura**; to Śyāmānanda, **Śyāmānanda Paṇḍita**; and to Śrīnivāsa (a little older, sharp, intelligent, an organizer type — "like our organizers with the GBC, or Tamal Krishna Goswami"), the name **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya.**

## Raghava Paṇḍita and the Parikramā of Vṛndāvana

- To further educate the boys after graduation, Jīva Gosvāmī took them to meet a hermit who lived in a cave next to **Govardhana Hill** — the great devotee of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, **Raghava Paṇḍita**, who did his bhajana inside a very dark cave in Govardhana, all in darkness, chanting Hare Krishna.
- Raghava Paṇḍita took **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura and Śrīnivāsa Ācārya on parikramā around Vṛndāvana** — described in detail in the **Bhakti-ratnākara.**

## The Mission to Bengal: Carrying the Gosvāmīs' Books

- Jīva Gosvāmī told the three boys to take the books of the Gosvāmīs to **Bengal**, because the Bengali Vaiṣṇavas were not getting any books — all the books were being written in Vṛndāvana. (Navadvīpa and Vṛndāvana are non-different — "the same transcendental abode.") The Bengalis were asking for **Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Sanātana Gosvāmī's *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*, Rūpa's *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*** — "come on, send us some books."
- So Jīva said: **"Take this bullock cart of all the books of the Gosvāmīs — the original copies — deliver them, they can copy them, and they will come back."**

## The Farewells in Vṛndāvana

- Before leaving, Narottama went to see **Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī at Rādhā-kuṇḍa**, fell at his feet, and cried, "How will I live without your association?" They embraced, both crying.
- Then he went to his Guru Mahārāja in the kuñja and fell at the lotus feet of **Lokanātha Gosvāmī:** "Guru Mahārāja, I have to leave you; I have to go on a mission." Lokanātha said, "That is very great — you have served the Guru. I give you **two instructions** you must always remember: **always remember Krishna by serving the Deities, and always be engaged in spreading the saṅkīrtana mission of Caitanya Mahāprabhu.**"
- Narottama was so happy simply to receive an instruction.

## Aside: The Joy of Receiving the Guru's Instruction

- **Prabhupāda too received an instruction** (from his guru): "You look like a nice educated man — why don't you spread the teachings of Caitanya Mahāprabhu in the English-speaking language?"
- **IDS's own experience:** hoping for a personal instruction, he was in Prabhupāda's room on the way to France in **1972** — to translate the books into French, distribute them, make devotees, and get temples. Prabhupāda leaned on the table and said, **"Preach boldly and have faith in the holy names."** IDS said, "Thank you, Prabhupāda."

## Departure for Bengal; Theft of the Books

- Jīva Gosvāmī arranged a **farewell party**; all the great devotees of Vṛndāvana came and gave their blessings to Narottama, Śrīnivāsa, and Śyāmānanda. Jīva walked with them and the bullock cart all the way to **Mathurā**, where there was a goodbye ceremony.
- Traveling nearly to Bengal, they came to a town called **Rāṇā Viṣṇupura** and fell asleep one night. That night the local king sent his soldiers to steal the "treasure." When the boys woke in the morning, **all the books were gone**, and they were devastated.
- But **a voice came from the sky**, like a loud oracle: **"Do not worry. The books will be returned."** **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** took this as a divine sign: "All right, I will find the books — but preaching is so important. You two go to your assigned places: Śyāmānanda, go to **Utkala**; Narottama, go to **Keturī**; and start preaching what you learned in the gurukula of Jīva Gosvāmī. Go now, immediately; I will find the books."

## Narottama Returns to Keturī

- Narottama had left home as a prince (having run away); he returned home a very famous personality — everyone in Keturī knew Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura. His father **Krishnānanda Datta** and mother **Nārāyaṇī** had passed away, and his cousin **Santoṣa** was now the king.
- When Santoṣa met Narottama, he said, **"The kingdom is yours."** Narottama replied, **"I am not interested in the kingdom."** He went outside the town, built a **little grass hut**, and sat there chanting **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare...** — and he began **preaching and accepting disciples.**

## Journey to Navadvīpa: The Town of Separation

- Narottama wanted to go to **Navadvīpa** to get the mercy of the great souls before they departed — "so many have left, but a few are still there." He knew everyone in Navadvīpa was living in a state of **deep vipralambha-bhāva** — deep separation, deep depression — because Mahāprabhu had passed away; he had heard the whole town was depressed.
- But when he entered Navadvīpa, it was **very bright and colorful** — people walking, laughing, joking, singing, and dancing. "Wait — that is not how I heard Navadvīpa was after the departure of Caitanya Mahāprabhu." He thought perhaps it had only been a rumor, perhaps Mahāprabhu had not left, and he began singing and dancing with the people amid bugles, flowers being thrown, babies laughing — everybody happy. People said, "We are going to participate in kīrtana — **Nitāi-Gaura!**" — and Narottama thought maybe it was all false, maybe they were still here, so he would also join the kīrtana.
- **Then all of a sudden the whole scene disappeared.** The streets were deserted; there was no noise except the **howling of the devotees, feeling the pain of separation from Gaura-Nitāi.**
- **Narahari Sarkar** describes the scene: even years after Mahāprabhu's departure, it was such devastation and lamentation that **the Gaṅgā was neither going this way nor that — standing still**; the cows' mouths were half-open to bite the grass but never closed, stunned in separation; the calves' mouths were on their mothers' milk-bags but they were not sucking milk; the bees hovered above the flowers but their wings were not even moving; the birds sat in the trees but did not sing; the people on the street stood **still like statues.** Narottama realized: "This is Navadvīpa after the departure of Caitanya Mahāprabhu."

## Aside: The Mood of Separation

- This helps us understand the **mood of separation.** **Śikṣāṣṭaka 7** — *yugāyitaṁ nimeṣeṇa...* — "considering a moment to be like twelve years; tears flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain; feeling all the world vacant in Your absence."
- **Gaura Govinda Mahārāja:** when a disciple asked, "What really is Krishna consciousness? How would you summarize its essence?" he answered, **"To cry, cry, cry, and cry."** Don't think, "I don't want to do that" — this is all on the **transcendental platform**, hard for us to grasp.
- In the mood of separation there is **intense remembrance of the Lord, and one's love increases a thousandfold**; therefore the devotee **cherishes** that separation. On this transcendental level the devotee actually feels completely happy in remembrance of God. In **meeting**, the devotee is concentrated on one particular activity of service; but in **separation**, the mind ranges over all the different aspects and activities of the Lord — so it is, in a sense, **even more relishable**: the love in separation exceeds the love in meeting.

## Sukumbara Brahmacārī Announces Narottama's Arrival

- As Narottama walked through this devastating scene — already feeling the pain of having missed the association of Mahāprabhu and of Rūpa and Sanātana — a devotee named **Sukumbara Brahmacārī** walked up and asked, "Excuse me, who are you?" Narottama said, "I am Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura, your humble servant."
- Sukumbara started dancing and turning: **"Haribol! Haribol! Narottama has come! Narottama has come — everybody, Narottama!"** He said, "You must come and meet all the devotees and give them light. We have heard that **you will rejuvenate the mission of Caitanya Mahāprabhu.** Oh, you look so much like Him — you are golden, just like Mahāprabhu!"

## Blessings of the Departed Associates

- He took Narottama to the house of **Dāmodara Paṇḍita**, a great devotee of Mahāprabhu. When Dāmodara Paṇḍita saw him, he embraced him and said, **"You will execute the will and the mission of the Lord."** Narottama began crying — feeling so blessed with the Lord's mission, yet feeling so unqualified, and longing for the mercy to be able to do it.
- **All the associates of Caitanya Mahāprabhu who had already departed were standing there before him** — not visible to ordinary eyes, but present — hundreds of them. One by one they came up, **embraced Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura, and gave him their blessings** to continue the mission of Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Because his mission was so important, he needed a great deal of mercy. **"Fortune favors the bold."**

## Gathering Mercy at Śāntipura and Kharadaha

- Narottama then went to nearby **Śāntipura** and met **Achyutānanda and Śrī Gopāla, the two sons of Advaita Ācārya**, to receive even more mercy. Achyutānanda told him: **"After you visit Purī, go back to Keturī, where you will popularize the saṅkīrtana movement like never before."**
- On the way to Purī he visited **Kharadaha**, where he met **Vasudhā and Mother Jāhnavā** (the wife of Lord Nityānanda), and received their grace.

## Gadādhara Paṇḍita — Humble Vaiṣṇava, Bold Preacher

- Further on he met **Gadādhara Paṇḍita**, described as the abode/expansion of **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.** Narottama was especially eager for Gadādhara Paṇḍita's mercy, because Gadādhara — though very humble and meek, always begging the mercy of the Vaiṣṇavas' lotus feet and trying to serve them in menial ways — **was also a very powerful, brave preacher.**
- **A story about Gadādhara Paṇḍita (begins):** He lived in a region of Bengal where there was a very cruel **Kazi.** The Kazi was lenient with Muslims when they committed some mistake, but with Hindus — even the slightest error in society — he would punish, torture, or kill them. He outlawed all public demonstrations of religion except for Muslims, and had a particular hatred for the Vaiṣṇavas: he could not stand it when the Vaiṣṇavas would chant — on their beads, in their houses, or in public — **"Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma..."**
- *[The transcript ends here, mid-story, as the account of Gadādhara Paṇḍita and the Kazi is beginning.]*
