## Opening: Reading Bhagavad-gita 7.3

- Invocation: *Om namo bhagavate vasudevaya.*
- **Bhagavad-gita 7.3** — *manuṣyāṇāṁ sahasreṣu kaścid yatati siddhaye / yatatām api siddhānāṁ kaścin māṁ vetti tattvataḥ* — "Out of many thousands among men, one may endeavor for perfection, and of those who have achieved perfection, hardly one knows Me in truth."

### Purport read from the book (Srila Prabhupada's commentary)

- There are various grades of men, and out of many thousands one may be sufficiently interested in transcendental realization to try to know what is the Self, what is the body, and what is the Absolute Truth. Generally mankind is engaged only in the animal propensities — **eating, sleeping, defending, and mating** — and hardly anyone is interested in transcendental knowledge.
- The first six chapters of the Gita are meant for those interested in transcendental knowledge — understanding the Self, the Superself, and the process of realization by *jnana-yoga* and discrimination of the self from matter.
- However, **Krishna can be known only by persons who are Krishna conscious.** Other transcendentalists may achieve impersonal Brahman realization, which is easier than understanding Krishna. Krishna is the Supreme Person, but at the same time He is beyond the knowledge of Brahman and Paramatma; the yogis and jnanis are confused in their attempts to understand Him.
- Although the greatest of the impersonalists, **Shankaracarya**, admitted in his Gita commentary that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, his followers do not accept Krishna as such; it is very difficult to know Krishna even with realization of impersonal Brahman.
- **Brahma-samhita 5.1** (paraphrased): "Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the cause of all causes, the primeval Lord Govinda" — *ishvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam.*
- It is very difficult for non-devotees to know Him. Although non-devotees declare the path of *bhakti* (devotional service) is very easy, they cannot practice it. If it is so easy, why do they take up the difficult path instead? Actually, the path of bhakti is not easy — the so-called bhakti practiced by unauthorized persons without knowledge of bhakti may be easy, but when performed factually according to the rules and regulations, the speculative scholars and philosophers fall away.

## The Yuga Cycle: A Progressive Decline

- This verse was spoken at the end of **Dvapara-yuga**, the next-to-last stage. Just as the year passes through seasons (spring "has sprung," then summer, then fall/autumn), the material creation passes through **Satya-yuga, Treta-yuga, Dvapara-yuga, and Kali-yuga** — each successively worse.
- This contrasts with modern scientific theory that things go from worse to better (that we are "more evolved" now than millions of years ago). Vedic science says the reverse.
- **Satya-yuga** — the Golden Age: 99.999% of the population was very interested in liberation, getting out of *samsara* (the repetition of birth and death). They took their spiritual vows very seriously and kept things simple so they could become liberated. Their motto: **"Simple living, high thinking."** (Nowadays life is so complicated we struggle to find a few hours for a program like this.)
- **Treta-yuga** — the Silver Age: the percentage interested in spiritual life dropped significantly, to about **75%**.
- **Dvapara-yuga** — the Copper Age (over millions of years later): only **50%** were seriously interested in the goal of life, in Krishna consciousness; toward its end this decreased most significantly.
- At the tail end of Dvapara-yuga — just over **5,000 years ago** — Bhagavan Sri Krishna appeared (not in Kali-yuga). Observing the situation, He spoke this verse: *manushyanam sahasreshu... kascid yatate siddhaye* — out of thousands and millions, only a few (the *sadhus, rishis, yogis*) are interested in the science of self-realization, and "hardly one has understood Me in truth," realized Bhagavan/God — although that is the purpose of life. A lamentable situation.
- **Kali-yuga:** What would the Lord think now? The percentage serious about Krishna consciousness has dropped to **25%** — and the speaker's experience is that it is deteriorating very rapidly.

### The Onset of Kali-yuga

- Kali-yuga began when the lotus feet of the Lord left this planet, His mission accomplished.
- **Bhagavad-gita 4.8** (paraphrased — *paritranaya sadhunam vinashaya ca dushkritam*): He came to deliver His devotees 5,000 years ago, to annihilate the miscreants — like Kamsa, Shishupala, Dantavakra, and Jarasandha — and to re-establish the principles of religion (*dharma, sanatana-dharma*). As soon as His mission was accomplished, He left; He does not waste time, for His devotees are waiting for Him in the spiritual sky.
- As soon as His lotus feet lifted just one inch above the earth, the darkness of ignorance began to prevail. **Sunset analogy:** the sun may be very bright, and everyone loves to watch it set, but after it sets we don't linger — darkness prevails, and darkness is scary; we lock ourselves in our houses at night and don't play outside in the dark. Darkness is compared to ignorance, which began to prevail on this earth.

## Vedanta-sutra — The Vedas in Code Form

- **"pato brahma-jijnasa"** — reference to the **Vedanta-sutra**, which is like the Vedas in code form.
- **Morse code analogy:** In the military the speaker was obliged to learn Morse code, because you cannot always speak openly over the radio (or nowadays internet/TV) — enemies may find out your plans. On the battlefield they used Morse code. "Sutra" means code; the Vedanta-sutra is the Vedas in code.
- The **Veda** is the vast body of works the Lord gave human society to liberate themselves from materialistic life, awaken love for God, and go back to the spiritual world. But it is vast — the four Vedas, the Upanishads, the Itihasas, the Puranas, and supplementary literatures like the **Mahabharata** and **Ramayana**. Who has time in Kali-yuga? We are all cramming for university exams.
- So the Lord gave the Vedanta-sutra — that knowledge in code form, so you can grasp it quickly. Time is short: previous ages lived far longer (in earlier ages hundreds of thousands of years; in the last days of Dvapara-yuga, a thousand years) — "now you're lucky if you live a hundred years."
- Modern parallel: traveling the world, the speaker sees signs like **"English in 10 easy lessons."** The idea is the same — because time is short, the Vedanta-sutra delivers the message quickly.

### The First Aphorism: "athato brahma-jijnasa"

- What is the first *shloka*/aphorism of the Vedanta-sutra? What do our ancient **rishis** — our *true* forefathers (not the seminal line of father, grandfather, great-grandfather, but the rishis, yogis, and sadhus) — say?
- They say: **"Now that we have come to the human form of life, now is the time for self-realization."** They are speaking to us across the eons of time. It is not that the knowledge of Vedanta, Upanishads, Puranas, Itihasas, and Vedas was only for our forefathers and is no longer needed. No.

### Two Paths: Pravritti-marga and Nivritti-marga

- There are only **two paths**:
  - **Pravritti-marga** — the path of material enjoyment.
  - **Nivritti-marga** — the path of gradual renunciation of material life, evolving into *mukti* (liberation), culminating in divine love for the Supreme Lord — the soul's original constitutional position.
- (Recollection: as a 17-year-old in high school, called before a group of teachers to choose his career/college for "the rest of your life," he just wanted to go to the beach.) Raised in Christianity, they used to say: "You take the high road, I'll take the low road."
- Actually, since we are in this world, we must **balance** the two — we cannot be artificial and run away to the forest. We must find the proper balance; now things are out of balance, too much material and too little spiritual.
- So: **If you consider the goal of life to be material comfort, pleasure, and progress, this book is meaningless to you. But if the goal of life is to awaken your divine consciousness — who you are, who is God, and what is your intimate loving relationship with God — then this book is your most valuable possession.**

He may not have understood it in perfection (he did not necessarily consider himself a Vishnu-bhakta), but he preached the eternal wisdom of the Gita to his countrymen and understood its supreme importance.

- Therefore this book, Bhagavad-gita, is as important now (he dates it "March 24th, 2007") as 5,000 years ago, and will be as important 5,000 years hence, because the purpose of life — Krishna consciousness — is the prime duty. The boy was left speechless.

## Intelligence: The Human Being's Special Gift

- **Bhagavad-gita 18.70** (paraphrased): One who studies the *shastra* / Gita "worships Me with his intelligence." God gave us intelligence for this purpose; it is our most valuable commodity, our most important quality.
- No other species has such advanced intelligence: **even the stupidest man is more intelligent than the smartest animal.**
- There are **8,400,000 species of life**, as the Vedas / God says — God knows more than the scientists. What have they seen? Like an ant in the corner of a room with a two-inch telescope — what can they see of this room, town, state, country, world, universe, with their limited minds and imperfect senses? We have to know from God, the creator: if you create something, you know it inside and out; logically, to know about it, you should ask the maker. God's own words (the Veda) state He created 8,400,000 species, of which the human is just one.

### God Respects All Life, But Emphasizes the Human Form

- **Bhagavad-gita 5.18** (*vidya-vinaya-sampanne brahmane gavi hastini / shuni caiva shva-pake ca panditah sama-darshinah*): The learned sage (rishi/yogi) gives equal respect to a learned brahmana/pandit, to an elephant, to a cow, to a dog, and to a dog-eater — because he realizes that although the *bodies* differ, the *souls* are equal.
- Special emphasis is placed on the human form because of intelligence. God is like a father — equal to all His children, blessing each with a different gift. He has endowed every species with something a little special and unique.

### The Gifts God Gave Each Species

- **Birds** — the ability to fly effortlessly. (For a human to "fly," you must work, save money, call the travel agent, book, drive to the airport, wait in long security lines — the speaker notes taking almost 21 flights across the country since arriving, almost daily, with long security lines. A bird just... flies.)

- **Butterflies** — always beautiful by God's design; they don't have to put on or take off makeup. (Humorous digression about ladies' efforts to be beautiful — kajal (black), plus green, blue, yellow, pink; powder to change skin tone; "ink" on the lips; dyeing the hair blonde, black, brunette, brown, changing daily — "and you get the real picture when you wake up in the morning.")
- **Human beings** — standing at the 8,400,000th place, "last but not least," we got the most important gift: **buddhi** (intelligence). This is **buddhi-yoga**, the yoga of intelligence, which Krishna speaks of.

### What Intelligence Really Means

- Intelligence means **to see things in their proper perspective.**
- Child's test analogy: give a small child (about one and a half years) a board with a round hole and a square hole, plus a round peg. If he is intelligent, he puts the round peg in the round hole — he sees things in the proper perspective. Intelligent people are organized, manage their time, study, and "get the picture" quickly.
- But true intelligence does not mean a high IQ. It means to **see the world as the Lord explains it in Bhagavad-gita** — to see beyond *maya* ("that which is not"), beyond the illusion that keeps us bound here birth after birth.
- **How the sadhu sees the world:** he does not see through his eyes but through his **ears** — he hears from high authority (guru, saints, God), who have a clear picture through their controlled minds and senses, pure hearts, and divine wisdom.

## The Real Nature of This World

- **Bhagavad-gita 8.16** (*abrahma-bhuvanal lokah punar avartino 'rjuna / mam upetya tu kaunteya punar janma na vidyate*): "From the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery where repeated birth and death take place. But one who attains My abode, O Arjuna, never takes birth again." **This is intelligence** — to perceive the world this way, not to think one can make a permanent settlement here and live forever happily.

### Yudhisthira and the Yaksha's Questions

- The brothers (Pandavas) went hunting, came to a lake, drank the water, and appeared to die. **Yudhisthira** went looking, found them lying on the bank, and lamented, "Oh my brothers, if only I could bring you back to life."
- A voice from the sky — **Dharmaraja / Yamaraja** — said: "If you answer the following twelve questions correctly, I will bring your brothers back to life." (This is a famous set of questions in the **Mahabharata**.)
- The last question: **"What is the most amazing thing in this world?"** (Not the Leaning Tower of Pisa or the Eiffel Tower or the wonders of the world.)
- Yudhisthira answered: **"Every moment, hundreds and millions of living entities are going to the kingdom of death all around me — yet I think I can stay here permanently to be happy. This is the most amazing thing."** Yamaraja declared he had answered correctly, and his brothers — Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna, Bhima — were revived.
- The lesson: we are all under the spell, thinking we will be here a long time to enjoy. But **"time and tide wait for no man."** We are here only a moment in cosmic time — like a drop of water on a lotus flower (or, for Western audiences who don't know the lotus, "a drop of water on a duck's back") — it is there for a moment and gone. That is our lifespan. The question is: **Are you preparing for the inevitable moment of death?**

## Living in the Future — and Never Being Happy

- We are always planning for the future:
  - As children, parents push us to excel in school so we will have a good career, so we will have money to pay the bills, so life won't be difficult. Kids today are so career-oriented that the best years of their life — youth — are given away for the future.
  - When we finally graduate and get a job, we put a good percentage aside through **Social Security** — again, for the future, because after 62 or 65 it becomes physically difficult to work (and you get "senior moments"), yet you may have 15–20 years left. The money is taken from your paycheck throughout your career for that future.
- We are practically **living in the future**, never happy now: "If I just get that girl / that boy / that job / build that house / take that trip, then I'll be happy." Because all our plans are materialistic, they never touch the real thing — the soul. Only on the spiritual platform is there real satisfaction.
- **Satisfying the senses is like pouring kerosene on a fire** — it does not extinguish it but makes it greater. The longer we live, our desires grow stronger and stronger, and we are not happy. We keep thinking "tomorrow will be a better day."
- Unfortunately we do not realize that **the future means eternity.** One day we must die and leave behind everything we worked for; **you cannot take a single penny.** And that death may be just around the corner. Where will you go?

This is the dichotomy of a strong Christian country that believes in eternity, yet teaches in its schools the **Darwinian theory of evolution**, which is atheistic — no life after death, no God in control, everything controlled by mechanistic laws.

### The Soul Passing Through Bodies

- Every Indian child (in the old days, "now they're singing Britney Spears") used to sing these verses. **Bhagavad-gita 2.13** (*dehino 'smin yatha dehe kaumaram yauvanam jara / tatha dehantara-praptih...*): "As the embodied soul continually passes from the body of a boy to youth to old age, so the soul passes into another body at death."
- This is **not outdated knowledge**, not religious dogma, not "just philosophy" — these are the laws of nature, the facts of life, the mechanics of this world. The body is constantly in flux; one thing remains the same: the **soul**.
- **Science parallel:** In pre-medical study, we learn that every seven years all the cells of the body change — meaning every seven years you have a new body. At 21 you have already had three bodies. Sitting here, billions of cells have died and been replaced; you don't even have the same body you walked in with. Yet the soul remains unaffected. **Having survived the change of so many bodies, you will survive the change of the final body.**
- **And where will you go? You don't know.** You cannot stand up and say, "Yes sir, I am going here." If you don't know, we must classify ourselves as fools.
- **Vacation analogy:** You pack the car, everyone's excited, the wife says "finally, a vacation" — and when they ask, "Where are we going?" you say, "I haven't got a clue." We laugh — but can *you* stand up now and say where you will go at death? If you cannot say even approximately, it is not something to laugh at but to cry about.

### Desire Determines Your Destination

- **Bhagavad-gita 8.6** (*yam yam vapi smaran bhavam tyajaty ante kalevaram...*): "Whatever mentality one has at the moment of death determines one's next state of being." In other words, your **desires** carry you to your next body.
- Analogy: In a couple of hours everyone in this room will leave and go to different places — one home to sleep, one to the store, one to get the mail, one to visit a friend — because we have different desires. At the moment of death there are **8,400,000 destinations** — not "choices" you can select, but destinations determined by how you have used or abused this life. (Like the final day of school: you don't choose your university; you go to the one your grades allow.)
- You should be concerned, because it is *your* future — the same future you are so concerned about materially.

A sensible person, though it's tough luck, finds a new apartment, puts down a deposit, and slowly moves furniture over, so that when the bulldozers appear on the horizon, he simply takes his last bag and moves in.

## Can Science Conquer Death? The Sculptor and Yamaraja

- The speaker read a scientific journal that night — "old wine in new bottles" of atheism. The scientists claim that within the coming century they will **first conquer all diseases, then conquer old age, and by the century's end make man immortal.** ("Do you think you'll live long enough to see it?")
- This recalled a story told by **Srila Prabhupada**:
  - There was an expert **sculptor/potter** who consulted an **astrologer** (a proper Vedic science). He asked when he would die. Astrologers usually don't tell, because most people aren't ready — but this one told him: "You will die next year, May 22nd, at 12 noon."
  - Troubled, the sculptor resolved: "No, I won't let Yamaraja take me. I'm too smart. I'll trick death." As April came ("April showers bring May flowers"), he began sculpting nine lifelike **dioramas of himself** — so expert that no one could tell the difference — in different positions (one talking, one lying down, etc.).
  - On May 22nd, at 10 a.m. he was laughing at his "cheap death." At 11:45, 11:50, 11:55... 11:59, he **froze in position among his nine images.**
  - Then **Yamaraja** arrived. (He is not a fictitious cartoon character like Spider-Man; great sages like **Vyasadeva** — seers of the truth in full control of mind and senses — speak extensively of such divine personalities in the Vedic scriptures, giving "the whole truth and nothing but the truth.") A very busy man, since every moment millions go to the kingdom of death, Yamaraja for a moment could not tell which was the real sculptor. Inside, the sculptor was laughing: "I've cheated death! I am immortal!" — betraying himself only by the joy bubbling over.
  - But Yamaraja — one of the **twelve Mahajanas**, one of the twelve authorities in the science of Krishna consciousness / bhakti — is no fool. (This movement is authorized, time-tested, and proven: follow the path and you will get liberation and love of God; "you judge a thing by the result.")
  - Yamaraja spoke aloud: "Look at this artwork — it's really shabby, so many faults. The hair is out of proportion, the color of the left ear is off, the leg is two centimeters too long. Disgusting — a second-class artist!" The potter could not resist: **"What do you mean? I'm known as one of the best artists!"** Yamaraja: **"In hell. You're coming with me, sir."**

## The Kali-yuga Concession: The Holy Name

- **Brihan-naradiya Purana** (*harer nama harer nama harer namaiva kevalam / kalau nasty eva nasty eva nasty eva gatir anyatha*): "In the age of Kali there is no other way, no other way, no other way" than the holy name. (*Purana* means ancient knowledge — this verse was given hundreds of thousands / millions of years ago, before Kali-yuga came.)
- The easiest and most sublime means of becoming self-realized and God-conscious, of awakening love for Bhagavan, is very simple: the holy name.

No — not by any material means, not by mere study, not by yoga (yoga may give some freedom from stress and peace of mind, but then in that peace one must think of something spiritual to get the advantage).

His guru came by God's grace and said: "O King, this age you were born in is just beginning; it is an ocean of inauspiciousness — but there is **one good thing left in this age**." (Not New York, "the Big Apple," Saks Fifth Avenue — all temporary, all will fade.) That one good thing: **"By chanting the names of God (kirtan of Krishna) one can achieve perfection and attain the supreme destination."**
- **Who is qualified? Anyone and everyone.** No one is considered qualified or unqualified; everyone is a candidate for liberation, for devotional service. We are going back to the very same kingdom of God that those who lived in Satya-yuga and Treta-yuga aspired for. Though very fallen, we have the same golden opportunity — by the mercy of the most recent incarnation of God.

## Knowing God in Detail — the Basis of Love

- **In India, we know.** **Brahma-samhita 5.1** again (*ishvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah / anadir adir govindah sarva-karana-karanam*). The Vedas are full of detail about the Lord — and **without that knowledge, how can you love God?** We speak of love of God, but if you don't know someone, how can you love them? It is simple logic; people aren't even logical anymore.
  - Analogy: "I love my friend, my best buddy." "Where does he live?" "I don't know." "What does he look like?" "I can't tell you." "What's his name?" "I haven't a clue." — There is no impetus for such love.
  - Why did you choose your husband? Because of the way he looks, his interests, his likes and dislikes (which you share), the way he walks, what he likes to eat — "and Krishna consciousness, yes, that's the main thing" (and "of course he's also very handsome"). **Birds of a feather flock together.**
- **Attention to detail is a sign of love.** When we worship the Deity, we express our love through all the detail — the curtain opens (*go-vindam adi-purusham tam aham bhajami*), "just look at the flower there, that little jewel." So the scriptures are full of detail about the name, fame, form, paraphernalia, pastimes, and abode of God — no dearth of information. As much as you want to know about God, you can understand from the scriptures.
- The progression: **Bhagavad-gita**, then the **Srimad-Bhagavatam**, culminating in the great **Chaitanya-caritamrita**, which explores the different **rasas** (mellows) of love one can experience — an ocean of love in associating with the Personality of Godhead. That is the gift we have to offer the world. We should learn this culture and share it with the entire world, giving people the opportunity.

## A Simple but Pure Process

- The process is very simple — even simpler than in previous ages, when people were more qualified. You have to **chant**, but you also have to **lead a pure life**.
- If you truly want to go back to your original nature in the spiritual world (recognizing "I am not of this world, I am a stranger in a strange land"), then — since the spiritual world is a very pure existence — you must live a pure existence. According to Vedic culture, this means:
  - **No meat, no fish, no eggs** — even more, **no onion, no garlic**;
  - **No intoxication;**
  - **No illicit sex** (no sex outside of marriage);
  - **No gambling.**
- "How is that possible?" (The speaker loves lecturing on the street, "giving the Absolute Truth.") People always ask this.
- **Chanakya Pandita** says in the **Niti-shastra** ("Is that part of the Bible? Not exactly"): you will give up something only if you get something better. This strong discipline — the clean, pure way of life, the foundation of any bona fide spiritual tradition — is possible only if you are getting something better.
- And what do you get? A **taste for transcendence** when you chant *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare.* That is the **param drishtva nivartate** ("having experienced a higher taste, one refrains from the lower" — *Bhagavad-gita 2.59*) — the higher taste that gives us the faith to keep going, to renounce all that is unnecessary for spiritual life and keep advancing out of this world of birth and death, back to the spiritual world, back to the kingdom of God.
