## The Verse: Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.8.34 (Narasimha Chaturdashi)

- On this most auspicious day celebrating the appearance of **Lord Narasimhadeva**, the reading is from the **Seventh Canto of Srimad-Bhagavatam, Chapter 8**, entitled "Lord Nrsimhadeva Kills the King of the Demons," **text 34**.
- **Translation (SB 7.8.34):** "The angry face of Lord Nrsimha was terrible to behold. The Lord radiated a bright effulgence, and, not seeing anyone in the assembly hall who could equal Him in strength and splendor, He sat down upon the excellent royal throne. All those present were overwhelmed with reverential fear before the Lord and did not dare approach Him to offer their obeisances."
- **Purport (SB 7.8.34):** When the Lord sat upon Hiranyakashipu's throne, no one objected; not a single supporter of Hiranyakashipu came forward to fight the Lord. This means all the demons at once acknowledged the superiority of Narasimhadeva. Moreover, although Hiranyakashipu had regarded the Lord as his greatest enemy, he had **formerly been Jaya, the doorkeeper at the Vaikuntha gate**, who had served the Lord faithfully. Although he had fallen because of the curse of the brahmanas (the Kumaras) and taken the nature of a demon, and although as Hiranyakashipu he had never offered anything to the Lord, the Lord — out of love for His devotee-servant — gladly sat upon the throne that Hiranyakashipu had put so much effort into creating.
  - This example shows that **whatever condition a devotee is in, fortune never leaves him.**
- Housekeeping notes: mothers are asked to keep children from disturbing the devotees during the lecture; mobile phones should be set to silent.

## After the Battle: Enjoying the "Trophy" Throne

- This verse describes what happened after the great battle between Narasimhadeva and Hiranyakashipu: with the battle finished, the Lord **gladly sat upon the demon's throne.**
- Just as in modern times, when a battle ends the victors enjoy the trophies (Sanskrit **vibhuti**) — everything the defeated enemy left on the battlefield.
- Kshatriyas in Vedic culture were somewhat proud people because they genuinely possessed serious qualification and had something to be proud of.
  - This showed in how they decorated themselves with precious necklaces, earrings, and bracelets, often going into battle adorned this way.
  - They would collect trophies from defeated kshatriyas — their necklaces, bracelets, earrings — and sometimes valuable weapons.
  - No one worried much about being killed in battle, because they understood that if they fought for a righteous cause they would attain heaven; so no one feared death.

### Story: The Bayonet Knife

- When Maharaja turned 13, his father came into his room and gave him a gift: a **bayonet knife** that his father had acquired on the battlefield while fighting in the war.
- He was very proud of the gift and carried it to school, so that all the bullies avoided him.
- The school principal discovered he had the bayonet knife and confiscated it. But he complained to his father, who came, took the knife back from the principal, and returned it to him at home.

### The Throne's History

- When Hiranyakashipu was defeated, Lord Narasimhadeva decided to enjoy the trophy throne of Hiranyakashipu — which the demon had been so proud of — **to add salt to the wound of the defeated demons.**
- Actually, that throne originally belonged to **King Indra**. Hiranyakashipu had in his time defeated all the demigods led by Indra, seized his throne, and used it. So the throne had changed several owners — but in the end it returned to the supreme owner of everything, Krishna.
- **Bhagavad-gita 5.29** — Krishna as the ultimate proprietor: "One in full consciousness of Me, knowing Me to be the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all planets and demigods, and the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities, attains peace." **Sarva-loka-maheshvaram** means Krishna is the owner and enjoyer of everything in this creation. Thus Narasimhadeva simply reclaimed the throne that was originally His own.

## The One Real Reason the Lord Descends

- As devotees of Narasimhadeva we know well the Lord did not come to seize anyone's throne. He appeared **exclusively to deliver Prahlada Maharaja from the hands of the asuras.**
- **Bhagavad-gita 4.8** (which Srila Prabhupada often quoted) — **"paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya ca duskrtam / dharma-samsthapanarthaya sambhavami yuge yuge"** — Krishna gives three reasons why He comes from Vaikuntha to the material world:
  1. **Paritranaya sadhunam** — the deliverance of the devotees.
  2. **Vinasaya ca duskrtam** — the destruction of the demons/miscreants.
  3. **Dharma-samsthapanartha** — the reestablishment of religious principles.
- Interestingly, Srila Prabhupada says in his commentary that the Lord does **not need** to come personally to fulfill the last two:
  - He can destroy demons naturally through cataclysms, diseases, or epidemics.
  - Religious principles can be reestablished by His sincere devotees. "For example, we go out on the streets to reestablish the principles of Dharma — and one can see that in Russia this is quite successful."
  - How much time would the Lord need to reestablish Dharma? Prabhupada said, almost jokingly, that **45 minutes** would be enough for Him — because He spoke the entire Bhagavad-gita in about that time. So He has no need to come here Himself for that purpose.
- Therefore there is only **one main reason** the Lord comes to this world: **to deliver His devotees.**

### The Lord Is More Eager Than We Are

- We often speak of liberation (moksha) and love of God, but freeing oneself from the clutches of material energy is not easy. Krishna admits this in **Bhagavad-gita 7.19** — **"bahunam janmanam ante"** — "After many births, one surrenders unto Me."
- Sometimes the Lord cannot wait for His pure devotees to return to the spiritual world, and He makes their task easier. **He is more confident that we will return to God than even we are.**
- It may be very difficult for us to follow the process, but the Lord constantly gives us opportunities to engage in new, interesting kinds of service to purify our hearts. Most importantly, He makes devotional service **far more attractive than anything else in this world.**
- In our age the process is very simple:
  - Loudly chant the holy names.
  - Dance like crazy (as you danced at the kirtan).
  - Listen to a little philosophy.
  - At the end, fill yourself to the brim with the wonderful **prasad** of Narasimhadeva.

## Who Are the "Pious" the Lord Delivers?

- Prabhupada says the main point of this verse is the deliverance of **pious personalities** — but what does that mean? Not social workers or philanthropists. The verse speaks of **sadhus**, who are on the level of **shuddha-sattva** (pure goodness), not merely mundane goodness.
- Lord Narasimhadeva did not appear merely because Hiranyakashipu was terrorizing the whole universe. He appeared mainly because **His own devotee, Prahlada, was being persecuted.**

### Why the Lord Usually Does Not Intervene in Mass Suffering

- Generally the Lord does not intervene when the masses suffer through natural phenomena, because such suffering is the consequence of their own sins — activities performed in forgetfulness of the Supreme Lord.
- The sufferings people undergo are a form of **purification.**
  - Analogy: when someone is sick, their temperature rises — and the fever is an indicator that the body is fighting the disease.
  - Personal example: Maharaja and **Badahari Das Prabhu** recently caught a very serious flu and could not shake it for two weeks (14 days).
- Although we have compassion for those who suffer, our duty is also to explain the cause: their suffering results from sinful acts, and sinful acts are committed because we turn away from Krishna.
- **But when the Lord sees His pure devotees attacked by demons, He immediately appears.**

### The Devotee's Suffering Is Not Karma

- The sufferings devotees undergo are not the result of their karma — because karma is burned up through **initiation**. At initiation devotees promise the spiritual master and the Lord never again to engage in sinful activity, so their hearts are pure.
- But if anywhere in the universe a pure devotee is attacked or troubled, the Lord comes and saves him.

## This Lila Is Not Ancient History — It Recurs

- This lila occurred many billions of years ago in **Satya-yuga** — yet it is not mythology or mere ancient history, because it happens in our time too.

### Recurrence ~500 Years Ago: Chand Kazi

- About 500 years ago, when **Lord Chaitanya** appeared, the Muslim magistrate **Chand Kazi** tried to stop the chanting of the holy name and halt the Sankirtan movement.
- At night, **Narasimhadeva** came to him and threatened to tear him to pieces if he did not stop persecuting the devotees.
- When Chand Kazi awoke, he saw deep scratches on his chest and realized it had not been a mere nightmare but reality — so he declared he would no longer persecute the devotees.

### Recurrence ~35 Years Ago: The Juhu Beach Land (Mr. Nair)

- Only 35 years ago, Narasimhadeva again intervened, at the dawn of the Hare Krishna movement, to protect His pure devotee **Srila Prabhupada.**
- The devotees had to fight seriously for the land where the Bombay temple now stands, on **Juhu Beach**:
  - A man (**Mr. Nair**) who owned the land became infamous with ISKCON. He sold the land to Srila Prabhupada, then sold the **same land** to several other people, collecting money from many.
  - Srila Prabhupada would not give up — he refused to give away even one rupee belonging to the Lord.
  - Prabhupada not only came to the land himself but **immediately installed the deities Radha-Rasabihari** there. By Indian law, if deities already reside on land, it can only be taken away in very exceptional cases.
- **Prabhupada's own words (English fragment):** He came to the Juhu Beach property and told the deities, "If you come here, I promise you, I will build you a temple."
  - As conditioned souls with a tendency to cheat, we sometimes make promises we can't keep. (There was a 1960s song, "Don't promise if you can't fulfill it.")
  - But if a **pure devotee** or the **Supreme Lord** makes a promise, that promise is **never broken.** So if we promise something to the Lord or to a pure devotee, it is equally dangerous to break it — we must never do so.
  - **Brahmananda Prabhu** noted last Kartik that the land never actually belonged to Mr. Nair — it belonged to another person living in Calcutta who didn't even know what was going on.
- Prabhupada went through much trouble to keep his promise to the Lord. In the end, after a long battle with Mr. Nair, Prabhupada — while in America — received a phone call. As he spoke, his face lit up with a smile, and his servants wondered what was so joyful.
  - He said: **"Mr. Nair died of a heart attack."**
  - The devotees asked how this became possible. Prabhupada said, "I simply prayed to Narasimhadeva to deal with him himself" — more specifically, "I prayed to Lord Narasimhadeva to destroy this demon."

### Answering the Objection — Killing by the Lord Is Liberation

- People might wonder: what kind of spiritual master, acharya, wishes death upon people? Why doesn't he, like an ordinary holy man, simply tolerate troubles instead of acting so aggressively?
- But Prabhupada knew perfectly well that **anyone killed by the Lord Himself attains at least liberation** — as seen with the demons killed by Krishna in Vrindavan, such as **Putana.**
- So there was nothing to grieve; one could actually **rejoice for Mr. Nair.**

## Understanding Beyond the Limited Mind and Senses

- Do not let your mind deceive you into doubting how there can be a being who is half-man, half-lion, with eight arms, or how someone can have four heads. Understand that these descriptions are **beyond the perception of our limited mind and senses.**
- Prabhupada pointed out that we have only a limited sphere on this small planet; we do not know what lies beyond the millions upon millions of universes — we only know a small corner.
- **Story:** Once a disciple challenged Srila Prabhupada, asking how one could believe that **Lord Brahma** has four heads and flies around the universe on a swan. Prabhupada replied: "You don't even know everything on this small planet, in this small universe — and you're trying to understand what happens on the universal level. That is beyond our understanding."
- Analogy — **blood transformed into milk:** How can we understand universal affairs when we cannot even understand how, within a mother's body, blood is transformed into milk? Every female body on this planet transforms blood into milk to feed its children, yet no scientist has any idea how it is done.
  - Prabhupada's related point: for those who so passionately wish to enjoy the blood of the cow, let them instead drink **milk** — for milk is nothing but transformed blood, and from it people obtain an enormous benefit.

## The Lord Recognizes His Devotee — and the Devotee's Faith

- The Pandit's analogy: just as a mother cow can recognize her own calf in a large herd no matter where it is, so the Lord can recognize His pure devotee in any crowd, wherever he may be, and show him mercy — for He feels the same love for His devotee and is always ready to protect him from any danger.
- The pure devotee's faith is so strong that we cannot find a single verse in Srimad-Bhagavatam where **Prahlada Maharaja prays for the Lord to come and protect him** — because he was fully confident the Lord would protect him anyway, without any prayer. Such is the faith of a pure devotee.
- The devotee thinks: **"If the Lord wants to kill me, no one can protect me; if the Lord decides to protect me, no one can kill me. So the most important thing for me is to continue my service to the Lord."**
- Example: when Hiranyakashipu attacked Prahlada, Prahlada never prayed for protection or for the Lord to punish his father — he simply continued to preach. When Hiranyakashipu challenged, "Where is your Lord? Is He in this jug, this tree, this column?" Prahlada answered: **"Of course He is there. Be careful."**

### Why the Lord Delayed for the Demigods

- Although the demigods prayed for the Lord to protect them from the "terrible headache" Hiranyakashipu created, Lord Narasimha was **not in a hurry** to come and protect them.
- Why? Because, with all respect, the demigods are **not pure devotees**: they want to enjoy in the material world in parallel with serving the Lord, so their prayers are not so effective.
- But when a **pure devotee** with no material desires prays, the Lord hears immediately.

## The Heart of a Vaishnava — and the Heart of the Lord

- Lord Chaitanya describes the mood, or heart, of a Vaishnava in one of the wonderful verses of the **Shikshashtaka**. Figuratively, we may look right into the heart of a Vaishnava, and what we see there is beautiful.
- The heart of a Vaishnava is so pure that it can be offered to the Lord, because it holds no material desires — **Shikshashtaka, verse 4: "na dhanam na janam na sundarim"** — "I do not want wealth, followers, or beautiful women; only causeless devotional service."
- Through this we can also look into the **heart of the Lord.** Would you like to look into the Lord's heart? In it there is nothing material — only the **loving relationships between Him and His devotees.**
- We do this not sentimentally, but through the prism of **Guru, Sadhu, and Shastra.**
- **Badahari Das Prabhu** knows this verse by heart — **Srimad-Bhagavatam 9.4.68** (the Ambarisha pastime): Krishna says, "The pure devotee is always within My heart, and I am always within the heart of My pure devotee. My devotee knows no one but Me, and I know no one but him."
  - So, if we look into the heart of the Lord, we find the devotees there; and if we look into the heart of the devotees, we find only the Lord there. **In this lies the unity between the Lord and His devotees.**
  - Just as Jesus Christ said, "I and the Father are one" — this does not mean they merged into one, but that they are united by **loving devotional service.**

## The Demigods' Anxiety vs. Prahlada's Faith

- Prahlada had full faith the Lord would protect him, so he never even called for help.
- Unlike Prahlada, the demigods watching the battle were terribly anxious — thinking, "What if Hiranyakashipu kills Narasimhadeva? What will we do then?"
  - "My dear prabhus, demigods — what kind of devotees are you?" Although they are Vaishnavas, this is not the highest level of devotional service.
- Therefore **Prahlada Maharaja is the real example of a pure devotee** — which is why he is counted among the **twelve mahajanas**, the greatest devotees of all.

### Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati's "Vaishnava Ke"

- In this connection: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura wrote a book called **"Vaishnava Ke"** ("Ke" meaning "what/which" — i.e., "What kind of Vaishnava are you?").
- He describes the whole process of devotional service — including what happens in our mind, all the material desires that persist in the heart alongside devotional service — so that already in this life we do everything possible to purify ourselves and become pure devotees.
- The level of a devotee (**Madhyama** and **Uttama**) is determined by the level of his **faith**.
- The Narasimha prayer: "I bow before Lord Nrsimhadeva, the wrathful form of Lord Vishnu, His many faces as though engulfed in flame — terrifying, yet most auspicious — who is **the death of death itself (mrtyor mrtyu)**." Prahlada had such faith.
- If our heart holds many material desires, we will never have firm faith — so it is vital to get rid of all doubts and fully take shelter of the Lord.

## Story: Prabhupada's Question to the GBC

- A GBC member came out of a GBC council meeting in Mayapur (1973 or 1974), when Srila Prabhupada himself participated in the councils. Asked how it went, he said it was very interesting because Prabhupada took part.
- Prabhupada began sternly. There were then only 12 GBC members, and he gave them a strong lecture: "You are the most influential devotees in the whole society, and a serious responsibility rests on your shoulders. I want to ask each and every one of you the same question, and I want each of you to answer."
- Such silence fell (recounted by **Bhagavan Das**) that "you could hear a pin drop." Everyone waited for the question. Prabhupada asked: **"Do you have full faith that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, or not?"** — "Yes, Prabhupada."
- Then Prabhupada said: **"You, as leaders, must first and foremost help other devotees develop full faith in Krishna. For that, you yourselves must have full faith in Krishna — and for that, you must fully surrender to Krishna."**
- Prahlada Maharaja had exactly such full faith that the Lord knows what He is doing, so he worried about nothing.

## Why the Lord Sat on the Throne Without Hesitation

- After killing Hiranyakashipu, Narasimhadeva sat on his throne. Srila Prabhupada notes in his commentary that the Lord did so **without any hesitation.**
- We might wonder how the supremely pure Lord could immediately sit on that throne, since nothing impure is offered to Him:
  - The throne had been used by a sinful demon who terrorized the whole universe.
  - The Lord could have asked the devotees to purify it, or at least place something on top — as when we offer something to the Lord, we first purify our hands, then the object, and only then offer it.
- Prabhupada explains why the Lord sat without hesitation: Hiranyakashipu was none other than **Jaya**, one of the doorkeepers of Vaikuntha, cursed and sent to the material world for a small offense — an arrangement **the Lord Himself wanted, because He desired to fight with someone.** By becoming Hiranyakashipu, Jaya satisfied that desire, so the Lord sat on his throne without any problem.
- This shows the Lord accepts only the positive; He pays no attention to the negativity — all the sinful acts Hiranyakashipu committed — and showed him full mercy, delivering him and sitting on his throne.
- This mood is embodied in **Lord Chaitanya**, who comes to show mercy to all fallen, conditioned souls.

## The Lord Never Forgets a Devotee's Service

- It is said that "women and elephants never forget insults." One may discuss this from various angles (and as devotees we should not dwell on it).
- By analogy: **the Lord never forgets even the smallest devotional service a devotee offers Him** — so kind is the Lord.
- The Lord's kindness is that **He wants us to return to the spiritual world more than we want it ourselves.** Even if, to purify us of material desires, He allows some very severe events to befall us — ultimately it is all a manifestation of His love.

### Prabhupada's Compassion for a Fallen Disciple

- Srila Prabhupada, as the pure representative of the Lord, also loved his disciples and was displeased when devotees related to one another negatively.
- When a former sannyasi in the movement fell down, another leader began criticizing him and publicizing it. When Prabhupada heard, he said: **"Why did you tell everyone about this? Now it is impossible for him to return. You have destroyed the possibility for him to return to the movement."**
  - He was very upset because he felt compassion for that disciple. Many lessons can be drawn from this small comment.
- **Bhagavad-gita 9.31** — Krishna says, **"kaunteya pratijanihi na me bhaktah pranasyati"** — "Declare it boldly, O son of Kunti, that My devotee never perishes."

## Concluding Glorifications

- **All glories to Lord Nrsimhadeva**, who appeared exclusively to protect His pure devotee, Prahlada Maharaja.
- **All glories to Prahlada Maharaja**, who demonstrated full **shraddha** (faith) in the Lord.
- Good luck to all the demons, that they may draw lessons about pure devotional service.
- We can even congratulate both **Hiranyakashipu and Mr. Nair**, who attained liberation directly from the hands of the Lord.
- In a sense, despite all his misdeeds, we should be grateful to Hiranyakashipu — because without his "creative approach," we would never have learned this lila; the Lord would never have appeared in such an unusual form or displayed such an unusual pastime, by which Gaudiya Vaishnavas take shelter of the Lord.
- Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati himself acknowledges this in an article for the **Harmonist magazine (1927)**, writing that Hiranyakashipu — even more than Prahlada — by means of **negative propaganda**, glorified the greatness of Lord Narasimhadeva.
