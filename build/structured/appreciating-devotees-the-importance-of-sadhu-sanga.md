## The Verse: Prahlada's Prayer for the Association of a Pure Devotee

- The chosen verse is from the **Seventh Canto of Srimad-Bhagavatam, Chapter 9, "Prahlada Pacifies the Lord with Prayer"** — one of Srila Prabhupada's favorite sections to lecture from. Of all the classes Prabhupada gave over the years, most come from the Seventh Canto.
- **The verse (Prahlada's prayer):** Prahlada asks the Lord to *"place me in touch with Your pure devotee, and let me serve him as a sincere servant."* A famous verse in which Prahlada, rather than accepting material opulence, prays to be engaged in the service of a pure devotee.

### Prabhupada's Purport (read aloud)

- By studying Srimad-Bhagavatam, every intelligent man can gain experience like that of Prahlada Maharaja through the historical incidents in this great literature of spiritual knowledge.
- Following in Prahlada's footsteps, one should learn through experience that **all material opulence is perishable at every moment.** Even this body — for which we acquire so many sensual pleasures — may perish at any time.
- The soul, however, is eternal: **"na hanyate hanyamane sarire" (Bhagavad-gita 2.20)** — the soul is never vanquished even when the body is destroyed. Therefore an intelligent man should care for the happiness of the spirit soul, not the body.
- Even a long-duration body like that of Lord Brahma and the great demigods will also be destroyed; so an intelligent man should be concerned with the imperishable spirit soul.
- To save oneself, one must take shelter of a pure devotee. If one wants to be saved from material nature's onslaughts (which arise because of the material body), one must become Krishna conscious and try to fully understand Krishna.
- **Bhagavad-gita 4.9** — one should understand Krishna in truth, and this can be done only by serving a pure devotee.
- Prahlada prays that Lord Nrisimhadeva place him in touch with a pure devotee and servant, instead of awarding him material opulence.
- Every intelligent man must follow Prahlada Maharaja — **"mahajano yena gatah sa panthah"** (follow the path of the great authorities). Prahlada did not want to enjoy the estate left by his father; he wanted to become **the servant of the servant of the Lord.** The illusory human civilization that perpetually endeavors for material happiness is rejected by Prahlada and those who follow him.

### Three Types of Material Opulence Rejected by Prahlada

- **Bhukti** — being situated in a very good position, like the demigods in the higher planetary systems, enjoying material sense gratification to the greatest extent.
- **Mukti** — being disgusted with material advancement and thus desiring to merge/become one with the Supreme.
- **Siddhi** — executing severe yogic meditation to attain the eight mystic perfections (*anima, laghima, mahima,* etc.).
- All who desire material advancement through bhukti, mukti, or siddhi are punishable in due course and return to material activities. Prahlada rejected them all, wanting only to engage as an apprentice under the guidance of a pure devotee.

## The Subject: Appreciating Devotees

- Sometimes even experienced lecturers get a difficult verse and must think hard about what to say. But a theme like "appreciating the devotees" comes very easily for Vaishnavas, because we are taught to see the good qualities in others.

### The Good Eye and the Bad Eye

- We may also see bad qualities, but we see them with the desire to help the other person.
- **Prabhupada's teaching:** "In Krishna consciousness we have to have a good eye and a bad eye." One must be able to see the good qualities in others; and if one is a leader, parent, or spiritual master, one must recognize the weaknesses and bad qualities — but with the desire to help that person overcome them.
- Generally, in our association we focus on appreciating the good qualities of other devotees, which are many.

### The Lord Decorates His Devotee with All Good Qualities

- **"yasyasti bhaktir bhagavaty akincana sarvair gunais tatra samasate surah" (Srimad-Bhagavatam 5.18.12)** — the Lord decorates His devotees with all good qualities, so the mass of people know whom to follow. Divine, spiritual qualities and love of Krishna are attractive even to non-devotees — even to demons.
- **Sad-Gosvamy-astakam** — the Six Gosvamis of Vrindavan were popular both with the gentle and the ruffians. Even ruffians (a polite word for someone even demoniac) are somehow attracted to the divine qualities of a *sadhu* or *sadhvi*.
- Krishna wants His devotees to be in the limelight, though devotees themselves are shy and do not want honor — they want to give all honor to Guru and Krishna. The Lord puts them into that position because **"whatever a great man does, the common man will follow; whatever standard he sets, the whole world pursues" (Bhagavad-gita 3.21).**
- There is a great need for spiritual leadership in human society — better to have Krishna conscious leaders who lead us out of ignorance to truth, out of darkness to light, out of the material world into the spiritual world, than leaders with no spiritual knowledge or qualification. Therefore Krishna decorates His surrendered, accomplished devotee with all spiritual qualities so the public can see: "Here is My devotee — follow in his footsteps."

### Story: The Decorated War Veterans of San Francisco

- We don't see this so much anymore, since the older generation (his parents' generation) has mostly departed. But after World War II, those who were heroes in battle were recognized by the government, awarded medals, and asked to display those medals on their chests.
- As a young boy going into San Francisco to shop with his mother or father, he would often see men (and sometimes women) walking down the street wearing their medals of distinction from World War II, and people would appreciate them.
- **The scene he remembers:** A decorated war veteran was approaching the door of a shopping center, and some children — recognizing the medals — stepped forward and opened the door for him. "Those days are gone," when the youth respected the qualities of the elders. In the same way, the Lord wants His devotee to be recognized.

### The Lord Places His Devotees in Responsible Positions — Yudhishthira as Emperor

- One reason the whole Kurukshetra war was fought — among several reasons for the greatest battle of all time, in which millions of men were killed for upholding **Dharma** — was to install **Maharaja Yudhishthira** as emperor of the world, the king of kings (an emperor is king over all the subordinate minor kings).
- 5,000 years ago, we hear from Srimad-Bhagavatam, the emperor was in charge of the whole world. The capital of the entire world was **Hastinapura**, very near modern-day New Delhi. Vedic culture was all over the world, and the emperor managed the affairs of the entire world so it would be in a good state — a state fit for becoming Krishna conscious.
- Krishna wanted Yudhishthira to be emperor. So the Lord places His devotees in responsible positions so the general mass can follow them — and so we can appreciate them for what they are.

## The Qualities of a Sadhu (Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.25.21)

- **"titiksavah karunikah suhrdah sarva-dehinam / ajata-satravah santah sadhavah sadhu-bhusanah" (Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.25.21)** — Kapiladeva's description of the qualities of a saintly person.

### Titiksavah — Tolerant

- The devotee is very tolerant because he is fixed on the goal. He is neither elated when there is an opportunity for sense gratification, nor discouraged when there is difficulty in his life. Fixed on Krishna, the Absolute Truth, and on his service to the Lord, even in the midst of the greatest calamity the devotee is unmoved, equipoised, situated in the Absolute Truth. **Santah** — peaceful.

### Karunikah — Merciful / Compassionate

- Although a devotee may be very advanced, very fortunate, and have received many blessings from Krishna, he is not a miser — he wants to share his good fortune with others. **This is the crest jewel of all Vaishnava qualities: his compassion for others.**
- **Prabhupada:** "The duty of a first-class man is not to subjugate others. The duty of a first-class man is to make a fifth-class man also a first-class man." Devotees want to elevate others; they are restless until they see all fourteen worlds chanting Hare Krishna (**Bhaktivinoda Thakura**).
- Any opportunity to share Krishna consciousness, the devotee takes:
  - It may be **organized** — the December book marathon, a collection drive, the Carnival Spiritual.
  - It may be **unorganized** — a devotee riding the bus to work who always carries a small book (*The Perfection of Yoga* or *A Reservoir of Pleasure*) in his pocket. People start conversations, inquire, and may see a little glimmer in his eye they don't see in others.
- **Prabhupada:** "Since my disciples have given up sense gratification and taken to devotional service, they are all bright-eyed and smiling and look like they've come from Vaikuntha." When someone asks, "Why are you smiling on this cloudy day?" the devotee takes out a book — because, as Prabhupada said, "What will your ten-minute lecture do? But if they get one of my books, they can read it and later become perfect." We always want to plant the seed.
- **Lord Chaitanya as gardener (Chaitanya-charitamrta):** "I am a gardener with these fruits of love of God. Alone, I cannot distribute them everywhere. Who will come forward and help Me?" And the devotees answer, "Me!"

### Suhridam Sarva-dehinam — Friend to Every Living Entity

- The devotee is everyone's friend. How is that possible?
- **Example — Lord Jesus Christ:** His holiday is coming (December 25th), the celebration of the birth of the Son of God. When He was on the cross, after all they had done to Him, He looked to the heavens and said, "Father, forgive them, for they don't understand what they are doing." Even meeting an inimical person, the devotee knows that is not that person's original nature.
- **"jivera svarupa haya krsnera nitya dasa" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 20.108)** — every living being is eternally the servant of Krishna, a loving soul. Only due to past sinful activity and ignorance do people become covered and possessed by lust, anger, and greed. Knowing this, even if someone yells an insult, chides him, or physically attacks him, the devotee remains understanding — a friend to every living entity.
- **"Maharaja Yudhishthira's enemy was never born"** — this is a devotee. **Ajata-satrava** — a devotee has no enemies. **Sattva-sattva-bhusana.**

## We Judge a Devotee by His Devotional Service, Not His Past

- If we look at devotees who have become cleansed, regardless of what they were before, we do not think of what a devotee was like before Krishna consciousness. We do not consider or judge a devotee by what he did before joining. That is not important. We look at a devotee on the basis of his devotional service since he joined.
- It is a very potent process, so transcendental qualities come forth very quickly. We can appreciate a devotee even after one week in the temple. "How can we give such recognition after seven days, when he has been in the material world for millions of births?" Because he has made a **quantum leap** — deciding to give up the false pursuit of sense gratification and his selfish endeavors, and making the attempt to become Krishna's devotee, to give pleasure to Guru and the Vaishnavas. We honor that.
- We think of devotees on the whole spectrum, **from the uttama-adhikari down to the kanistha-adhikari.** Even a devotee who may be fallen — who may even have left the movement — is honored for the service he has done, which Krishna never forgets; and we try to make friends and bring such a person back into the fold of Krishna consciousness.

## This Festival Honors Every Devotee — The Harmonium Analogy

- The festival is meant to honor each and every person sitting there — not just the courageous leaders. Special recognition is given to the temple leader **Varanayika Prabhu** and his team, and to elders like **Korma Prabhu**, and to other God-brothers and God-sisters who participate.
- But actually every single person makes up the whole of the congregation and temple of Krishna consciousness; for everyone's efforts, big or small, we honor them, "because without you it wouldn't be the same."
- **Analogy — the harmonium:** The speaker really likes the harmonium he is playing, but he knows that the little screw is as important as all the big keys. "I play the keys a thousand times, but if this little screw falls out, the whole thing falls apart." So everyone's service, whatever it is, is important to the community and especially to the propagation of Krishna consciousness.

## The Two Essential Ingredients of Krishna Consciousness

- **Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya:** associating with devotees and the **Yuga-dharma** (chanting Hare Krishna) are the two essential ingredients of becoming Krishna conscious in this age.
- When a newspaper reporter asks, "What is Krishna consciousness in a few words?" one reply is: *singing, dancing, a little philosophy, feasting.* Another reply is: **chanting Hare Krishna and the association of devotees.** Essentially, that is what it is — these two ingredients.
- **Scriptural support** (paraphrased): In this age of quarrel, the remedy for the fallen souls bitten by the serpent of *samsara* is the holy name of the Supreme Lord and menial service to the exalted Vaishnavas.
- **"Keep packed up tight" (Prabhupada):** He came to a temple where so many devotees had come from all the surrounding centers that in the Bhagavatam class you couldn't even move — if one person moved, everyone had to move. Prabhupada said, "This is very nice. We are packed up tight. This is how we want to be — safe in the association of devotees."

## We Take On the Quality of Our Association

- Advancement is very quick for one who regularly associates with devotees, because, as Prabhupada explains, **we take on the quality of that with which we associate.**
- **Examples:**
  - Associate with a businessman long enough and you start dressing, talking, and acting like a businessman.
  - Associate with an athlete and you start lifting weights, taking protein powder, and buying a shirt two sizes too small.
  - Associate with (or want to be) a rock star and you start dressing like one and croaking in the shower like one.
- So if we associate with devotees — by chance, by good fortune, or consciously — we begin to take on their qualities. It is mysterious.

### Why We Are So Easily Influenced — Tatastha-shakti

- We are the **tatastha-shakti** of the Lord, in a special category — the minute living entity. God is great and we are very small. Being spiritual but infinitesimal, tiny parts and parcels of the Lord, we are apt to become influenced by matter or by spirit, depending on which association the living entity falls into.

### Analogy: The Chameleon Lizard

- As a child, the speaker used to buy chameleon lizards (sent from Indonesia in boxes through the post — which he now notes was cruel). He could play for hours: put the lizard on a green leaf and it turns green (nature's defense so predators can't see it); on red it turns red; on brown it turns brown; on yellow it turns a kind of orange.
- ("Wow Mom, look!" "I've looked a thousand times, son.")
- We are like that — being tatastha-shakti, very tiny, it depends on *with what and whom* we associate in our lifetime. We must be very careful to choose our friends. A person is known by the friendship he keeps — not only because you are seen with that person, but because through that *sangam* (association) you begin to manifest those qualities.

## We Go Out to Give Association, Not to Take It

- We are always in association with materialistic people because we have to work — 98% of the community has to work. But we are not *taking* their association; essentially we are *giving* our association. That is the secret of how to live "out there" and not get affected: you are not intimidated by them.
- **Prabhupada's instruction to a sannyasi in Mayapur, 1975:** "Never look back and think you've left anything important behind, and never envy the position of the materialistic sense enjoyers." So we are not out there feeling unlucky and forlorn — we are there to *give* association.
- **The lunch-break example:** At the coffee break you open your lunch and there's no meat, fish, or eggs. People say, "Wow, that's radical — what's that?" You say, "Well, I don't eat dead bodies; you won't find any corpses in my lunch bag." "What do you mean?" "Then I'll tell you. And try this — it's the best; it's made by Korma."

### Subtle Association: Television, Music, the "One-Eyed Guru"

- Associating with materialistic people doesn't just mean being physically in their presence. Even more, it means hearing their philosophy and hearing them pour out their hearts in their music ("I love you, you love me, oh I'm happy...").
- **Television** — "the most sinister way that Kali pervades in Kali-yuga; we call it the one-eyed guru." Through soap operas, news broadcasts, and movies, the karmis pour out and reveal their hearts. It appears innocent, but this is Maya's trick. By watching television, hearing the radio, and following your favorite bands, there is a subtle influence that collects over time and becomes a material desire — and you wonder, "Where did that come from?"
- So association also means hearing mundane discussions. What do people discuss? What they do for pleasure — go to the pub and stand outside with a beer talking for hours; go to the beach or the pub.
- With all due respect — because ultimately they are brothers and sisters, and we speak in a mode of compassion, not disrespect — they have lost their way. That type of association is called **"asat-sanga-tyaga, ei vaisnava-acara" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.87)** — from the mouth of Mahaprabhu and reiterated by Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Gosvami: a Vaishnava should not associate intimately with ordinary (worldly) people. Even a little association can get you off the path.

## The Danger of a Moment's Bad Association: Ajamila

- **Story of Ajamila (Srimad-Bhagavatam, Sixth Canto):** He was a *paka* brahmana boy, born in a brahmana family — which is very rare, meaning that in previous lifetimes he had acted very piously and was learned, earning birth in a righteous family. All the *samskaras* were done and he wore the sacred thread.
- One day, walking through the forest to his guru's ashram, Ajamila happened to see a lusty couple hiding, kissing, and embracing in the forest. That is nothing compared to what a five-year-old can see on TV today, but in those days it was enough to agitate his mind. Within a short time he gave up his pure lifestyle. As he grew up he married a prostitute, had a family, and maintained them by being a thief.
- How did a pure brahmana boy become a thief? **Just one moment of inattention was enough to cause him to fall away.**

### Analogy: Swine Flu (H1N1)

- Swine flu is not a giant monster with "H1N1" on its chest that walks up and says, "I'm going to gobble you up." It is a virus — a microbe so small and difficult to detect that sometimes even doctors, examining your blood under the microscope, might miss it. But when they catch it, you have H1N1.
- It is very tiny, yet it can bring down the biggest, most influential, most important person — the big "Hulk" athlete, the pole-vaulter ready for the meet, and the next day: *achoo* — "my tummy, my head, I can't go to the meet." It doesn't take much to bring down even a transcendentalist. We have to be very careful.
- Scriptures give many examples of great souls who, due to a little inattentiveness, some past conditioning, an unwashed desire in the heart, or just a weak moment when they weren't chanting and were thinking of something else — *boom.*

### Prabhupada: "You're Not Afraid of Maya" & The Tortoise and the Hare

- **Prabhupada told one of the speaker's God-brothers:** "Your problem is you're not afraid of Maya. You're so confident, you're so advanced now, you think you don't have to be afraid of Maya." We must be on guard: the race is not over till it's over.
- **The tortoise and the hare:** The hare (rabbit) was way ahead, so he took a nap (Prabhupada said "snap"). The turtle kept going. Just before the hare crossed the finish line, he woke and tried to run to catch up, but couldn't — the turtle won. The rabbit was very confident: "I'm so fast, I've made so much progress, I'm practically there, I can take a nap, take it easy." But the turtle was very determined — a *sadhaka bhakta*, chanting his rounds, following the principles, reading the books, going to the temple. The rabbit became proud and complacent. Maya can attack at any moment, so we have to be careful.

## The Same Principle Works for Spiritual Association

- Just as brief moments of bad association (like watching TV) affect us, the same principle holds for becoming Krishna conscious. (Aside: after this lecture, nobody will want to go home and watch TV tonight — "you'll be too scared; at least this lecture will carry you till midnight.")
- We are **tatastha-shakti** — small. If we place ourselves under the care of Hari, Guru, and the Vaishnavas, even a little of their association can give us perfection.

### The Value of a Moment's Association (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.18.13)

- **Sukadeva/Suta Gosvami (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.18.13):** The value of a moment's association with a devotee of the Lord cannot be compared even to attaining the heavenly planets or liberation from matter — what to speak of worldly benedictions in the form of material prosperity, which are meant for those who are destined to die.
- You cannot compare a moment's association with a devotee to any material benediction, mystic perfection, liberation, or merging into Brahman — because a moment's association with a devotee gives you the opportunity for **pure devotional service, the supreme occupation for all mankind.**
- **"sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhoksaje / ahaituky apratihata..." (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6)** — what we should be trying to attain is that by which we can reach the pure devotional service of the Lord; that service must be **unmotivated and uninterrupted.** With that type of *bhakti*, that love for Krishna, you are completely happy — nothing else can give that.

### How Do You Catch Bhakti? Like Catching a Cold

- How do you get *bhakti*? From another devotee — just as you catch a cold when somebody sneezes and three days later you come down with it.
- When a devotee is speaking the Bhagavata, the nectarean vibration collects like dew drops in the air and falls on the heads of the audience, who experience ecstatic symptoms. It is a "good disease" — you get the good disease of Krishna consciousness by staying in the association of devotees, even if you can't understand a word they are saying.

### Story: "Why Are You Sleeping When I'm Speaking?" (South India)

- Traveling with Srila Prabhupada in South India, a group of devotees went down to Hyderabad, where Prabhupada was to install the Radha-Krishna deities in the new temple. Afterwards they went out to the farm.
- Prabhupada sat down to give a lecture to some guests (this, the speaker jokes, is "the result of eating a feast before your lecture" — he could hardly stay awake). Prabhupada was speaking to the guests in Hindi, which the speaker didn't know, and the speaker fell into a deep sleep.
- He heard the words, "Why are you sleeping when I'm speaking to the guests?" He thought he was dreaming, woke up, and said, "But Prabhupada, I don't speak Hindi." Prabhupada replied, **"It does not matter that you don't speak Hindi. It is transcendental subject matter. It will act. Stay awake."** The speaker didn't blink for one hour. Even if we don't understand, just being there in that environment and atmosphere has the effect.
- Therefore we should come into physical association with as many devotees as possible; a sustained period of time has such an effect.

### Mahaprabhu on Sadhu-sanga (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.54)

- **"sadhu-sanga, sadhu-sanga — sarva-sastre kaya / lava-matra sadhu-sange sarva-siddhi haya" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.54)** — the verdict of all revealed scriptures is that by even a moment's association with a pure devotee one can attain all success. What to speak, then, of being constantly in the association of devotees.

## How to Get Association When You Can't Live in the Temple

- It doesn't mean you must live in the temple — the temple isn't big enough for the whole congregation. It means coming as often as possible and participating: see the deities; take prasadam (the maha-prasad of **Radha-Govinda** — nothing in the three worlds compares to it); hear the discourses; go on the harinamas; join the Nama-hatta; visit devotees' houses; go to Mayapur and Vrindavan.

### "There's Always Room for Jello"

- It's not easy because we are all busy as bees. But there is always room. **Analogy — the Jello marketing campaign:** "There's always room for Jello." By the end of a meal you think, "I'm full," but there's always room for the sweet. Someone comes around with sweet rice: "Oh, you look too full." "No, no, it's okay, I'll take some sweet rice."

### The Sweetest of the Sweet

- This *bhajan* — chanting Hare Krishna and the association of devotees — is the sweetest of the sweet.
- **Vallabha Sampradaya (Madhurashtakam by Vallabhacharya):** Vallabha, the originator of the sampradaya, sings of the sweetness of Krishna's feet, His body, His flute — "madhuram, madhuram" — everything about Krishna is sweet.
- If your senses have become a little purified by chanting Hare Krishna, then you will hanker for that sweetness and for that association — and no one will have to tell you to come to the temple; you'll already be there. Rather, "Where are they? We have to find them and bring them" — because they have probably been there all day doing devotional service.

### The Magnet Analogy

- The same principle that takes us away from Krishna consciousness can, by a moment's association, bring us into good association.
- **A magnet:** if you bring the two negative poles near each other, they repel and move apart; but turn them around and put the positive poles together and they attract. It is all in how you use it. If you use your life to associate with non-devotees, you'll be pulled away from Krishna consciousness; but the same principle of association is *dovetailed* in Krishna consciousness.
- **Prabhupada** said he created the International Society for Krishna Consciousness for one reason only: so that we could come and associate with devotees.

## In the Society of Devotees You Find Devotees at Every Level

- Naturally, in the society of devotees you get devotees on different levels — some more advanced due to their years in Krishna consciousness or their sincere practice over many births.
- Some devotees had just a little touch of subtle desire left to cleanse, so they took another birth, and for them everything appears so easy from day one — natural to take to the path, chant and dance, remain aloof from politics, backbiting, criticizing, and fault-finding; not prone to laziness; able to control their senses and minds; and they always make the time to associate with devotees.
- So just by being in the movement you will have the association of advanced souls.

### The Need for Friendship at Your Own Level

- Association with devotees at your *same* level of Krishna consciousness is also good. You need a strong bond of friendship in Krishna consciousness — someone at the same level with whom you can talk about the struggles, the trials, the joys, and the victories; someone who understands, who can cry when you cry and laugh when you laugh. If you don't have that here, you'll have to go "out there" to get it. We need friendship.

### Devotees Less Advanced Than You — Spiritual Mathematics

- You will also always find devotees less advanced than you. That shouldn't make you feel superior ("look at you"); rather, a sense of compassion should rise in your heart, and you should go to them and give whatever you have.
- "But if I give them whatever I have, I'll have nothing left." No — that is **mundane mathematics.** **Spiritual mathematics** means *one minus one is a million* — if you give, you get back more by Krishna's mercy. Krishna is very happy when we take care of our devotees; He blesses you.

## The Five Most Potent Limbs of Devotional Service

- Associating with devotees is listed as one of the **64 items of devotional service.** There are five most essential items — because life is short and we want to get in the fast lane, take the elevator, and get home quickly and safely. (There are so many risks — we may get deviated or lose interest.)

### "There's No Place Like Home"

- **Aside:** His mother, every time they returned from vacation, would say as they pulled into the garage, "There's no place like home." His father would say, "That's what ladies feel like" — the shelter, the home, the kids, the family. So where is our *real* home? — the spiritual world.

### The Five (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.128)

- **"sadhu-sanga, nama-kirtana, bhagavata-sravana / mathura-vasa, sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.128)** — "If you learn any verse, learn that one." One should:
  1. **Sadhu-sanga** — associate with devotees.
  2. **Nama-kirtana** — chant the holy names.
  3. **Bhagavata-sravana** — hear the Bhagavatam.
  4. **Mathura-vasa** — reside in Mathura.
  5. **Sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana** — worship the deity with faith and veneration.
- **"If I told you all the rules, you'd faint" story:** A devotee once asked, "Prabhupada, are there any more rules we have to follow besides chanting Hare Krishna?" (Prabhupada hadn't even introduced the four regulative principles yet.) Prabhupada said, "Yes — if I told you all of them, you'd faint."

### Association Is the Salt That Makes the Other Four Effective

- All five are important, and one could say the *Yuga-dharma* (chanting Hare Krishna) is the essential one for achieving liberation. But one could also say the essential of the five is **associating with devotees**, because the success of the other four depends on the association of devotees — we really need devotees to do those other things.
- **Salt analogy:** Just as you need to add salt to appreciate the taste of food, association with devotees brings out the flavor and the results of every other devotional activity.
  - "I can't imagine if Korma didn't cook with salt today." **Story:** Twice since being here, the speaker received sweets with the ingredient missing — one day a *ladu* with no sugar (he was so hungry he said the offering prayer really fast, bit in, and "there wasn't one grain of sugar"), another day a *sandesh*-type sweet with no sugar. He didn't want to say anything because it had been offered to Govinda. So salt (association) is essential.

## Nama-kirtana: The Glory and Science of Chanting

- Association brings out the essence and results of any devotional service, including chanting. There are many ways to chant — even if you just mimic the devotees, "Oh, the Hare Krishna...", you are still saying Krishna's name.
- **Prabhupada:** If someone chants Krishna's name even once, the Lord notes it, marks it down in a little black book, and follows that person birth after birth until He can make him into a devotee. So if you have someone you don't really like, just have them say "Hare Krishna."

### Even One Syllable: The "Haram" Story

- Even if you say just part of the syllables — "Haram, Haram" — there is benefit. **"Haram"** is an Arabic word meaning "how horrible."
- **Prabhupada's analogy:** In the old days, warriors would go to the forest to hunt and sometimes be attacked by a wild animal — perhaps a wild boar. For Muslim brothers and sisters, the most horrific thing is to be killed by a pig (they avoid eating pigs). So if you got stabbed by a boar, you'd cry, "Haram, haram — how horrible!" But you woke up in another birth (Prabhupada's point being that even that syllable of the holy name carries benefit): you take birth again, this time perhaps in a devotee family, and though you might be a little rascal when young because of past conditioning, that syllable of the holy name acted.
- There is no difference between the name, fame, form, pastimes, and paraphernalia of God and God Himself — **the holy names are identical to Krishna.**

### But for Prema, You Must Take Initiation and Chant Properly

- To get **prema**, the ultimate benefit of chanting Hare Krishna, you must receive the mantras of the guru-parampara — you must take initiation from a bona fide spiritual master in Krishna consciousness. Then you become blessed to get the full benefit.
- It is a science — Krishna consciousness is a science, with a hypothesis, an experiment, and a result — and you need someone to train you in the finer aspects of chanting.
- **"trnad api su-nicena taror api sahisnuna / amanina manadena kirtaniyah sada harih" (Siksastaka 3 / Chaitanya-charitamrta Adi 17.31)** — one should chant in a humble state of mind, thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street, more tolerant than a tree, devoid of all false prestige, ready to offer all respects to others. In such a state one can chant the holy name constantly and get the full benefit. It is not just the lips moving — there is a science, and you need someone to train you in it.
- To get initiated means that someone has taken the responsibility to educate and enlighten you about the process and the glories of chanting Hare Krishna. To get the full benefit, you take initiation in the Brahma-Gaudiya Vaishnava Sampradaya, and then only can you get prema.

### Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's Prophecy of the Vaishnavas

- **Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya (prayer):** "From the moment the holy names of Hari appeared on the earth, Vaishnavas became manifest everywhere — adorned with faultless tilak and neck beads and equipped with the Maha-mantra. In the midst of the age of Kali, they purified the atmosphere, chanting 'Hari! Hari!'" The speaker calls this "a prophecy by Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya 500 years ago about ISKCON, essentially" — Vaishnavas became manifest everywhere (Australia, America, China). "So indeed, it came to pass."

## Bhagavata-sravana: Hearing the Bhagavatam

- **"nasta-prayesv abhadresu nityam bhagavata-sevaya..." (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.18)** — by regular attendance in the Bhagavatam class and by rendering service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service to the Supreme Lord (who is praised with transcendental songs) is established as an irrevocable fact.
- You have to hear the Bhagavatam; if you can't hear it here, you must hear it at home. "That's why Krishna gave the intelligence to the scientists to make iPods — Mr. Steve Jobs."
- **"tesam satata-yuktanam bhajatam priti-purvakam / dadami buddhi-yogam tam yena mam upayanti te" (Bhagavad-gita 10.10)** — to those who serve Him with love and devotion, the Lord gives the understanding by which they can come to Him. So the Lord gave Steve Jobs the intelligence: "Do it like this. Call it iPod." You may not be at the temple, but you can hear while cooking at home ("om namo bhagavate vasudevaya").
- If you have chosen and are following a spiritual master, you must hear from him regularly — you cannot advance in Krishna consciousness without hearing the Lord's representative. We hear from all gurus, but in particular from our own spiritual master, who is meant to inspire us, who has taken responsibility to take us back to Godhead, and whose footsteps we want to follow. The essential relationship between guru and disciple is one of **hearing**: **"tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya"** — approach such a person, inquire submissively, and render service.

### Hear from the "Person Bhagavata"

- **"bhagavata pada giya bhagavata-sthane"** (Mahaprabhu) — one should hear and study the Bhagavatam only from the *person* Bhagavatam. The Bhagavatam doesn't mean you just sit and read (we read Prabhupada, of course); you have to hear from a devotee. The speaker must be, as far as possible, a transparent via-medium, because it is the Lord speaking through him — he repeats the message of Godhead in order to instill Krishna consciousness in the hearts of his audience.
- If there is selfish motivation, it does not have the same effect. **Bhagavat-saptaha** — where someone reads all 18,000 verses in seven days and afterward goes around asking for a donation — is reading the Bhagavatam not to enlighten but as a business, a profession to make money; hearing from such a person will not give the same effect. Hearing from a devotee who does it out of mercy, sharing his realizations, does.
- **Story — Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati and the arati:** He was giving a lecture in a hall near the deity room. In the middle of the lecture the arati bell and conch sounded, and a few students stood up and walked out to see the arati, returning half an hour later. Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati stopped and asked, "Where were you?" They said, "Guru Maharaja, we went to see Krishna." He replied, **"You've only seen stone. If you want to hear Krishna, you sit and listen to your spiritual master — then Krishna will be revealed."**

## Mathura-vasa: Residing in a Holy Place

- Many people misconceive a holy place — thinking that just by visiting, you get purified.
- **The new highway example:** There is now a four-lane highway from Delhi to Agra (built for tourists, though the Muslims who built it didn't know it would facilitate the Vaishnavas). You can speed to Vrindavan in about two hours. On weekends Vrindavan is chock-a-block full of Delhi-wallas driving big Tata Sumos through the tiny streets, angry at the people walking ("out of our way!"), not wanting to get out and walk. They see the deity, buy some sweets, and go back to Delhi, saying, "I have been to Braj. I have seen Krishna. Have you seen Krishna, Swami?"
- There is still much benefit in going. But **Prabhupada** said the real benefit of going to a holy place is to associate with the holy people living there — because those *sadhus* are always speaking, and that is how we become realized.
- **The example of Lord Brahma:** How did Brahma become realized? His guru was Krishna — the Adi-guru. How did Krishna enlighten him? He played His flute (Venu). From His Vamsi came the Gayatri Mantra, which entered the right ear of Brahmaji and into his heart, and he became Krishna conscious.
- So when you go to Vrindavan, spend a lot of time hearing the Bhagavatam classes and going on Parikrama (the speaker's Vrindavan Parikrama guru is **Dina Bandhu Prabhu**).

## Sri-murti Seva: Serving the Deity

- Serving the deity is very potent. But how would we know anything about the deity if not for Prabhupada, who introduced deity worship to the Western world? Westerners thought anything with even a scent of deity worship was idol worship — yet now it flourishes in the West, with thousands of temples, many Westerners having become Vaishnavas who approach the Lord with folded hands and great faith.
- **Saksi-Gopal (Chaitanya-charitamrta):** The young brahmana prayed, "My dear Lord, You are not a statue; You are directly the son of Maharaja Nanda. Please bear witness — come back to my village in Orissa and stand witness for me." The deity said, "How can I go? I am a deity; how can I walk?" The boy said, "Lord, if You can talk, You can walk." So we have this faith that the deity is not just stone.
- **"sri-vigraharadhana-nitya-nana-srngara-tan-mandira-marjanadau..." (Gurvastakam 3)** — the spiritual master is always engaged in the temple worship of Sri Sri Radha and Krishna; he also engages his disciples in such worship — dressing the deities in beautiful clothes and ornaments and cleaning the temple.

### The Real Meaning: We Are Assisting the Spiritual Master

- When we worship "our" deities, we should think: "Who am I to approach Krishna? I am still an aspiring devotee, still a *sadhaka*, still a *kanistha-adhikari*. I am assisting my spiritual master in the worship of this deity. This is not my deity — I don't own Krishna; Krishna owns me."
- Before his puja, the speaker worships his Guru Maharaja and takes his blessings: "Guru Maharaja, please let me assist you in the worship of this shalagram; let me assist you in the worship of your beloved Radha and Krishna." Everything is to train us to be the servant of the servant of the servant.
- When we go back to the spiritual world, we don't have a direct relationship with Krishna — we are not like the sakhis and special manjaris. We will assist the liberated souls; we will assist our spiritual master in his service to *his* spiritual master, to Krishna. We are forever the servant. Don't get discouraged — "it's very nice to be the servant of a glorious master." With a glorious, benevolent, realized master, you are safe and secure; he provides everything you need to get out of this world. **Prabhupada writes:** "For the sincere disciple, the spiritual master takes full responsibility for his deliverance."
- Even as masters, we are servants. All who serve as spiritual masters sit at the feet of Prabhupada — and **"Prabhupada" means "he at whose feet all the masters sit."** He is the Founder-Acharya of ISKCON, and anyone acting as a spiritual master can do so only on his authority, as a proper representative, spreading the message exactly as Prabhupada did.

### Radha-Gopinath Were Brought by Prabhupada

- What do we know of Krishna? "I know He's blue, I know He plays a flute" — we know little. Gradually it will all be revealed by mercy. Even the temple's **Radha-Gopinath** — how did we get them? **Prabhupada brought them here, with his own arms.** That is a special grace not found everywhere in the world. "You have no excuse for not becoming pure devotees in your lifetime."
- **Prabhupada:** "Here is Krishna. Take Him." You cannot achieve Krishna through any material activity — millions of years of mystic practice or merging into Brahman. But you meet one pure Vaishnava like Prabhupada, and: "Here He is. Take Him." That is the shortcut — the association of a pure devotee and his representatives.

### Salt: Summary of the Five

- Altogether, these five most essential items of devotional service are like the salt. You "salt" all the other activities of Krishna consciousness with the association of devotees, and you become Krishna conscious.
- **Prabhupada:** Anyone who thinks he can be Krishna conscious outside the association of devotees, whenever we can get it, is living in a hallucination.
- The medicinal herb to purify the minds of fools absorbed in sense pleasure is faithful service to Sri Guru; the diet is the remnants of prasadam taken by Vaishnavas. ("Are there any remnants left over today? Does anyone know where Korma Prabhu's plate is? We can take a little remnants.")

## Srila Prabhupada's Association Is Always Available

- We must take advantage of this priceless treasure of associating with devotees while we can. This verse mentions in particular **pure devotees**, and the association of Srila Prabhupada is always available.
- **"He reasons ill who tells that Vaishnavas die when thou art living still in sound. The Vaishnava dies to live, and while living tries to spread the holy name around."** Prabhupada is as present now as when he was physically present — for the sincere practitioner.
- Why do you think he wrote the purports to his books from 1:00 to 4:00 a.m. practically every day of his later life? Not just for the few direct disciples who had him for a few years. He said, "One day my books will become gospel." That is how you get his association.
- So we should also hear the taped lectures of Srila Prabhupada — our Founder-Acharya, our Param-guru — read his books very carefully, and associate with senior members in the movement.

### Recognize the Advanced Devotees in ISKCON

- Although we respect all devotees, there are senior members who have grasped and are practicing the message. If that were not the case, it would fail to attest to the spiritual power of Srila Prabhupada to elevate his disciples to an exalted level.

- So you will find such devotees. The speaker names: **Radhanath Swami, Sivarama Swami, B.B. Govinda Maharaja, B.B. Krishna Goswami, Bhakti Vaibhava/Bhaktivedanta Vishnu Swami, Korma Prabhu, and the matajis**; and on the GBC, **Malati** — "not by gender; it's a question of the heart." We should recognize these great souls, take their association, serve and assist them while they are here, because they will not be here forever. Another generation will take over, and in it too there will be uttama, madhyama, and kanistha devotees.

### Every Generation Is a Wave

- There was something special about Prabhupada's generation. The speaker remembers going with Prabhupada to programs at the homes of extremely wealthy yet extremely Krishna conscious Indian families in Bombay — how they received Prabhupada, the culture, how they dressed, how the sparkling-clean house shone, how perfect and opulent every preparation and every deity was, how they talked and interacted. "It's gone now." He feels fortunate to have seen that generation, so rich with Chaitanya-ism and Vaishnavism; the food was remarkable and you couldn't stop the devotees from eating. India is losing it quickly now.
- Then there is this next generation of Vaishnavas in ISKCON — something unique that we must grasp while it is here. Then the coming generation — the **Gurukuli** generation (or those who took up service after) — will have their own unique contribution, keeping the flow going.
- **The video story:** His God-brother **Yadubara Prabhu** received an anonymous old video in the mail — Prabhupada's 1974 visit to Geneva — showing the speaker "all over the place with Prabhupada, playing mridanga and leading the kirtan." No opulence in the world could be a better present than that video. Yet it is so far back that he can hardly remember those moments. He reflects that it is the same now with his own disciples: they look as he looked 35–40 years ago — "smooth face, bright, and strong" (naming **Rani Priya, Balabhi, Kalindi, Gaurahari**). One day they too will look back at wrinkled, old photos "with Gurudev at the Carnival Spirituality" and see it was a long time ago. Every generation is like a wave — no two waves and no two snowflakes are the same. Prabhupada's generation was a wave; the speaker's humble attempt to follow is another; and the present devotees will be yet another. For now we still have the advantage of senior men and women in the movement.
- **Narottama dasa Thakura** — "**se saba sangira sange je koilo bilas / se sange na paiya kande narottama das**": being unable to attain the association of Lord Gauranga accompanied by all the devotees in whose association he performed his pastimes, Narottama simply weeps.
- **Ramananda Samvada (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 8.248):** Chaitanya Mahaprabhu asked, "Of all kinds of distress, what is the most painful?" Sri Ramananda Raya replied, "Apart from separation from the devotee of Krishna, I know no unbearable unhappiness."

## The Silent Soldiers and the Householders

- We should also recognize and honor those who are "up and coming." Every generation has its devotees who lead the congregation, and among the congregation there are many **silent soldiers.**
- Korma didn't cut all the subjis himself — he had a team who worked many hours (he started cooking two days ago). There are silent soldiers who do the cut-ups, clean the temple, work in administration, go out "with the buckets," and raise their children at home.
- Raising two or three children is not an easy service — you are fully engaged all the time. The benefit is a long-term investment: **shreyas and preyas.** (Shreyas = ultimate benefit; being a householder is really the long path — you wait a long time until the child is 21 and says, "Ma, I want to thank you for all you did for me." "Oh, really? I thought you'd never say.")
- Everyone is contributing; everyone is a special soul, part of this movement, and should be recognized and honored — because they have taken the bold step of turning their back on Maya.

### "Just Say No" — Turn Your Back on Maya

- **The anti-drug campaign analogy:** A few years ago America had the slogan "Just say no" to keep kids off drugs. When the drug pusher offers marijuana, crack, or speed at a reduced price — just say no. If you can turn your back on the material energy and the propensity to enjoy, and turn around to Krishna and say "I do," we honor you.
- **Lord Ram (Ramayana):** Anyone who comes to Him and says, "My dear Lord, for so long I have forgotten You, but now I am Your surrendered servant" — Ram takes charge of that person. In the untold stories of the Ramayana (heard in a lecture by **Radhanath Swami**), Ram says that even if Ravana himself were to come and say, "Ram, for so long I have forgotten You; now I am Yours," He would take charge and take care of him.
- Turn your back on the illusory energy of Maya, and by turning your back on the darkness you will face the light — face Krishna consciousness, a whole new experience.

### Getting Up When We Fall

- Anyone here today, we honor — because to a greater or lesser degree you have turned around and are facing Krishna, and in your heart of hearts you want to be Krishna's devotee and go back home. You are trying as best you can. Sometimes you slip, fall, or stumble.
- **His Muslim friend in Oman** asked, "Swami, did anybody ever fall down in your movement?" "Yes." "In Islam too." "Really?" "Oh yeah, yeah — shh." But we say: **if you fall down nine times, pick yourself up ten times.**
