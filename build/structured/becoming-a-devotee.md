## The Verse: The Onset of the Age of Kali

- This morning's reading is from **Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 1, Chapter 15 ("The Pandavas Retire Timely"), Text 36.**
  - Translation: *"When the Personality of Godhead, Lord Krishna, left this earthly planet in His self-same form, from that very day Kali, who had already partially appeared, became fully manifest to create inauspicious conditions for those who are endowed with a poor fund of knowledge."*
- Kali can impose his influence only on those who are not fully developed in consciousness of God; by keeping oneself fully under the supreme care of the Personality of Godhead, one can counteract all of Kali's effects.
- The age of Kali began just after the Battle of Kurukshetra, but it could not exert its influence because of the **presence of the Lord.** As soon as the Lord left in His own transcendental body, the symptoms of Kali-yuga — which Maharaj Yudhishthira had already foreseen before Arjuna's arrival from Dwaraka — began to manifest, and Yudhishthira rightly conjectured the Lord's departure.
- As Prabhupada explains, the Lord left our sight just as the sun, when it sets, goes out of our sight.
- Kali is the **fourth of the four ages** through which the material cosmos passes. More detail on the anomalies of the age is given in the **Twelfth Canto** of the Bhagavatam, and unfortunately the predictions are not very good.
  - It is stated (Twelfth Canto) that **religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, tolerance, mercy, duration of life, bodily strength, and memory** will all diminish day by day due to the powerful influence of Kali. So we should give this prediction very serious thought.

## "Don't Come Back" — Finishing in One Lifetime

- A disciple once told Prabhupada, "Prabhupada, I am so attached to this service [book distribution] that I would be happy to come back life after life to do it."
  - That is a nice sentiment — **ahaituky apratihata**, unmotivated and uninterrupted devotional service.
  - But in that particular instance Srila Prabhupada **frowned** and said, "**No, don't come back.** This Kali-yuga is only going to get worse. Better to finish up in this one lifetime and go back home, back to Godhead."
- By the causeless mercy of the spiritual master and Lord Chaitanya, that is possible — especially for the devotees of Lord Chaitanya.
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 7.19** ("bahunam janmanam ante jnanavan mam prapadyate"): after many, many births, one who has truly developed knowledge surrenders unto Krishna.
  - **Lord Chaitanya, who is Krishna Himself, is even more merciful than Krishna** — there is no difference between Chaitanya and Krishna, but He is more magnanimous. If one is very, very serious about Krishna consciousness, it is possible to finish this business quickly.
- In a lecture in the **early 1930s, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakur** said he saw no reason why all of his disciples could not go back to Godhead in one lifetime. But one has to be **very serious.**
  - Serious means being very strict in observing the rules and regulations, and particularly in chanting Hare Krishna.
  - And, to the best of our ability, we must help our spiritual master and Lord Chaitanya spread this movement.
- If we do have to come back and take birth again, we should know the situation will be **worse** than it is now — and it is already bad enough.
- These predictions will come true because they are **Shastra.** This is not like Nostradamus, whose predictions were subtle, some coming true and some not. Shastra is *apaurusheya* — not created by any mundane being — so it is perfect, and whatever is stated there is going to happen.
  - Twelfth Canto predictions: there will be **famine** and very **heavy taxes**, so people will be forced to eat leaves, roots, flesh, flowers, and fruits; with no water, they will eventually become completely ruined.
  - Right now we complain when the prasadam is not perfect — a little uncooked, a little overcooked, a little too spicy — but as Kali-yuga advances, people will be eating leaves. (Half-jokingly: if for that reason alone there won't be excellent prasad, then we should get out of here.)

## The Worst Prophecy: No Devotees, and the Need for Association

- Perhaps the **worst** prophecy of Kali-yuga is that in the future there will be **no devotees.**
- As members of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, we often preach and voluntarily put ourselves in austere conditions, but we can tolerate it because we have the **association of other devotees.**
- **Vishnujana Swami's letter:** a disciple once wrote to Prabhupada describing the hard conditions of Sankirtan in England (complaining). Prabhupada wrote back: "I can understand — sometimes there is not enough food, traveling is inconvenient, you cannot rest well; but because this is Sankirtan, we should take it as a child's play. Because we are Krishna's devotees preaching together, we can tolerate all conditions, because we are together. But without the association of devotees, it is very difficult to perform devotional service."
- So Kali-yuga will get worse and worse, but the worst condition is the absence of devotees.
  - Even now, Shastra says everyone is born with the qualities of a **shudra** (by birth). In Kali-yuga, demons get the facility to be born in good families — so everyone is born with shudra qualities, and even those born in brahmana families may actually be demons.
- We can tolerate anything for Krishna's service; the one thing we cannot tolerate is the lack of the association of His representatives, the devotees.
- Even where devotees are preaching in hell, how is it possible? They must also have the association of devotees, so for them it is like children's play in the pastimes of the Supreme Lord. And wherever you are immersed in those things, you are actually in **Vaikuntha**, actually in **Goloka.**
- There will be fewer and fewer Vaishnavas; at the end there will be only the demoniac, and **Kalki** will come with His sword. It is not a good age to be born in — but, as Prabhupada said, "we have to **make the best use of a bad bargain.**"

## The Sadhu Reveals the Fault, Then Gives the Solution

- Prabhupada points out the anomalies of the age, and in the very next sentence gives the positive alternative — a *turning* toward the solution.
- This is the work of the devotee: **"sadhu" means one who cuts** — he cuts our material attachments by revealing the futility of material life, and then gives us the solution to the problem.
- You don't even need the Bhagavatam to understand the evil of this age — just pick up one Monday newspaper, and it is full of descriptions of Kali-yuga. But can a newspaper give any conclusion? No.
- **The philosopher who gave no conclusion:** there was a philosopher very expert at analyzing the material world; his books read like a summary of the Gita.
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 8.15** ("duhkhalayam asasvatam"): this is a temporary place, full of suffering.
  - He took apart the material energy piece by piece and analyzed it — showing that all problems arise from having a material body and mind — but he gave **no conclusion**, and the result was that he **committed suicide.**
- Prabhupada also studied the material world thoroughly, but he gave the **conclusion**: we need to catch Krishna's hand.
  - As Jesus said, you cannot worship mammon (matter) and God simultaneously.
  - Prabhupada said, "**Sense gratification and devotional service go ill together**" (the actual quote) — trying to enjoy sense gratification and practice Krishna consciousness at the same time is a formula that does not work.
- So the duty of the spiritual master is to convince the disciple to give up the materialistic life and take shelter of the Lord's lotus feet — using so many examples and angles of vision to convince the student to give up.

## Buddha and the Mustard/Rice: Death Is All-Pervading

- **Buddha's grieving disciple:** a woman came to Buddha crying, "My lord, my infant child has just died. Why has this happened to me?"
- Buddha told her to go house to house begging for rice — but she could only accept rice from a household where **no one had ever died.** She had to ask first, "Has anyone in your family died?" and could take the rice only if they said no.
- She returned at night **empty-handed.** Buddha asked why. In the first house the father had died; in the second, the father; in the third, a daughter; in the next, a brother; in the next, a cousin — and so on, the whole village.
- Buddha told her, "So you are not special. You came asking why your child had died — but **death is all-pervading in this world.** Take it as a lesson to become renounced." That is what we must learn.
- First we must become internally clean, free of all material contamination, and then our devotion can begin.
  - Prabhupada said in a lecture that **real devotional service begins when one is completely free from lust.** Unfortunately, we still tightly cling to these things — so the duty of a saint is to convince the disciple, in various ways, to give up these contaminated things.

## Neutralizing Kali by Taking Shelter of the Lord

- We have an alternative — we don't have to kill ourselves in frustration; we have an alternative to all these problems.
- After describing the difficulties of Kali-yuga, Prabhupada quickly says: **one can neutralize the effects of Kali by taking shelter of the Lord.** No matter how bad the situation, we can find a conclusion.
- **The tornado analogy:** reading a website about tornadoes in America (which strike particularly the middle of the country) — powerful whirlwinds that destroy everything in their path: houses, cars, telephone poles. How does anyone survive a tornado? A picture showed a town in the Midwest with nothing left standing — yet the article said **everyone survived.** How? Because they knew where to take **shelter**: in the underground **cellars** of their houses.
- **The bomb-shelter analogy:** when the speaker was a child in the 1950s, everyone was afraid the Russians were coming to bomb America with nuclear bombs, so it was popular to build **bomb shelters** stocked with food — if the Russians attacked, people would hide there and survive.
- So however bad the situation, if we have knowledge we can find a way through.

## The Crabs and the Ocean: Taking Shelter in the Supreme Great

- **The crabs on the beach:** Prabhupada was once walking on the beach with some scientist-devotees. As they approached, all the crabs (which move sideways, not forward or back) disappeared into the ocean.
- Prabhupada asked why they did that, and explained: the crabs saw that danger was coming, and although they are very small, they knew the **ocean is very great**, so they would be **protected** in the ocean.
- In the same way, we are very small and there are so many dangers in this world — but we can take shelter in **Krishna, who is the Supreme Great**, and be safe.
- — **Queen Uttara's prayer** (**Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.8.9**): "O Lord of lords, Lord of the universe, You are the greatest of all mystics. Please protect me, for there is no one else who can save me from the clutches of death in this world of duality."
  - Krishna can protect a person, and can also kill a person. If Krishna wants to kill someone, who can protect him? And if He wants to protect someone, who can kill him?

## Shiva, Parvati, and Ravana: The Lord's Protection Is Supreme

- (From the teachings of Queen Kunti.) **Parvati** was seated on the lap of her husband **Shiva** in the higher planetary systems, and they were watching the battle at Lanka as Ramachandra fought Ravana.
- Toward the end of the battle, Ramachandra placed His arrow in His bow and was about to shoot Ravana for the final time.
- **Ravana** is a great devotee of Shiva — he worshipped Shiva, and sometimes Shiva is "like blind," giving demons boons and blessings.
- So Parvati turned to Shiva and said, "Prabhu, just see your bhakta — your devotee is about to be killed by Ramachandra; you should save him."
- This is very instructive to those who think that Shiva is supreme. (Some think Shiva is supreme — an exceptional personality in his own tattva, with his own followers and the Shiva Purana.) Here **Shiva himself** says: if the Supreme Lord wants to protect someone, who can kill him; and if He wants to kill someone, who can protect him?
- This is the mercy of the Lord: those who surrender to Him receive some protection **even in this world of birth and death**, so we can peacefully continue our devotional service.

## The Real Protection: Never Forgetting the Lord

- Of course, we are all still subject to birth, old age, disease, and death. When these things befall us, we should **not lose faith** ("In class we heard the Lord protects us, but…"). If such things happen, we should keep our faith and not conclude that we are not being protected.
- The **real prayer of a devotee** is not necessarily to protect the physical body, but to protect us from the influence of the **illusory energy**, so that we never forget the lotus feet of the Lord. That is the real protection.
- **Prahlad Maharaj's mood.** Nrisimhadeva offered Prahlad a benediction, and in their discussion (in the especially instructive **Seventh Canto**) Prahlad said:
  - "I do not fear you. You are very frightening to look at — your angry eyes, your pointed ears, your lolling tongue, your mane soaked with the blood of my father — but I am not afraid of these things. What I *am* afraid of is coming under the influence of this powerful **material energy.**"
- This is the only fear a devotee knows: the material energy is very powerful. Even great demigods like Brahma, Shiva, and Indra sometimes fall under its influence — what to speak of puny little earthlings like us.
- Our whole life, our whole effort in Krishna consciousness, is to get some **smaranam** — to be able to remember the Lord under any and all circumstances, and to remember Him **in love.**
  - You can only remember a person if you love them or hate them. **Kamsa** remembered the Lord constantly because he **hated** Krishna — but we want to remember the Lord in the mood of loving devotion.
  - According to the **Brahma-samhita**, this comes in small increments — little tiny steps; it is not easy. That which is gained after many lifetimes we don't want to lose — that precious memory of the Lord and our devotion. So we pray for that protection: "Let me never forget Your lotus feet."

## When It Is Right to Pray for the Body: Gajendra

- We should pray for the protection of the body only when we want to use it for **devotional service** — because that is selfless, meant for the Lord's service.
- This is described in the **Eighth Canto**, in the deliverance of **Gajendra.** In his purport, Prabhupada raises the question: is it right for a devotee like Gajendra to ask for physical protection — isn't that selfish?
  - The answer: **No.** If the devotee asks for protection of his body so that his service to the Lord remains **undisturbed** (which means that Krishna is not disturbed), that type of prayer is **pure bhakti** — a prayer full of devotion.
  - If that is our mood, the Lord will protect us.

## The Lord's Promises of Protection, in Proportion to Surrender

- The Bhagavad-gita is full of verses where the Lord promises to protect His devotee:
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 9.31** ("kaunteya pratijanihi na me bhaktah pranasyati"): "O son of Kunti, boldly declare that My devotee never perishes."
  - — **Bhagavad-gita 9.13** ("mahatmanas tu mam partha daivim prakrtim asritah"): the great souls, who are not deluded and not in maya, work under the protection of the divine energy.
- There are also many practical examples in the Bhagavatam of devotees who took shelter of the Lord and were protected, so they could continue the service that gave great pleasure to Krishna:
  - **Pariksit** was protected in the womb; also **Kunti**, the **Pandavas**, **Maharaj Ambarish**, and **Prahlad Maharaj.**
- Protection also comes in **proportion to surrender** — like anything: protection, mercy, or love. We are all asking Krishna for these things when we chant Hare Krishna. We are very small and tiny, begging the Lord for protection, for service, for mercy — ultimately for **love.**
- We would like everything the great devotees have — but we receive these things only in relationship to how surrendered we are to Krishna:
  - We'd like Lord Ram to embrace us, as He did Hanuman.
  - We'd like the Lord to appear as Nrisimhadeva, as He did to protect Prahlad Maharaj.
  - We'd like the affectionate glance of the Lord, as He gives to the Brijbasis in Vrindavan.
- But to get those things we must have the **same bhakti** as those devotees.
  - — **Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.6** ("sa vai pumsam paro dharmo yato bhaktir adhoksaje / ahaituky apratihata yayatma suprasidati"): devotion has to be of a particular quality — **unmotivated and uninterrupted** — and then Krishna can reciprocate fully with the devotee. Our desire should be *nishkama* — completely selfless. That is how Krishna will reciprocate with all of us.

## Draupadi: Full Protection Comes With Full Surrender

- **Draupadi** came to Krishna after her painful experience of being lost in the gambling match and then humiliated by **Dushasana**, who tried to strip her naked before so many men. She asked, "Lord, why did it take You so long to come to my rescue?"
- Krishna smiled and explained:
  - "First you took shelter of **Arjuna and Bhima** — you said, 'Can't you see what is happening to your chaste wife? My chastity is being demonstrated right before your eyes.' At that time I thought, 'I'm not really needed; you didn't call for Me.'" (Though, in a sense, Arjuna and Bhima did nothing.)
  - "Then you took shelter of **Drona**, the teacher of the Kauravas and Pandavas, asking him, 'You are a brahmana — how can you tolerate this?' So you didn't call Me; I thought I was not needed."
  - "Then you took shelter of **Bhishma**, the powerful warrior and respected grandfather of the dynasty. He didn't help, so you turned to **Dhritarashtra** and so many kings."
  - "Finally, you tried to protect yourself by holding onto your sari with two hands. I thought, 'She thinks she is very strong and can protect herself, so perhaps I should stay away.' Even as you raised your hands, you were still holding onto your sari with one hand, trying to protect yourself."
  - "**Only when you let go and raised both of your hands and called out, 'Hey Govinda! Hey Krishna!' did I come** — because I knew you wanted only Me."
- The point: **full protection comes when we fully surrender to Krishna.** This whole verse is about the anomalies of Kali-yuga and how we can be protected from its influence by taking shelter of Krishna — and to the degree that you surrender, you will be protected.

## Once Protected — What Then? Responsibility to Preach

- Once we get that protection, then what? Krishna protects His servants because they have a **very important responsibility.**
- **The soldier analogy:** a government spends millions of dollars supplying and equipping the young men who go to fight on behalf of the country — the best weapons, the best clothes — because they are doing very responsible service.
- Similarly, why do we pray for this protection, mercy, and love? Because we have an important responsibility: **to always share our good fortune with others.**
- The Lord Himself comes to this material world — not whimsically, but with a particular mission to deliver the conditioned souls — and those who help Him naturally receive some special facilities for that purpose. So we must always remember that this is a **preaching movement**: the Lord descends, and His representatives descend, for that purpose.
- Therefore, if we are very strict in our own practice of Krishna consciousness — strictly following the rules and regulations and chanting Hare Krishna — and if, according to our capacity, we are very active in helping our spiritual master and Lord Chaitanya preach Krishna consciousness, we can expect these things to appear in our own lives as well: **protection, mercy, and love.**
- Because a devotee's heart is so pure, the unfortunate things of Kali-yuga will not befall the devotee.
- So do not be in anxiety, do not be fearful. Just be a good devotee, and the Lord will protect you in your important services.
