## Opening: The Verse — Bhagavad-gita 9.34

- Greetings on the second night of the visit to San Jose, California. The class is from **Bhagavad-gita, Chapter 9, "The Most Confidential Knowledge," text 34** — the speaker is confident all the "shloka-walas" know the Sanskrit.
- **"man-mana bhava mad-bhakto mad-yaji mam namaskuru / mam evaisyasi yuktvaivam atmanam mat-parayanah" (Bhagavad-gita 9.34)** — "Engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee, offer obeisances to Me, and worship Me. Being completely absorbed in Me, surely you will come to Me."

### Prabhupada's Purport (read aloud)

- This verse clearly indicates that Krishna consciousness is the only means of being delivered from the clutches of the contaminated material world.
- Sometimes unscrupulous commentators distort the clearly stated meaning — that all devotional service should be offered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Krishna — and divert the reader's mind to something not at all feasible.
- Such commentators do not know that **there is no difference between Krishna's mind and Krishna Himself.** Krishna is not an ordinary human being; He is the Absolute Truth. His body, His mind, and He Himself are one and absolute.
- This is stated in the **Kurma Purana**, as quoted by **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Gosvami in his Anubhasya commentary on Chaitanya-charitamrta, Adi-lila, Chapter 5, verses 41–48** — there is no difference between the Supreme Lord and His body. Because the commentators do not know the science of Krishna, they hide Krishna and divide His personality from His mind or body — sheer ignorance — and some men make a profit out of misleading people.
- (A Nrisimha prayer is recited during the class: "O my Lord, O transcendental lion, I offer my obeisances to You, along with Mother Lakshmi. Sometimes flying in the sky, sometimes moving on foot, Your mane hairs glow with a wonderful brilliance; Your glance and the touch of Your nails are the source of auspiciousness.")

## The Essence of All Instruction: Always Remember Krishna, Never Forget Him

- BG 9.34 is one of the most important verses in the Gita — so important that it is repeated almost verbatim twice: here in Chapter 9, and again in **Bhagavad-gita 18.65** (the first two lines: "engage your mind always in thinking of Me, become My devotee, offer your obeisances to Me, and worship Me").
- **Padma Purana** gives a similar instruction: **always remember Krishna and never forget Him.** This is the essence of all instructions. All the rules and regulations we follow, all our chanting and service, are meant to bring us to the stage of always remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him.
- **The t-shirt story:** In Australia, a brahmachari came out of the ashram wearing a t-shirt that said on the front, "Always remember Krishna." As he walked past, the back read, "And never forget Krishna." "That was the best t-shirt I ever saw." People wear slogans showing where they're at; this is where a devotee is supposed to be at — to always remember the Lord and never forget Him, even for a moment.

### Measuring How Far You Are from the Goal

- From Bhakta Joe to the very advanced devotee, it is not easy to always remember the Lord. People want to measure how far they are from the goal — you can measure it by how often you remember Krishna versus how much time you spend in forgetfulness.
- **The stopwatch experiment:** Try one day — start the stopwatch when you wake up ("Krishna!"), stop it when you forget, start again when you remember. By the end of the day, maybe two or three minutes. It can't be done mechanically. The only way to remember someone 24 hours a day is if you **love** them — then it springs forth from the heart without trying. Like Jesus said, "My cup runneth over with love."

### Love vs. Lust

- Even this material world is a very perverted reflection of the spiritual world, where love becomes lust. **Prabhupada:** "Lust means for me, and love means for Krishna." Here in the material world, where we have come to forget God, it is "I, me, and mine" — all lust in various forms. In the spiritual world it is "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna."
- In Vaikuntha and Goloka Vrindavan, not a moment goes by without thinking of Krishna, because the residents are fully in love with Him. **Prabhupada** said, "There is never a moment that I forget the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna." How is it possible? Because he was a lover of God.
- So to fulfill this instruction of the Acharyas, the Shastras, and God — to always remember Krishna — we must awaken our love for God.

## We Are Unchaste to the Supreme Lover

- It is difficult because we are not **chaste.** To be **unchaste** means to have someone you love but to keep a lover on the side — an unchaste man is married but has a girlfriend (a "paramour"). In French, love is *amour*. To be unchaste means to have your childhood sweetheart but to cheat on her.

### The New York Governor Story

- Recently in the news, the Governor of New York — a very powerful man who presented himself as very moral, fighting crime, prostitution, and drug trafficking, "squeaky clean himself and just down on the bad guys," some said even over the top — was found by the FBI to have used taxpayers' money to visit prostitutes, paying between $5,000 and $10,000 an hour. "This is a Gita class," so the speaker won't go further, but this is **unchaste** — having a lawfully wedded wife in whom you can repose your loving sentiments, and yet keeping a lover on the side.
- The governor was publicly embarrassed and had to step down. "Who knows, maybe he's just a street sweeper now" — though he probably has money set aside.

### Pointing One Finger, Three Point Back

- We laugh, but we are laughing at ourselves — "He who laughs first laughs last." Pointing one finger at the accused governor, three fingers point back at us. How are we unchaste? "No, I'm a moral man — I have my wife and children and don't even look at other women."
- **We are unchaste to the Supreme Lover, God**, because we maintain affection for Maya, for Maya-devi's kingdom (this material world) — even as devotees. One of the **ten offenses to the holy name — the eighth offense — is to maintain material attachments even after hearing so much about the matter.**
- As said the previous day: 50% of the philosophy is to have nothing to do with this material world, and 50% is to devote yourself to God. We have nothing to be proud about; we are unchaste.

### Vaishnavas Are Always Humble

- Therefore Vaishnavas, however advanced, are always very humble — until that last vestige of independence, material enjoyment, sense gratification, and material desire is rooted out of the heart. And even then they are humble, because when you realize God you cannot help but be humble: He is so great that you realize how small you are.
- **Analogy:** Standing before a great mountain in material nature, you feel humble. "I'm a big guy," but out in nature's forces you feel small — "God help me." In the presence of someone very great, you feel humbled. This is how **Prabhupada defeated our pride** — he simply walked among us, the saintly person that he was, a Maha-bhagavata, and we felt very humbled.

### You Can't Love Mammon and God

- At present we maintain some affection for the material world, still holding on to our unchaste nature. **Jesus:** "You can't love mammon and God at the same time" (mammon being a very old English word, translated in the Bible perhaps in the Middle Ages). Or as **Prabhupada** said very aptly for the modern age: **"Material enjoyment and Krishna consciousness don't go well together."**

## To Love Krishna, Begin by Purifying the Heart

- If we really want to love Krishna — which is the goal — we must begin with purification of the heart.
- **How did we get here?** Trace it back through the eons of time — **"brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 19.151)** — since time immemorial we have been here, but the original starting point is Krishna. We were created by Krishna, and we hear He is the most lovable person. Logic says: I must have a loving relationship with Him.
- (The speaker jokes about testing the American audience on their history — Thomas Jefferson, John Hancock, "Give me your John Hancock" — versus his experiences elsewhere, e.g. Chile.)
- Once convinced that Krishna exists, is the source, and is the Supreme Lover — "two plus two equals four" — I must have a loving relationship with Him. And I am unsatisfied in this world: even the best loving relationship terminates at some point.
- **Prabhupada:** "Faith means to put your trust in something sublime." Even the best this world has to offer is really personal relationships. Without personal relationships you have everything but no love, and you are miserable; with love, even a poor man can be relatively happy. So divine love is everything.

### Love Means Knowing What Pleases and Displeases the Beloved

- Hearing Krishna is the Supreme Lover, we should want to love Him. To love someone, you ask them what pleases them and what displeases them, because if you please them there is love, and if you displease them, "Hey, get away."
- So loving God is not mere sentiment ("I just love Him") accompanied by all kinds of nonsense. **Love means to follow the laws of God.** **Prabhupada:** the laws of God = **religion** ("Religion is following the laws of God"). So study the Bible and learn to love God; study the Koran and learn to love God; study the Torah and learn to love God. Study the Lotus Sutras of Buddha — but there is no conception of God there; Buddhism is popular and attractive but is actually voidism.

### Approach a Bona Fide Spiritual Master (Bhagavad-gita 4.34)

- Where do you start to find out what pleases God? **"tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya / upadeksyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darsinah" (Bhagavad-gita 4.34)** — just try to learn the truth (the Absolute Truth, not some relative truth) by approaching a bona fide spiritual master; inquire from him submissively and render service to him. The self-realized souls can impart that knowledge because **tattva-darsi** — they have seen it, either with their eyes or with their ears.
- The guru who has actually seen God with his eyes is very rare. **Prabhupada says in the Upadeshamrita (Nectar of Instruction):** if such an uttama-adhikari is present, accept that person as your guru; but if such an uttama-adhikari is not present, or you are not in that part of the world, you can also accept the Lord's representative who has "seen" — **we see by hearing** — one who has full faith in Guru, Shastra, and Sadhu, who is himself fixed in that knowledge, exemplary; you can follow him.
- **The postman analogy:** Just as the postman takes the letter from the postbox and delivers it to the addressee, the spiritual master takes the message of God and gives it to the disciple. If you follow that message, you can become Krishna conscious.

### Tattva-darshi: We All Follow Prabhupada

- (Story: the speaker gave a disciple the name **Tattva-darshi** — "one who has seen Krishna" — *tattva* = truth, *darshan* = to see. The disciple wanted a different name, but "Tattva-darshi" it was.)
- Prabhupada is the actual **Tattva-darshi**, and we are all following him. Whether you took initiation from Prabhupada or from a follower of Prabhupada, everyone has equal opportunity to reach perfection.
- **The electrical-line analogy:** If a power line runs through the neighborhood, whether you plug in at the beginning, middle, or end, you get the full potency — it is not weaker at any point. So those who never saw Prabhupada have no less a chance; the message and the example are there to follow. **Prabhupada is the shiksha-guru for the whole world throughout the entire Kali-yuga** — an intimate associate of the Lord — and we can ask him, "Sir, what pleases your beloved and what displeases Him?"

## What Pleases Krishna

- Prabhupada tells us Krishna's likes: His favorite instrument is the **flute**; His favorite dhoti is **pitambara** (yellow); His favorite devotee is **Radharani**; His favorite place is (Vrindavan); and He likes foodstuffs like **halava and puri** — not lasagna and pizza.
- Of course we *can* offer lasagna and pizza, but Krishna is a person with His likes and dislikes, so let us maintain the tradition and not let that part become weak.
- **The changing prasadam story:** Traveling around now, the speaker finds lots of lasagnas, pizzas, and veggie burgers. He asks the Hindu mataji, "Can you cook some nice kofta balls?" "I'm sorry, I don't know how to cook." "How about some kitchari, rice, dal, and halava and puri?" "No, I can give you some Cheerios." **Prabhupada:** "Don't let this movement become like Christianity" — not an offensive statement, but meaning that Christianity has been watered down and compromised through the centuries. We must uphold our tradition; our ladies and men should learn all the original dishes, which are so nice. At least on the altar, everything should be very *paka* (properly prepared).

## What Displeases Krishna: The Demons — Shishupala

- We must also avoid what Krishna doesn't like. He doesn't like demons; He gets very mad when they blaspheme.
- **Shishupala's story:** He came out of the womb cursing God — speaking perfect language immediately, and immediately cursing Krishna. This went on and on. Krishna came to kill him for his own good, but Shishupala's mother pleaded, "He's a good boy; it's just bad association." (Even if their sons are murderers, rapists, or serial killers, mothers always think, "But he's a good boy, judge — why did you put him in jail?") Krishna agreed: "I'll give him 999 chances; the thousandth time he blasphemes Me, off with his head."
- **The Rajasuya sacrifice:** In the august assembly of the Kurus and Pandavas, they wanted to elect the special person to be honored first. Different people were recommended, and Bhima proposed Krishna. Shishupala objected: "This guy is born as a Vaishya but acts like a Kshatriya, has lots of girlfriends, he's just a rascal..." — Krishna counted 997, 998, 999, and Shishupala said "and he's just a rascal" (the thousandth offense). Krishna stood up (as in the famous painting) — "off with his head." **As much as He loves the devotees, He dislikes the demons.**

### The Magnet: Attracting Devotees, Repulsing Demons

- Like a magnet, Krishna has the equal ability to attract and to repulse. The devotees are His **"bosom friends"** (an antiquated word — "no one uses that anymore") — He embraces them chest to chest. (Aside about the temple president's big dictionary full of "fancy antiquated words.")
- Just as the Lord brings devotees close to His chest, the non-devotees are repulsed away from Him.
- **Definition of auspiciousness (given the previous day):** *Auspicious* means whatever brings you closer to God; *inauspicious* means whatever repels you further from God. So even in our neophyte, covered stage, we practice what the Acharyas say will make us bosom friends and lovers of the Lord.

### The Child Matures to Appreciate

- In the beginning we don't really understand — does a child know what's happening while the mother nurses, changes diapers, gives medical attention, and trains? As he matures, he begins to appreciate.
- **The argument with his mother:** At 17 the speaker had a big argument with his mother. In the end she started crying — **Prabhupada** said "a woman's ultimate weapon is her tears." She said, "Don't you remember all those times I changed your dirty diapers?" And he relented: "Oh Mom, okay — I'll mow the lawn, wash the car."

### More of What Displeases God

- We are all ears when the sadhus tell us what displeases God:
  - **He doesn't like [irreligion / false] religion** — He personally comes to destroy it.
  - **He doesn't like animal slaughter, especially of His favorite animals, the cows.** **Prabhupada writes** that World War II took place because of the mass slaughter of cows — "and only devotees will understand this." This is **global karma** (as opposed to individual karma): the whole world felt the effect of that horrific crime of killing so many innocent animals and cows.
  - **He doesn't like intoxication.**
- As we stop the bad things and do the good things, we get cured of the disease of material existence.

### The Doctor's Do's and Don'ts — the "Do Not" Shop

- **The doctor analogy:** When you go to the doctor, he gives you two things — the do's and the don'ts. Don't go out in cold weather, do stay home; don't overeat, do take fruit juice; don't go to work, do stay home. These are the **rules and regulations** — the rules are the don'ts and the regulations are the do's. Hardly anybody in society uses that phrase; it is like a phrase coined in Krishna consciousness.
- **The donut-shop story:** After a class at 26 Second Avenue, Prabhupada walked down the Bowery and looked in a donut shop where devotees were dunking donuts in coffee and smoking cigarettes. Prabhupada asked, "What is this shop?" Told it was the donut shop, Prabhupada said, "No, it's the **'do not' shop** — we have the do's and we have the don'ts."
- By avoiding the don'ts and doing the do's, the heart becomes purified, giving a chance for the dormant seed of love of Godhead to sprout in favorable circumstances. "It's very scientific."

## Become a Philosopher and Fight the Material Energy

- When Krishna consciousness is explained according to Guru, Shastra, and Sadhu — logically and reasonably — it simply makes sense. No one else has a philosophy like this. Everything else is just the impulse of the senses; even so-called philosophers merely speculate; and modern religion is superficial.
- We are blessed with knowledge, and we should all become philosophers — not the "thinker in an ivory tower," but philosophers in our own right. Armed with knowledge, we **stand and fight the material energy.** Unless you defeat the material energy, you can't go on to victory in Krishna consciousness.

### The Goal Is to Love God

- When you reach the point of giving up all attachments to the world, and the seed of *bhakti* grows and you love Krishna — that is the goal. The goal is to love God.
- **"yugayitam nimesena caksusa pravrsayitam / sunyayitam jagat sarvam govinda-virahena me" (Siksastaka 7):** "O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I consider a moment to be like twelve years; tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I feel all vacant in the world in Your absence." This is the goal — the reason we are here. Our success will be when you chant and you cry. It may be a long way off, but at least we know the goal.
- **The sailor analogy:** A sailor in the middle of the ocean has hope because he knows land is some distance away — he is a creature of the land, not the water. We are not creatures of this earth but of the spiritual sky. Though far out in the ocean of material existence, we know what the *param* (supreme goal) is.

### Pleasing the Spiritual Master

- When we come to that goal of always remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him, we give great pleasure to our Guru. That is what he wants — not our flower garlands or monetary contributions, but that his disciples make progress in Krishna consciousness.
- **Like parents:** "Do what you want — I just want you to be happy." So take any kind of service; but in the long run, after all is said and done, the question is: have you awakened your love for Krishna?
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura:** the real test of your bhajan comes at the moment of death — if you can call out Krishna's name spontaneously at death, that's it.

## Remembrance Must Be Favorable: Kamsa vs. the Devotee

- **Kamsa is an excellent example of Krishna consciousness** — in one sense. Once he heard the voice from the sky (the oracle): "You fool Kamsa! Don't you know that the eighth child of your sister Devaki will be the source of your death?" He took out a sword and grabbed his own sister by the hair, ready to slice her throat. "This is a demon."
- Her husband **Vasudeva**, a very good preacher, convinced him: "Not now — we promise you, when the eighth child comes, we'll give you the baby. And you have a bad enough reputation; people will think badly of you." (Demons care about reputation; **devotees are equipoised in honor and dishonor** — just thinking of Krishna, they don't want honor.) So Kamsa relented, and from that point on he never forgot Krishna — always thinking of Him.
- **But — Rupa Gosvami's criterion:** "always remembering, never forgetting" must be done **favorably**, in a loving mood, not in a mood of hatred. You can always remember someone if you love them *or* if you hate them. Kamsa hated. The real definition: **"anyabhilasita-sunyam jnana-karmady-anavrtam / anukulyena krsnanu-silanam bhaktir uttama" (Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.1.11)** — when first-class devotional service develops, one is devoid (cleansed) of all material desires, any tendency toward merging into God, and all fruitive activities; the devotee must serve Krishna **favorably**, as Krishna desires. Kamsa was doing nothing favorable.

### The Devotee: Every Detail Is a Sign of Love

- A devotee's every act is favorable — even the smallest. **Prabhupada** said, "Attention to detail is a sign of love."
- **Prabhupada and the Paris deities:** When installing **Radha-Parisisvara** in the Paris temple (four Radharani deities came from India), the deities were being bathed. From a distance Prabhupada saw a little mark on Radharani — he thought it was dirt from the packing in India. As the ceremony went on, Prabhupada got off the Vyasasana, looked at Radharani with such devotion, walked over, and gently wiped Her cheek. Actually it was a little blemish in the marble (not dirt or a flower petal), so Prabhupada checked it and walked back. "This is where I got the impetus to do deity worship — from seeing how much love and care my spiritual master had for the deity."
- **Saksi-Gopal (the young brahmana's prayer):** "O my Lord, You are not a statue; You are directly the son of Maharaja Nanda. Come back and witness. You are not a statue — You can walk, You can talk."
- **Cleanliness and punctuality are the essence of deity worship** — where the flower garlands go, the position of the flute, the offering, the Tulasi leaf on every preparation, the artiks on time, everything down to the detail.

### Follow the Footsteps of the Devotees, Not the Demons

- So although Kamsa is remembering and never forgetting, it is not favorable — so he is not listed among the great devotees. We want to think of Krishna favorably, following in the footsteps not of Kamsa, Hiranyakashipu, or Kumbhakarna, but of the humble devotees.
- As **Vaisasikha Prabhu** indicated the previous day: the easier path — the path of least resistance — is to become a very humble and insignificant devotee. Humility endears one to the Lord. Give up the sense of being master and become the humble servant. **Bible:** "The meek shall inherit the earth." Krishna, pleased with such an attitude, blesses the devotee with the highest reward.

## The Highest Reward: Going Home

- What is the highest reward? **Going home.** "I want to go home, Mommy — we're on vacation." Even after the longest vacation, there's no place like home; you have everything there — a great hotel room, the beach — but "I want to go home."
- We came to this world thinking it would be like a vacation, but after a while we realize there's no place like home. **Home is where the heart is.** If your heart is in this world, you'll think it's your home — and you'll "live in the ghetto even if you live in the best part of town," never happy. But realize your home is nothing less and nothing more than the highest realm.
- There is nothing higher than the transcendental lotus world of **Sri Vrindavan Dham.** As Gaudiya Vaishnavas — followers of Lord Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — our goal is not Vaikuntha but **Goloka Vrindavan**, and we are chanting the mantra to get there: **Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.**

## Practice Now — Be Honest About Where You Are

- We must practice now, because practice makes perfect. We can't falsely claim to be advanced — that is **sahajiya** ("sahaja" means to jump over). We know the goal, but we are honest about where we are.
- **The Woodside height story:** As a boy the speaker went to his wealthy grandparents' home in Woodside (he was born in Palo Alto, lived in Marin — south is Woodside, where "the upper crust lives"). Each year they marked how much he had grown. His grandfather (six foot one) wanted him to be "a real strapping boy," but he only reached six foot one-quarter, and his grandfather was displeased: "eat more, more, more." So we should honestly know where we are in Krishna consciousness and where the goal is.
- **Bob Marley** (song *Exodus*): "We know who we are, and we know where we're going" — Exodus, out of the material world, back home, back to Godhead.
- **Sadhana-bhakti** means devotional service in practice. We are doing the same things they do in the spiritual world — you will be doing these eternally — but there they do it in full Krishna consciousness, while now we do it "according to the stopwatch, a couple minutes a day." Let's grow to be "six feet one" — let's make our spiritual master proud by maturing into **unmotivated, uninterrupted** devotees of the Lord. That will please Gurudeva, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and the divine couple Sri Sri Radha and Krishna.

### The Little Boy Gadai and the Dinosaurs

- **Story of Gadai (a little boy in the audience, age seven):** He is one of the speaker's best friends. They have made a pact to go together to South America and find dinosaurs — the T-Rex and the Brontosaurus (Gadai calls it "Bronchiosaurus"). Why? Because years ago, when the speaker spent several months in the Amazon, coming in contact with nomad tribes going to the small tributaries deep in the jungle, they told him of "big lizards as big as a house." He first thought they were exaggerating, but so many nomads described and drew them (just as Gadai can draw "really good dinosaurs"). So they plan to go.
- But in our heart of hearts, "we're looking forward to the day that Gadai can sit here and give a very nice class on the Bhagavad-gita and Srimad-Bhagavatam, quoting shloka" — becoming a "shloka-wala" who knows the philosophy and leads nice kirtan. We are happy with this progress now and willing to be part of his "fantasies — I mean, his reality." But we know the ultimate reality, and we look forward to when he grows up and we "pass the staff of the renounced order over to him" as we depart this world: "You carry on with the mission of Srila Prabhupada."
- **The point:** The guru is okay that we have our fantasies now. We chant Hare Krishna, but when we walk home alone we have our fantasies about enjoying — and the guru tolerates it, but not forever. After some time we expect the child to grow up and understand the reality. Giving the guru that maturity is the greatest pleasure.
- **The greatest gift we can give our spiritual master** is to advance in Krishna consciousness and join him in his *nitya-lila* (eternal pastimes) in the spiritual world. That is the essence of this very beautiful verse of Bhagavad-gita.

## Sankirtana Story: The Woodstock Festival in Poland

- Because the audience are all Sankirtana devotees who "thrive on Sankirtana stories," the speaker shares an "up-and-coming" story — a work in progress.
- **The Woodstock Festival, Poland** — the speaker's group takes part every year. The main organizer, **Mr. Jurek Owsiak**, is a great philanthropist and humanitarian in Poland who raises 10–15 million dollars a year for poor and crippled children needing medical attention, through a January telethon. To thank the kids who help him raise the money, he puts on a gigantic rock festival that "makes the original Woodstock look like kindergarten": around 400,000 kids come to a wooded area, put up tents, and for three days it's rock and roll — with the motto "no violence, no drugs" (though they bring their beer).
- Many years ago Jurek called the Hare Krishnas because of some trouble the previous year: "If you guys come, there'll be peace" — he had seen them in action and taken their prasadam. They agreed and started small.
- **Now, for years:** the Hare Krishnas have a huge tent the size of an American football field, plus 10–20 tents depicting different aspects of Vedic culture, taking up an acre or two of land. **B.B. Govinda Maharaja** called it "the greatest show on earth." They give out (or sell for a very moderate price) **110,000 plates of prasadam in 72 hours**, distribute "crores and crores" of books, and fill the tent with 5,000–10,000 people in the evenings for rock concerts (Shelter came one year, and other groups — "the kids love it"). There is **Rathayatra three times a day** up and down the street that breaks the field, so everyone sees it; the kids chant and dance.
- One time Jurek said, "Are you part of my festival, or am I part of your festival?"
- **Jurek's stature:** During the life of the Polish Pope John Paul II — the most popular man in the country — Jurek Owsiak was number two. People really like him, and he likes the devotees.

### Deepening the Relationship: India, Nepal, Radha Kund

- The relationship "grew and grew." The speaker took Jurek to India (mostly Vrindavan). The next year they went to Nepal, climbing all the way up the **Kali Gandaki** to **Muktinath**, where 108 sacred rivers/spouts have been built by the sadhus at the top of the Himalayas. The freezing, icy water sprouts from 108 fountains, and the hardy pilgrim strips to his *kaupins* and runs through all of them. The speaker did it ("you can see I'm still blue over here"), almost passing out several times. They were filming a documentary. Asked the benefit, he said, "All the sins in your heart are destroyed." Jurek — a big man — stripped down and ran through.
- Back in India, at **Radha Kund**, Jurek looked at the water (green algae, film) and asked, "You're going to bathe in there?" "Yes. Any sins committed in your lifetime are washed away by bathing in this Radha Kund. Plus, you get love of God." Later a devotee followed Jurek back and saw him bathe **five times** in Radha Kund. "So if we have shraddha — faith — we'll know the effect of that."

### The Shalagram Shila

- **The shalagram story:** This year Jurek wanted to return to India to film sadhus for his television program. They went back to Vrindavan (Jurek was put off by how overcrowded and dirty Vrindavan has become). They visited **Padmanabha Gosvami**, in charge of the **Radha Raman temple**, for a special lunch. Goswamiji explained: the Radha Raman deity self-manifested from the **Shalagram Shila** — "not the stone, but the Vishnu... He is the God; the stone is the God, and He is self-manifest — no one created Him."
- Then Jurek reached into his pocket and pulled out a **Shalagram Shila.** Padmanabha Gosvami exclaimed, "Where did you get that?" Jurek said, "Maharaja took me to the Kali Gandaki. He was always talking about Vishnu as the stone, self-manifest, God Himself. I'm a Catholic; I can have faith in these things — God is all-powerful. I took one stone and have carried Him with me everywhere in the world for the last five years — but not in the bathroom. And this — **He**, not it — He is always protecting me." The speaker thought, "Oh my God, Krishna is so amazing" — this big-ego, big man carries a shalagram.

### The Indian Ambassador and "Woodstock India 2008"

- Last week the new Indian ambassador came to Poland. The devotees planned to meet him. Jurek found out and wanted to come too, to show the ambassador some of the India documentary footage and get help bringing a musical group to Woodstock.
- Jurek sat there in his blue jeans and tattoos while Nandini spoke about Woodstock. The ambassador said, "So, you're all from ISKCON, isn't it?" "Yes." "I saw your Kunti Mala. I'm a follower of ISKCON — they've done so much good work in my country and around the world, spreading our culture." Jurek was amazed.
- Then the ambassador turned to Jurek: "You're also ISKCON?" Jurek said, "You know George Harrison?" "Yes, of course — the Beatles." "You know, he was a kind of devotee. So I'm that kind of devotee." Nandini and Jayatam were stunned at what he was saying.
- The ambassador said: "Then you're the big Woodstock? We should work together — let's do **Woodstock India, 2008**." Jurek: "What?!" The ambassador: "Yes — if we work together, I'll get the official cooperation of the Polish government and the Indian government, and we'll do the biggest Woodstock ever. Just drop a list of what you want and you'll have it." They made a list of 17–18 points — this artist, that dancer (Anup Jalota, Hema Malini), Bollywood stars, yoga, an Ayurvedic school — with the stipulation that you can't present pure Krishna consciousness alone but must include other groups, lots of singing and dance, huge posters (the Himalayas, Govindaji, Taj Mahal, yoga), a main stage — "the church is just going to turn over in their graves when they see this." Everyone would think Jurek became a full-fledged Hare Krishna.

We're always thinking, how do I get some people from India? It's so hard, the fees are too expensive."
