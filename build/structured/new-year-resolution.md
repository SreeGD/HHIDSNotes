## The Theme: New Year's Resolutions

- Good evening to all on the eve of the new year 2025 — "we're leaving '24 behind, bye-bye" and coming to 2025.
- Maharaja has been asked to speak on the theme of **New Year's resolutions** — a tradition in the West that has somehow become a tradition in Bharat (India) as well, where people make resolutions on the eve of the year and try their best to follow them.

### New Year's Is Not on the Vaishnava Calendar

- Every New Year has somehow become a major event in ISKCON. But, "forgive me," this New Year's event is not in our annual **Vaishnava calendar.** Many of us follow the Vaishnava calendar because of its many holy days — Janmashtami, Nrisimhadeva's Appearance Day, Radhashtami, and so many more — yet "I studied it last night" and there is no New Year's Eve festival on December 31st.
- **The Vedic (lunar) New Year:** calculated by the lunar moon, the first day of the month of **Chaitra** (falling in April/May) is considered New Year's Day. "That's your New Year's" — not today. "We're Bharat. Jai Hind. Jai Sri Ram. So our new year is not tomorrow — it's the first day of the month of Chaitra."
- **The Gaudiya calendar New Year:** As a tighter-knit family, the **Gaudiya Vaishnava Sampradaya** — with the Yuga-avatar Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu as our Lord and Master — begins its new year on **Gaura Purnima** (Lord Chaitanya's Appearance Day). "That's our new year, not tomorrow."

### Keep the Tradition Strong — Don't Mix East and West

- With all due respect, celebrating December 31st is "mixing east and west." "But east is best, west is less — and I'm American."
- He recalls the line (attributing it to "Aurobindo Thakur," i.e. Kipling's famous verse): "East is East and West is West, and never the twain shall meet." "I'm sorry, sir — this Krishna conscious movement has united the East and West under one banner: the International Society for Krishna Consciousness."
- Someone may say, "Maharaja, don't be fanatic — this New Year's is celebrated from New York to London to France to China to Sydney; devotees can add it to the Vaishnava calendar." But **we have to keep our tradition as it is, because it is the very best tradition on this planet: Vedic culture.**

### Vedic Culture Once Spread Everywhere; Krishna Consciousness Is the Hope for the World

- Vedic culture once spread throughout the entire world, with **Maharaja Yudhishthira** as emperor. There were very few sinful activities; everyone was pious — Krishna Bhaktas or Rama Bhaktas (devotees of any incarnation). Due to the influence of the age of Kali, that has all deteriorated now.
- The best hope for the world is Krishna consciousness, because we see everyone with an equal vision. **"vidya-vinaya-sampanne brahmane gavi hastini / suni caiva sva-pake ca panditah sama-darsinah" (Bhagavad-gita 5.18)** — the enlightened person does not see the material body; he sees with equal vision the learned brahmana, the cow, the elephant, the dog, and the dog-eater.
- **The ABCs of spiritual knowledge (Krishna Consciousness 101):** "I am not the body; I am a spirit soul." Although there is a variety of spirit souls, we are all the same quality — God is very great, we are very small, but we all have the same spiritual quality. This is the **vision of unity** — "the United Nations of the spiritual world" — the peace formula: seeing everyone equally on the spiritual platform. That tradition can save the world.
- **Prabhupada:** "If it wasn't for this Krishna conscious movement spreading all around the world — in every town and village, with loud chanting of Krishna's holy name, distribution of prasadam, protection of the cows, gurukuls, etc. — if we hadn't started in 1965, this world would be in a much worse condition than it is now." That is the power of the holy names.

### Don't Water Down the Tradition

- We don't want to water down this tradition — the peace formula that can send everyone back home to the spiritual world where there is no birth, disease, old age, and death. When we introduce new things, it waters it down.
- So let's celebrate the new year on **Lord Chaitanya's Appearance Day (the full moon).** How? Not by going out to bars and restaurants and partying — but with **Kirtan.** "That's all — that's everything." The name of Krishna is absolutely non-different from Krishna, so if our party centers on the glorification of Krishna, that's the best.
- We can't convince the whole world to stop partying tonight, but we can keep our own tradition strong, and the pious people of the world will eventually join.
- **Prabhupada:** "This is my credit in preaching Krishna consciousness — as it is, like *Bhagavad-gita As It Is.* I didn't add anything that our Mahaprabhu or our previous acharyas gave, and I didn't subtract anything. **The package is complete; it just has to be delivered** — not by DHL, but by all of you going out and preaching this divine command."

### The Danger of Adding Things: The Cat in the Basket

- **Prabhupada's story of the cat in the basket:** At a prominent family's wedding, the bride entered with her bridesmaids. One bridesmaid noticed a sleeping cat along the aisle (someone might trip over it, or it might get scared and disrupt the ceremony). Nearby was a wicker/straw basket with a lid; she quickly put the cat in the basket, closed the lid, and the procession went on.
- Two years later, the naive younger sister, planning her own wedding, ordered someone to put a cat in a basket — "because that's what my sister did when she got married." So in her marriage too, someone bought a cat and put it in a basket beside the aisle.
- Many years later, when her daughter grew up and married, among the preparations she ordered a cat in a basket "because, well, it's tradition." Questioned about it, she said, "Don't you know it's part of our Vedic culture? It's obvious — my mother and grandmother did it; it's been a tradition for hundreds of years."
- **The point:** Was it a real tradition, or did somebody just add something? In that family it went on for generations and became "tradition," simply because one time one lady put a cat in a basket. So we must be careful about observing festivals that are not necessarily part of our Vedic tradition — when we go a little off course, it becomes watered-down "tradition."

### Taking Advantage of the Free Time

- If anything, we can participate in the **time off**, since New Year celebration means getting off work early and no school, everyone getting dressed up to go out. We can take advantage of that free time — as we are doing tonight. "This is the best, quote-unquote, Western New Year celebration: we come together in front of our beautiful deities and we chant Hare Krishna."
- We want to take advantage of every free moment to chant, and never miss an opportunity to chant. **Prabhupada:** while your friends go to office and school parties, "you're here at the real party — the *yajna* party, the *maha-mahotsava.*"
- **Vishnu Purana:** it is written very clearly that we don't want to waste even a moment of our lives — **"if even a moment of remembrance of Vasudeva, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is missed, that is the greatest loss, the greatest illusion, and the greatest anomaly."** So if you weren't invited to a party tonight, don't lament — you are using your time wisely, coming to the temple to hear Hari-katha, the best of celebrations. If this opportunity is missed, it's the biggest mistake we can make.
- **Kali-yuga Astakam:** "Aho, what sorrow — more painful than any other misery in this world — mistaking it as a mere piece of glass, the people have forgotten the real jewel: the holy names of Sri Hari. The holy names of Sri Hari are everything." So as soon as a devotee gets an opportunity — a Harinama party, chanting his rounds, or a significant temple event — he runs to chant.

## What Most People Do: Resolutions That Don't Last

- On this day most people drink and eat, then raise a glass and make a resolution — "I saw my parents do it, my neighbors, my friends."
- **But it's a joke:** research shows only **9% of Americans** who make resolutions complete them. They resolve to eat less sugar, stop smoking, not be late for work — but only 9% follow through. Further, about **23% give up their resolution by the end of the first week, and 43% quit by the end of January.**
- **The man on the plane:** A couple of days after New Year's Eve, a man asked Maharaja his resolutions. "No meat-eating, no intoxication, and 10–16 rounds a day." "That's unusual." "But it's very nice, and I keep my resolutions." "Really?" "I've kept them for 52 years." The man replied that last year he had "kept all my New Year's resolutions — tucked away in a newspaper on my bookshelf." In other words, he made them, put them in a book, and forgot about them. "What a joke."

## We Have Already Taken the Most Important Resolution

- We don't have to make new resolutions. As Vaishnavas, we've already taken the most important and significant resolution: **to be Krishna's devotee.**

### Hanuman's Resolution (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 34–37)

- **Hanuman famously says (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda, verses 34–37):** "I have made my resolution firm and unshaken in purpose. As a servant of Ram, I cannot act contrary to His will. My heart and soul are bound to serve Him, and I will never deviate from the path of Dharma." ("That's a good resolution." — "Jai Hanuman ki! Sitaram Lakshman Hanuman ki! Death to Ravana!")

### Love Is Reciprocal — Make a Vow, and Krishna Makes a Vow

- If a devotee is firm in his faith — "I will never deviate from the path of Dharma; I'll never deviate in my service to Radha and Krishna, Gaura-Nitai, Sita-Ram, Lakshmi-Narayan" — the Lord reciprocates, because **love is reciprocal.** If you love someone, they love you back. So if we make a vow to Krishna, Krishna makes a vow toward us — He is real, He is there, not just some concept of "love energy," but the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

### Lord Ram's Vow (Ramayana; quoted in Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.34)

- **Lord Ram's vow (Ramayana, also quoted in Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 22.34):** "It is My vow that if one only once sincerely surrenders unto Me, saying, 'My dear Lord, from this day I am Yours,' and prays to Me for courage, I shall immediately award courage to that person, and he will always remain safe from that time on."
- Maharaja has the audience repeat Ram's vow, so they have faith in taking their own vow and He will reciprocate: *"It is My vow, Ram says, that if one only once seriously surrenders unto Me, saying, 'My dear Lord, from this day I am Yours' — and prays to Him for courage: 'Ram, give me courage to control my senses' — Ram says, 'I shall immediately award courage to that person, and he will always remain safe from that time on.'"*
- **Are we safe in this world?** There is danger at every step. You can hire a security guard, make sure your tires have enough air — but danger still comes. **If Krishna wants to kill you, no one can save you; if Krishna wants to save you, no one can kill you.** But you have to be a Krishna Bhakta and show your allegiance. Like a boy says "I love you," the girl says "prove it — bring me flowers, buy me a diamond ring." So you must prove your love to Krishna, show Him you really love Him and want His protection, and He will reciprocate.
- **"ye yatha mam prapadyante tams tathaiva bhajamy aham" (Bhagavad-gita 4.11)** — "All of them, as they surrender unto Me, I reward accordingly."

### The Avanti Brahmana's Resolution (Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.23)

- **The Avanti Brahmana's resolution** — which every sannyasi is bound to repeat after chanting his Gayatri mantra: **"I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of Krishna. This was approved by the previous acharyas, who were fixed in firm devotion to the Lord, Paramatma, the Supreme Personality of Godhead."**
- "This is a good resolution — let's take it. You're not sannyasis, but you're all renunciates in one sense or another, because you can't practice Krishna consciousness while holding on to material desires. Let go. Take shelter of the lotus feet of the Lord." We must be resolute in purpose to give up material desires. Maharaja has the audience repeat the resolution.

## The Result of Such Resolutions: How Krishna Reciprocates

- If I make that resolution, what will the result be? How will Krishna reciprocate? We are expecting Him to reciprocate.
- **Prabhupada** was once asked the meaning of the Hare Krishna Maha-mantra. He said it means, "**My friend, my friend, my friend.**" Krishna is our ever-well-wishing friend, because when we fall from the spiritual world, He comes with us as the Supersoul and stays with us until we get back — even if we're a tree, a dog, or a cat. Krishna is the best friend of every living entity.
- So if you pray with this resolution — "I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in Your service; that's my New Year's resolution, Krishna" — how does He reciprocate? How do we know He's listening?
- **Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.23 (quoted as 11.23.31):** "Lord Krishna continued: His mind thus determined, that most excellent Avanti Brahmana was able to untie the knots of desire within his heart. He then assumed the role of a peaceful and silent sannyasi mendicant. By that resolution, Krishna was pleased with him and relieved him of his material desires."

### Becoming Free from Material Desires: Chanting

- How do we become free from material desires? Not by becoming a sannyasi and chanting the Avanti verse after every Gayatri. **We become free by chanting Hare Krishna.**
- **"nama cintamani krsnas caitanya-rasa-vigrahah / purnah suddho nitya-mukto 'bhinnatvan nama-naminoh" (Chaitanya-charitamrta Madhya 17.133 / Padma Purana)** — Krishna is fully present in the sound of His name. We chant His name.
- **The shower analogy:** Just as when you take a shower and look at the drain, all the muddy water is going out — when you chant Hare Krishna, after some time you look in the mirror and see, "I'm not getting older, I'm getting younger; there's that effulgence."
- **Prabhupada in London:** Walking across the street, a policeman grabbed the arm of Shyamasundar Prabhu (Prabhupada's secretary) and said, "Look, your teacher's glowing like the sun." Prabhupada was 74 — "glowing like the sun."

### Be Resolute in Purpose (Bhagavad-gita 2.41)

- **"vyavasayatmika buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana / bahu-sakha hy anantas ca buddhayo 'vyavasayinam" (Bhagavad-gita 2.41)** — "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one" — not diverted by a new car, new house, beauty pageant, or cricket. "O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- The stricter you are, the better.

### The Result of Strictness: Love of God (Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.2.40)

- **"evam-vratah sva-priya-nama-kirtya jatanurago druta-cittah..." (Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.2.40):** "By chanting the holy name of the Supreme Lord with fixed attention and resolute in purpose, one comes to the stage of love of God. Then the devotee is fixed in his vow as an eternal servant of the Lord, and he gradually becomes very much attached to a particular name and form of the Personality of Godhead. As his heart melts with ecstatic love, he laughs loudly, or cries, or shouts; sometimes he sings and dances like a madman, and he is indifferent to public opinion." ("Today's the day for taking this resolution... someday your heart's going to melt, if you're strict and stick to the process.")
- **Vishnujana Swami's question:** He asked Prabhupada, "We're on the street dancing like crazy people — should we stop?" Prabhupada said, "No, don't worry about the nonsense. You just chant and dance to your heart's content."

## Reaffirm the Vows You Have Already Taken

- Our duty today is not to make up new, trivial vows ("I'm going to brush my teeth three times a day, take three baths, not speak harshly to my wife, not hide money from the government"). We should already be fixing our vows as Brahmana-Vaishnavas. We don't add new vows — we must be **strict in the vows we've already taken.** It's not impossible or difficult.

### These Are Strict — "Terrible" — Vows

- **Prabhupada on the Jaladuta:** As the boat plied toward the coast of America, before landing in Boston, Prabhupada thought: "If I tell these American people to stop eating meat, stop taking intoxication, stop illicit sex and gambling — which I'll have to tell them — I think they'll say, 'Old man, take your message back to India.'" He thought they would reject him, because those vows are, in his word, "horrible" — for a materialistic person, worse than death.
- **Bhishma's vow:** The prince **Devavrata** took a vow of celibacy so his father the king could take a second wife (one queen wanted her own son to be king, not the elder son). When Devavrata vowed to remain a brahmachari his whole life, the demigods shook in fear and showered flowers on him, saying, "Your name is **Bhishma** — one who has taken a terrible vow." (Celibacy.) The demigods are mixed devotees with some material desires, so the idea of no sex, no intoxication, no gambling is "horrible" to them — but Bhishma took it, earning the honorable name "one who has taken a terrible vow."
- **"You are also Bhishmas and Bhishminis"** — you've taken terrible vows: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no pan, no promiscuous sex, no gambling. How can you do it? Because you're getting the higher taste by chanting Hare Krishna. **Krishna is the reservoir of all pleasure, and He is present in the sound of His name.**
- **Chanakya Pandit:** "You can't give up something unless you get something better." If you have ten rupees and I give you a crore, no problem — you can give up the ten. So what's the best of the best? Hare Krishna.

### Material Desires Fade Away with Chanting

- If you're a sincere chanter, material desires won't be an obstacle. They may come to your mind for some time — just chant, "because fear personified is afraid of the holy names."
- **The bubbles analogy:** In a lake or pond, the mud gets agitated and bubbles rise to the surface — a disturbance below from fishes swimming by. **Prabhupada** said even transcendentalists, due to their past, sometimes get little material desires arising in the mind; it happens to every chanter until you're a pure devotee. But chant "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna" — and where are the bubbles? Where are the material desires? They've faded away.

## Gratitude: Lord Chaitanya and Prabhupada Made the Process Easier

- We should be thankful that Lord Chaitanya made this process easier for the people of this age, and ever more grateful to Srila Prabhupada for adjusting Lord Chaitanya's instructions to the 21st century.
- **Lord Chaitanya was very strict** as a sannyasi, and His followers were almost as strict — the ladies could offer obeisances only from 100 meters away; He was very strict with His brahmacharis, sannyasis, and even grihasthas. That was required in Vedic society 500 years ago.
- **Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Thakura** made it a little easier: for the purpose of spreading Krishna consciousness to the British English-speaking people, he sometimes dressed as an English gentleman and rode in motor cars (though sannyasis are meant to walk everywhere barefoot). The British thought, "Here's one intelligent Indian gentleman — let me hear what he has to say." And he would tell them, "You English people, you chant Hare Krishna." That was appropriate for the 1930s.

### Prabhupada in Tompkins Square Park

- When Prabhupada came to the West, it was full of hippies. (Someone asked, "Prabhupada, do you know what a hippie is?" He said, "You know better than me — no, I saw them, I've lived with them.")
- Prabhupada didn't give a long-winded sermon or threaten anyone with hell. He sat down in **Tompkins Square Park** among the young students with a **bongo drum** — "like the little size of a mridanga" — and in a weak, elderly voice chanted "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna." The immigrants (Polish, Greek, Indian) in that part of the city would come. **Brahmananda Prabhu** told Maharaja that the first kirtan Prabhupada chanted there, he sang for **three and a half hours**, and out of the crowd of 200 (Brahmananda counted them), no one moved an inch — no microphone, no sound system, his elderly voice not projecting far.
- **Why?** The transcendental sound vibration chanted by a pure devotee of the Lord. **Prabhupada:** "The chanting of Hare Krishna is best heard from the lips of a pure devotee." The same with preaching — we listen to Prabhupada's recorded lectures (many in Hindi, some in Bengali) and hear his kirtans (*Vande 'ham Sri Guroh...*).

### Shabda-brahma: Prabhupada's Recorded Kirtans and Lectures

- In ISKCON we have our kirtan melas. "My favorite kirtaniya is Srila Prabhupada." His kirtan should keep ringing through our temples, sound systems, iPhones, and home speakers, because that sound vibration is truly **ceto-darpana-marjanam** (cleansing the mirror of the heart) and will water the seed of bhakti — **Shabda-brahma.**

### Hyderabad Story: "Why Are You Sleeping When I'm Speaking?"

- Maharaja traveled with Prabhupada and some devotees from Kolkata to Hyderabad, where Prabhupada installed the big, tall Radha-Krishna deities present there today. On arrival, Maharaja was exhausted — he'd had only "some crackers, not firecrackers" and hadn't slept in two days.
- Prabhupada went straight to the farm outside the temple because some villagers wanted to hear about Krishna. He sat under a tree; 20–30 villagers came. At the end, a lady asked, "Swamiji, can you please tell us why Lord Ram descended to this planet in Treta-yuga?" Prabhupada smiled and said, **"To teach us the difficulty in accepting a wife"** — a humorous reply (referring to what He went through with Sita).
- Then Prabhupada began speaking in Hindi, and the tired Maharaja fell asleep. Suddenly he heard Prabhupada's voice: **"Indradyumna, why are you sleeping when I'm speaking?"** ("When the spiritual master chastises you, you feel smaller than an ant.") "Prabhupada, because I don't speak Hindi." Prabhupada said, **"That is nonsense. Not understanding Hindi — that's nonsense, because this is Shabda-brahma, transcendental sound vibration. It will purify your heart. Hindi, English, Bengali, whatever — you sit and listen."** Maharaja "never slept in another class in the last 52 years."
- So even those of us who don't know Bengali or Hindi, and don't know the meaning of the songs Prabhupada sang, still get as purified when we hear them, because it is transcendental sound vibration. Prabhupada made it easier than his Guru Maharaja, who made it easier than Lord Chaitanya — "so much so that no one can escape this network of Sri Krishna's Sankirtana."

## Prasadam: Lord Chaitanya's Secret Weapon

- If you can't do anything else, Prabhupada has given us **Bhagavat-prasadam**, which will also purify us.
- **The Paris priest story:** Prabhupada was with the devotees in Paris and was upset that they hadn't invited enough important people to see him: "I'm coming all the way from Vrindavan and you don't have anyone important to see me. I'm bearing the most important message of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Go out and bring some people." They all ran out. Maharaja went to a famous priest — the second priest in charge of **Notre Dame** — whom he'd met while doing Sankirtana outside Notre Dame (the priest had questioned whether this was a different religion; Maharaja had said, "There's one God"). The priest wasn't really interested but came one evening as a favor.
- Dressed in his robes, with a cross and a long beard, the priest listened as Prabhupada preached that the Krishna conscious movement is the solution to all problems in society. Prabhupada asked, "Are you interested?" The priest looked at Maharaja as if to say, "Why did you bring me here?" So Prabhupada said, "All right — bring prasad." (Prabhupada knew that if the priest wouldn't hear the philosophy or chant, taking prasadam would still act.)
- **Prasadam is Lord Chaitanya's secret weapon.** People come to Govinda's restaurant not to chant or hear philosophy, but to eat — "one of the greatest pleasures in life." "People live to eat; they don't eat to live. We eat to live," and respect food as prasadam.
- **The gulab jamun:** It had been a feast day (Balaram's Appearance Day). One devotee had made big gulab jamuns dripping with juice. (Aside: "Here I always get gulabs a little hard, but in the West we make them soft — we've improved upon your gulabs.") Prabhupada deftly took a dripping gulab and told the priest, "Put your hand out." He placed the dripping gulab in the priest's hand and said, "Take, eat." The priest hesitated — "Eat this?" Prabhupada said, "Yes, in our Indian tradition we eat it in one bite." Not wanting to be impolite, the priest ate it, juice going all over his beard and tunic. Prabhupada said, "That is all — you can go now." He had taken prasad from Radha-Krishna.

### There's Something for Everyone, A to Z

- There is something for everyone in the Hare Krishna movement, from A to Z. It is our duty as followers of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and Srila Prabhupada to conceive ways that people can either hear the chanting of Hare Krishna, take a book to read, dance with us, or at the very least take prasadam.
- **Book distribution:** During his 11 years of book distribution, Maharaja would take the mangala sweets from the mangala-arati, cut them into little pieces, and give a piece of maha with every book sold. "Oh, thank you." "Thank you means you eat it now" — he was a little bold — "because it'll get stale after a while." He'd make them eat it: "Thank you very much. Now you can go."

## Stay Fixed on the Goal

- Our resolution today and tomorrow is simply to **maintain and improve upon the vows we've already taken**, and to always remain fixed on the goal. So we must hear about Maya-purdham/Mathura, the glories of Vrindavan, and the pastimes and personalities there — who the devotees are who live there with Mahaprabhu and Nitai and the Panca-tattva. That's our goal. "We know who we are, and we know where we're going," and if we forget the goal we may lose our determination to follow our vows.

### The Disneyland Story

- It's not easy to remember the goal, but you must remember it constantly. **Mundane example:** In the summer of 1957, as a young boy, Maharaja's parents said they would drive from San Francisco to Los Angeles to go to **Disneyland** — "every American boy's dream, to see Mickey Mouse and Donald Duck" (just as the young boys here go to the airport to see Gaura-Nitai). On the hot 12–15-hour drive, squashed and fighting with his siblings in the back seat, he kept asking, "Mom, are we there?" "No, son, ten more hours." He fixed his mind on Disneyland — Donald Duck, Mickey Mouse, the rides — the whole way, and because he stayed fixed on the goal, he made it. In an absolute way, we can tolerate any austerities, difficulties, or challenges on the path home by refocusing over and over on the goal.

### The Goal: Prema (Chaitanya Mahaprabhu)

- The goal is described very clearly by Lord Chaitanya — not in a long-winded lecture ("elegance is truth spoken concisely"). **"prema pum-artho mahan"** — the perfection of Krishna consciousness is to achieve **prema**, pure love for Krishna.
- **"O Govinda, feeling Your separation, I consider a moment to be like twelve years or more; tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain; I'm feeling all vacant in the world in Your absence"** (Siksastaka 7). Is anything equal to that? No. That is the highest perfection.
- How do we achieve it? Through **strict practice of Krishna consciousness** — following the rules and regulations, and most importantly our chanting. **Prabhupada: "Work now, samadhi later."** You have to work every day to achieve the goal.

### Special Mercy on Rare Occasions

- Sometimes aspiring devotees get special mercy — **Guru-kripa** or **Krishna-kripa.**
- **Tota-Gopinath in Jagannath Puri:** When Maharaja first went there in 1972, he was told that even an aspiring sadhaka, if he dances in ecstasy in kirtan before Tota-Gopinath, is sometimes blessed with **Prema-bhakti.** He asked the pujari if it had ever happened; the pujari said yes, on two or three occasions — an aspiring devotee came and danced for ten hours and suddenly was crying genuine tears of ecstasy.
- **Mahaprabhu's mercy:** Famously, through the **Vasudeva-amrta-prapa**, He delivered the leper Vasudeva. And He delivered the lions, tigers, snakes, birds, and deer in **Jharikhanda.** These are not made-up plays — they were dumb animals. As Mahaprabhu passed through chanting the holy names ("Hari Haraye Nama Krishna Yadavaya Nama..."), the forest animals were delivered.
- **The footprints in Jharikhanda:** Maharaja went to the Jharikhanda forest, where a temple with some sadhus worships the place where Mahaprabhu delivered the animals. The ecstatic kirtan was so powerful that even the rocks melted — "I saw it" — and because the rocks melted, the footprints remain: big imprints of elephants, the devotees' footprints, and nearby a tiger's footprint and a deer's footprint (many of them). As **Chaitanya-charitamrta** says, in the kirtan the animals were freed from envy, and on the level of *bhava* the deer embraced the tigers, their natural enemies, dancing together. Maharaja saw the footprints — and the snakes' trails — extending for **two kilometers** as the kirtan went through the forest.
- **Mahaprabhu's footprint:** In one place they saw the two footprints of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in the rock. Maharaja offered dandavats, putting his head on the footprint, and a little rock went into his head, bleeding — "very nice, no problem." He later took that little rock (part of Mahaprabhu's footprint) and wears it in a **kavaca** on special events.
- Following the footprints two kilometers into the forest, the guide stopped him: "You have to stop here — there are still tigers, and you're not Lord Chaitanya; they'll eat you. They're the ones that weren't delivered." So the Maha-mantra can deliver even animals — but those are special cases. **Our process is slow, but steady wins the race.**

### "Why a Minimum of 16?"

- **The godsister's initiation:** Taking initiation before Srila Prabhupada, she was asked the rules and regulations, then, "How many rounds will you chant?" She said, "Srila Prabhupada, I vow to chant a minimum of 16 rounds every day." Prabhupada looked over and said, **"Why a *minimum* of 16? Why not more?"** So we can chant more.

### The 18 Years to Study Prabhupada's Books

- We can also read Prabhupada's books. One devotee scholar estimated it requires **18 years to thoroughly study** them: two years for the 25 small books; two years for the 12 medium books (learning all the verses); one year for Bhagavad-gita; ten years for the 18,000 verses of Srimad-Bhagavatam (memorizing verses); and three years for Chaitanya-charitamrta. "Prabhus, we have a lot of work to do — so don't procrastinate."

### Don't Procrastinate: Prahlada's Friends

- Don't say, "This year I've got college / a new job / a new baby." **Life is short and then you die.** To put off for tomorrow what can be done today is procrastinating — and we don't know if we'll see tomorrow.
- **Prahlada Maharaja's friends:** When the teachers left the gurukul room, Prahlada's friends had "a New Year's celebration" — dancing and all kinds of nonsense. Prahlada said, "Stop, my dear friends. Human life is not meant for these frivolous activities; it's meant for becoming Krishna conscious." **"kaumara acaret prajno dharman bhagavatan iha / durlabham manusam janma..." (Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.6.1)** — one who is sufficiently intelligent should use the human form from the very beginning of life, from the tender age of childhood, to practice devotional service, giving up all other engagements.
- His friends rebelled: "Prahlada, we're only five — we're young, we're meant to play now; we'll become Krishna conscious when we're old." That's procrastinating. Prahlada asked, "What is your definition of old?" "Old means just before you die." Prahlada replied, "Don't children die? Don't middle-aged people die? Yes. So if old means just before you die, then young boys die too — you could all be old *now.* So take to Krishna consciousness immediately." Because he was a pure devotee (one of the twelve Mahajanas), his words were potent, and they all became devotees.
- **Prabodhananda Saraswati (Vrindavana-mahimamrta):** "Friend, when will you die? Do you know? Do not even infants sometimes die unexpectedly? With clear intelligence, without attachment to the body and senses, and without stopping to think, run to Vrindavan." So do not procrastinate — "this may be your last year, last month, or last day." Take it very seriously; if you have time, chant Hare Krishna. There will be challenges, but push ahead.

### Bhaktivinoda Thakura's Poem (Saragrahi Vaishnava)

- In conclusion, **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** encouraged us in one of Maharaja's favorite poems:
  - *"Push thy onward march, O soul, against an evil deed that stands with soldiers, head in lust. A hero be indeed. Maintain thy post in spirit world as firmly as you can, and never let matter push thee down. O stand, heroic man. O Saragrahi Vaishnava's soul, thou art an angel fair; lead, lead me on to Vrindavan, and spirit's power declare. There rests my soul from matter free upon my Lover's arm — eternal peace and spirit's love are all my chanting charms."*
